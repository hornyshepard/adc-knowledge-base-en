**Verdict on SRC-029:** a solid, systematic textbook, the backbone of the DIG skill. Strengths: the authors' own models (the Zahay definition, Labrecque's 5Cs, the Valid Metrics Framework), step-by-step processes (a website in 7 steps, the PPC campaign in 5 stages, content marketing in 4 steps, demand generation in 6 steps), useful cases (Domino's, Joshie/Ritz-Carlton, Vacation Matchmaker, #EmpowerMoves, Olivia Rose, Magnolia Bakery, La Brea vs. Square). Limits: a direct marketing perspective (the creative execution in a "secondary role", the 40/40/20 rule with no evidence), many statistics from vendors (HubSpot, Constant Contact), inconsistent definitions (CTR p. 299 vs. p. 315; CTOR 32% vs. 26%), data that go out of date (the cookie calendar, Expanded Text Ads). The pages cited are the PDF pages (printed = PDF − 14).

**Verdict on SRC-030:** a thin, generic book, with no sources at all, probably AI-assisted; the title promises SEO, but there is no SEO chapter; it recommends discontinued tools (Google Optimize). I extracted only what adds something beyond SRC-029: the steps of the A/B test, the typology of attribution models, retargeting by stage, over-personalization, the KFC "FCK" and Spotify Wrapped cases and the explanation of Share a Coke. No notes of its own on segmentation, AI, trends (generic).

## Feedback on the taxonomy

- **CRM and retention** (CLV, IDIC, loyalty programs) have no clear home: the DIG scope mentions email, MKT mentions customer value. I put CRM in DIG; it would deserve an explicit mention in the DIG or MKT scope.
- **Digital customer service** (the move from the call center to self-service, crowd servicing, predictive; omnichannel) fits well in neither EXP nor DIG; I left it unextracted as a separate note.
- **B2B demand generation / ABM / lead scoring** sits between DIG and CLI; I chose DIG, with `also: client-business`.
- **UX and information architecture** (web conventions, navigation, usability) overlap with ART and PRT (the UX portfolio); an explicit "UX" mention in the ART or DIG scope may be worth it.
- **Law and privacy** (GDPR, CCPA, CAN-SPAM, the FTC on native ads and influencers) appear across skills; there is no ethics/regulation skill outside AIM. A `30-maps/` map "Data, privacy and law" would be useful.
- **Measurement and attribution** appear in ADV (effectiveness), DIG (KPIs) and SOC; a cross-cutting map would avoid three parallel vocabularies.
