**Verdict:** a core book for the EXP skill. It has a complete vocabulary for the brand experience (immersion, flow, theming, sensory marketing, emotional touchpoints, phygital, storyliving) and over 60 mini-cases, many useful for activations. Limits: its own frameworks (7E, ETM, EXQUAL) are not empirically validated; the style is academic and repetitive; the cases are descriptive, with few measured results; some technology data are out of date (iBeacon, Dash Button, Google Glass). **Warning:** the text copy is missing the contents of several figures and tables (Tables 2.1–2.3, 4.1 EXQUAL, Figures 4.1, 6.1, 7.1, 12.1 etc.). The notes describe only what appears in the text and flag the gaps.

### EXP (experiential-marketing)
- EXP-1201: The experiential marketing mix (7E) replaces the 7Ps with decisions centered on the customer experience
- EXP-1202: The customer experience is a process with drivers, markers and outcomes (the customer experience marketing framework)
- EXP-1203: The customer experience has five markers: holistic, cumulative, subjective, assemblage, contrasting
- EXP-1204: A successful experience produces four outcomes: value, transformation, competence, memorability
- EXP-1205: Customer experience has gone beyond CRM: marketing moves from consumption as buying to consumption as living
- EXP-1206: The experience unfolds in four stages (pre-consumption, purchase, core, remembered) and each is designed separately
- EXP-1207: Immersion is the gateway to a pleasant experience and depends on five factors
- EXP-1208: A memorable experience does not necessarily require the extraordinary: the ordinary vs. extraordinary debate (*debate*)
- EXP-1209: Theming unifies the setting and turns an ordinary offering into an experience
- EXP-1210: Sensory marketing builds five brand signatures: olfactory, auditory, tactile, visual, gustatory
- EXP-1211: The Experience Territory Matrix crosses the respect the customer perceives with the firm's customer-centricity
- EXP-1212: The quality of the experience is measured beyond satisfaction: EXQUAL extends the logic of SERVQUAL
- EXP-1213: Exchange: in the experiential mix, price gives way to value co-created with the customer
- EXP-1214: Collaborative marketing has three levels of involvement: DIY, collaboration, co-innovation
- EXP-1215: Extension: the experience must be conceived as a continuum with no breaks, from the store to the home
- EXP-1216: Empathy capital replaces "People": employees must first think like the customer
- EXP-1217: Emotional touchpoints: the touchpoint is defined by the emotion it produces, not by the channel
- EXP-1218: The "emotional touchpoints" cards: a six-step workshop to find out what the customer feels at each contact
- EXP-1219: The emic/etic process: the experience is designed by looking at the consumption culture from the inside and from the outside
- EXP-1220: The phygital experience combines the reassurance of physical contact with the interactivity of digital, with no breaks between channels
- EXP-1221: Phygital experiences have six typologies: connected, web, VR/AR, socio-digital, re-creative, cyborg
- EXP-1222: Storyliving: brands that make customers live their story outdo brands that only tell it
- EXP-1223: Case: the Coca-Cola pavilion at Expo Shanghai 2010 turns the "Happiness Factory" universe into a multisensory route
- EXP-1224: Case: "Badoit Express" links a mineral water to gastronomy through a surprise dinner in the Paris metro

### INS (consumer-insight)
- INS-1201: The experiential perspective: consumption also means fantasies, feelings and fun (Holbrook & Hirschman)
- INS-1202: Flow: total absorption occurs when the challenge is high and matched to the person's skill
- INS-1203: Consumer emotions are primary or secondary and fluctuate throughout the experience
- INS-1204: Holbrook's typology of value: eight values with which any experience, physical or digital, can be analyzed
- INS-1205: The empowered consumer stands on four pillars: competence, (re)creation, awareness of rights, resistance
- INS-1206: The postmodern consumer is fragmented and paradoxical and buys cultural meanings, not products
- INS-1207: Semiotics decodes the signs of a category to find the cultural meaning that consumers cannot put into words
- INS-1208: Empathy capital is built with the empathy map and the six aspects of the McLaren model
- INS-1209: Projective techniques bring to the surface what consumers do not say directly (the Haire/Nescafé case)
- INS-1210: Experiential ethnography works with consumers, not on them: socialization, collaboration, self-confrontation (+ netnography, mobile auto-ethnography)

### BRD (brand-strategy)
- BRD-1201: Emphasis: experiential communication moves from brand content to brand culture
- BRD-1202: Cultural branding (Holt): a brand becomes iconic by delivering a myth that resolves a cultural contradiction
- BRD-1203: Case: Nike "Just Do It" built the myth of the solitary hero who wins through willpower

### MKT (marketing-fundamentals)
- MKT-1201: Customer-dominant logic (CDL) puts the customer at the start of the value chain, not at its end (GDL → SDL → CDL)

### STO (storytelling-content)
- STO-1201: The brand story is built from the positioning or on the hero's arc and persuades through narrative transportation

## Feedback on the taxonomy

- **Research methods** (ethnography, projective techniques, netnography, diaries, introspection) went into INS, but they are numerous and cross-cutting. A "consumer research" sub-path in the INS SKILL.md would help.
- **Measuring the experience / service quality** (SERVQUAL, EXQUAL, NPS) has no clear home: it sits between EXP, MKT and ADV (effectiveness).
- **Retail / shopper marketing** appears in the EXP scope only as "retail", although many notes (phygital, servicescape, Amazon Books) are in fact shopper marketing.
- **The employee experience / internal branding** (empathy capital, employees as "emotional motivators") does not fit well anywhere; I put it in EXP and INS, with `also: client-business`.
- **Phygital** sits on the EXP–DIG border. I put it in EXP, with `also: digital-marketing`.
- The skill hubs (`[[EXP]]`, `[[INS]]` …) do not exist yet in `20-skills/*/SKILL.md`. I avoided them as links, so that they would not produce a "broken link" at `check`.

The sharing economy (Surf Air, the Airbnb/Couchsurfing/Wwoofing study), influencer strategies (Pitkin), consumer socialization (Piaget/Bandura), the eight dimensions of "empathetic marketing" (Ingwer), the B&B case. They are either treated briefly by the author or of little relevance to ADC. The qualitative diary and subjective personal introspection are mentioned only in INS-1210.
