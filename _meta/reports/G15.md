**Verdict:** the core textbook of the ADV skill and the most rigorous strategy source in the library. It is built on the Rossiter–Percy framework: four communication effects, the five-step planning process, the buyer groups and the loyalty model, the BSM, the general model of positioning, expectancy-value and the benefit focus, the involvement × motivation grid with creative tactics per quadrant, media selection by processing requirements, minimum effective frequency and reach patterns, VisCAP, pretesting and tracking. Limits: the authors' model dominates (Ehrenberg-Bass/Sharp appears only in the bibliography); polemical positions against digital and against unconscious processing; many numbers from older research; mostly British and FMCG examples.

**Features of the source (audio):** no pages; references are "ch. N (audio NN)". The figures and the ads are not available, but most of the tables are read out by the narrator and could be reconstructed. Passages garbled by Whisper: the end of ch. 5, part of Table 9.4, the Cheetos case in ch. 13, endings with repetitions. Proper names were corrected ("Maguire" = McGuire, "Rossiter-Persi" = Rossiter-Percy, "Lars Pint Anderson" = Lars Pynt Andersen). The 6th edition works with **four** communication effects. *Purchase facilitation* appears only in a bibliographic reference (Triandis, ch. 9), which is flagged in ADV-1502 and in the source sheet.

### ADV (advertising-strategy)
- ADV-1501: Advertising "turns" people toward the brand over the long term, promotion "moves" sales "forward" now, and it does not need an incentive
- ADV-1502: Any communication can produce four effects, but brand awareness and brand attitude are always objectives
- ADV-1503: The communication response sequence has four steps with compounded probabilities, so a message fails if a single step fails
- ADV-1504: Planning goes through the response sequence in reverse, and a mistake at one step does not cancel the steps before it
- ADV-1505: Numerical targets per ad for exposure and processing, per campaign for effects and action
- ADV-1506: Strategic communication planning has five steps, from choosing the target to the media strategy, and is still needed in digital
- ADV-1507: The target audience is chosen from five buyer groups, and each group calls for trial or repurchase, at different costs
- ADV-1508: Loyalty is read from the perceived risk of switching and from satisfaction, not just from buying behavior
- ADV-1509: The general model of positioning asks two questions, "what is it?" for awareness and "what does it offer?" for attitude
- ADV-1510: Category need becomes an objective in only two cases, when it must be reminded or when it must be sold, and selling it takes a whole campaign
- ADV-1511: Recognition brand awareness (brand → need, at the shelf) and recall brand awareness (need → brand, before the purchase) call for opposite creative and media tactics
- ADV-1512: The attitude objective has five forms (create, increase, maintain, modify, change) and is chosen only after you know what the target already believes
- ADV-1513: The Rossiter-Percy grid combines involvement (risk) with motivation (negative or positive) into four attitude strategies, each with its own creative tactics
- ADV-1514: The low-involvement informational strategy calls for problem–solution, a single benefit presented in an extreme way, and the ad does not need to be liked
- ADV-1515: The low-involvement transformational strategy calls for emotional authenticity tied to the brand, and the benefit is the execution itself, so the ad must be liked
- ADV-1516: The high-involvement informational strategy must convince, with claims kept within the target's latitude of acceptance or with a refutational approach
- ADV-1517: The high-involvement transformational strategy requires the target to identify personally with the brand as portrayed, and the liking to pass from the ad to the brand
- ADV-1518: Corporate advertising needs name recognition and almost always a transformational strategy, with high involvement when the target must be convinced
- ADV-1519: For Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott, new jargon ("platforms", "metrics", "brand ideals") does not change how advertising works (*debate*)
- ADV-1520: Theories of how advertising works are ordered on two axes, active or passive audience and individual or cultural explanation
- ADV-1521: How much you adapt a campaign across cultures depends on the category's involvement and emotion and on cultural distance, on a gradient from global to local
- ADV-1522: Whopper Detour turned McDonald's restaurants into ordering points for Burger King, to get the app downloaded (*case*)
- ADV-1523: "Probably the best beer in the world" worked for decades in some markets, but the modest irony of "probably" did not translate in Russia and Asia (*case*)
- ADV-1524: The campaign budget (the task method) and its tracking follow the same response sequence, and wear-out is diagnosed from the plan toward the execution
- ADV-1525: Media are chosen by three processing requirements (visual content, processing time, frequency potential), matched to the awareness and attitude strategy
- ADV-1526: The media plan is judged by reach at the minimum effective frequency, not by GRPs or average frequency, and when in doubt it is better to choose frequency
- ADV-1527: The reach pattern is chosen by the purchase cycle and the decision time, and a new product can hardly be overpromoted
- ADV-1528: The effectiveness of digital advertising remains unproven, and the book sees social networks as a "negotiated" medium, affected by fraud and poor viewability (*debate*)
- ADV-1529: Media vehicles are chosen by direct matching with the audience's brand behavior, not by demographic matching
- ADV-1530: The pretest is built on the communication objectives, with questions in a fixed order, and is never done through focus groups or ad recall
- ADV-1531: The promotion technique is chosen by the action objective (trial or repurchase), but it must also build the brand, carrying the same benefit and look
- ADV-1532: Social marketing communication must change attitude and behavior, so it adds the social norm (Fishbein-Ajzen), the stages of change and a moderate, resolved fear
- ADV-1533: Advertising and promotion used together produce a "ratchet effect", and their weight is chosen by life cycle, differentiation, loyalty and market position

### INS (consumer-insight)
- INS-1501: Every purchase decision has five roles, and advertising speaks to an individual in a role, not to a group
- INS-1502: The behavioral sequence model (BSM) puts the decision stages on a grid and, for each one, who, where, when and how
- INS-1503: Involvement is the risk perceived by the target, not an attribute of the product: at low involvement curiosity is enough, at high involvement the message must be believed
- INS-1504: We buy for three negative motives (problem removal, problem avoidance, incomplete satisfaction) or two positive ones (sensory gratification, social approval)
- INS-1505: The VisCAP model chooses the presenter by the communication objective (visibility, credibility, attractiveness, power), not by popularity
- INS-1506: Brand attitude is a relative evaluation, from beliefs and emotions, of the brand's ability to satisfy the current motivation
- INS-1507: Personality does not choose the target, but it changes how the message is processed, through stable traits and temporary states
- INS-1508: An ad is processed through four responses (attention, learning, acceptance, emotion), element by element, and reflexive attention lasts under a second
- INS-1509: The mere presence of a smartphone reduces attention resources and memory, so ads on the phone are processed more superficially
- INS-1510: Emotional memory enters working memory before declarative memory and frames the processing, and emotion is conveyed through authentic faces
- INS-1511: Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott argue that unconscious or subliminal processing of an ad cannot change attitude or behavior (*debate*)

### BRD (brand-strategy)
- BRD-1501: Central positioning is reserved for the leader that defines the category, and almost all other brands must differentiate
- BRD-1502: User-oriented positioning makes sense only for a niche or for the social approval motive; otherwise the product is the hero
- BRD-1503: The positioning benefit is chosen with the expectancy-value model, which shows which benefits matter, who delivers them and where you can shift importance
- BRD-1504: Zendium differentiates itself from the leader Colgate by what it does not contain and by a story of harmony instead of "the normal narrative about killing off germs" (*case*)
- BRD-1505: A look & feel kept consistent for years ends up triggering the brand without the name, and executions must vary within it, not be identical

### ART (art-direction)
- ART-1501: When the target does not share the same cultural schemas, the image must reach universal biological schemas, such as prospect and refuge
- ART-1502: The image persuades through iconicity, indexicality and the lack of an explicit syntax, which lets it suggest what could not be said
- ART-1503: The image attracts and holds attention if it is large, colorful, concrete, shows the product in use and is seen before the text

### BRF (creative-brief)
- BRF-1501: Before the strategy, the briefing document answers five marketing questions, starting with "What are you marketing?", described for someone totally unfamiliar with it
- BRF-1502: The positioning statement is written in one sentence: X is the brand for [target audience] in the market for [category need] that offers [benefit]
- BRF-1503: The creative brief fits on one page, in three blocks (the task, the objectives and strategy, the execution), and only the target and the benefit support are detailed

### CPY (copywriting)
- CPY-1501: With negative motivation you focus directly on the benefit, and with positive motivation on its emotional consequence
- CPY-1502: Unexpected words attract attention in the headline, but learning calls for familiar, concrete words, active sentences and no negations or obscure wordplay

### MKT (marketing-fundamentals)
- MKT-1501: Demographics define the target market, but the target audience for communication is segmented only by attitude
- MKT-1502: Consumers define a brand's market, through a hierarchical partitioning by type, end benefit, usage situation and brand

### IDE (creative-ideation)
- IDE-1501: The creative idea must first dramatize the key benefit, then attract attention, be catalytic and extendable; you generate at least six and test them

## Taxonomy feedback

- **Promotions / sales promotion, trade marketing, retail**: they have no clear skill. I put them in ADV, but they could sit in MKT or in a "point-of-sale communication" section.

- **Advertising research** (pretest, tracking, measurement) has no clear home: ADV covers it in part, INS not at all.
- **Social marketing and communication for NGOs** (the theory of reasoned action, the stages of change, charity) has no skill. I put it in ADV.
- **The message source / casting / endorsement** (VisCAP): between INS, SOC and CPY. I put it in INS.
- **Music and audio branding** fit neither in ART (visual) nor in CPY. I did not make a separate note; the music tactics are in ADV-1515 and BRD-1505.
- **Packaging as advertising that continues after the purchase** (Tropicana 2009) would deserve an ART or BRD note in future phases. I did not make it, so as not to exceed the target.
