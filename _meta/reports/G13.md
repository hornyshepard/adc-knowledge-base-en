**Verdict:**
- **Struhl (SRC-038)** is the only rigorous book in the group and the main source of the notes. It covers research literacy (sampling, significance, validation, focus groups, the biases of ratings), trade-off methods (discrete choice, conjoint, MaxDiff), market simulators, classification trees, Bayesian networks and a reasoned skepticism toward opaque models. It predates generative AI.
- **Richards & McLean (SRC-040)** is a book on content strategy, not on generative AI. It has memorable concepts (the point of mutuality, pillars, the "Long Idea", the critique of Volvo's "Epic Split", personas vs. segments), plus the part on decision engines, the O2 case and uplift modeling. It is self-published and explicitly admits the lack of evidence.
- **Noil (SRC-037)** and **Najafov (SRC-039)** are introductory, repetitive and have numbers that cannot be checked. Najafov promises prompting methods but offers only five simple prompt examples. The useful part: AI transparency and labeling, the ethics checklists, the framework for overseeing the brand voice.
- **James (SRC-041)** and **Iroegbu (SRC-042)** are very thin, probably generated or assembled automatically, with no real cases. They produced only 3 notes and a few enrichments.

### AIM (ai-marketing)
- AIM-1301: AI personalization works in four registers: real-time, behavioral, micro-segment and predictive
- AIM-1302: Segments built with AI are dynamic: customers migrate between them according to behavior
- AIM-1303: Predictive analytics is a five-step cycle, not a report
- AIM-1304: A good chatbot knows when to hand the conversation over to a person
- AIM-1305: Data ethics in AI marketing has four basic rules: consent, access and deletion, transparency, minimization
- AIM-1306: Validation on a holdout sample separates real patterns from overfitting
- AIM-1307: The methods for predicting choices form four families, each with an implicit picture of people
- AIM-1308: Historical data predict repetition, not novelty: they do not answer "what if" and do not show perceptions
- AIM-1309: Share of preference is not market share: the forecast must be adjusted for awareness, understanding and distribution
- AIM-1310: Trade-off methods (discrete choice, conjoint, MaxDiff, Q-Sort) answer different questions
- AIM-1311: Market simulation shows when a price cut increases share but lowers revenue
- AIM-1312: A good choice study is built before the questionnaire: a ten-point checklist
- AIM-1313: Classification trees turn a database into a few "if–then" rules for targeting
- AIM-1314: Accuracy or transparency: ensembles and neural networks predict but do not explain (*debate*)
- AIM-1315: Bayesian networks start from the idea that when you change one variable, they all change
- AIM-1316: You choose the analysis method by the marketing question, not by technology fashion
- AIM-1317: A marketing prompt is a mini-brief, and the model's answer is a draft, not a deliverable
- AIM-1318: Disclose when you use AI: a transparency kit and an eight-point ethics checklist
- AIM-1319: A language model summarizes text well but does not prove causes (*debate* Najafov vs. Struhl)
- AIM-1320: The human formulates the hypothesis, the AI runs it, the human interprets it: the division of labor in testing and leads
- AIM-1321: The ICE maturity model: ten principles and five levels, from transaction to intelligent engagement
- AIM-1322: O2 Vision: the decision engine took conversion from under 2% to over 70%, then audience fatigue set in
- AIM-1323: Uplift modeling looks for the persuadables: a response model also credits itself with sales that would have come anyway
- AIM-1324: Offer what the customer is most likely to accept, not what brings the highest margin: one "yes" brings another "yes"
- AIM-1325: Visual listening finds the brand in images, where it is not named in the text
- AIM-1326: Can AI create a brand's identity? (*debate* among the 5 books)

### INS (consumer-insight)
- INS-1301: Literary Digest, 1936: the volume of data does not save a wrong sampling frame
- INS-1302: Statistical significance does not mean importance
- INS-1303: The focus group is good for language and for detecting disasters, not for the final decision
- INS-1304: Asked directly what matters, people say that "everything is important": importance ratings do not predict behavior
- INS-1305: An attribute's importance changes with the life stage ("a brand I trust" for children's clothing)
- INS-1306: Personas, not segments: Ozzy Osbourne and Prince Charles have the same demographic profile

### STO (storytelling-content)
- STO-1301: The point of mutuality is the topic that both the brand and the audience want to talk about
- STO-1302: Content pillars pass three tests (editorial interest, actionable, sustainable) and combine four functions
- STO-1303: The hero's journey is the buying cycle seen by the customer, and the hero is the customer, not the brand
- STO-1304: Reputation is managed from the editorial plan: risk audit, alignment, monitoring, double approval

### ADV (advertising-strategy)
- ADV-1301: The elements of an ad can be tested like an experiment: message conjoint beats the A/B test
- ADV-1302: Tested with data, the hierarchy of effects came out in a different order from the theory
- ADV-1303: A Big Idea is not enough without a "Long Idea" (Volvo Trucks, "Epic Split")

### CPY (copywriting)
- CPY-1301: The brand voice does not come from a prompt but from a cycle: examples → draft → human editing → feedback → final check
- CPY-1302: The journalistic intro does three things in a single sentence, and the text is written as an inverted pyramid

### BRD, DIG, EXP, SOC
- BRD-1301: In choice models, the brand is a "container" of attributes, and its value shows in what remains after the attributes
- DIG-1301: In AI-driven email, the timing and frequency of sending are optimized per individual, not per list
- EXP-1301: Conjoint optimization of services shows where you over-deliver and where you under-deliver
- SOC-1301: With AI, an influencer is chosen by authenticity and audience fit, not by the number of followers

## Taxonomy feedback

- **Research and measurement literacy** (sampling, significance, power, validation, experimental design) has no clear home. I put it in INS ("consumer research") and AIM. Suggestion: the INS scope should explicitly mention "research methods and interpreting data", or a cross-cutting "Research and measurement" map.
- **Choice modeling and price** (discrete choice, simulators, elasticity) sit between MKT (price) and AIM. I put them in AIM, because the AIM scope includes "predicting behavior".
- **CRM, decision engines, next-best-action, contact strategy** have no dedicated skill. I put them in AIM and DIG. A "CRM and customer engagement" skill or map would help.
- **Customer service / operational CX** (over- and under-delivering services) went into EXP, although EXP is oriented toward experiences and activations.
- Level recommendation: notes AIM-1310–1315 are level 2–3 for a creative school. They deserve to be marked in the SKILL.md as an "advanced / planning" path.
