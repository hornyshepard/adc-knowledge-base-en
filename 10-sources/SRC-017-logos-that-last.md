# Logos That Last: How to Create Iconic Visual Branding

**Allan Peters, 2023.** Rockport Publishers (The Quarto Group), Beverly, MA. ISBN 978-0-7603-8317-9. The author is a partner and chief creative officer at Peters Design Company (PDCo), formerly a designer at small studios, an art director at an advertising agency and an in-house designer at Target.

> Note on references: the EPUB edition has no pages (the printed book has more than 200 pages, mostly images); the notes refer to the chapter and the section title.

## At a glance

- **What it is about:** the full process PDCo uses to create visual identities: the ten pillars of a lasting logo, the brand mark process (brand nouns, fifty sketches, combining brand nouns, vectorizing and gridding, presentation, system), hunting for vintage inspiration, badge design, brand extensions, the evolution of existing logos, plus case studies (cities, churches, startups) and passion projects.
- **Central thesis:** a logo that lasts for generations does not follow fashion; it is simple, original, memorable and functional, the result of a disciplined process and a passion for the craft.
- **For whom and when:** the courses on art direction and visual identity (levels 1–2), logo workshops; also useful for the seminars on presenting creative work and on the portfolio.
- **Relevance for ADC:** ★★★★☆ (4): very practical, with named and repeatable techniques (brand nouns, the five combining techniques, the hat test, balance words, the "what was working / what was not working" audit). Limits: the perspective of a single small American studio; a short text (about 24,000 words) that relies on images missing from the text version; much autobiographical and personal content (including religious); no data or audience testing beyond reactions on social media.

## Key ideas → permanent notes

- [[ART-013-a-logo-lasts-through-ten-pillars-seven-of-which-the|ART-013]]: three pillars the designer cannot control (time, budget, product) and seven they can (passion, beauty, originality, functionality, color, story, simplicity); the size tests and the "hat test".
- [[ART-014-the-brand-noun-list-sets-the-subject-of-the-logo-before-the|ART-014]]: the brand noun process, with the client involved from the start.
- [[ART-015-five-techniques-for-combining-two-nouns-into-one-original|ART-015]]: overlapping geometry, negative space, visual rhythm, an unexpected twist, visual flow.
- [[ART-016-the-brand-mark-process-fifty-sketches-before-any-polish|ART-016]]: 50 sketches, 15 vectorized, 3 systems; the presentation in black and white.
- [[ART-017-a-modular-identity-system-has-a-brand-mark-a-horizontal|ART-017]]: the modular system: mark, horizontal, vertical, badge; balance words and shape containers.
- [[ART-018-the-geometric-dna-of-the-brand-mark-extends-into-patterns|ART-018]]: the geometric DNA of the mark extended to every element; the answer to "make the logo bigger".
- [[ART-021-a-logo-redesign-should-be-an-evolution-not-a-revolution|ART-021]]: evolution, not revolution; the "what was working / what was not working" audit.
- [[PIT-036-a-strong-logo-is-sold-with-the-designer-leading-not-with|PIT-036]]: how to lead the presentation and reframe the client's feedback (the silent vote, the Kwikbit case).
- [[IDE-013-original-visual-inspiration-is-found-outside-the-pinterest|IDE-013]]: inspiration from outside the bubble of design platforms.
- [[PRT-005-passion-projects-bring-into-the-portfolio-the-work-you-want|PRT-005]]: passion projects as an engine for the portfolio and for clients.

## What is missing / what to read critically

- The claims about "universal beauty" and about the saturated colors of long-lived logos are personal observations, not research findings.
- The book deals almost only with the visual symbol; brand strategy (positioning, audience research) is assumed, not discussed. Read it together with [[SRC-014-kellogg-on-branding|SRC-014]] (brand design in System 1, [[ART-011-brand-design-works-in-system-1-and-starts-from-3-4-target|ART-011]]).
- The examples are mostly small, local clients (cities, churches, startups); there are no cases of complex global identities.
- Detailed visual analysis needs the printed book: the extracted text loses the images the explanations rely on.
- The autobiographical and religious tone takes up a large part of the volume; useful as a model of professional passion, but technically irrelevant.
