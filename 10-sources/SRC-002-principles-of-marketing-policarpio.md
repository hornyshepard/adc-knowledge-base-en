# Principles of Marketing

**Dr. John Ericson A. Policarpio, 2024.** Toronto Academic Press, Burlington (Ontario). ISBN 978-1-77469-712-2 (e-book 978-1-77956-124-4). 251 PDF pages, about 78,000 words. The author is an associate professor and the program chairperson of Tourism and Hospitality Management at PATTS College of Aeronautics (Philippines), with hotel experience in sales and marketing.

> Citation note: the pages in the derived notes are the book's printed pages. In the text file, PDF page = printed page + 17 (ch. 1 starts on printed p. 1, PDF p. 18).

## At a glance

- **What it is about:** a short introductory textbook in seven chapters: marketing and the marketing process (including selling vs. marketing, the environment, the 7Ps), consumer behavior (including consumer rights and the types of buying behavior), segmentation and targeting, the product (levels, mix, branding, new product development, the life cycle), pricing, distribution channels and physical distribution, then "recent developments" (social, direct, online, service, green and rural marketing, consumerism). Each chapter has objectives, key terms, "Did you Know?" boxes, review questions and multiple choice questions.
- **Central thesis:** modern marketing starts and ends with the consumer: it creates value for customers and captures value in return, through profitable relationships (the Kotler-Armstrong formula).
- **For whom and when:** as a vocabulary review in the first weeks of the MKT module, or as a second source for concepts already learned from Grewal and Levy. It is not suited to be a main source.
- **Relevance for ADC:** ★★☆☆☆: it covers the classic table of contents (the 7Ps, the five management concepts, the five product levels, the matrix of buying behavior types, the eight stages of product development, the "stability" criterion for segments, consumer rights), but it is a compiled, repetitive and often confusing text, with errors of substance. Almost nothing about advertising, creative work or the brand as a system of meanings. The examples come from mixed contexts (USA, India: AGMARK/ISI, rural marketing, anti-dowry campaigns).

## What is missing / what to read critically

- **A compiled text, probably AI-assisted:** paragraphs that repeat almost word for word, forced logical links ("This is because…"), generic examples, sometimes unrelated to the section title (for example, "Greater Risk of Misassumptions" is about remote customer service, not about segmentation).
- **Errors of substance:** a reductive definition of marketing ("promoting a product") next to the correct one; "dissonance-reducing" buying described as a FOMO tactic; micromarketing defined as targeting an age group; confusing definitions of high/low and penetration pricing; skimming described as "tricking" consumers; social marketing confused with marketing on social media; consumerism mixed up with the ideology of consumption.
- **Multiple choice questions with wrong or contradictory answers:** ch. 4 ("NPD has six steps", although the text describes eight), ch. 5 (the definition of advertising has "promotions" as its answer; the same question appears twice with different answers), ch. 1 ("the oldest concept: exchange").
- **Outdated or unsuitable data and examples:** the Kotler quote about "computer and modem" in online marketing; online channels described as forums and bulletin boards; the attribution of the mix to Culliton, simplified.
- **Missing:** any discussion of integrated communication, creativity, positioning as a perceptual map, brand equity or measurement. The references are standard (Kotler, Keller, Aaker, Kapferer), but they are not used in the text.
- **Use it only** as a control vocabulary, checked against Grewal and Levy ([[SRC-001-marketing-grewal-levy|SRC-001]]) or against Kotler (the series in the base).
