# The Art of Presentation And Confident Public Speaking

**Bharat Bhuval Nishad, 2024.** Self-published (cover, design and editing by the author). About 11,000 words, with no page numbers: references are to the section titles.

## At a glance

- **What it is about:** a short guide for students who have to present in class, at interviews or in student campaigns. The steps: choose a topic you can state in one sentence, write down the result you want, reduce the message to a few points, add examples and stories, speak from a single sheet of notes and, above all, film yourself again and again until you like what you see.
- **Central thesis:** success in public speaking depends less on gestures and voice and more on selecting the message and on practicing on video; nerves go away when you know exactly how you look and sound.
- **For whom and when:** first-year students, before their first seminar presentations; useful as a self-filming exercise.
- **Relevance for ADC:** ★★☆☆☆. A few good practical tips (the video loop with "one flaw per round", the single sheet of notes, the "triple audience" of a class presentation), but the text is thin, repetitive and seems to be the transcript of a video course (the word "video" appears replaced by "chapter": "chaptertape yourself"), with promotional sections unrelated to the subject (TikTok, the "$10,000 Guarantee", a consultation program, requests for reviews).

## Key ideas → permanent notes

- [[PIT-023-rehearsing-on-video-works-when-you-correct-a-single-flaw|PIT-023]]: rehearsing on video, with a single flaw fixed per round, until you like what you see.
- [[PIT-033-is-stage-fright-treated-with-calming-techniques-or-by|PIT-033]]: debate: is stage fright treated with calming techniques (Colenso) or by removing the unknown through video (Nishad)?
- Enrichments: [[PIT-004-a-pitch-is-organized-like-a-tree-one-headline-three-key|PIT-004]] (five vs. three messages), [[PIT-021-present-from-frameworks-and-key-words-not-from-a-memorized|PIT-021]] (the single sheet of notes), [[PIT-009-you-visualize-a-pitch-s-goal-feel-it-and-only-then-say-it|PIT-009]] (the result first, not a "wheelbarrow of facts"), [[PIT-007-audience-research-separates-verifiable-facts-from-feelings|PIT-007]] (the triple audience of a class presentation), [[PIT-015-stardust-five-kinds-of-evidence-that-make-a-message|PIT-015]] (examples and stories).

## What is missing / what to read critically

- The empirical claims ("I've done this for 30 years" with audiences all over the world, "more than 10000 people" trained) are unsupported and seem to be taken from another trainer's video course; treat the attribution of the text with caution.
- An internal contradiction: one chapter asks for five messages, another for "no more than three".
- It deals with presentation structure, slides, Q&A or the sales pitch only in passing.
- Almost a third of the text is self-promotion or platform instructions.

## Quote

> "The world is short quite often on insights perspective critical thinking." (section "5 Message Points")
