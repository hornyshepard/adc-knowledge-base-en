# Marketing 3.0: From Products to Customers to the Human Spirit

**Philip Kotler, Hermawan Kartajaya, Iwan Setiawan, 2010.** John Wiley & Sons, Hoboken (ISBN 978-0-470-59882-5). Foreword by Susilo Bambang Yudhoyono. The concept was first developed at MarkPlus (Indonesia), starting in 2005. The pages cited in the notes are those of the `=== [page N] ===` markers in the text copy (the PDF pages), not the printed pages.

## At a glance

- **What it is about:** the first book in the "Marketing X.0" series. It describes the shift from product-centric marketing (1.0) and consumer-oriented marketing (2.0) to values-driven marketing (3.0), which treats people as whole human beings (mind, heart and spirit). It shows how a company "markets" its mission to consumers, its values to employees and channel partners, and its vision to shareholders, then applies the idea to wellness, poverty and the environment.
- **Central thesis:** in the age of participation, of the globalization paradox and of the creative society, companies differentiate themselves through values built into the business model, not through the product or positioning.
- **For whom and when:** years 1 and 2, after the marketing fundamentals (STP, 4P), as an introduction to brand purpose, the brand platform and responsible marketing.
- **Relevance for ADC:** ★★★★☆ (4/5). It offers models that can be used directly in strategy and briefs (3i, VBM, the good mission, the story with character, plot and metaphor, cultural brands) and many memorable cases (New Coke, the IKEA font, Timberland, The Body Shop). Limits: data from 2008-2009, an optimistic and normative tone, little on creative execution.

## What is missing / what to read critically

- **Aging data and examples:** statistics from 2007-2009 (Twitter, MySpace, blogs) and cases that have changed since (The Body Shop, later sold to L'Oréal; Wal-Mart, Dell).
- **Weak sources:** many figures come from surveys by PR and consulting firms (Cone, Edelman, McKinsey), often declarative ("I would pay more"), with no link to actual buying behavior.
- **Vague concept:** the "spirit" and "spiritual marketing" are not defined operationally and have no method of measurement. The claim that Maslow wanted to invert his pyramid is taken secondhand (Zohar) and is debatable.
- **Normative optimism:** the book assumes that values bring profit and pays little attention to trade-offs, the risk of *purpose-washing* or failures. The examples are chosen to confirm the thesis.
- **Little creative communication:** it deals with actual advertising only in passing (UGC, stories, conversation).
- The part on the bottom of the pyramid and the Millennium Development Goals is useful as perspective, but marginal for an agency.
