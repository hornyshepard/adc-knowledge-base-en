# Intelligent Customer Engagement: The Future of Content Marketing

**Aly Richards & Scott McLean, 2014.** The Intelligent Marketing Institute (timihub.com), United Kingdom, print-on-demand. ISBN 978-0-9930211-0-7. Foreword by Hugh Wilson (Cranfield School of Management). About 65,000 words. Aly Richards led the *Vision* project at O2, the book's anchor case.

## At a glance

- **What it is about:** a content strategy manual extended to the level of the whole organization (an "audience engagement strategy"), supported by decision technology: real-time decision engines, propensity models, automated campaigns and a future "engagement strategy planner". It has five sections: the why, the maturity model, the components of the strategy (personas, point of mutuality, journeys, pillars, reputation, participation, storytelling, B2B, video), the technical architecture and the implementation plan, and the business outcomes.
- **Central thesis:** brands have to move from isolated campaigns that "broadcast" messages to a sustained conversation with the audience. The conversation starts from a shared subject and is orchestrated through content across all touchpoints, from marketing to the call center.
- **For whom and when:** strategists, content and CRM planners, students in the STO, AIM and DIG modules. Good for content strategy projects and for understanding the link between creative work and data systems.
- **Relevance for ADC:** ★★★☆☆. Memorable and applicable concepts (the point of mutuality, pillars with three tests, the "Long Idea" vs. the Big Idea, the critique of the "Epic Split", personas vs. segments, the three-act structure). The "AI" part is pre-generative (decision engines, propensity, NLP for tagging). The text is long, repetitive and in places written like consulting material.

## What is missing / what to read critically

- **Self-published by the authors' institute** and written partly as a presentation of services. The "mentor" in the hero's journey is their own institute; the implementation chapter refers readers to consultants.
- **The authors acknowledge the lack of evidence:** they say explicitly that they have no case studies with solid figures. The only hard figures (O2) come from a project led by the author herself. The benchmark (450 companies) is self-reported, correlational research; the text says "12 countries" but lists nine.
- **The promised technology did not exist** in 2014 (the strategy planner, the decision engine for content). Today part of it exists in other forms (customer data platforms, AI content recommendations), but the book cannot evaluate them.
- **Dated data and examples:** references to Twitter, blogs, Ocado as an "early adopter", the tabloid *News of the World* (closed in 2011) in a persona's profile.
- The personas in the bank example are built on stereotypes of class and of newspaper readership, which is questionable as a method.
- **A very repetitive text:** the same ideas (silos, audience-centricity, "evolution, not revolution") come back in almost every chapter.
