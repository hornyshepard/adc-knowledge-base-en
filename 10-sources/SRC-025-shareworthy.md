# Shareworthy: Advertising That Creates Powerful Connections Through Storytelling

**Greg Braun and Robin Landa, 2024.** Columbia University Press (Columbia Business School Publishing), New York. Foreword by David Lubars (BBDO). Braun was global associate creative director at Commonwealth/McCann (Chevrolet, Toyota, Hyundai); Landa is a professor at Kean University and the author of textbooks on design and advertising.

## At a glance

- **What it is about:** a book about the advertising people choose to share. Eight chapters (why stories, the audience, the brand, emotional affinity, narrative structure, the North Star idea, the brand as an activist for diversity, equity and inclusion, stories "without a playbook"), each with many award-winning case studies and interviews with creative directors (Mischief, Amazon, MRM, Innocean, AlmapBBDO, McCann, 72andSunny, BBDO Singapore).
- **Central thesis:** a story becomes "shareworthy" when it has use value for the audience (it entertains, informs, inspires, helps), is authentic to the brand's values and backed by facts, so that the audience becomes a co-owner and shares it willingly.
- **For whom and when:** the STO and IDE modules, after the basics of the brief; very useful for portfolios (recent cases, 2015–2023) and for discussions about *purpose* and representation.
- **Relevance for ADC:** ★★★★★ (5): named tools (C.O.W. + B→T, A.L.T.E.R., the three Gs, the emotional power and responsibility checklists) and contemporary cases. Limits: almost only award-winning campaigns and figures from award entries.

## What is missing / what to read critically

- **Survivorship bias:** almost all cases are winners at Cannes, One Show or Effie; failures appear rarely (Bud Light, H&M) and briefly.
- **Promotional figures:** impressions, "earned media value" and growth figures come from the agencies' award entries and are rarely checked independently; many survey statistics (Sitecore, Zeno, Adobe) come without a methodology.
- **Undiscussed tensions:** the book cites that 59 percent of respondents prefer brands to remain neutral on social issues, yet otherwise argues for brand activism.
- The perspective is mostly American. The interviews are often self-promotional; some phrasing repeats across chapters.
- The C.O.W. + B→T formula is not explained letter by letter; the interpretation in [[STO-003-the-advertising-story-is-a-transformation-the-brand|STO-003]] is ours.
