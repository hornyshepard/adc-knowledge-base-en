# Digital Advertising in the Post-cookie Era: Strategic Campaign Planning Across the Customer Journey

**Alexander Schwarz-Musch, Alexander Tauchhammer, Bernhard Guetz, 2025.** Springer Gabler, "Business Guides on the Go" series, ISBN 978-3-658-47100-2 (eBook). A translation of the German edition *Digitale Werbung in der Post-Cookie-Ära* (2024). The authors teach at Carinthia University of Applied Sciences (Villach, Austria); Tauchhammer led global digital marketing at Dr. Oetker.

## At a glance

- **What it is about:** a short practical guide (about 50,000 words) in five chapters: (1) how third-party cookies worked, why they are disappearing and what targeting options remain (contextual, semantic, predictive, cohort, first-party), plus identity and data clean rooms; (2) a three-step campaign planning process: brief, buyer persona (including the minimum viable persona), customer journey, campaign groups, budget, media planning, design, booking, quality settings, KPIs by stage; (3) social media advertising (Facebook/Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, TikTok); (4) search engine advertising (Google, Bing); (5) display and programmatic advertising (ad server, RTB, DMP/CDP, dynamic banners, brand safety, fraud).
- **Central thesis:** in the post-cookie era, effectiveness comes from knowing customers (personas, first-party data) and from structuring each campaign by persona and by the stages of the journey, with objectives and KPIs for each stage, combining old targeting (contextual) with new solutions (clean rooms, universal IDs).
- **For whom and when:** years 2–3, after an introductory digital course; very useful for strategists, media planners and any digital campaign project.
- **Relevance for ADC:** ★★★★☆ (4). Current (2024–2025), a European perspective (GDPR, e-Privacy, examples from Austria and Germany), good cases (the kitchens with a DMP and dynamic banners, the e-bikes with two personas, food delivery with weather targeting). Limits: chapters 3–4 describe the platform interfaces at length (they date quickly), little on creative work and the idea.

## What is missing / what to read critically

- Some of the figures and example boxes are missing from the extracted text (for example, the diagram of the journey phases); the phases used (Aware, Appeal, Ask, then the purchase) correspond to Kotler's five A's model, cited in the bibliography.
- Inconsistencies: viewability is defined once by the MRC standard (50% of pixels, 1 s; video 2 s) and elsewhere as "over 2 s and at least 80% visible"; table 2.10 calls "CPV" a "cost per conversion".
- Traces of hasty translation ("post-cockie", "Wallet Garden" instead of *walled garden*).
- The descriptions of the interfaces (objectives, buttons, menus) date quickly.
- It presents intrusive techniques with enthusiasm (digital billboards that "recognize" the passerby, purchased real estate data) without discussing their ethical and legal limits at length.
- Almost nothing on the creative idea; creative work is reduced to formats and design rules.
- The pages cited in the notes follow the PDF numbering of the extracted text.
