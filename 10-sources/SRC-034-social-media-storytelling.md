# Social Media Storytelling

**Marie Elisabeth Mueller and Devadas Rajaram, 2023** (CIP 2022). Routledge, Abingdon / New York. An English translation of the German book "Social Storytelling" (Rheinwerk Verlag). Contributions from five experts: Sascha Gottschalk (FMD, Filmemacher Deutschland), Deana Mrkaja (journalist), Adil Sbai (the weCreate agency, TikTok), Tim Hendrik Walter (lawyer, @HerrAnwalt on TikTok), Maximilian Wolf (Youth Against AIDS / FAQ YOU).

> The pages cited in the derived notes follow the PDF numbering (the `=== [page N] ===` markers in the text), not the printed numbering; the offset is +17 (printed p. 38 = PDF p. 55).

## At a glance

- **What it is about:** a practical handbook, written by two professors of mobile journalism, on how to find, build, produce with a smartphone and distribute stories for social platforms (Twitter/X, TikTok, Snapchat, Instagram, Facebook). It combines a "dramaturgical toolbox" for micro-stories (story anatomy, story beats, faces and voices, multimedia layers, cross-platform methods), a "multimedia ABC" of mobile production (apps, framing, audio, editing), a tour of the native genres (hashtags, memes, stories, toks, live, social audio) and a chapter on measurement written by Sascha Gottschalk, based on FMD's tourism campaigns (Uganda, Japan, Usedom).
- **Central thesis:** on the social web, the great linear narrative of the hero no longer works; the storyteller becomes a guide, the story is cut into native micro-stories for each platform and distributed according to a plan, following the "all-in-one" strategy, mirroring the customer journey.
- **For whom and when:** students in the storytelling and content course, once they master the basics of narrative; also useful in the social media module (platforms, community, live, measurement). Chapter 3 (production with a smartphone) is good for practical workshops.
- **Relevance for ADC:** ★★★☆☆ (3/5): very practical and generous with examples (SAS "The Arrivals", @HerrAnwalt, FAQ YOU, National Geographic, FMD). It has a few distinctive models (the 7 questions derived from the hero's turning points, story beats, story DNA as a central metaphor, the three levels of attention on an influencer post). Limits: a journalistic rather than an advertising perspective, much technical description of apps that dates quickly, repetition, few verifiable data.

## Key ideas → permanent notes

- [[STO-014-on-social-media-the-guide-replaces-the-hero-of-the-classic|STO-014]]: on social media, the guide replaces the hero: the authors reject the hero's journey as a template for short, conversational, nonlinear stories.
- [[STO-015-the-anatomy-of-story-you-choose-the-dramatic-moment-that|STO-015]]: story anatomy: you choose one dramatic moment that represents the whole ("pars pro toto"), as SAS does in "The Arrivals".
- [[STO-016-the-seven-turning-points-of-the-hero-s-journey-become-seven|STO-016]]: the hero's 7 turning points (weakness, desire, opponent, plan, battle, self-revelation, new equilibrium) recast as 7 brand questions.
- [[STO-017-story-beats-keep-the-pace-and-push-attention-toward-what|STO-017]]: story beats: changes of rhythm, tone or perspective that push attention forward; the first seconds decide.
- [[STO-020-the-serial-format-is-the-secret-weapon-of-micro-storytelling|STO-020]]: the serial format ("One minute of law") saves effort and creates a habit.

- [[STO-030-the-all-in-one-strategy-produce-once-distribute-in|STO-030]]: the "all-in-one" strategy: platform-agnostic material → moment → micro-stories on the platforms → long-form.
- [[STO-011-story-dna-one-central-metaphor-applied-to-all-layers|STO-011]]: story DNA: a single central metaphor carried into the narrative, the design and the UX.
- [[STO-018-each-multimedia-layer-must-add-new-meaning-not-repeat|STO-018]]: multimedia layers must add meaning, not duplicate it; the W questions and "why".

## What is missing / what to read critically

- Unsupported or exaggerated neurological claims (images reach the brain "more than 60,000 times faster" than text; dopamine as a universal explanation): treat them as metaphors, not as data.
- The rejection of the hero's journey is polemical: the authors in fact keep the beginning-middle-end structure and the archetypes; the critique is aimed mostly at length and linearity.
- A large part of chapters 3–4 describes apps and features (Fleets, IGTV, Twitter Moments, Clubhouse) that have since disappeared or changed; the lasting value lies in the principles, not in the tutorials.
- The examples are mostly journalistic, German and from NGOs; few classic advertising campaigns and almost nothing about paid media budgets.
- The measurement chapter is anecdotal (percentages estimated "from experience" for the three levels of attention), with no methodology.
- A mild conflict of interest: the expert who writes about measurement (FMD) presents his own campaigns as "best practice".
