# Digital Marketing: Foundations and Strategy (5th edition)

**Debra Zahay, Lauren Labrecque, Brooke Reavey, Mary Lou Roberts, 2024** (copyright 2024, edition released in 2023). Cengage Learning, Boston. An American university textbook, 448 PDF pages, 14 chapters plus a semester project. Earlier editions were titled *Internet Marketing: Integrating Online and Offline Strategies* (2003–2018). The edition is dedicated to the memory of Mary Lou Roberts.

> The pages cited in the derived notes follow the PDF numbering (the `=== [page N] ===` markers in the text), not the printed numbering; the offset is +14 (printed p. 3 = PDF p. 17).

## At a glance

- **What it is about:** a complete digital marketing textbook in three parts: foundations (the digital landscape, the value chain, business models, the customer journey, measurement), creating visibility and attracting customers (website and mobile, SEO, paid search and paid social, social media, display and mobile) and maintaining the relationship (email, content marketing, B2B demand generation, CRM). Each chapter has glossary definitions, figures taken from the industry (HubSpot, Google, Smart Insights, IAB) and short case studies.
- **Central thesis:** digital marketing is the heir of direct marketing; every digital tactic must be judged through the four generic strategies (acquisition, conversion, retention, customer value growth), through measurable objectives and through the customer's cumulative experience.
- **For whom and when:** the digital marketing module (years 2–3), as a reference textbook for vocabulary and processes (SEO, PPC, programmatic, email, KPIs). Chapters 9 (social), 12 (content) and 5 (measurement) are the most useful for an agency's strategists and creatives.
- **Relevance for ADC:** ★★★★☆ (4/5): very systematic, with the authors' own models (the Zahay definition, Labrecque's 5Cs, the application of POST) and step-by-step processes that are easy to teach. Limits: a direct marketing and sales perspective, not a brand-building one; creativity is treated as marginal; many Statista/HubSpot-type statistics date quickly; some chapters show traces of hasty editing (Google Optimize, AdWords, "Chapter 8" instead of 12).

## What is missing / what to read critically

- **A direct marketing perspective.** The brand is treated as a side effect of tactics; there is no discussion of building long-term brand memory, broad reach vs. narrow targeting (the Ehrenberg-Bass school) or the balance between brand and activation (Binet & Field). The 40/40/20 rule (creative = 20%) is cited without evidence.
- **Secondhand statistics.** Many figures come from vendors' blogs (HubSpot, Constant Contact "a $36 return for every $1", Campaign Monitor +26%), without a methodology. Read them as sales arguments, not as evidence.
- **Inconsistencies.** CTR is defined once as clicks/opens (p. 299), then as clicks/sends (p. 315); the worked example gives a CTOR of 32%, the dashboard 26%. The paid search chapter still talks about "Expanded Text ads" although Google has retired them; Google Optimize (cited in other sources) no longer exists.
- **Outdated data.** Google's timeline for phasing out third-party cookies (2024) changed several times; treat it as an illustration of the trend.
- **Multi-touch attribution** and media mix modeling (MMM) are almost absent; see [[SRC-030-digital-marketing-strategies|SRC-030]] for the basic typology.
- Chapters 2 and 3 (supply chain, blockchain, NFTs) are of little relevance to an advertising school.
