# Digital Marketing

**Raj Sachdev, 2024.** McGraw Hill (International Student Edition), ISBN 978-1-266-14309-0. The author is an assistant professor of marketing at Plymouth State University, a consultant and a lawyer (California, England and Wales), with a privacy certification (CIPP/US). Note: this book is NOT the Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick textbook; it contains neither RACE nor SOSTAC, but a framework of its own (the 7As).

## At a glance

- **What it is about:** an American undergraduate textbook in 12 chapters: three foundational ones (the digital environment, strategy, consumer behavior and customer experience), seven "how-to" chapters (website, SEO, SEM, email, social, mobile, brand/trust/reputation), two closing ones (legal, ethical, privacy, security; trends) and an appendix with a sample strategic plan. Every applied chapter has the same structure: value and objectives → strategies and tactics → content → analytics.
- **Central thesis:** digital marketing means creating value through digital means, starting from a strategic plan tied to business objectives, measured continuously and built within the limits of the law and of privacy.
- **For whom and when:** students in years 1–2, an introductory course in digital marketing; very good as a template for the semester project (the strategic plan in eight sections).
- **Relevance for ADC:** ★★★☆☆ (3). Current (2024), clear, with a glossary and many brand examples; its distinctive strength is the legal and privacy part (the author is a lawyer) and the 7A framework. Limits: a lot of generic textbook content, a US perspective, very little on creative work and the idea.

## What is missing / what to read critically

- The 7A framework is the author's own teaching construct, not validated empirically; the acronyms partly overlap with AIDA and the 4Ps.
- The legal perspective is American (FTC, CCPA, COPPA); GDPR appears only in boxes. For Romania it has to be supplemented with European sources (see SRC-032).
- The section on cookies is already outdated: the book says Google is phasing out cookies in Chrome, but in July 2024 Google reversed that decision (SRC-032).
- Many figures come from vendor blogs (HubSpot, Hootsuite, Salesforce) and are not discussed critically (e.g. "CX-oriented companies have revenues 4–8% higher").
- The screenshots and the instructions for tools age quickly.
- Almost nothing on the creative idea, insight or execution; the tables on the meaning of colors are simplistic.
- The pages cited in the notes are those of the PDF numbering of the extracted text (about the printed page + 19).
