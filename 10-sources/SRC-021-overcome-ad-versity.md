# Overcome AD-versity: How to Put Persuasion Power in Advertising

**Barry Milavsky, 2024.** Business Expert Press, New York, Marketing collection (editor Naresh Malhotra). The author has a master's in communication from the University of Pennsylvania, worked at Young & Rubicam, Benton & Bowles and Grey (Caracas, Toronto), then founded his own agency, Calexis (Subway, Molson, Sunoco, Chubb). About 67,000 words; pages are cited from the book's index.

## At a glance

- **What it is about:** a guide for managers who commission and approve advertising, built on nine steps: understanding the product, the communication strategy, credibility, the message, the formats, evaluating ideas, approval, production and assessing the results. Each step is explained through psychology (Kahneman's "fast brain" and "slow brain", Hovland and McGuire, Miller) and illustrated with cases the author lived through (Joy, Jell-O, Tang, Pampers, Subway, Texaco, Molson). It ends with a detailed case study (Jell-O Pudding with Bill Cosby) and advice for retailers.
- **Central thesis:** advertising is applied social science; it reaches almost only the audience's fast brain, so it has to be built from a written strategy that starts from the target's current belief, promises a single credible end benefit and is executed clearly, provocatively, memorably and with patience.
- **For whom and when:** very good for the strategy, copywriting and client relations modules, from year 2; also useful for those who will work in client service or on the client side. The cases are short and easy to discuss in a seminar.
- **Relevance for ADC:** ★★★★☆: practical, honest, full of behind-the-scenes detail on production and approval, with a psychology of persuasion explained in plain terms. Limits: the examples are almost all American and Canadian from the '70s–'90s, centered on TV and consumer packaged goods; digital and social media are almost absent; the scientific sources are few (eight references) and some generalizations ("three exposures", "10 percent for production") are the author's rules of thumb.

## What is missing / what to read critically

- The cases are the author's memories, with no published data; the results (for example, the mayonnaise that went "from fifth to second place") cannot be checked.
- The "fast / slow brain" is used as a simplified metaphor for Kahneman, sometimes mixed with the "lizard brain"; it is not neuroscience.
- Almost nothing on digital, social, data or performance; nothing on long-term brand building in the sense of Ehrenberg-Bass or Binet & Field (although the author argues for patience and cumulative effects).
- A few debatable opinions: that advertising is never political, and "ethnically ambiguous" casting as a solution for inclusion; worth discussing critically in a seminar.
- The mayonnaise demonstration (thickness presented as flavor) raises an ethical problem the author does not discuss.
