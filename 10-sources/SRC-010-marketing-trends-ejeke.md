# Marketing Trends (63 Digital Marketing Trends…)

**Patrick Ejeke, copyright 2021** (the file is labeled "2023"; the content refers to 2022). Self-published: the opening pages promote the author's other books (Wix SEO, Wix Blog) and PDF "bonuses". About 20,000 words.

## At a glance

- **What it is about:** a list of 63 digital marketing trends for 2022, from metrics, short videos, podcasts and influencers to voice and visual search, live stream shopping, personalization, native advertising, chatbots, geofencing, first-party data, the metaverse, NFTs, the creator economy, CSR and responsible consumerism. Each trend gets 1–3 pages with statistics and a few examples.
- **Central thesis:** marketers have to follow trends constantly and combine them to stay competitive after the pandemic.
- **For whom and when:** first-year students, as a quick glossary of digital tactics from 2021–2022, and as material for fact-checking exercises.
- **Relevance for ADC:** ★★☆☆☆: a few useful cases and checklists (Bloom and Wild, inclusive advertising, consent for data, micro-moments, membership vs. loyalty). Much of the text and many of the charts come explicitly from Singlegrain.com and other blogs ("Source: Singlegrain.com" appears dozens of times).

## What is missing / what to read critically

- **Dependence on a single secondary source** (Singlegrain.com, plus HubSpot and Oracle), with statistics taken over without checking. Some are well-known myths, for example the attention span of "8 seconds", "no better than a goldfish".
- **Overrated or outdated trends:** "Quantum Computing Is Making a Big Impact" in marketing, NFTs in social commerce, Meta's metaverse, third-party cookies phased out "by the end of 2022".
- **A list structure:** no hierarchy among the trends (it does not tell mega, macro and micro trends apart) and no method for choosing. The numbering is wrong (two trends numbered 38, trend 20 without a title).
- **CSR treated instrumentally**, as a way to "look more humanitarian". There is no discussion of greenwashing or of the gap between what consumers say and what they do.
- The examples (EasyJet, Cadbury, Bloom and Wild, 76/Waze) are good, but presented in a few lines, without context.

## Quote

> "People prefer relevant advertisements, but they may freak out" if you show them how much you know about them. (trend 18)
