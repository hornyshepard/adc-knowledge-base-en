# Effect models and customer journey models, compared

Almost every book in the library has its own model of "how a person gets from not knowing to buying". The map puts them side by side, so you can choose the model that fits the problem and see where they contradict each other.

## Tensions to remember

- **Linear or not?** The classic models ([[ADV-005-hierarchy-of-effects-models-aida-lavidge-steiner-dagmar|ADV-005]]) assume an order; Struhl's data ([[ADV-008-tested-with-data-the-hierarchy-of-effects-came-out-in-a|ADV-008]]) and the digital journey ([[DIG-016-the-digital-customer-journey-is-not-a-funnel-traveled-in|DIG-016]]) show something else.
- **Funnel or circle?** The 5A path ([[MKT-036-the-five-a-s-customer-path-aware-appeal-ask-act-advocate|MKT-036]]) and the digital journey add advocacy as the final stage; Percy puts the emphasis on brand attitude ([[ADV-007-any-communication-can-produce-four-effects-but-brand|ADV-007]]).
- **Involvement changes everything.** FCB ([[ADV-039-the-fcb-grid-vaughn-orders-think-feel-do-by-involvement-and|ADV-039]]), Rossiter-Percy ([[ADV-034-the-rossiter-percy-grid-combines-involvement-risk-with|ADV-034]]) and the definition of involvement as risk ([[INS-051-involvement-is-the-target-audience-s-perceived-risk-not-an|INS-051]]) converge: no single model holds for every category.

## How to choose

1. For **communication and creative strategy**: the response sequence + the Rossiter-Percy grid ([[ADV-010-strategic-communication-planning-has-five-steps-from|ADV-010]] links them).
2. For **a digital plan and measurement**: the journey built on a persona + KPIs by stage ([[DIG-040-an-online-campaign-is-planned-in-three-steps-key-campaign|DIG-040]]).
3. For **experiences and retail**: Batat's stages ([[EXP-003-the-experience-unfolds-in-four-stages-pre-consumption|EXP-003]]).
4. For **a presentation to the client**: the 5A path, because it is easy to explain.
