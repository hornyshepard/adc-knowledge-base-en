# The creative brief (BRF)

The brief is the one-page document that turns strategy into a problem a creative team can solve and against which its work will be judged. The core of the skill is *The Creative Brief Blueprint* (McTigue and Rucker, Kellogg) and the five real agency forms, completed by Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott (the briefing document and the one-page brief), by *La boîte à outils de la publicité* (the flow through the agency) and by the creative's view (Ingledew, Braun and Landa). The insight, the third component of any brief, has its home in [[20-skills/consumer-insight/SKILL|INS]]; communication objectives, in [[20-skills/advertising-strategy/SKILL|ADV]]; positioning, in [[20-skills/brand-strategy/SKILL|BRD]].

## What mastering this skill means

Observable competencies ("you can…"):

- **C1** You can explain why a brief is at the same time a map for the creative work and a criterion for evaluating it, and you can tell the client brief from the creative brief.
- **C2** You can write a one-page brief with the six McTigue-Rucker components, with a falsifiable communication objective ("from X% to Y%, in target Z, in T months").
- **C3** You can choose and prioritize the target, check an insight with the tests of meaningful, human and true, and write the positioning statement (target, frame of reference, point of difference, reason to believe).
- **C4** You can reduce a list of benefits to a single point of difference, with the benefit ladder and the four tests, and defend the choice to the client.
- **C5** You can fill in the same case in at least two agency forms and say what each one optimizes.
- **C6** You can evaluate a brief you receive with the ten red flags and return the right questions to its author before the first idea.
- **C7** You can run a creative feedback session (love / like / questions / red flags) and translate "I don't like it" into ADPLAN questions.
- **C8** You can write the measurement strategy into the brief before the campaign and decide when an idea is worth testing.

## How an AI agent uses this skill

1. **When you are asked to write a creative brief** (from scratch or from a client brief): extract the context with [[BRF-004-the-briefing-document-comes-before-the-strategy-product|BRF-004]], then build on the skeleton of [[BRF-005-the-mctigue-and-rucker-brief-has-six-components-and-fits-on|BRF-005]]: the objective following [[BRF-007-the-objective-in-the-brief-is-a-communication-objective|BRF-007]] and [[ADV-007-any-communication-can-produce-four-effects-but-brand|ADV-007]], the target following [[BRF-008-targeting-means-prioritization-three-typical-errors-and-the|BRF-008]], the insight following [[INS-092-an-insight-is-a-meaningful-human-truth-and-has-to-pass|INS-092]], the positioning following [[BRF-009-the-positioning-statement-in-the-brief-target-frame-of|BRF-009]] with a single benefit chosen on the ladder in [[BRF-011-the-benefit-ladder-climbs-from-features-to-emotional|BRF-011]] ([[BRF-012-one-benefit-not-five-an-overpacked-position-blocks-the|BRF-012]]), the execution following [[BRF-013-the-execution-section-of-the-brief-has-four-pillars|BRF-013]], the measurement following [[BRF-014-measurement-is-written-into-the-brief-before-the-campaign|BRF-014]]. Close with the Get/To/By sentence ([[BRF-016-the-get-to-by-brief-puts-the-whole-strategy-into-one|BRF-016]]) as a test of clarity and check everything with [[BRF-023-ten-red-flags-in-a-brief-from-an-objective-that-is-not|BRF-023]]. All on one page.
2. **When you are asked to evaluate or improve a brief**: run it line by line through [[BRF-023-ten-red-flags-in-a-brief-from-an-objective-that-is-not|BRF-023]], rewrite each weak section on the "good → great" model in [[BRF-015-the-stark-cloud-solutions-case-how-a-good-brief-becomes-a|BRF-015]] and word the questions for the author following [[BRF-024-receiving-the-brief-you-reduce-it-to-its-essence-check-it|BRF-024]]. For each problem you find, cite the ID of the red flag and of the detailed note.
3. **When you are asked for a brief in a particular format** or "the right format": choose following [[BRF-021-agency-brief-models-share-the-same-core-but-each-one|BRF-021]] (fast social → [[BRF-016-the-get-to-by-brief-puts-the-whole-strategy-into-one|BRF-016]]; a relaunch with history → [[BRF-018-naked-brief-an-informative-and-inspirational-brief-built-on|BRF-018]]; a production with many approvers → [[BRF-019-the-red-cell-brief-eight-requirements-execution-hints-and-a|BRF-019]]; a parity category where the evidence decides → [[BRF-020-the-singleton-ogilvy-mather-brief-the-role-of-the|BRF-020]]; an insight that is hard to find → [[BRF-017-the-euro-rscg-australia-brief-model-the-task-three|BRF-017]]; creators → [[BRF-022-the-influencer-brief-gives-direction-but-leaves-room-for|BRF-022]]) and add in the margin the frame of reference and the measurement, which most forms lack.
4. **When you are asked for feedback on an idea or an execution**: reread the brief, sort the observations into the four categories in [[BRF-025-creative-feedback-is-organized-in-four-categories-things-i|BRF-025]], turn each reservation into an ADPLAN question ([[BRF-026-adplan-six-questions-for-an-execution-that-is-on-brief-but|BRF-026]]) and, if the idea is still a concept, use the grids in [[IDE-023-idea-evaluation-grids-converge-on-three-questions-does-it|IDE-023]]. Do not propose execution solutions in place of the creatives.
5. **When the client wants "all the benefits"** or an emotional ad "because that's what works": argue with [[BRF-012-one-benefit-not-five-an-overpacked-position-blocks-the|BRF-012]] (plus the evidence in [[INS-033-ask-customers-for-ten-reasons-and-you-persuade-them-less|INS-033]] and [[CPY-005-one-good-reason-beats-a-list-of-37-at-most-three-support|CPY-005]]) and with the debate in [[BRF-027-debate-the-emotional-benefit-is-not-automatically-better|BRF-027]]; present the tension between the authors, not a rule.
6. Cite the IDs of the notes you used, so the answer can be checked. For details or exact wording, open the source text in `90-library/text/` (`the-creative-brief-blueprint.txt`, `brief-*.txt` for the forms, `audiobook/11.txt` and `audiobook/28.txt` for Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott) at the chapter given in the note.
