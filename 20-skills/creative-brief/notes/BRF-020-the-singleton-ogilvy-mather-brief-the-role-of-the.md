# The Singleton Ogilvy & Mather brief: the role of the communication, a helpful insight, think/feel/do and the single most important point with its evidence

> **The idea in one sentence:** the Australian form puts the "single most important point" next to its evidence and accepts three kinds of evidence: emotional, sensual or rational.

## Explanation

The header asks for the client, the brand, the job number, the agency lead (SO&M Lead), the budget, the requirements, the client contact and the date the material is required. Seven questions follow:

| # | Question | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the role for this communications? | "role", not "objective": what job the communication has in the larger plan |
| 2 | What helpful insight do we have about our audience (their behaviour, beliefs and feelings)? | the insight described on three levels |
| 3 | What do we want them to think, feel or do? | the desired response |
| 4 | What is the single most important point? | the single proposition |
| 4b | What evidence (emotional, sensual or rational) makes this believable? | sits next to point 4 |
| 5 | What aspects of the brand and its personality are important in this communication? | not the whole personality, only what is relevant here |
| 6 | What can't be left out of the communications? | the mandatories |

At the end: the client's signature and the date.

**The philosophy.** Three details set it apart. (1) The insight is "helpful", that is, judged by how much it helps the creative work, not by how spectacular it is. (2) The single point is placed next to the evidence, as a pair: a claim without evidence does not pass. (3) The evidence can be **sensual**, that is, sensory: the texture, taste or sound of the product are arguments as legitimate as figures or emotion. The brand appears selectively: which aspects of its personality matter *now*. Milavsky reaches the same conclusion by another route: in his hierarchy, sensory promises are the strongest and the hardest to copy ([[ADV-023-promises-have-a-hierarchy-of-strength-sensory-delivery-body|ADV-023]]). The order is telling too: the insight comes before the desired response and the message.

## Application

- When you fill in 4b, deliberately look for evidence from each category (emotional, sensual, rational) and choose the strongest.
- In section 5, choose two or three traits of the brand, not the whole brand guide.
- The client's signature turns the brief into the shared reference point for feedback (see [[BRF-025-creative-feedback-is-organized-in-four-categories-things-i|BRF-025]]).

## Example

Cheer proved its color protection with a side-by-side comparison of a shirt washed with and without the product ([[BRF-009-the-positioning-statement-in-the-brief-target-frame-of|BRF-009]]): evidence the viewer sees, not just reads. That is exactly the "single point + evidence" pair that section 4b asks for.

## Questions

- What would sensory evidence be for a digital service, with no physical product?
- "Role" or "objective": what do you gain by asking about the role of the communication instead of its objective?
