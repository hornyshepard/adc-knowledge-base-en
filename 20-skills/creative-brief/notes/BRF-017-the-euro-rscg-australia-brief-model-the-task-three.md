# The Euro RSCG Australia brief model: the task, three triangulated insights, the creative and a chain of approvals

> **The idea in one sentence:** the form asks for three insights from three different sources and only then for a "single-minded thought" built "based on the insights above", approved in four steps.

## Explanation

The form has an administrative header (client and brand, project name, project owner, job number, media mix, budget, briefing date, creative due date) and three numbered blocks, each with a subtitle that states its role:

| Block | Subtitle | Questions |
|---|---|---|
| 1. THE TASK | "why we are here" | the client's business objective; the target audience; how success will be measured |
| 2. THE INSIGHTS | "the perceptive, underlying truth" | the key consumer insight; the key product or brand insight; the key industry or category insight |
| 3. THE CREATIVE | "guiding the response" | the *single-minded thought* to be brought to life, based on the insights; how the target audience will be motivated, or why they will care; the personality of the brand; the mandatories |

In the corner of the page sits the order of approvals: 1. GAD (probably *group account director*), 2. Strategy, 3. CD (creative director), 4. Client. The brief therefore passes through account management, planning and creative before it reaches the client.

**The philosophy.** The form treats insight as a **triangulation**: a truth about the person, one about the product, one about the market. The single-minded thought comes only after them and is explicitly asked for "based on" them. The question "why will they care?" forces a reason from the target's point of view. Measurement is built into the task itself, not left to the end.

## Application

- When you fill in block 2, check whether the three insights meet at one point; that is usually where the single-minded thought lies.
- Rewrite the "business objective" as a communication objective too (see [[BRF-007-the-objective-in-the-brief-is-a-communication-objective|BRF-007]]); otherwise the creatives are left with nothing but "sell more".
- Use the chain of approvals as a rule: the brief does not go to the creatives without the signatures of strategy and the creative director.

## Example

For an exercise, take the Stark case ([[BRF-015-the-stark-cloud-solutions-case-how-a-good-brief-becomes-a|BRF-015]]): the consumer insight (the fear about security), the product insight (encryption) and the category insight (the cloud is seen as less secure than one's own servers) lead naturally to the single-minded thought "more secure than your own server".

## Questions

- What do you gain and what do you lose by asking for three insights instead of one? The risk: three weak observations in place of one strong truth.
- Why does the creative director approve the brief before the client does?
