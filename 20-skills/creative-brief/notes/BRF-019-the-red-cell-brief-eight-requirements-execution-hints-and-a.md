# The Red Cell brief: eight requirements, "execution hints" and a timetable of approvals on a single page

> **The idea in one sentence:** Red Cell puts the strategy into eight short questions and surrounds it with process: named teams, five deadlines and five signatures.

## Explanation

The form (internal file "Creative brief TRC.doc", marked "Str. 1/1", meaning a single page) has three areas.

**1. Administration:** the account team, the job, the creative team, the client, the brand, the assignment, the budget.

**2. Timetable:** the briefing date; the initial creative concepts presentation (internal); the creative concepts presentation (internal); handing over the ready materials; the presentation to the client. So there are two internal reviews before the work reaches the client.

**3. Requirements, eight questions:**

| # | Question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is this advertising for? | the objective |
| 2 | How should the consumer respond to the advertising? | the desired response |
| 3 | Who is this advertising for? | the target |
| 4 | Proposition | the single message |
| 5 | Support | the reason to believe |
| 6 | End benefit to the consumer | what the person gains, not what the product does |
| 7 | Mandatories | none |
| 8 | Execution hints: tonality, restrictions, media hints, creative starters | a starting point for the creatives |

At the end: approval with signature and date for SD/SP, AD/AE, PD, CD and the client. Our reading of the abbreviations: strategy director or planner, account director and executive, production, creative director.

**The philosophy.** This is the brief most focused on **process and accountability**: every stage has a deadline, every role a signature. Strategically, it clearly separates the proposition from the support and from the end benefit, which is the benefit ladder in three lines. The "creative starters" section is unusual: the planner is allowed to suggest starting directions, not just constraints.

## Application

- Use the timetable as a template for any student project: two internal reviews before the client.
- Check that the "end benefit" belongs to the consumer (for example "I sleep easy") and is not an attribute ("encryption").
- Use "creative starters" with care: a starting direction, not a written ad (see [[BRF-002-the-brief-fences-in-the-yard-strategic-constraint-gives|BRF-002]]).

## Example

Sections 4–6 filled in for Stark ([[BRF-015-the-stark-cloud-solutions-case-how-a-good-brief-becomes-a|BRF-015]]): the proposition "more secure than your own server", the support "industry-leading encryption", the end benefit "sleep easy, the company's data are safe". The difference between support and end benefit is immediately visible.

## Questions

- Insight is missing as a separate section. Where would it go: in "Who is this advertising for?" or in "Support"?
- Do five signatures raise the quality, or only the duration? See how ideas erode in the approval process ([[CLI-005-ideas-erode-in-the-approval-chain-each-level-that-asks-for|CLI-005]]).
