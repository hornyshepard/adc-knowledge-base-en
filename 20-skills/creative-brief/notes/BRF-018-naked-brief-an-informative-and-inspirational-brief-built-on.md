# Naked Brief: an "informative and inspirational" brief, built on context, learning and three types of measures

> **The idea in one sentence:** Naked organizes the brief around five simple questions (where are we, why, what, with whom, how will we know) and explicitly asks for the communication history, what was learned, and hard, soft and buzz measures.

## Explanation

It is the only form in the set that explains itself. It opens with an instruction: a brief needs to be informative and inspirational, the template is only meant to help, anything can be changed, and pictures or other stimuli can be added. The structure:

| Section | What it asks for |
|---|---|
| Project management | project name, date of brief, client, brand/product, client contact, any other info |
| What this all about? | "the brief in brief": a one-line summary |
| Where are we? (background) | brand and product information (position, background, description); market context (macro factors, competition); your history and learning from past communication |
| Why are we communicating? | the business problem or objective, set within the overall business strategy and what this project requires |
| What are we communicating? | the messaging and the desired effect |
| Who are we communicating with? | the audience: "The richer the picture, the better the result"; insight into drivers and behaviors, "or ask us to find some" |
| How will we know if it works? | evaluation criteria: **hard** (sales, existing brand trackers), **soft** (for example the requirement for qualitative research), **buzz** (web chatter) |

**The philosophy.** The brief is a conversation about context, not a list of mandatories. The emphasis falls on **learning** (what we did before and what we found out) and on **evaluation**, with a category of measures that the classic forms do not have: buzz, that is, tracking earned media ([[ADV-062-a-brand-s-touchpoints-are-paid-owned-earned-or-shared-poe|ADV-062]]). The audience is described "richly", and the agency offers to find the insight itself if the client does not have one. The form has no sections for budget, tone or mandatories; at Naked, these are left to the conversation.

## Application

- The "brief in brief" section is an excellent test: write it last and check whether the rest supports it.
- Always fill in "history and learning": it is the section most often skipped and the most useful for creatives.
- Define the three types of measures before launch (see [[BRF-014-measurement-is-written-into-the-brief-before-the-campaign|BRF-014]]).

## Example

Filled in for the Stark case ([[BRF-015-the-stark-cloud-solutions-case-how-a-good-brief-becomes-a|BRF-015]]), "Where are we?" would cover the cost of their own servers and the perception that the cloud is less secure, and "How will we know?" would have a hard measure (the perception of security, from 15% to 35%), a soft one (interviews with IT managers) and a buzz one (the discussions in specialist forums).

## Questions

- "Ask us to find some": who is responsible for the insight, the client or the agency? McTigue and Rucker make the brief the client's job, helped by the account planner.
- Is the absence of sections for budget and mandatories freedom, or a risk of the needless rounds described in [[BRF-013-the-execution-section-of-the-brief-has-four-pillars|BRF-013]]?
