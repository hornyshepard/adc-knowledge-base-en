# The objective in the brief is a communication objective: specific, falsifiable and tied to the main barrier

> **The idea in one sentence:** sales are the business objective; the brief has to say which barrier in the target's mind the communication moves, by how much and by when.

## Explanation

McTigue and Rucker separate two levels. The **business objective** (sales, share, profit) can be reached along several paths: new entrants to the category, stealing occasions from a competitor, increasing usage or maintaining loyalty. Each path requires a behavior change, and each change is blocked by an awareness or attitudinal barrier. The strategist isolates the most important barrier: that is the **communication objective**.

A good objective has two properties:

1. **Specific**: it targets one thing (awareness, product knowledge, attitude change, direct action).
2. **Falsifiable**: it is possible to collect data that differentiate a successful and an unsuccessful performance. The simplest way: a quantity and a time period (*a 5 percent lift in awareness and a 20 percent change in trial within six months*).

The Tesla exercise shows the difference: *Make an ad for Tesla* (asks for nothing) → *Increase sales for Tesla* (business, with no quantity and no role for communication) → *Change perceptions about Tesla's suitability for driving long distances from 25 percent to 45 percent positive* (a communication objective).

The authors name three traps: vague objectives, written so that no one can be caught failing; no objective at all, which leaves the creatives to insert their own (awards, entertainment); and objectives that advertising cannot solve, such as a bad product. As the old saying in advertising goes: "The fastest way to kill a bad product is with good ads."

**Convergences.** Barre and Gayrard-Carrera ask for the same thing in other words: there is a single communication problem, of awareness, image or behavior ([[ADV-012-there-is-a-single-communication-problem-of-awareness-image|ADV-012]]). Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott name four possible communication effects, of which brand awareness and brand attitude are always objectives ([[ADV-007-any-communication-can-produce-four-effects-but-brand|ADV-007]]), and set numerical targets only where they make sense ([[ADV-078-numerical-goals-are-set-per-ad-for-exposure-and-processing|ADV-078]]). All three sources separate the business objective from the communication objective; only the vocabulary of the barrier differs.

## Application

- Identify the barriers through qualitative research (interviews, social listening, sales force feedback), then rank them quantitatively: which one, once overcome, brings the most business?
- Write the objective as "from X% to Y%, in target Z, in T months".
- Translate the business goal into concrete behavior, so that anyone on the team can picture it.

## Example

A brand of mayonnaise wanted 5 percent growth in a flat market. The math: about 15 percent of the user base would need to buy one more jar a year, meaning approximately four hundred thousand existing users making one more sandwich a month. The communication objective became: increase knowledge of other mayonnaise-based sandwich recipes from an average of two to four.

## Questions

- Can the business objective appear in the brief? The authors accept it, provided that the communication objective is written too.
- When does a falsifiable short-term objective become dangerous for long-term brand building?
