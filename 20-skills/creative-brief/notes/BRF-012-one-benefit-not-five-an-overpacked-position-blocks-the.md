# One benefit, not five: an overpacked position blocks the creative idea

> **The idea in one sentence:** if the brief gives five benefits, the agency either guesses the right one (a 20 percent chance) or crams them all in, and the audience remembers none.

## Explanation

McTigue and Rucker call the mistake "overpacking the position". It usually begins with the client saying that there are so many good things about the brand that they just can't decide. Creative teams try to tell a compelling story; when the benefit is unclear they have no starting point, and when there are five benefits they have no path. The ideas get stuck. The solution is not a vote but going back to the insight: the insight decides which benefit matters. Nor can the client hand the positioning over to the agency. The stories about the agency that happened upon the exact right benefit on a hunch are the 1 percent.

The idea appears, under other names, in every source of the group:

| Source | Wording |
|---|---|
| Euro RSCG Australia | the *single-minded thought* to be brought to life |
| Singleton Ogilvy & Mather | the *single most important point* |
| Red Cell | a single *Proposition*, separate from the *Support* |
| Ingledew | simplify at every stage; a ten-page brief brought down to 15 words |
| Braun & Landa | "A clear, singular insight. One message, one story." (Will John, Saatchi & Saatchi); shared ideas start from a simple, single-minded idea (Simon Usifo) |

The psychology of persuasion adds an empirical argument: people who have to recall many reasons are persuaded less than those who recall one, because they read the difficulty of finding them as a sign that the reasons do not exist ([[INS-033-ask-customers-for-ten-reasons-and-you-persuade-them-less|INS-033]]). Milavsky limits the support to two or three arguments, because the weakest one becomes the target of counterarguments ([[CPY-005-one-good-reason-beats-a-list-of-37-at-most-three-support|CPY-005]]).

## Application

- Quick test: can you state the message to be taken away in one sentence, without "and"?
- Rank the benefits by the insight. The rest become reasons to believe, rungs for other channels or topics for other executions.
- For creatives: if you receive five benefits, go back to the planner with the question "which one do we choose, and why?" before you start working.

## Example

Blackberry, Super Bowl 2013: a man bursts into flames, grows elephant legs, a crashing semitruck turns into a thousand little rubber ducks, and the voice-over says that it is quicker to show you what the Z10 *can't* do. The authors cite it as an example of a big budget with no point of difference, and the ad was largely viewed as a failure.

## Questions

- When a product does have several equal benefits, how do you spread them over time and across channels without losing coherence?
- Is the single-minded thought a constraint on creativity or a release? See [[BRF-002-the-brief-fences-in-the-yard-strategic-constraint-gives|BRF-002]].
