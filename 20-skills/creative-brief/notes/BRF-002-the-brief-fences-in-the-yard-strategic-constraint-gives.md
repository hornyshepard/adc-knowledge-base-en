# The brief "fences in the yard": strategic constraint gives creatives a place to start

> **The idea in one sentence:** creatives do not want a blank sheet; they want a yard big enough to play in and fenced in enough that they know where to start.

## Explanation

McTigue and Rucker reject the myth that any input from the client restricts the agency's creative freedom. They asked a copywriter whether he would rather start from a blank sheet of paper. His answer was no, absolutely not. His task is to create ideas that help accomplish the client's objectives. He does not want to be told what the ad should be (an ad where a spotted dog tells an orange cat how good the food is), but having no constraints is equally terrible: without them there is nowhere to start. In his words: "I want the client to fence in the yard." The key distinction is between **strategic constraint** (objective, target, insight, a single benefit) and **dictating the execution** (what the ad looks like).

Two related myths: the brief as an unchangeable **contract** (the authors: it is a road map; be open to changes you agree on, not to loose interpretation) and the project that is **"too small"** for a brief (the resources scale down, the strategy does not go away).

Other sources confirm the principle from the creative's side:

- Ingledew: in commercial work, the idea answers a client's problem. The first step is to understand the brief fully and reduce it to its essence, asking any naive question.
- Braun & Landa: for Reddit's "Superb Owl", the lack of money, time and a celebrity became the catalyst (Kevin Koller); Greg Hahn tells clients that they do not have the budget to be boring.
- Glenister: with creators, a brief open to interpretation attracts partners better than a big budget does ([[BRF-022-the-influencer-brief-gives-direction-but-leaves-room-for|BRF-022]]).

## Application

- When you write a brief, check every line: does it limit the *search space* (good) or the *solution* (bad)?
- When you receive a brief that is too broad, ask for the fence yourself: "what is the one thing the target must believe?".
- When a brief changes along the way, ask for a new written and approved version, not a verbal agreement.

## Example

The Stark constraints (the target with old servers, the fear about security, encryption as the proof) do not say what the ad looks like, but they say clearly what it has to solve ([[BRF-015-the-stark-cloud-solutions-case-how-a-good-brief-becomes-a|BRF-015]]).
