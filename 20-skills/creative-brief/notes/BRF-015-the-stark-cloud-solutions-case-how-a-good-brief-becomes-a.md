# The Stark Cloud Solutions case: how a "good" brief becomes a great one, section by section

> **The idea in one sentence:** the brief that "seems okay" is dangerous; each section becomes great when it moves from generalities to a concrete barrier, target and proof.

## Explanation

Throughout the book, McTigue and Rucker follow a fictional B2B cloud data storage company. The first version of the brief is rejected: a vague goal, an "insight" that is only an opinion about the category, a positioning built on "the greatest functionality" and five benefits. In the authors' words, it is "a dangerous brief because, on the surface, it seems okay". The second version is "good", the usual level in the industry, and from there the book takes it to great. (The briefs appear in the book as images; the table reconstructs the changes from the authors' comments.)

| Section | The "good" version | The great version | The lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objective | increase sales by 5% | the perception "Stark is secure" from 15% to 35% (data from the sales force and focus groups) | a communication objective, not a business one |
| Target | small and medium-sized businesses (30.2 million businesses, 99.9% of the businesses in the United States) | SMBs of a given size, with hardware servers near the end of their ~5-year life span | a target with a trigger |
| Insight | customers need to protect their data (a fact) | maintaining their own servers is expensive and draining on resources, but they fear the cloud is less secure than their own solution | from the "what" to the "why" |
| Positioning | the most secure cloud service | frame of reference: all data storage, including their own servers; difference: superior security; proof: industry-leading encryption | the frame of reference moves to where the real competition is |
| Execution | "graphic assets", digital media | a described personality, mandatory logo, "Stark Blue" to be considered, primary media, translations into French (Canada) and Spanish (Mexico), budget and time line | four pillars filled in |
| Measurement | the execution adheres to the brief, plus a figure | tied to the perception of security; a prelaunch test and in-market testing of the finished ad | the measure mirrors the objective |

In chapter 8, the first ad received from the agency is "on brief" (the color, the message) but weak. It becomes the exercise for feedback and for ADPLAN.

## Application

- Use the table as a seminar exercise: take a real brief and rewrite each row on the "good → great" model.
- For each row, ask: what new information did the great version bring (data, a trigger, a reason, a proof)?
- Before rewriting, run the first version through the list of red flags ([[BRF-023-ten-red-flags-in-a-brief-from-an-objective-that-is-not|BRF-023]]).

## Example

In ch. 1, the authors ask readers to critique the first Stark brief themselves, in writing, before reading their comments. The exercise can be repeated in a seminar with real briefs: individual critique first, then a comparison with the authors' list.

## Questions

- What research would have been needed to reach the revised insight?
- Would the same positioning work for large enterprises, which prefer Microsoft and Amazon?
