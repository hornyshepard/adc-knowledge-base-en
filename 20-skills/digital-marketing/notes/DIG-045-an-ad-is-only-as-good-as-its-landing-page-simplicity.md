# An ad is only as good as its landing page: simplicity, continuity with the ad and congruence with the context

> **The idea in one sentence:** a paid click is worth nothing if the page is slow, crowded or says something other than the ad; congruence between the ad, the page and the setting in which the ad was seen raises clicks, conversion and trust.

## Explanation

The landing page is the page people reach after the click, ideally built for a single offer or a single channel. Zahay et al. and Sachdev name the same mistake: dumping the click on the home page, where people have to search again for what they saw. A good page loads in about three seconds and contains exactly the product that was advertised. Its experience also counts toward the Quality Score in search.

Zahay et al. take three criteria from MarketingExperiments, settled on after extensive testing:

- **Simplicity**: one call-to-action button, no website navigation bar (any other click interrupts the conversion path);
- **Continuity**: the headline and images mirror the promise made in the ad;
- **Relevance**: proof for that target, for example customer testimonials with numbers.

The form needs a balance: every extra field lowers completions but makes qualifying the lead cheaper. The answer is to test the form and to track the line at which people stop.

**Schwarz-Musch et al. take the rule one level up.** A banner placed on a website whose content matches the ad gets a higher click-through rate (*context matching*). Because programmatic advertising often optimizes only clicks and impressions, the proposed website lists must be reviewed. **Sachdev** applies it across channels: the same concept everywhere, not identical messages, because contradictory messages look like manipulation.

## Application

- Write the page headline in the same session as the ad headline: it has to sound like a continuation.
- One ad group, one page: the same offer, the same words, the same image on the first screen.
- Remove the navigation bar, leave one button, put the social proof next to the call-to-action.
- Measure the ad, the page and the form separately: the improvements multiply.

## Example

For an unidentified business software company, MarketingExperiments first optimized the PPC ad (+21% click-through rate). It then removed the navigation bar from the landing page, left a single call-to-action button and added customer testimonials with numbers (+54% click-throughs to the forms page). The form, redesigned to look simpler, brought +97% forms submitted. Overall conversion improved by 272 percent.

## Questions

- Why is it counterintuitive for a brand to take its own navigation bar off the page?
- When is a deliberate break with the context useful: a surprising ad in an unexpected setting?
