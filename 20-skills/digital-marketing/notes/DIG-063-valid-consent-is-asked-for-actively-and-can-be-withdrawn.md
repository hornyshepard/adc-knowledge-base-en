# Valid consent is asked for actively and can be withdrawn just as easily: equal buttons, unticked boxes, purposes explained, proof kept

> **The idea in one sentence:** without valid consent there is no first-party data strategy, and the design "tricks" that extract agreement have become a legal risk of up to 4% of annual turnover.

## Explanation

Schwarz-Musch and his colleagues put consent management at the start of any first-party concept: at every touchpoint, before data is collected. The requirements, as they sum them up:

- consent is **informed**: you say exactly what data you collect and for what purpose;
- it is **active**: implicit consent by clicking or scrolling must no longer be used;
- it can be **withdrawn as easily** as it was given;
- the "opt-in" button and the "opt-out" button are **the same size**;
- checkboxes are **not pre-populated**;
- the company must be able to **prove** consent, which is why it uses a consent management tool.

All these "tricks" were popular because they made consent forms look better; today they can bring fines of up to 4% of annual turnover.

Sachdev (SRC-031) adds two ideas: show clearly the value people get if they accept, without exaggerating it; and offer real control. For email, the GDPR requires consent before sending and a simple unsubscribe; Canada requires explicit consent for commercial email.

## Application

- Audit the client's cookie banner against the six criteria.
- Treat the consent text as a copywriting brief: explain the benefit honestly, in plain language, without pressure.
- Do not measure the banner's success only by its acceptance rate; a high rate obtained through manipulation is a risk, not an achievement.

## Example

Shell's website, cited by Sachdev, explains its cookies by category (analytical and performance cookies, personalization cookies, advertising and social media cookies, functional cookies), does not switch anything on by default and offers a visible "Decline All" option for everything that is not required.

## Questions

- Can a consent banner also be an expression of the brand without becoming manipulative?
- What acceptance rate is "healthy" for a brand that actually follows the rules?
