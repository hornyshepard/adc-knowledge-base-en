# Contextual marketing brings digital personalization into physical space: sensors, triggers and three levels, informative, interactive, immersive

> **The idea in one sentence:** what a seasoned salesperson does (the right product, for the right customer, at the right moment and in the right place) can be done at scale if you install sensors, define triggers and choose how deep to personalize.

## Explanation

The long-term goal of IoT and AI is to replicate human **situational awareness**. The mechanism has two parts: an ecosystem of sensors and **triggers**, then **responses** (Fig. 10.1, 10.2).

**The infrastructure** comes in three variants:

- **Proximity sensors at the point of sale**: beacons that communicate with the smartphone or a wearable, through the brand's app. The trigger can be the presence of a customer with a given profile, or the context (the weather: when it is hot, you promote cold drinks).
- **Biometrics**: the face, the voice, the gaze. Facial recognition for profiling and payment ("Smile to Pay"), emotion detection for ad testing (Kellogg's with Affectiva), voice analysis in call centers.
- **A direct channel to customer premises**: smart speakers (P&G's Alexa skill for Tide), appliances with screens, 3D printing.

**Three levels of personalization:**

| Level | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Personalized information** | the right offer: message, product, promotion; location-based marketing and *geofencing* | Burger King "Whopper Detour": a Whopper for a penny, but only when ordered near a McDonald's |
| **2. Customized interaction** | a multistep dialogue that moves the customer along the path to purchase, often gamified | Shopkick: rewards for walking into the store, scanning, trying on |
| **3. Total immersion** | the customer is surrounded by digital experience in the physical space | AR navigation in the Lowe's app; Ralph Lauren's smart fitting room |

The goal of level 3 is to blur the border between physical and digital. Marketing 6.0 takes up the mechanism as **spatial marketing** (proximity + context + augmentation) and insists on a permission-based model.

## Application

- For an activation, write the rule "if X (person, place, moment, weather), then Y (message or offer)" before you choose the technology.
- Choose the level by objective: traffic → informative; conversion → interactive; brand and experience → immersive.
- Build consent into the mechanics (app, login, opt-in): the book insists on permission for facial recognition.

## Example

Walgreens' smart coolers (Cooler Screens) estimate the shopper's age, gender and emotion without identifying the person, combine the data with the weather and local events, and display products and ads on the door. Traffic, purchases and ad revenue grew.

## Questions

- Where does contextualization turn into surveillance? What would your client say if they found out how it works?
- Whopper Detour won major creative awards. What makes the idea more than a geofencing mechanic?
