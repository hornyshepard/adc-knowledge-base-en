# A persona useful for campaigns answers five questions: why now, what it expects, what stops it, who influences it, where you find it

> **The idea in one sentence:** instead of a standard template, the authors propose five questions, each tied to a communication decision.

## Explanation

The starting point: "What should the criteria be used for?" Standard templates miss the details that matter in a particular industry.

1. **Why does the customer engage now?** (a) The *trigger*: changes in personal circumstances (relocation, marriage, a child), socio-economic changes (completing education, a promotion), the expiration of a contract, dissatisfaction with the current product, a recommendation. (b) *Motivation*, read as the "job to be done" (Christensen) in three dimensions: functional, emotional, social (Osterwalder).
2. **What does the customer expect?** The Kano model: basic requirements (taken for granted), performance requirements, enthusiasm requirements. Plus the usage profile: someone who drives an electric car only for short trips in the city has a different range anxiety from someone who regularly drives long distances.
3. **What can prevent the purchase?** Functional pain points (does it really work?), emotional ones (is it worth the money?), social ones (what will others say?) and environmental barriers.
4. **Who influences the decision?** Friends, opinion leaders, influencers, family; in B2B, the buying center: the buyer, the user, the decider, the influencers, the gatekeepers.
5. **What channels can reach the persona?** Traditional and digital touchpoints; for advertising, the paid touchpoints matter.

## Application

- Use the five questions as a guide for interviews with customers and with the sales staff.
- Turn each answer into a decision: the trigger becomes the moment and the awareness message, the barrier becomes the argument you have to dismantle.

## Example

Someone who lives in an apartment does not even consider an electric car, because there is nowhere to charge it at home. The manufacturer has to show the charging solution as early as the awareness stage, or the journey never starts. Likewise, someone who wants to bake but lacks the right utensils gives up, so the recipes of a baking-ingredients brand have to suggest alternatives.

## Questions

- Which of the three "jobs" (functional, emotional, social) is most often ignored in the briefs you receive?
- What question would you add for an impulse category, where the decision takes a few seconds?
