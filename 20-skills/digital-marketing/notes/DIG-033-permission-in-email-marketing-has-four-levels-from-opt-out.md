# Permission in email marketing has four levels, from opt-out to double opt-in

> **The idea in one sentence:** effective email is sent only to people who have agreed to receive it; the more active the agreement, the smaller the list, but the more receptive.

## Explanation

Email remains, the authors say, the workhorse of digital marketing: for brands they already have a relationship with, email is consumers' preferred channel (52%). It is a better **retention** tool than an acquisition tool, because it works with the company's own list (the *house list*). The foundation is Seth Godin's **permission marketing**: customers want to interact with companies, but on their terms and in their time frame.

Spam is ruled out: "No responsible marketer uses or sanctions the use of spam. Ever. Period." Between spam and a solid relationship there are four levels:

| Level | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Opt-out** | the box is already checked; the person has to do something in order not to receive messages: passive agreement, the minimum acceptable standard |
| **Opt-in** | the person actively checks a box to receive messages |
| **Confirmed opt-in** | after checking the box, the person receives a confirmation email, with no reply required |
| **Double opt-in** | after checking the box, the person has to confirm by replying: two actions, a well-qualified prospect |

The opt-in vs. opt-out debate is the old direct marketing dilemma: more leads of lower quality or fewer leads of better quality. The marginal cost of an email is virtually zero, but the annoyance is high, so quality wins. Mind the difference in context: in the EU, the GDPR does not accept pre-checked boxes (see [[DIG-063-valid-consent-is-asked-for-actively-and-can-be-withdrawn|DIG-063]]), so the opt-out level, the minimum acceptable standard for the American authors, is not valid here.

## Application

- Create a clear value exchange for signing up (a discount, content, a service) and say what the person will receive and how often.
- For brand campaigns that need new lists, prefer confirmed opt-in; you lose volume, you gain engagement.
- Link email to social: social media brings in new people, email turns them into customers.

## Example

**Magnolia Bakery** asked for addresses through three routes (online purchases through Shopify, a pop-up offering a 10% discount and a birthday giveaway raffle promoted on social media) and put each segment (new customers, abandoned carts, gift-givers, deal seekers) on its own automated journey. The result: conversion rates up 39% and revenue lifts of 6–8% on the days emails were sent.

## Questions

- Is opt-out ethical if it is legal in some jurisdictions? What does the GDPR say?
- Why do people tolerate unsolicited physical mail more easily than unsolicited email?
