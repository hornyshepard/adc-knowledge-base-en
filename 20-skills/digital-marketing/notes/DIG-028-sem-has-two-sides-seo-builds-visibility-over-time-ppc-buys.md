# SEM has two sides: SEO builds visibility over time, PPC buys it immediately, and they work best together

> **The idea in one sentence:** search engine marketing (SEM) combines organic optimization (SEO) and pay-per-click advertising (PPC); the first is slow but lasting, the second is immediate but lasts only as long as the budget.

## Explanation

For the authors, search marketing is the heart of the web: it puts you in front of the customer at the exact moment they are researching or ready to buy. You do not know who they are, but you know what interests them. SEM is defined as promoting websites by increasing their visibility in search engine results pages (SERPs), and it has two sides:

| | SEO (organic, natural, algorithmic) | PPC (paid search) |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | no payment for placement, but a lot of working time | you pay per click, with a controllable daily budget |
| Speed | months before results show | immediate |
| Predictability | ranking is hard to anticipate | assured volume, easy to test |
| Duration | long-term effect, returning traffic | only while the campaign runs |
| Risk | algorithm changes | you can lose your position if others bid more; unqualified clicks |

Natural search brings in the majority of visitors (60 to 80%, depending on the estimates), because people trust organic results and referrals from friends more than ads. Still, the authors insist that "search is search": the two campaigns have to work together. iCrossing found that when the keywords purchased in paid search were also ranked in natural search, clicks increased by 92%, time on the site by 39% and orders by 45%.

## Application

- Use PPC as a fast lab: test keywords and headlines, then build the SEO content around the ones that convert.
- The other way round, the terms the site already wins organically are candidates for PPC, so you take up more of the SERP.
- Landing page copy can be reused in the ads, for a consistent message (and a better Quality Score).

## Example

On mobile, the first result matters even more: on desktop the first position gets 20 to 30% of the clicks, and on a phone people scroll even less. Mobile conversion, however, stays lower (1.25 to 1.34% against 3.7 to 4% on desktop, in retail).

## Questions

- Is it worth it for a big brand to pay for its own brand name if it already ranks first organically?
- What happens to "trust in organic" when the SERP is full of ads and AI-generated answers?
