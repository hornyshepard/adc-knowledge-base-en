# The IDIC model (identify, differentiate, interact, customize) puts the customer, not the product, at the center of CRM

> **The idea in one sentence:** relationship marketing replaces the question "who do we sell the product to?" with "what is each customer worth and what do they need?", and the IDIC model turns every interaction into information that deepens the relationship.

## Explanation

The textbook translates CRM not as "management" but as **customer relationship marketing**. The central contrast:

| Transactional marketing (product-centered) | Relationship marketing (customer-centered) |
|---|---|
| product, single transaction | customer, relationship cycle |
| acquisition, market share | retention, customer lifetime value (CLV) |
| one-way, mass communication | two-way dialogue, personalized messages |
| product profitability, trial and error | customer profitability, test–measure–refine |

Of the three CRM strategy models cited (IDIC, the Quality Competitive Index, Porter's Value Chain), the authors detail **IDIC** (Peppers and Rogers), which many CRM experts consider the one that best captures the essence of CRM:

1. **Identify** customers (individual or household, name, address);
2. **Differentiate** them by needs and by actual or potential value: the "value sieve", first CLV, then needs segments (who buys only during a sale, who orders every month, who costs money through returns);
3. **Interact** with them according to their needs, more cost-effectively, and use each interaction to add to the database;
4. **Customize** some aspects: communications, the website, special offers.

The study by Zahay, Peltier, Schultz and Griffin found that relational data collected at many touchpoints predict performance better than transactional data.

## Application

- Before you propose a loyalty program, ask what data the client has at the "identify" step and how they will use it at "differentiate".
- Plan communication along the lifecycle: acquisition, repeat purchase, risk of attrition, win-back.
- Encourage customers to "message in" to the brand (Jennifer Aaker), not only to receive messages.

## Example

A baby food marketer knows that leaving the category is natural: the child grows up. The wise marketer has a line of toddler foods ready and the data to know when to offer it to each household: the essence of relationship marketing.

## Questions

- Does IDIC apply to a fast-moving consumer brand sold through supermarkets, with no individual data?
- What happens to "customization" when consumers react badly to too much personalization?
