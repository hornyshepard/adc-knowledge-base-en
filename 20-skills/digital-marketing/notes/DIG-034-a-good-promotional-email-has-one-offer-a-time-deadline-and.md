# A good promotional email has one offer, a time deadline and a subject line under 50 characters

> **The idea in one sentence:** the promotional email inherits the rules of direct marketing (a clear offer, a deadline, a CTA) and those of web design (the golden triangle, above the fold), plus a few rules of its own: preheader, short subject line, legal compliance.

## Explanation

The authors' checklist (Table 11.2):

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Preheader | the short text after the subject line: restates the offer, reminds of the relationship, links to the online version |
| Subject line | short (under fifty characters, the number most email systems display), with the brand, a call to action and urgency; it is often the deciding factor in open rates |
| Offer / CTA | specific, clear, meaningful, and **only one**; multiple offers cause fatigue and reduce clicks |
| Time deadline | not only what the customer should do, but by when |
| Design | the offer in the golden triangle and above the fold; tested on several browsers and on mobile |
| CAN-SPAM | labeled as advertising, a valid reply-to address, a street address, a working unsubscribe |
| Viral and social | "forward to a friend" and links to the networks where the audience hangs out |

The campaign process (Table 11.1) has 11 steps, from building the list, profiling and the communication calendar to writing the copy, testing, measuring and integrating the lessons learned. The copy should be only as long as it needs to be for the action; the destination is a dedicated landing page, not the home page. Transactional and welcome emails have higher open rates and are an overlooked source of cross-selling. Send time is tested: for B2B, Monday mornings and Friday afternoons are bad; for consumers, evenings and weekends are preferred.

## Application

- Write the offer in one sentence first, then the subject line (under 50 characters), then the preheader that completes it.
- Cut any second offer; if you must, send a second email.
- Watch for words that trigger spam filters: "free", the best-performing word in direct mail, can block the email at some providers.
