# Digital customer experience (DCX) is checked with Morville's user experience honeycomb: useful, usable, desirable, findable, accessible, credible, valuable

> **The idea in one sentence:** moving the customer relationship to digital is not enough; the experience has to pass seven UX criteria, and a few laws of psychology show where it breaks.

## Explanation

Sachdev defines customer experience (CX) as the whole of the interactions that shape a customer's perception of an organization; when they take place mainly through digital means, we speak of DCX. User experience (UX) is the part of it that concerns how a website, an app or an ad "feels" and works.

**The user experience honeycomb** (Peter Morville):

| Criterion | The question |
|---|---|
| Useful | does it meet a real need? |
| Usable | is it easy to use? |
| Desirable | do the images, the identity and the design evoke emotion? |
| Findable | is the content easy to find and navigate? |
| Accessible | can everyone use it, regardless of ability? |
| Credible | do people trust the claims? |
| Valuable | does it bring real value? |

**The laws of UX** (Jon Yablonski) cited in the book: the aesthetic usability effect (what looks pleasant seems easier to use); Hick's law (the more options, the longer the decision takes and the more people abandon); the Pareto principle (about 20% of the elements bring 80% of the conversions); the Zeigarnik effect (interrupted tasks are remembered better, so a cart that does not work spoils the memory of an otherwise good experience).

## Application

- Audit a landing page against the seven criteria, then apply Hick: how many options does a person see at checkout?
- Test: click testing, preference testing, eye-tracking.
- Keep the brand voice constant and adjust the tone to the channel and the situation.

## Example

Icons and colors are read through culture. Apple's cylindrical trash can icon on the Mac reminded British users of the red street-side postboxes, and some of them "posted" their emails into the trash. Flipkart uses blue for trust, red for sales and purple for quality gadgets.

## Questions

- Where does "desirable" clash with "usable" in a highly creative campaign?
- Who on the agency team is responsible for the "accessible" criterion?
