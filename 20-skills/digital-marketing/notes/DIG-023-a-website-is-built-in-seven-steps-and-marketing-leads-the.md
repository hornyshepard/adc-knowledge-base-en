# A website is built in seven steps, and marketing leads the first ones: otherwise you get technology without an objective

> **The idea in one sentence:** an effective website is one that meets its objectives; that is why the process starts with SMART objectives and the target market, which are marketing tasks, and ends with continuous measurement and refinement.

## Explanation

The process the authors propose (Fig. 6.6):

1. Establish the website objectives (derived from the marketing objectives and the corporate objectives, in a "cascade");
2. Identify and describe the target market (usually through personas);
3. Assess the website development needs (domain, hosting, CMS, ecommerce);
4. Design the look and the functionality (information architecture, sitemap, wireframe);
5. Conduct usability tests;
6. Deploy the site and tune it;
7. Measure, evaluate and refine.

The first steps belong to marketing, the last ones to the technical team; designing the site structure is shared. The authors warn that one of the worst mistakes is to simply tell the technical experts "design us a website": the result will be loaded with technical bells and whistles but have no marketing objective.

**Usability testing** has three levels: concept testing (concept boards, in a focus group, cheap), prototype testing (design complete, partial functionality) and full usability testing (a working site, not yet public), in which users get tasks (locate the cheapest blender) and talk aloud. Jakob Nielsen shows, with a Pareto curve, that **five users** find over 75% of the usability problems, and 15 find all of them; several small tests at different stages are worth more than one large one.

## Application

- In a website pitch, present the objectives and the persona first, then the design. The client has to see why it looks the way it does.
- Ask for at least two rounds of tests in the budget, each with five users from the target.
- After launch, watch for warning signs: sudden rises in cart abandonment at the payment page, in the bounce rate or in load time.

## Example

The objectives cascade in the book: a long-term corporate objective ("Achieve Full Digital Maturity") breaks down into yearly goals for each department. The website team chooses a CMS in year 1, reduces the time to upload content by 20% in year 2, and improves the satisfaction score of internal content contributors by 25% in year 3.

## Questions

- What is lost when the creative agency delivers the design and another firm builds the site without having seen the persona?
- Are five users enough for a B2B site with several roles in the buying group?
