# "Bring your own persona" (BYOP) challenges the classic persona: people come with different levels of digital capability and of trust in sharing data

> **The idea in one sentence:** instead of predictable patterns that can be packaged into personas, each consumer brings their own level of digital comfort, and it cannot be deduced from demographics.

## Explanation

Sachdev presents the concept of *bring your own persona* (BYOP) as a different view of personas. BYOP still categorizes people, but on two axes that do not appear in the usual persona:

- **digital capability**: from limited to advanced;
- **trust**, meaning the extent to which people share their data: low or high.

The combinations are not predictable. A consumer who is not digital-savvy may have a high amount of trust and share personal information easily; another, just as unfamiliar with digital, may do the exact opposite. Over time, digital savviness grows, and people become more and more aware of privacy. The author asks marketers to recognize these profiles and adjust.

The tension with the classic persona (see SRC-032): the persona is built from age, interests, jobs and touchpoints, on the assumption that similar people behave similarly. BYOP says that, when it comes to the relationship with data and with the interface, demographic similarity guarantees nothing. The two are not mutually exclusive: BYOP can be a layer on top of any persona.

## Application

- Add two fields to every persona: "digital capability" and "trust in sharing data".
- Design consent, onboarding and forms around them: control and transparency for the distrustful, simplicity for the less digital.
- When you build a first-party data strategy, offer value in proportion to the person's level of trust.

## Example

The chapter's privacy box suggests an exercise: a customer with high digital capability and high trust versus one with low capability and low trust. To the first you can offer rich personalization in exchange for data; to the second, a simple path, with clear explanations of what data you ask for and why.

## Questions

- Is BYOP an alternative to personas or only a dimension missing from them?
- Is it ethical to personalize more aggressively for people with high trust but low digital capability?
