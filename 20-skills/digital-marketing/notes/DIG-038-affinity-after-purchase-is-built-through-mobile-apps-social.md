# Affinity after purchase is built through mobile apps, social CRM and gamification

> **The idea in one sentence:** the sales cycle ends at the purchase, but digital marketing sets itself apart by what comes next: turning buyers into advocates through three combined engagement techniques.

## Explanation

For the authors, the path from act to advocate is what separates digital marketing from traditional marketing. Three techniques, which do not exclude each other:

**1. Mobile apps** have three uses: a content medium (Pokémon Go), a self-service channel (Walgreens) and part of the core product itself (the My BMW Remote app unlocks the car). They are built in three steps: the use cases, starting from customer frustrations (L'Oréal Makeup Genius solves the problem of not being able to imagine how the products will look on you); the key functionalities and interface, following the **SoLoMo** principle (social, location, mobile); back-end integration (the Walgreens app is connected to pharmacies and doctors).

**2. Social CRM** is customer-driven, not company-driven; it knows no business hours, it is conversational and public (anyone can see the response). It has three use cases: listening to the voice of the customer, joining conversations, and handling complaints before they go viral. The steps: build sense-and-respond capabilities (a social listening algorithm that filters and prioritizes), develop and empower empathetic human agents with a knowledge base, then leverage community involvement (loyal advocates answer in place of the brand, as in Cisco's communities with reputation points and badges). It differs from social media marketing, which delivers messages. Brands choose between separate accounts for marketing and support (Nike) and a single account (Seamless).

**3. Gamification** uses game principles in non-game contexts, mostly in loyalty programs and communities. The steps: define the actions to trigger (purchase, referral, reviews, personal information, healthier habits); define enrollment and tiering (bronze, silver, gold, possibly with downgrading); determine recognition and rewards (exclusive access, instant gratification). It works because it taps the desire for status and achievement, it is financially accountable (points are a liability on the balance sheet) and it generates data.

## Application

- Before you propose a brand app, write a single sentence: "the app solves frustration X". If you cannot, do not make it.
- Treat social CRM as a public stage: every response is also an ad.
- In a loyalty program, choose the actions you reward according to the 5A stage where the blockage is.

## Example

TripAdvisor uses TripCollective badges, with six tiers from New Reviewer (one review) to Top Contributor (more than 50), plus expertise badges and passport badges, to ensure a steady supply of new reviews.

## Questions

- When does gamification become manipulative (addiction, status pressure)?
- Can a small brand afford human, round-the-clock social CRM?
