# Demand generation takes the prospect from content to qualified lead in six steps, and in B2B the decision is made by a group

> **The idea in one sentence:** in B2B, buyers make most of the decision before they talk to a salesperson; that is why marketing leads the demand generation process: content, leads, nurturing, qualification, conversion, metrics.

## Explanation

The B2B buyer goes through a **buy cycle**: recognizing the need → research → consideration and comparison → purchase. The major change: it is suggested that 85% of the decision is made before the buyer contacts a salesperson, 94% of buyers research online, and search and content have become the shop window. Marketing no longer hands sales leads seen as poor quality; it nurtures them.

The six-step process (Fig. 13.9): **1. content creation** → **2. lead generation** → **3. lead nurturing** (tracks by campaign, industry or persona) → **4. qualification** → **5. conversion** → **6. metrics**, with a feedback loop. The "pull" component is **inbound marketing** (being found through content, not interrupting), the "push" component is outbound. In the HubSpot study cited, a lead cost on average $346 with mostly outbound methods and $135 with inbound.

Qualification uses a **lead scoring** model in marketing automation systems (Marketo, Eloqua, Pardot): points for repeat visits, a video watched, a demo request; points deducted for inactivity or unsubscribing. A lead that crosses the threshold becomes an **MQL** (*marketing qualified lead*) and is passed to sales.

The next step is the **buying group**: typically six to ten decision makers (Gartner), up to 19 for deals above $250,000. Hence **account-based marketing (ABM)**: you treat the client organization as a whole and personalize communication for each decision maker. The main obstacle is organizational, not technological.

## Application

- In a B2B pitch, propose content for each stage and each role in the buying group (who chooses vs. who uses).
- Ask the sales team for the qualification criteria before you write the content offer (white paper, webinar).
- Measure the cost per converted lead, not just the cost per lead: channels differ hugely in conversion.

## Example

Kaiser Permanente Colorado captured leads through landing pages driven by telemarketing, email, paid search and social, nurtured them across channels by persona and stage of the cycle, and raised the win rate from leads from 3% to 33%, with $23.4 million in marketing-sourced revenue in 18 months.

## Questions

- What role is left for B2B brand advertising if the decision is made "from content"?
- How does the copy change when you write for a committee, not for one person?
