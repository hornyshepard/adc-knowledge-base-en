# The digital customer journey has to be short: every step is a barrier

> **The idea in one sentence:** the path from inspiration to purchase has to be as short and intuitive as possible, because at every step some users drop out, and the next attraction is always just a click away.

## Explanation

Sascha Gottschalk (FMD) describes a typical customer journey in five steps:

1. **Inspiration**: an ad, post, photo, video or recommendation.
2. **Insight and information about the product**: website, landing page, YouTube.
3. **Quality check**: customer reviews on Amazon, independent tests, forums.
4. **The visit**: the website, the online shop or the real shop.
5. **Purchase**, subscription, sponsorship.

The journey varies with the product: a car purchase adds a consultation and a test drive; for cheap everyday products, steps two and three are omitted. The shortest version is the "one click away" solution in the literal sense: a button under the post that leads straight to the purchase page (with the verification steps the law requires). The author's main lesson: anything inconvenient or out of tune with the platform risks users abandoning the journey. The shorter the journey, the easier it is to understand and to measure; in long processes (a travel booking), the interaction often breaks off abruptly and you do not know why.

Other rules: evaluate the *whole* journey, not a single stage; make the journey as captivating as a novel, with every newcomer addressed directly and carried along; the landing page should offer exactly what the people who arrive there are looking for, and the link to the booking must never be far away. The uncertainty of attribution remains: as with cough drops, you do not know for sure whether the campaign led to the purchase.

## Application

- Draw the real journey of the campaign and count the steps (clicks, screens, forms); remove the ones that add no trust.
- Put one clear next step (CTA) in each post, not five.
- Offer options without forcing users off the platform (a link in the description, the info box).

## Example

In the Japan campaign, users came from stories and YouTube videos to the "Bucket List Japan" landing page, where they found photos, itineraries, practical information and direct links to flight bookings with partners.

## Questions

- For a category with a long decision (a loan, a car), is a shorter journey always desirable? When does a step add trust?
- How does the principle fit with the strategy of building the relationship before conversion?
