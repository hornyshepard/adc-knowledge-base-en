# The customer journey is built on a persona in five steps, and involvement decides how long the pre-purchase phase lasts

> **The idea in one sentence:** the persona is a "snapshot" of the customer, the journey is the film of their information-seeking and buying process, and the two have to be built together.

## Explanation

The journey serves two purposes: selecting the touchpoints for each stage and shaping the message for the moment the person is in. The authors propose five steps:

1. define the buyer persona;
2. clarify the phases: pre-purchase, purchase, post-purchase; the pre-purchase phase is subdivided (the authors use Aware, Appeal, Ask, as in Kotler's 5A model);
3. describe what the persona does in each phase: what information they look for, on what channels, whom they talk to;
4. note the gain and pain points in each phase;
5. identify the key touchpoints, with the focus on the paid ones.

Steps 3–5 are usually gathered together, in interviews.

**Involvement** changes everything:

| | Low involvement | High involvement |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | mineral water, potato chips, household cleaners | computers, cars, bicycles, further education, insurance |
| Features | lower price, lower risk, little variation between offers | higher price, higher risk, large differences in quality |
| Behavior | little information sought, impulse, repeat purchases | active involvement, more information |
| Pre-purchase phase | brand awareness and sympathy for the brand | longer, with a detailed evaluation |

Involvement also depends on the situation (urgency) and on personality. Customers with low involvement can hardly be convinced by arguments; with high involvement, you need very good arguments.

## Application

- For each phase, write one line: what the persona does, what they hope for, what stops them, where you find them.
- For impulse categories, invest in awareness and sympathy, not in long argumentation.

## Example

The range of an electric car is at the same time a gain point (a criterion for choosing between manufacturers) and a pain point (range anxiety), so it appears in the appeal and ask phases. But someone who lives in an apartment with nowhere to charge does not even start the journey; their problem has to be solved as early as the awareness phase.
