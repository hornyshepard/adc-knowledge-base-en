# Contextual targeting places the ad next to the topic already on the reader's mind, and the semantic version also reads the sentiment of the context

> **The idea in one sentence:** the oldest form of targeting becomes central again after cookies, but it needs exclusion lists and, ideally, AI that understands the context.

## Explanation

The authors distinguish **contextual targeting** (the method: you analyze the content of a website or app by topics, keywords, sentiment) from **contextual advertising** (the result: the ads presented to the user). The principle is simple: tennis shoes are best placed on the sports pages, in the tennis section. The basic digital version is keyword targeting; from a broader perspective, even Google keyword advertising is contextual. Zahay et al. note the same comeback: contextual advertising was popular in the early days of the internet, lost ground to cookies and is returning as third-party data is restricted.

**Risks:**

- homonyms: "recipes" can be cooking recipes or drug prescriptions;
- the emotionally wrong context: brand safety calls for a "black list" for murder, terrorism, sexual content;
- the inventory problem: if too many advertisers want the same ad environment, the space runs out; before, cookies made it possible to find tennis fans on other sites too, and now the answer is first-party data.

Niche sites (watches, fly fishing) minimize waste but have higher CPMs and CPCs, because of their lower reach.

**Semantic targeting** is the evolution with AI: it recognizes not only text but also images, video and sound, and judges whether the environment is positive or negative. The difference from behavioral targeting: behavioral targeting is based on the past (profiles built from browsing), while semantic targeting works in real time and reaches people at the moment they are interested in the topic.

## Application

- Build the keyword list and the exclusion list together; think of the news stories that could make the ad embarrassing.
- For high-involvement products, try niche publishers: a higher CPM, but little waste.
- Ask the programmatic vendor for proof of semantic analysis, not only of keyword matching.

## Example

A food company booked ads for a defibrillator on a reputable newspaper website, using the keyword "cardiac arrest." The match was perfect, but the ads appeared next to the news of several deaths from cardiac arrest at a mountain running event in Germany, in bad weather and sub-zero temperatures, and readers rated them very negatively.

## Questions

- Who is responsible for an ad that appears next to a tragedy: the advertiser, the agency or the publisher?
