# In the search auction, position is won with relevance, not only with money: Ad Rank combines the bid with the quality of the ad and of the landing page

> **The idea in one sentence:** the search engine does not sell the top spot to whoever pays the most but to whoever has the best Ad Rank, so a relevant ad with a good page can appear higher and pay less than one with a bigger bid.

## Explanation

Search advertising (*paid search*) is an auction on keywords, usually paid per click (CPC). Zahay et al. and Sachdev describe the same mechanism. **Ad Rank**, which decides whether and where the ad appears, combines:

- **the bid**: the maximum amount accepted for a click;
- **quality** (*Quality Score*): the expected CTR, the ad's relevance to the search and the landing page experience;
- **the thresholds**, determined dynamically at each auction by device, location and the search terms;
- **the expected impact of ad extensions**: phone, address, links to specific pages (*sitelinks*).

The exact formula is a closely guarded secret, so that advertisers cannot game the system. Google assumes that irrelevant ads annoy users, and it may show no ad at all. The CPC actually paid can be lower than the maximum CPC.

The three books complement each other. Zahay et al. note that the second or third spot can be as profitable as the first. Sachdev adds the economics of keywords: some are very expensive (insurance, attorneys, mortgages), while long-tail phrases are usually cheap. Schwarz-Musch et al. add the precision tools: match types, negative keywords and sitelinks. They also observe that Bing has less competition, so CPCs are often lower.

## Application

- Before you raise the bid, raise the relevance: an ad closer to the search and a better page are the cheapest route to a lower CPC.
- Group keywords into coherent ad groups and build the list of negative keywords from the start.
- For small budgets, look for long phrases with a clear intent, not contested generic terms.
- The copywriter writes a modular set of headlines (benefit, offer, proof, urgency), not a single ad.

## Example

Sachdev compares "divorce lawyer los angeles", a search with a high CPC, with a long phrase such as "I want to get a divorce and need a cheap but good attorney". The long phrase usually costs much less per click, although the intent is just as clear.

## Questions

- If the quality algorithm is secret, how much do you optimize knowingly and how much only through testing?
- What do you lose when you buy only cheap, niche keywords?
