# In direct marketing, the offer and the list weigh more than the creative execution (the 40/40/20 rule)

> **The idea in one sentence:** in direct response logic, 40% of success comes from targeting, 40% from the offer and only 20% from the creative execution, and a good offer states clearly what, by when and why.

## Explanation

Direct marketing has its own mix, different from the four Ps:

- **The offer**: product, price, positioning and everything that makes up the complete proposition;
- **The list**: the targeting vehicle (today: the database, the audience);
- **The media**: any medium can be a direct response medium with the proper implementation;
- **The creative execution**: which, the authors say, tends to play a secondary role in this action-oriented context;
- **Service and support**: key where the physical shopping experience is missing.

The most important element is **the offer**. A good offer answers three questions: *What* (what the customer has to do), *By When* (the deadline) and *Why* (the benefit). The textbook's example: "Free Shipping if ordering by midnight": you order (what), by midnight (when), to get free shipping (why). Second comes the list, meaning a strong database.

In the chapter on conversion, the authors cite the time-honored direct marketing rule: **40/40/20**: 40% targeting, 40% offer, 20% creative execution.

## Application

- Before you work on the headline, check the offer: does it have a What, a By When and a Why? If the deadline is missing, add urgency.
- For a banner, an email or a PPC ad, write the offer and the CTA in one sentence first; only then look for the visual idea.
- When performance is weak, do not change the creative first: test the list (whom you show it to) and the offer.

## Example

The Hu email analyzed in the email chapter (Fig. 11.14, p. 310) is a clean example: free shipping that expires within a stated period, a simple design, a single offer, no competing messages.

## Questions

- The 40/40/20 rule is cited without any empirical source. What would the IPA studies (Binet & Field) say about the role of creativity in effectiveness?
- Does the rule also work for brand communication, not only for direct response?
- Is an advertising school entitled to challenge the secondary role given to creative work?
