# Micro-moments: four intents (I want to know, go, do, buy) that the brand answers within seconds

> **The idea in one sentence:** on the phone, many decisions are made in short moments triggered by a precise intent. The brand wins if it is present at exactly that moment, with an answer to that intent, not with a generic message.

## Explanation

Ejeke takes up Google's classification of micro-moments (trend 47):

| Micro-moment | The intent | What the brand has to offer |
|---|---|---|
| **I want to know** | information, comparison | a clear answer, educational content |
| **I want to go** | location, opening hours | local presence (Google Business), a map, opening hours |
| **I want to do** | practical learning | tutorials, step-by-step guides, video |
| **I want to buy** | transaction | a visible offer, a short path to payment |

The link with **search intent** (trend 25): Google tries to understand *why* someone searches (to learn how to do something, to buy something or to go somewhere) and ranks the results accordingly. A "how-to" search puts tutorials first. "Amazon" with no other context means the website, not the rainforest. The MUM upgrade allows more complex queries. The book's example: for "fish oil", Google understands that you want the health benefits and the side effects.

Ejeke's advice: be present on the platforms where your audience is, invest in short ads that answer the question of that moment, and never be boring. The typical mistake: a discount ad with only a logo and "50% off". If you want to attract people who do not know you, show them the product.

## Application

- For a category (DIY, for example), make a map: which "I want to know" and "I want to do" questions does a person have before "I want to buy"? Produce one piece of content for each.
- Write mobile ads as answers, not announcements: one promise, a visible product, one action.

## Example

The 76 gas stations campaign on Waze (trend 24, geofencing): the logo appeared on the map as drivers approached a station, with entry into a competition if they filled up. Ejeke reports a 6.5% increase in navigation to the stations. It is an "I want to go" micro-moment caught at exactly the right time.

## Questions

- Ejeke cites the claim that attention span fell from 12 seconds to 8, "no better than a goldfish", attributed to Microsoft. The claim has been widely disputed. Why do such figures survive in trend books?
- How do micro-moments change when people ask an AI assistant instead of searching on Google?
