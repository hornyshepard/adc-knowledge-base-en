# Spotify Wrapped: customer data becomes a personal gift that customers share themselves

> **The idea in one sentence:** Spotify Wrapped shows how usage data, usually invisible and suspect, can become a content product people want: personal, visual and made to be posted.

## Explanation

Connors (SRC-030) describes Wrapped as an annual event that users eagerly anticipate: each user gets a personalized summary of their year of listening, with their most-streamed songs, artists and genres and the number of minutes. The author credits its success to a combination of three elements:

| Element | Role |
|---|---|
| **Personalization** | the experience is unique and "feels like a gift to its users" |
| **Data visualization** | the numbers become fun and easy to digest |
| **Shareability** | the option to share on social media turns the campaign into a viral phenomenon; millions of shared summaries generate organic buzz |

Connors's lesson is also about privacy: in a world where people worry about how their data is used, Wrapped shows that data used **transparently and creatively** can enhance the customer experience and strengthen brand loyalty. It is the opposite of "creepy" personalization: people see exactly what the brand knows about them, and the information is about their taste, not their private life.

The book gives no figures or sources for the case, so claims about its scale have to be checked in primary sources (festival cases, Spotify reports).

## Application

- When a client has usage data, ask: what story about the *customer* (not about the brand) can these data tell?
- Design the format for sharing from the start: story dimensions, an identity "label" ("you're in the top 1% of listeners of…"), elements of pride.
- Run the transparency test: if people saw all the data being used, would they feel flattered or watched?

## Example

The mechanics are related to Share a Coke: there too, the personal product (the name on the bottle) becomes content that people post. The difference: Wrapped personalizes on the basis of behavior, so it needs a higher level of trust.

## Questions

- Would a "Wrapped" work for a bank or an online store? Where does data-based personalization become embarrassing?
- Is Wrapped content marketing, a product or advertising?
