# The digital customer journey is not a funnel traveled in order, but a path with interruptions and loops that closes in a loyalty loop

> **The idea in one sentence:** the customer does not move neatly down from awareness to purchase; people stop, go back, skip steps, change devices, and after the purchase they keep reevaluating the brand.

## Explanation

Both textbooks start from the classic funnel (AIDA, introduced by St. Elmo Lewis). They reach the same conclusion, but by different routes.

**Sachdev keeps the funnel as a map.** His digital conversion funnel has the stages Awareness (an ad view or a video view), Interest (a website visit), Desire (feature review, shipping costs, chat) and Action (buy, subscribe). Its practical value lies in identifying where the person stops and what message gets them moving again. Every point of contact is a *touchpoint* and a source of insight.

**Zahay et al. change the model.** They use the *customer decision journey* (popularized by McKinsey): trigger → initial consideration set → active evaluation (search, reviews, friends, the store) → moment of purchase → postpurchase experience → **the loyalty loop**. In the loop, people enjoy the product, become advocates for the brand and bond with it through service and content. So the marketer produces content for loyalty too, not only for the sale. In B2B, the journey looks like "a bowl of noodles".

Both insist on **friction points**, which customers expect the brand to eliminate. Kotler reaches the same conclusion: his 5A path also ends with advocacy.

## Application

- Draw the journey for the specific product and segment, not a generic one.
- For each stage, note what the person asks, where they search, what friction stops them and what content helps them.
- Plan the post-purchase content explicitly; the loyalty loop is a separate brief.
- Do not judge an awareness ad by the immediate sale: it may only have triggered a later search.

## Example

In Sachdev's case, Seema ignores the first ad for a smart TV. The second one prompts her to search on Google, where she narrows down to three models. She is distracted by work, discovers another brand through a search ad and resumes in the evening, on her phone. She drops out because shipping will take 21 days and comes back when a sales representative offers her fast shipping. She returns the product because the assembly was not explained to her. The ideal path had five steps; the real one has twice as many and two drop-outs.

## Questions

- For which decisions (media planning, reporting) does the funnel remain a good tool?
- What does the loyalty loop look like for a low-involvement category such as mineral water?
