# A valid digital objective is chained to a marketing objective, to a value for the customer and to a metric

> **The idea in one sentence:** the digital objective follows from the marketing objective, which follows from the business objective, and it has to say what the customer gets and how it is measured.

## Explanation

Sachdev proposes a table with three columns, plus a metric for each row:

| Marketing objective | Digital objective | Value for the customer |
|---|---|---|
| Sales +2% | Website conversion +6% | access to information and easy ordering |
| Brand exposure +5% | Social media mentions +10% | easy-to-make choices about brands |
| Customer service +7% | +18% customer service issues resolved through AI chatbots | easy returns, without telephone support |

The book lists seven typical digital objectives: **conversion** (with conversion rate optimization and an explicit CTA), **gaining customer insights**, **retention and loyalty**, **expertise**, **interactive sales and exceptional customer service**, **brand image, reputation and trust**, **providing information**. Some go hand in hand (interactive sales increase conversion).

The central argument: an objective with no value for the customer can be reached on paper and missed in the business. If you push the number of subscribers without giving them value, you get high returns, low retention and a constant need for new customers; sometimes it is more profitable to keep the ones you have won. And an objective with no metric cannot be evaluated.

## Application

- In every digital brief, ask for the full table: marketing objective → digital objective → value → metric.
- The "and what does the customer get?" test: if you cannot fill in the third column, the objective is only yours.
- Pick 1–3 relevant objectives; do not tick all seven.

## Example

CAROL, the London AI-powered exercise bike: Instagram sends people to the website, the website states the benefit right away ("fittest, fastest"), and a Google search ad offers $250 off. Each channel has a distinct role on the way to conversion and a clear value for the consumer.

## Questions

- Who defines "value for the customer": the company or the customer? The author insists that value counts the way the consumer sees it.
- When does a vanity metric (likes, impressions) become a legitimate objective?
