# A buyer persona is not the target group: segmentation says whom you choose, the persona gives the segment a face and says how it decides and where it can be reached

> **The idea in one sentence:** the target group is the result of market segmentation; the persona is an archetype of the desired customer, built from data, so that you know what message to give it, on what channel and at what moment.

## Explanation

Schwarz-Musch et al. compare the two concepts:

| | Target group | Buyer persona |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | market segmentation (Smith, 1956) | Alan Cooper (1999), software design |
| What it is | buyers who react homogeneously to marketing | a fictional person, an archetype of the desired customer |
| Criteria | few, the ones that best explain differences in buying behavior | goals, motives, benefits sought, pain points, barriers, role in the decision, touchpoints |
| Use | choosing the target, traditional media | digital communication, content, product |

In digital, segmentation is not enough. It does not say which touchpoints people use in each phase, what they expect and what frustrates them. Platforms allow targeting options (hobbies, relationship status) that are absent from traditional descriptions. And part of the decision is made before the brand appears.

**Zahay et al. reach the same idea from the design side.** Segment data are cold, so marketers give them a name, a photo and "a day in the life". The persona becomes the means by which marketing passes the target's needs on to web designers and programmers. For "Penny", a sales assistant in London, a *storyboard* was even drawn, a technique taken from traditional advertising, showing the decisions she makes when buying paint for a room. **Sachdev** adds a rule of proportion: a persona that is too detailed no longer resembles most of the target.

The difference between the sources: Zahay's persona is biographical (hopes, fears, a quote), Schwarz-Musch's is geared to the communication decision.

## Application

- Start from a single target group and check whether it hides several personas.
- Tie each statement in the persona to a source (data, interviews); otherwise mark it as a hypothesis.
- Use the persona as a filter for ideas: would this person stop scrolling for this?

## Example

A bank targets double income no kids couples (DINKS) in Germany, aged 30–39, with the motive "retirement planning". The segment looks clear, but it is heterogeneous in risk tolerance and knowledge of financial products. The resulting matrix has four quadrants, so four personas. The bank starts with the most important one.

## Questions

- If the persona is fictional, how do you avoid projecting the team's prejudices onto it?
- When does a persona become a stereotype that narrows ideas instead of inspiring them?
