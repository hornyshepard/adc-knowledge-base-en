# Digital engagement follows three stages (listen, analyze, do), and a social media campaign has five steps

> **The idea in one sentence:** firms first learn what customers say online, then measure who they are, what they do and where they come from, and only then launch tactics; analysis without action is useless, and action without listening is blind.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy describe a three-stage process (Exhibit 3.4):

**1. Listen.** Systematic monitoring of social networks, blogs and review sites, as at a party: you listen first, then join the conversation. If no one is talking about the brand, the objective is awareness. If the conversation is negative, you respond immediately. If it is positive, you find out where it takes place so you can propagate it. **Sentiment analysis** assesses how favorable the comments are.

**2. Analyze**, on three levels:
- *traffic*: hits, unique visitors, page views;
- *who they are and what they do*: the **bounce rate** (they leave after one page, like walking into a store, taking a quick look and leaving), the entry and exit pages, **click paths**, the **conversion rate** (the percentage who take the desired action; a sudden drop can signal a broken navigation path);
- *where they come from*: search, referral, keywords.

**3. Do.** Taco Bell launched a Snapchat filter that showed users' faces as a taco (224 million uses) and a themed hotel for one weekend.

**The social media campaign in five steps** (Exhibit 3.5, the Facebook example): (1) identify strategy and goals (awareness, likes, sales); (2) identify the target audience (location, age, interests; big enough, but not too broad); (3) develop the budget (daily cap, tools for scheduling posts); (4) the campaign: experiment and engage (a clear call to action, strong images, content updated almost constantly); (5) monitor and change.

## Application

- Start any social media strategy with a listening period and a sentiment report, not with a posting calendar.
- Choose one main metric per objective (conversion for sales, mentions for awareness) and track it at step 5.
- Unlike TV, do not run the same execution for months: people expect content that changes.

## Example

Hilton (the opening of chapter 3) has about 200 staff members, qualified as local experts, who respond to general requests for advice. When a marathoner asked what to do after the Chicago Marathon, the Hilton Suggests account suggested the Magnificent Mile. The listening then fed the "Expect More. Expect Hilton." campaign.
