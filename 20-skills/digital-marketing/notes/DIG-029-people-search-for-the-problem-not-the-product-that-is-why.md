# People search for the problem, not the product: that is why SEO starts from search intent

> **The idea in one sentence:** modern search engines answer the intent behind the query (*user intent*), not the word; the marketer has to find out how the target audience phrases its problem and build content and SERP presence for that phrasing.

## Explanation

SEO used to mean finding the keywords and putting them on the page. With **semantic search**, queries have become phrases: "where can I find cheap pink purses?" leads to discount outlets, "who makes the best pink purses?" to designers. Google tries to understand the entire query (an "entity"), and the Hummingbird, RankBrain and BERT updates also move toward intent. Voice search and AI reinforce the trend.

The SEO process the authors propose therefore starts with people: (1) define the target market; (2) find out what they search for and **why**; (3) develop the keyword and phrase strategy; (4) align the content strategy; (5) redesign the site around them; (6) register the site with search engines; (7) optionally, a paid campaign to inform the organic campaign.

The SERP is no longer a list of ten links but a mosaic of "answers", each with an action for the marketer:

- **Related questions** → answer them on the website;
- **Top stories** → seek press and blog coverage;
- **Knowledge panel** → work on the brand as an entity;
- **Local 3-pack** → fill out all the information in Google My Business;
- **Star reviews** → direct customers to the review sites;
- **Videos and images** → produce them and tag them properly.

## Application

- Do insight research before keyword research: interviews, social listening, questions from "related searches".
- Write content that answers the person's real question, in their language, not in the jargon of the category.
- Check the SERP for your term while logged out of your account: personalized results can mislead you.

## Example

"Monster Spray" products, based on the book by Cherith Clark and Kirstin Stokes, promise to scare monsters away from children's bedrooms. But parents do not search for "monster spray"; they search for "my child is afraid of the dark", and that is where the brand has to be.

## Questions

- What happens to SEO when the answer comes directly in the SERP or from a chatbot, with no click?
- How does intent change in local search, where fewer than 40% of searches start from a search engine?
