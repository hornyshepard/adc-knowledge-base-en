# SEO rests on three pillars (technical, content and off-site), and Google weighs content, links and RankBrain the most

> **The idea in one sentence:** a website rises in organic search if it can be indexed and used easily (technical), if it offers content with a purpose (content) and if others cite and confirm it (off-site).

## Explanation

Search engines send out robots (*spiders*) that crawl the web and index content; then, for each query, an algorithm guesses which pages are the most relevant. Google revolutionized search by taking into account factors outside the page: incoming links work like citations for academic papers (Page and Brin, Stanford). The textbook takes three factors from Dave Davis (Search Engine Land):

| Pillar | What it includes |
|---|---|
| Technical | indexing, a single URL, sitemap, working internal links, HTTPS, page speed, mobile-friendly, relevant above-the-fold content |
| Content | purposeful content that attracts links and social signals, keyword research, title tag and meta description, headings (H1, H2), optimized images with alt tags |
| Off-site | consistent name, address and phone (Google My Business), social media consistency, legitimate backlinks, domain authority |

Google confirmed (2016) that the top three factors are **content, links and RankBrain** (the machine learning component that guesses the user's true intent). "Black hat" practices (keyword stuffing, invisible text, link farms) are penalized by the Panda and Penguin updates. The recent trend: articles of 1,200 to 1,500 words and the **pillar page + topic clusters** architecture: a pillar page of 5,000 to 10,000 words on a broad topic, linked to detailed articles.

## Application

- **The title tag is an advertising headline**: put the keyword portion first (how you want to be found), then the branding portion, in 50–60 characters. The book's example: "University of Texas System | Fourteen Institutions. Unlimited Possibilities."
- Write the meta description like a small ad: if you leave it empty, Google picks a snippet itself.
- Describe images in the alt tag for accessibility and SEO, without stringing keywords together.

## Example

HubSpot has a pillar page on Instagram marketing strategies, linked to articles on goals, audience, competitor analysis, the editorial calendar, brand consistency and growing the number of followers.

## Questions

- If Google ignores the meta description in ranking, why does it still matter for the click?
- What does "purposeful content" mean for a consumer brand with little to say?
