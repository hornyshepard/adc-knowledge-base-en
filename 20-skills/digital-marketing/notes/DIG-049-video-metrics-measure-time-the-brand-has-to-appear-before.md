# Video metrics measure time: the brand has to appear before the viewer leaves

> **The idea in one sentence:** unlike a page, a video measures its success in time watched; since the average view duration is short, the brand name and the call to action have to be placed early.

## Explanation

The textbook groups video content marketing metrics into four families (after Wistia):

| Family | Metrics |
|---|---|
| Reach | number of views, play rate, audience characteristics |
| Engagement | percent of video viewed, comments, social shares |
| Relevance | click-through rates, subscriptions/signups, conversions |
| User experience | bounce rate, time spent on page (metrics of the website that hosts the video) |

Engagement is the primary contribution of video and is most often measured by the **percent of the video viewed** (from hitting the play button to clicking away). A caution: each platform defines differently what portion of the video must be viewed to count as engagement. **Play rate** is less rigorous: it only shows that the person started watching. The fourth family has a subtlety: the user experience metrics are not produced by the video but by the website; if the video is doing its job, they should improve.

In the book's YouTube Studio example, the average view duration was about a minute and a half. The authors' conclusion: mention the brand name early, because many viewers will never get to the call to action at the end.

## Application

- When you script a brand video, check what people see and hear in the first 5–10 seconds: does the brand appear? Is the benefit clear?
- Choose one primary KPI according to the objective: view-through rate and Brand Lift for consideration, clicks and signups for action.
- Do not compare "views" across platforms without checking each one's definition.

## Example

Procter & Gamble made a YouTube video about Tide (an "intrinsically boring" subject: getting dirty clothes clean) with **brand consideration** as its main objective, and chose **view-through rate** (VTR) as its primary KPI. Result: Tide's highest-ever VTR and a high Brand Lift score (Google obtains Brand Lift from online surveys and search results in the days immediately following the airing). In the second iteration, retargeting members of its YouTube channel and subscribers to the creators' channels lowered the cost per view (CPV) by 50 percent.

## Questions

- Does the "brand early" rule contradict storytelling that saves its reveal for the end? When is the risk worth taking?
- What does a high play rate with a low percent viewed say about the thumbnail and the first frame?
