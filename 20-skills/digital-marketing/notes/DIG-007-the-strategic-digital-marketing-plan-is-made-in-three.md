# The strategic digital marketing plan is made in three stages and written in eight sections, one of them devoted to legal issues and privacy

> **The idea in one sentence:** Sachdev separates the planning process (three stages) from the resulting document (eight sections), and the digital strategy has to follow from the corporate and marketing strategy.

## Explanation

**The process** (the strategic digital marketing planning process):

1. An overview of the organization and its marketing: mission, vision, overall marketing strategy and objectives.
2. The situation analysis, then digital objectives, value proposition, digital STP, strategies and tactics (including privacy, security, legal and ethical issues).
3. Analytics, resources and the implementation plan.

**The document** has a cover, an executive summary (three to five paragraphs) and eight sections:

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Organizational mission, vision and objectives |
| 2 | Overall marketing strategies and objectives (not digital ones) |
| 3 | Digital SWOT: digital marketing talent, digital brand image and digital trust, high costs with poor return, changing privacy rules, obsolete technologies |
| 4 | Digital objectives, digital STP, digital value proposition |
| 5 | Strategies and tactics: 4P's, 7A's, competition, website/SEO/SEM, email, social, mobile, brand and reputation: "the heart of the strategic digital marketing plan" |
| 6 | Privacy, security, legal, ethical |
| 7 | Analytics, evaluation, measurement (KPIs per channel) |
| 8 | Implementation and control plan, budget, contingency plan |

The key distinction: the **strategy** sets the target at a high level (for example, web traffic +X%), the **tactic** is the concrete action (the website optimizations). Tactics out of line with the strategy do not serve the organization's goal.

## Application

- Use the structure as a template for the semester project or for a digital strategy pitch.
- Check traceability: every objective in section 4 has to support one in section 2.
- In section 8, budget for human capital too, not only for media.

## Example

The implementation table in the book: a three-month search campaign, with $10,000 per month for the search engine and $2,500 per month for human capital, controls through analytics monitoring and a contingency plan if the results do not come. The opening case: the adidas CEO said e-commerce would account for more than 40% of the industry's sales by 2025.

## Questions

- How does a three-to-five-year plan square with the pace of agile sprints that the same author recommends in ch. 12?
- What section is missing for a creative agency (insight, idea, creative platform)?
