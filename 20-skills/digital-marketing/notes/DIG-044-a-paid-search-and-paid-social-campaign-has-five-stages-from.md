# A paid search and paid social campaign has five stages: from keyword research to testing

> **The idea in one sentence:** whether you bid on Google or promote a post on Instagram, the process is the same: research, copy, landing page, setup, tracking and testing.

## Explanation

The stages the authors propose (Figure 8.5, "Paid Search Process"):

1. **Keyword Research.** Trends (Google Trends), traffic to your own site, asking customers how they search; then you narrow the list to what you can afford to bid on.
2. **Ad Copywriting.** You make the final selection of keywords (Google's keyword planner, Moz) and the match type. An effective ad is persuasive, specific, concise and distinguishes the firm from the competition; it contains the keyword the user is searching for, an offer, a call-to-action and, ideally, a time deadline.
3. **Landing Page Development.** The page loads in about three seconds or less, contains good visuals, navigates easily and contains the product that is being advertised.
4. **Campaign Setup.** Several ad groups, geographic targeting (in the United States, down to metropolitan areas), showing ads only on certain days and times (*Day Parting*), budgets monitored daily.
5. **Tracking and Testing.** Spend, clicks, click-through rate, CPC, conversions; A/B tests, refining while the campaign runs.

**Match types** decide how widely a keyword "catches" searches:

| Type | Notation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Broad | cat food | raw, freeze-dried, dry (kibble) and wet cat food ads will appear |
| Phrase | "cat food for seniors" | the exact phrase, in that order |
| Exact | [cat food sensitive stomach] | only the precise meaning |
| Negative | -raw | excludes the term |

Broad match will generally not be the most cost-effective: you pay for irrelevant clicks. For **paid social** (in-feed display and promoted posts) the steps are the same, and the platform is chosen with two basic questions: where your target audience is found and what their intent is.

## Application

- Start with a small trial (one day, one region), analyze it and only then extend the budget.
- For social, choose platforms for which content can easily be repurposed, and keep testing ("TEST, TEST, TEST," the authors insist).
- Tie every ad group to its own landing page.

## Example

**Olivia Rose**, a website that offers Black hair tutorials to English- and French-speaking women, created a 20-second video that could be used on both Facebook and Instagram. It targeted women aged 13 to 40 in French-speaking countries outside France (Guadeloupe, Reunion). The one-day Sunday trial was so successful that the campaign was broadened to English-speaking countries and ran for four days: 15,647 new Facebook fans, 1,784 new YouTube subscribers and 1,758 new Instagram followers.

## Questions

- Which stage do agencies skip most often, and why?
- Is day parting a media decision only, or also a creative one (what you say in the morning vs. in the evening)?
