# The 4E framework: digital marketing has to excite, educate, let customers experience the product and engage them

> **The idea in one sentence:** Grewal and Levy propose four objectives for online, social and mobile activities (Excite, Educate, Experience, Engage), and the first three set the stage for the fourth, engagement, which brings a relationship and loyalty.

## Explanation

**The 4E framework** (Exhibit 3.1):

| E | What it does | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| Excite | relevant offers, personalized from CRM and loyalty data | Lush: #LushLife, customers' posts may appear on the official page; Staples: a coupon on the phone in the store |
| Educate | a clear call to action that brings people to the website or the store, where they learn the value proposition | #KnowYourLemons: a dozen lemons showing 12 signs of breast cancer, understood even by people with limited literacy skills |
| Experience | simulating use of the product before purchase | Sephora video tutorials, the Sephora Community, YouTube |
| Engage | action, a relationship, possibly loyalty; engagement can also be negative | IHOP → IHOb (a 6,477 percent increase in mentions in one day); IKEA Place (AR); the adidas #DareToCreate failure |

Negative engagement has the potential to be more damaging than positive engagement is beneficial: adidas's personalized jerseys ended up displaying racist Twitter handles.

For websites and blogs, the authors add the **7C framework** (Exhibit 3.2): core goals, contextual elements (design and navigation suited to the target), content (relevant, not always about the product; Fidelity gives tax advice in April), community (corporate and professional blogs, crowdsourcing as at Betabrand), communication (chat, Twitter), commerce (many channels, consistent) and connection (call-to-action buttons: Warby Parker has four). Here too comes the distinction between **organic search** (ranked by relevance, free) and **SEM** (search engine marketing, paid search, cost per click).

## Application

- Give each digital piece one dominant E. A social media post that tries to do all four does none of them well.
- Design the call to action together with the idea, not at the end.
- Before a participatory campaign (a hashtag, user-generated content), think about how it could be hijacked and how you will moderate it.

## Example

#KnowYourLemons (Worldwide Breast Cancer) used a simple, amusing image to educate about breast self-exams, with a link to a scientific microsite. Donations rose sharply.

## Questions

- Is the 4E framework a list of objectives or a required sequence? Can there be engagement without education?
- Is "Experience" possible online for sensory products (perfume, food)?
