# Data-driven marketing targets "segments of one" and starts with a narrow objective, not with an IT project

> **The idea in one sentence:** big data makes segmentation possible down to the individual and in real time, but data projects fail when they are treated as IT investments, as silver bullets or as systems on autopilot.

## Explanation

The opening case is famous: Target's algorithm inferred from her purchases that a teenager was pregnant, and her father found out from the coupons that arrived in the mail. Data gives power, but also risks.

**Four methods of segmentation:** geographic and demographic (top-down, actionable, but less meaningful and static), psychographic and behavioral (bottom-up, meaningful, but harder for salespeople to identify). Good segmentation combines them: you cluster people by psychographics and behavior, then add the geographic and demographic profile. The result is a **persona**. Big data adds two things: granularity down to the individual (***segments of one***, Peppers and Rogers's one-to-one idea) and **dynamic** segments (the same air traveler prefers business class for a business trip and economy for leisure travel).

**Three reasons for failure:** the project is treated as IT instead of marketing, big data is considered the silver bullet in place of traditional market research (ethnography, taste testing), and the system is left "on autopilot."

**Three steps:**

1. **Define the data-driven marketing objectives**: one or two, with the biggest impact, for quick wins and early buy-in (Figure 8.2).
2. **Identify data requirements and availability**, across six sources (social, media, web traffic, POS and transactions, IoT, engagement). Use a **data matrix** (Figure 8.3): looking horizontally, you see whether you have enough data for the objective (triangulation); looking vertically, what you extract from each source.
3. **Build an integrated data ecosystem**, tied to a unique customer ID or, if privacy does not allow it, to a demographic segment as the common denominator, in a single data management platform.

## Application

- When a client says "we want to be data-driven," ask first: for which decision, exactly?
- In strategy, build the persona from behavior and motivations, then add the demographics, not the other way around.
- Plan first-party data (logins, loyalty apps) so that you can connect the sources to each other.

## Example

The persona in the book: "John," 40, a digital marketing manager at a major consumer-packaged-goods company, measured on brand awareness and online conversion rates, cost-conscious, with a team of five people and two agencies. It is a useful profile for a digital marketing agency looking for clients.

## Questions

- The Target case shows where prediction becomes intrusive. What ethical rules would you propose?
- How does step 3 change in the era without third-party cookies and with strict GDPR rules?
