# Third-party cookies made retargeting, lookalikes and cross-site profiles possible, and the law and browsers are taking them out of the game: power shifts to first-party data

> **The idea in one sentence:** a technology invisible to the public was the basis of personalized digital advertising, and its disappearance forces the industry to rethink targeting and measurement on the brand's own data.

## Explanation

A **tag** (pixel) is a piece of code that records behavior on a page. A **cookie** is a small text file in the browser that recognizes the user. Cookies were developed at Netscape so that shopping carts would remember what a customer had added.

| | First-party | Third-party |
|---|---|---|
| Who sets it | the site visited | another domain (ad server, network) |
| Where it works | only on that site | on any site that loads that code |
| What it is for | login, cart, analytics | cross-site profiles, retargeting |

**What third-party cookies were used for** (SRC-032): retargeting, targeting by financial situation, *lookalike* modeling and optimization during the campaign rather than only after it.

**Why they are disappearing:** the e-Privacy Directive (2002/2009) and the GDPR (2018) require consent; the CCPA gives Californians the right to know, to delete and to opt out of the sale of their data. Safari and Firefox have blocked or limited third-party cookies since 2019–2020. Apple's ATT (iOS 14.5) requires consent for tracking across apps: fewer than half of users consent, from 20% in Germany to almost 50% in the United Arab Emirates.

**Where the sources contradict each other.** Zahay et al. and Sachdev write as if Google were about to eliminate cookies from Chrome. Schwarz-Musch et al. record that in July 2024 Google reversed the discontinuation and left the decision to the user. The lesson: textbook deadlines date quickly; the direction does not.

The alternatives discussed by Zahay et al. are a Universal ID, contextual advertising, the irreversibly *hashed* email and, above all, first-party data.

## Application

- Inventory the client's tactics that depend on third-party cookies: retargeting, lookalikes, frequency capping, attribution. Note the alternative for each.
- Every new strategy answers: what data of our own do we collect, and in exchange for what value?

## Example

Magnolia Bakery (SRC-029, ch. 11) built its own lists through a paid social media campaign that offered new customers a 10 percent discount and entry into a raffle for a birthday giveaway in exchange for their email address. Email subscribers spent 35 percent more time on the website than non-subscribers.

## Questions

- Is behavioral targeting incompatible with respect for the customer, or does it only have to be done transparently?
- What do small brands without large databases lose in the post-cookie world?
