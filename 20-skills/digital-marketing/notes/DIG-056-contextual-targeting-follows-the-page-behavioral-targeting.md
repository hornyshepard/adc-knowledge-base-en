# Contextual targeting follows the page, behavioral targeting follows the person

> **The idea in one sentence:** you can choose who sees the ad by the content of the page the person is on (contextual) or by what the person did before (behavioral), and the second option, more precise, is the one most exposed to privacy problems.

## Explanation

**Contextual targeting** matches the ad to the content of the site: diapers on a parenting website, baking sheets on a page with a cookie recipe. It was popular in the early days of the internet, lost ground as advertisers began to use cookies, and is now increasing in popularity again as new regulations limit third-party data.

**Behavioral targeting** presents content based on users' previous activities and has three basic types:

1. **Identified users**: those who are logged in (Amazon: wish list, recommendations); visible and broadly accepted.
2. **Unidentified users**: tracked through the advertising network's cookies (someone who read about hotels in San Francisco sees hotel ads on a blog about dogs); this is where retargeting and microsegments ("architectural history traveler") come from.
3. **Predictive models**: you start from a profile of high-value customers and look for a "lookalike audience," a standard option on Meta as well.

On mobile, **geo-fencing** is added: a virtual perimeter around a place (the Rogers Centre stadium in Toronto) makes it possible to target everyone who passes through it, and the context (a baseball game vs. Disney on Ice) changes which advertisers fit.

## Application

- Choose the type of targeting by objective: context for relevance in the moment and brand safety, behavior for conversion, lookalike for scalable acquisition.
- Put a "common-sense rule" in the brief too: which data we do not use, even if we have it.
- Think of the creative work in modules, so it can be assembled by segment.

## Example

**Vacation Matchmaker** (Tennessee tourism, Spongecell's dynamic ad technology) edited personalized videos in real time from hundreds of prerecorded clips, based on interests identified from search behavior, website visits and social media: a user interested in history, rock climbing and country music saw the Chickamauga & Chattanooga National Military Park, rock climbing in Sunset Park and live music venues in downtown Chattanooga. In four weeks: a CTR three times the industry average, a 46 percent increase in website traffic, and 93 percent of people surveyed said the ads inspired them to plan a vacation to Tennessee. A private online college that used a lookalike model got a 32 percent higher enrollment rate, at a cost 21 percent lower than the campaign target.
