# Voice search calls for content written as answers to spoken questions: an exaggerated forecast, but a useful practice

> **The idea in one sentence:** people speak differently from how they type: long, natural questions that start with "how", "where", "what is the best". Content that answers such questions directly wins both in voice search and in the answer snippets that search engines display.

## Explanation

The two sources agree on the technique but not on the pace. The **DX Review** (SRC-009, ch. 4) claims that more than 50% of searches will be made by voice by 2024. It recommends conversational long-tail keywords, a mobile site, concise and direct answers, structured data (*schema markup*) and continuous updating. **Ejeke** (SRC-010, trend 13) is more cautious. He notes that the 2019 forecasts about voice were not borne out ("it was not as well-received as we had hoped") and gives more modest figures: 20% of searches by voice, 27% of smartphone searches. He also admits that voice traffic cannot be measured in Google Analytics.

Practices combined from the two sources:
1. Write content as a conversation, with question phrases and trigger words: who, why, when, how, where, should.
2. Answer the anticipated question concisely and directly, at the very start of the section, not after an introductory paragraph.
3. Use structured data, so the engine understands the entities (product, opening hours, location).
4. Write product descriptions that **sound fantastic when read aloud** (Ejeke).
5. Prepare for zero-click search: if the answer appears directly on the results page (position zero), the user no longer visits the site. Choose topics that need longer explanations (SRC-010, trend 50).

Voice commerce works mainly for small, recurring products: food and groceries (21%) and consumer packaged goods (16%), according to Walker Sands. The book's examples are Walmart Voice Order and AmazonFresh on Alexa.

## Application

- For a local client (a restaurant, a clinic), build a page of frequently asked questions phrased exactly as someone would say them in the car.
- Copywriters can test their texts by reading them aloud: if they cannot be spoken, a voice assistant will not pick them.

## Example

Domino's lets people order pizza by voice, and PayPal lets them transfer money through Siri (SRC-010). Both are simple, repetitive, low-risk tasks. That is where voice is more convenient than a screen.
