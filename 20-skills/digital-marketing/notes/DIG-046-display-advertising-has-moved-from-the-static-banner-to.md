# Display advertising has moved from the static banner to rich media and video, but the creative idea stays human

> **The idea in one sentence:** display formats are standardized by the IAB and increasingly interactive, but technology only multiplies the ways of executing an idea: "Creativity is still the product of human minds, not technology," the authors write.

## Explanation

A display ad (banner ad) is primarily visual and includes a URL that links to a website where people can learn more or make a purchase. It usually has a headline, short body text and a dominant visual; the brand name and logo should be prominently displayed. The first online ad (AT&T on Wired Magazine's Hotwired website, 1994) said "Have you ever clicked your mouse right here? You will," and AT&T reportedly paid $30,000 for three months at the top of the site.

Three families of formats:

| Family | Examples |
|---|---|
| Static | the IAB fixed sizes: leaderboard 728×90, medium rectangle 300×250, skyscraper 160×600, billboard 970×250, smartphone banner 320×50 |
| Rich media | *expanding* (grows larger when the user interacts), *push-down* (pushes the page content down), *interstitial* (floats on top of the page, fills the screen on mobile; flexible, but seen as invasive) |
| Video | in-stream, in-feed, 6-second bumper, masthead (YouTube) |

The payment models: **CPM** (cost per thousand views), **CPC** (cost per click), **CPA / CPE** (cost per action or engagement: a form, a like, moving the cursor over the ad) and a flat fee for a *roadblock*, when one marketer takes over the entire page.

Constant Contact's tips for display: eye-catching images to break through the clutter, animation to overcome banner-blindness, an offer (discount, free download, free trial), a spotlight on the product, urgency, a statistic instead of "the best," a solution to a want or need.

## Application

- Design the system of formats from the start: the idea has to work at 320×50, not only on a billboard.
- Put the brand in the first frame of an animated banner; many people never see the last one.
- On mobile, avoid placements that cause accidental clicks: 85% of respondents have accidentally clicked on a mobile banner.

## Example

The **Heineken Legends** campaign: a square banner with no obvious call-to-action led to an expandable leaderboard with a QR code that started the game on the phone (after age verification). The campaign won both praise for its creativity and criticism for its "legends" being paid actors.

## Questions

- Is the interstitial ad a violation of the experience or a legitimate form of impact?
- Why does IAB standardization help creatives instead of limiting them?
