# Retargeting works only when segmented by journey stage, capped and consented: the most effective tactic of the cookie era is also the most exposed after it

> **The idea in one sentence:** retargeting (remarketing) shows the ad again to people who have already interacted with the brand; it works when the message fits how close to a decision the person was, when frequency is capped and when the tracking is consented.

## Explanation

**The mechanism**, described by all the sources: a pixel or a tracking code (for example the Meta pixel) recognizes the visitor, who then gets ads on other websites, on YouTube, in Gmail or in the feed. Dynamic retargeting shows the very product the person viewed, with image and price. Zahay et al. place it in the journey with its loops: sometimes customers only need reminding about their intended purchase decision.

**Connors's method** (SRC-030): you segment by behavior (someone who visited only the homepage is in the early research phase, someone who left items in the cart is much closer to a purchase). You match the message to the stage: early on, content that builds trust, without being too aggressive; near the end, answers to objections and incentives. You cap frequency, refresh the ad creative and allow for the fact that the same person uses several devices.

**Why it is exposed.** Without third-party cookies, retargeting across networks is no longer possible (SRC-032). Google has renamed retargeting in its reports ("your data"), is phasing out "similar audiences" and prohibits "sensitive" information in remarketing lists. Sachdev asks for consent, only the necessary pixels and the right to opt out of tracking. The contrast is instructive: Nishad's guide (SRC-033) shows the steps on Facebook (the pixel in the header, the ViewContent event, a conversions campaign), but says nothing about consent.

## Application

- Build separate audiences (visitors, product viewers, cart abandoners, customers) and write a different message for each.
- Set the frequency cap and the length of the window from the start; exclude recent buyers.
- Build the lists on consented first-party data (subscribers, logged-in customers), not only on pixels.
- The second ad does not repeat the first; it answers the likely reason the person left.

## Example

In the analysis of the electronics supplier Zedesco (SRC-029), the authors ask what to do if visitors added something to the cart but left after seeing the shipping charges. A retargeting message with "free shipping" answers that concrete objection. The other side appears in SRC-032: a poorly set up system shows you the airline's ad weeks after you booked your flight.

## Questions

- Why does retargeting almost always win under last-click attribution? Would people have bought anyway?
- When does retargeting become harassment? What frequency would you accept?
