# Personalization works only with relevance, respect and control; when it comes too early, it produces reactance

> **The idea in one sentence:** personalization raises response, but when it shows that the brand knows too much too early, people reject the message; the rule of the three Rs (relevance, respect, recipient control) is the protection.

## Explanation

The textbook separates three terms that are often lumped together:

- **Targeting**: you direct communication to people identified as valid prospects (visible or invisible to them);
- **Personalization**: you create specialized content for a prospect with a known profile by choosing from an array of existing content modules;
- **Customization**: you create new content, services or products for an individual (Nike by You); rare in practice.

Personalization does appear to work: Campaign Monitor states that using the recipient's name in the subject line can increase response by 26 percent (a vendor's figure, the authors imply, since it depends on the industry). But research by White, Zahay, Thorbjørnsen and Shavitt shows that highly personalized messages received **too soon in the relationship** produce **reactance**: people feel that the fit between the offer and their personal characteristics is not justified. In short, "Too much personalization is usually considered creepy." Connors (SRC-030) calls the risk **over-personalization**: aggressive retargeting or too many "personal" emails drive customers away; the answer is transparency about which data is used and a way to opt out.

The three "Golden Rs" of email marketing:

1. **Relevance**: content applicable to the recipient's needs and lifestyle;
2. **Respect**: data is guarded against unwarranted or frivolous use;
3. **Recipient control**: beyond permission, people decide what content they receive and how often.

## Application

- Grade personalization by stage: first a generous, non-invasive offer, only later recommendations based on history.
- Say explicitly how you know something ("because you bought X..."); the justification reduces reactance.
- Offer a preference center, not only an unsubscribe button.

## Example

In 2019, Mother's Lounge bought a "bad" direct mail list and sent cards with coupons congratulating women on their pregnancies; most of them were not pregnant. They felt targeted on something highly personal. Magnolia Bakery, by contrast, started the relationship with a 10 percent discount: nothing threatening.

## Questions

- Where is the line between "useful" and "creepy" for different generations?
- Does reactance contradict the AI promise of one-to-one personalization, which Connors also promotes?
