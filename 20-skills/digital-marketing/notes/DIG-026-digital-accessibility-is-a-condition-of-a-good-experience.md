# Digital accessibility is a condition of a good experience and a legal risk: alt text, captions, information not carried by color alone, compatibility with screen readers

> **The idea in one sentence:** a website, an email or a post that a person with a visual, hearing or mobility impairment cannot use loses customers and can bring lawsuits, so accessibility is designed in from the start, not added at the end.

## Explanation

Sachdev defines website accessibility as the characteristics that let people of all abilities use a site, "whatever their hardware, software, language, location, or ability." The standards come from the W3C and the ISO. Zahay et al. put accessibility on their list of web design rules and cite the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). Their case: the National Federation of the Blind sued Target, and in 2008 the company agreed to pay $6 million in damages.

Sachdev extends the rule to every channel and asks for all content to be checked before launch:

| Channel | What accessibility requires |
|---|---|
| Website | alt text on images, captions on video, compatibility with screen readers; color labeled, not just shown (for people with color blindness) |
| Email | not only images; a text version; information not conveyed by color alone |
| Social media | common language, without jargon and acronyms; captions and alt text on images; closed captioning on video |
| Apps | compatibility with screen readers, larger buttons and tap zones, larger fonts |

Both books treat it as a legal obligation and as part of a good experience. Sachdev points out that not every measure is required in every situation, but the marketer has to know how the law applies to the campaign. One item in his social media list, about avoiding contrasting colors, is ambiguous. The W3C standards he himself cites require enough contrast between text and background.

## Application

- Write descriptive alt text for every key image, not a string of keywords.
- Add captions to every campaign video.
- Check the email with images turned off: the offer and the CTA must stay clear.
- Put accessibility in the brief and in the handover checklist, with a named owner.

## Example

Zahay et al. tie the rule to the convention of the clickable logo at the top left: the image link needs an alt tag. It helps the person using a screen reader, and also the one with a slow connection.

## Questions

- Does accessibility limit creative freedom, or does it widen the audience for the idea?
- Who on the agency team is responsible for the "accessible" criterion: the designer, the copywriter or the developer?
