# Pull marketing: Attract, Assist, Affiliate, measured by "return on attention"

> **The idea in one sentence:** in John Hagel's "pull" model, the brand gets people to seek it out (Attract), helps them before and after the purchase (Assist) and brings in other participants who are useful to them (Affiliate); success is measured in attention and information, not only in assets and investment.

## Explanation

Wuebben takes from *The Power of Pull* (Hagel, Seely Brown, Davison) a model he considers in the same vein as content marketing and inbound (ch. 10):

| Step | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Attract** | you motivate people to seek you out and find you |
| **Assist** | you help them get more value before and after the purchase. You do not wait for the question: you become a trusted advisor and make recommendations based on what you know about them. |
| **Affiliate** | instead of one-to-one marketing, you bring into the ecosystem anyone who can help customers at the relevant point in time, even if it is not you |

Hagel redefines two classic metrics. **ROA** becomes *return on attention*, and **ROI** becomes *return on information*: how much it costs to accumulate information about a customer, divided by what you can earn by using it more effectively. Wuebben takes the idea further: if attention is so valuable, brands may end up paying people directly to look at their content. He cites Kevin Kelly, who calculated that an hour of media has consistently cost us around $3 (1995, 2010, 2015).

For simplicity, Wuebben recommends that any strategy evaluate just three metrics: awareness, sales and advocacy. One of them has to be chosen as the primary objective, or the strategy falls apart (ch. 10).

## Application

- Audit a content plan against the three steps. Usually there is "Attract" (posts), "Assist" is missing (post-purchase content, usage guides) and "Affiliate" does not exist at all (partners who help the customer).
- Choose a single primary objective from the three metrics before you ask for the budget.

## Example

Wuebben gives the Salesforce and Android ecosystems as examples of successful "Affiliate": partners' apps make the platform more useful for the customer, and the customer does not leave (ch. 10).

## Questions

- How would you measure "return on attention" concretely for a video campaign?
- Where does data-driven "Assist" become an intrusion into private life?
