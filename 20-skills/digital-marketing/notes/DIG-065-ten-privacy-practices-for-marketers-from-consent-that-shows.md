# Ten privacy practices for marketers, from consent that shows the value provided to data minimization

> **The idea in one sentence:** Sachdev, a lawyer by training, turns the legal framework into a list of dos and don'ts that any marketing team can apply before a campaign.

## Explanation

The starting definitions: **digital marketing privacy** is the right to know how information and data are collected and used, not a promise that they stay 100% private; **security** protects systems and data from unauthorized access, that is, it preserves their confidentiality, integrity and availability.

The ten practices:

1. **Obtain consent and show the value provided** in exchange, without exaggerating the benefits.
2. **Track only as needed**: tracking pixels only with consent, only the necessary ones, with an option to refuse.
3. **Provide consumer choice and control** over the collection and use of data.
4. **Employ at least reasonable security and privacy measures**: strong passwords, updates, minimal access, multifactor authentication, privacy and security built in from the design stage (*privacy/security by design*), access logs that are reviewed.
5. **Follow all applicable laws and rules**, including sector-specific ones (children, health, finance, education) and the GDPR when you target people in the EU.
6. **Avoid false or exaggerated claims and promises**, above all "100% secure."
7. **Have clear privacy policies and be transparent**.
8. **Minimize the data you collect** and delete the data you no longer need.
9. **Employ due diligence when using third-party services**.
10. **Keep up with evolving privacy and security environments**: differential privacy (Apple, Google), AI that processes data locally, on the device.

## Application

- Go through the list before launch, together with IT and the lawyer; in the strategic plan, it fills section 6.
- For the GDPR, the book recommends: a documented legal basis, "appropriate" technical and organizational measures (Article 32: encryption, pseudonymization), the principles of Article 5 (lawful, fair, transparent; limited purpose; limited storage), training the team.

## Example

In 2021, Amazon was fined approximately €746 million in Luxembourg under the GDPR for advertising targeting conducted without appropriate consent, the largest fine ever issued under the GDPR, according to the book.

## Questions

- Which practice on the list most often conflicts with the client's request for "as much data as possible"?
- Who in the agency signs off that a campaign is compliant?
