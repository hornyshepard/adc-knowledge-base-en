# The first-party data strategy starts from the question "What data can I use to drive sales?" and gets the data through invitation and value exchange, not hidden collection

> **The idea in one sentence:** your own data is valuable because it is shared voluntarily and signals interest, but collecting names and dates of birth does not by itself make advertising more effective; what counts is the decision you use the data for and what people get in return.

## Explanation

**The types of data** are described the same way by Schwarz-Musch et al. and by Ejeke. *First-party* data is collected directly (CRM, loyalty, behavior on the website, purchases). *Second-party* data comes from a partner, also with consent. *Third-party* data is aggregated by brokers, of doubtful quality and legality. A DMP stores and processes audience data. A CDP (Consumer Data Platform) is the "single source of truth" for your own data, fed from every touchpoint.

**The cycle of three questions** (SRC-032):

1. *Which data do I use to raise sales, and how does that compare with other advertising options?* Sometimes contextual targeting is simply cheaper. The data serves analytics, advertising (personalization, lookalikes through data clean rooms) and marketing automation.
2. *What data do I need to execute my marketing plan?* The question is asked again after every campaign, to fill the "white spots" in the customer profile.
3. *How do I get people to give me their data voluntarily and correctly?*

For the third question, Ejeke (SRC-010) gives five practices. You collect data at each touchpoint, with the user's permission. You make a very visible, explicit and individualized invitation, not a hidden checkbox. You present the form as if you were delivering a loyalty program invitation. You explain every data attribute you gather. You offer a preference center. On top of that: you do not ask for more than you need, and opting out is simple.

All three sources call for a value exchange. Schwarz-Musch et al. add a practical warning: contests often produce false data, while birthday vouchers, customer clubs and product tester communities work better.

## Application

- Write the three questions on one page before buying a CDP or launching a form.
- For each field you ask for, note the marketing decision you use it for; if you do not know, do not ask for it.
- The wording of the request is copywriting: write it in the brand's tone and say what people get.

## Example

Bloom & Wild put the option of not receiving Mother's Day messages in its preference center, turning an obligation into a brand gesture.
