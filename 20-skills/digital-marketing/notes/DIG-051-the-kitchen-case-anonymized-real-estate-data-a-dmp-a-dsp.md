# The kitchen case: anonymized real estate data, a DMP, a DSP and dynamic banners turn a flyer for everyone into an offer for each household

> **The idea in one sentence:** when you know what stage of life people are at and what they can afford, the same campaign can show each of them a different kitchen, at a different price, on the same page that adapts.

## Explanation

**The problem.** A furniture store in Austria sells kitchens at every price point. It sent monthly flyers with special offers to all households with people aged 18 to 60. The waste was high: the segments (students, young families, singles, DINKs) had different needs, and the store did not know where each one was in the customer journey. Also, an affordable kitchen lasts around 10 years and a high-end one 20 to 30, so a household that has just paid around €30,000 for a kitchen will not need another one soon.

**The solution, step by step:**

1. The store acquires anonymized data from a large real estate website and integrates it into its DMP. The logic, backed by market research: people who search for houses or apartments are about to move and will need a new kitchen.
2. The property search criteria (price range, square footage, location) make it possible to estimate household income.
3. A demand-side platform (DSP) launches a high-reach campaign to these user groups, in advertising networks.
4. A Dynamic Banner Tool builds the right banner automatically, in real time: users who searched for a 45 sqm apartment at 500 euros a month see an affordable kitchen for 1,490 euros; users looking for a 200 m² house in Vienna Döbling see a "Designer Edition" kitchen.
5. After the click, the website adapts to the user profile; on a return visit, retargeting shows the same matching page; the user's interactions improve the assessment of their customer journey stage.
6. Next comes *lookalike* modeling: reducing the match rate of the profiles from 100% to 90% increases reach, but it has to be checked in real time.

## Application

- For creatives: the creative work becomes a system of modules (images, prices, headlines) assembled by rules. Art direction designs templates and rules, not a single banner.
- For strategists: look for the "life-stage signal" in the client's category (moving house, a wedding, a child) and for the legal data source that reveals it.

## Example

The case itself is the example; the book gives no numerical results, which makes it a demonstration of a mechanism more than proof of effectiveness.

## Questions

- Is it legal and ethical to buy real estate search data and infer a household's income from it? What does "anonymized" mean if the banner is personalized?
