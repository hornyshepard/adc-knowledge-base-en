# Omnichannel means channels that reinforce each other: it is built on the critical purchase scenarios, with the silos broken, and measured by customer value, not by clicks per channel

> **The idea in one sentence:** multichannel means being present in many places; omnichannel means the places are connected, so customers continue the same relationship wherever they come in.

## Explanation

**The definition.** The DX Review (SRC-009) draws the distinction: in omnichannel, the website, the app, social media, the store and the call center are interconnected and mutually reinforcing. Kotler et al. (SRC-004) say the same thing from the customer's side. Customers are *channel-agnostic*: they care about the continuity of the experience, not about the channel. The book describes two typical scenarios. In *showrooming* you see the product on TV, try it in the store and buy it online. In *webrooming* you see a banner ad, search online and buy in the store. Omnichannel buyers have a 30% higher lifetime value (IDC). Wuebben (SRC-008) cites Salesforce: 64% of top-performing teams have a "single view of the customer", against 4% of underperformers.

**Kotler's method, in three steps:**

1. **Map all possible touchpoints and channels** (communication and sales channels) across the five A's.
2. **Identify the critical scenarios**: by the Pareto principle, about 20% of the scenarios are followed by 80% of the customers.
3. **Improve and integrate**: merged budgets and goals, or cross-incentives. The contact center agent is rewarded even when the sale closes online.

**Measurement** (SRC-009): clicks per channel give a limited view. Compare customer lifetime value (CLV) between customers exposed to the campaign and those who were not. Use attribution models, engagement metrics and predictive modeling.

The sources agree: the problem is more one of organization (silos, data) than of communication. Ejeke's figures (SRC-010), such as 90% higher retention, circulate without a method and have to be checked before a pitch.

## Application

- In a proposal for a retailer, draw 3–5 real purchase scenarios and mark the breaks between channels.
- Give each channel a role (discovery, evaluation, purchase, support), not only a budget.
- Check the handover between channels: does the cart in the app also show up on the website?

## Example

Macy's discovered that a dollar spent on search engine optimization drove six dollars of in-store purchases. The company combined the two budgets. Customers now check stock at a nearby store on their phone and choose where to buy.

## Questions

- When is a single excellent channel preferable, for example for a small D2C brand?
- In-store location data raises privacy issues. How do you explain it to the customer?
