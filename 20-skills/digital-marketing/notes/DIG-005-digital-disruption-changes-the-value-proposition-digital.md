# Digital disruption changes the value proposition; digital transformation is a strategic response, not a technological one

> **The idea in one sentence:** disruption is what happens to you (new technologies and models make your offer outdated), transformation is what you do, and it succeeds only if it starts from strategy, culture and the customer.

## Explanation

The authors separate two concepts that are often confused:

- **Digital disruption**: the change that occurs when new digital technologies and business models affect the value proposition of existing goods and services. Netflix made the fixed schedule of broadcast TV (the "linear model") look like a burden: choice, convenience and personalization.
- **Digital transformation**: the profound transformation of a firm's business activities, processes, competencies and models to leverage technology, in a strategic and prioritized way.

Companies are quick to sense the disruption, but slow to find a good way to deal with it: CBS launched All Access (rebranded as Paramount+) and reached 56 million subscribers, far from Netflix's 221 million. Research by Bonnet and Westerman among over 1,300 executives groups transformation into five business fundamentals: customer experience, operations, business models, employee experience and digital platforms. Brian Solis (Altimeter) describes six stages of maturity, from "business as usual" to "innovative and adaptive". The authors' conclusion: it is a journey, not a destination, and strategy leads it.

## Application

- When a client wants "an app" or "something with AI", ask what the business value of the change is. The first step of the 7-step model is "Align on the why", then "Prepare for culture change"; technology comes only at step 4.
- For the brand, the transformation can be a story: what new promise becomes credible through technology?

## Example

**Domino's.** The company started with the perception of the brand and the quality of the product: it improved its pizza and digitally transformed the supply chain for its ingredients. Only after that came the "Pizza Tracker" (following the delivery) and "Anywhere" (ordering through Amazon Echo, Facebook, Twitter). On that basis it pivoted quickly to contactless delivery during the pandemic. $1,000 invested in 2011 was worth approximately 23 times that at the time of writing.

## Questions

- Is Domino's "a pizza company" or "a technology company that sells pizza"?
- What role does brand communication play in a digital transformation that starts from the product? The book does not discuss Domino's campaigns: what should a strategist add?
