# Predictive targeting moves the question from "who the person was" to "what they will probably do" and can use context triggers such as weather, place and time

> **The idea in one sentence:** algorithms find patterns in the data that indicate the probability of an action, and the ad is triggered when the predicted conditions are met.

## Explanation

Predictive targeting uses machine learning on collected data (product pages visited, purchases, ad clicks, location, device type) to calculate probabilities for future user actions. It does not rely on demographic characteristics or on past behavior as such, but on the patterns that link it to the future. Large ad networks use it to make up for the loss of cookies: they analyze historical data, enrich it with additional first- and third-party data and estimate age or buying behavior regardless of the context of the website. The better the quality of the data and of the predictive algorithm, the more accurate the predictions.

An accessible variant is targeting by **context triggers**: weather, place, time. The authors warn, however, that combining too many targeting options shrinks the reach until the campaign becomes ineffective.

## Application

- Ask: "when is the need greatest?" The answer (cold, rain, mealtime, payday) becomes a serving rule and a creative idea.
- For clients with their own data, look for simple repurchase patterns and turn them into offers at the right moment.

## Example

The book gives four examples: (1) e-bike buyers who visit the store's blog at least three times a week are very likely to be interested in touring backpacks too; (2) someone who often buys large quantities of laundry detergent online is probably a large family that will order again every three weeks or so, and so gets a "Buy 2 packs and get 1 free" offer at the right moment; (3) a beer company that bought small regional breweries knows that more beer is consumed when it is at least 20 degrees outside, so the ad server shows the regional brand exactly where it is currently warmer than 20 degrees; (4) a food delivery service: forecast rain + geo-targeting + 4 p.m. trigger an ad on social media (a rainy evening, a romantic movie, a delivery order), then a digital poster at the subway shows Asian food to the person who has already seen the first ad, and at the first commercial break the ad appears again on the smart TV.

## Questions

- The poster that "recognizes" the passer-by looks effective, but on what legal basis and with what consent does it work?
- Where does useful prediction turn into a feeling of surveillance?
