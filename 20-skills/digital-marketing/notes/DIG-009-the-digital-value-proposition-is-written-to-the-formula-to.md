# The digital value proposition is written to the formula "To [target market], [organization] is [what] which/that [benefit and difference]"

> **The idea in one sentence:** it is not enough to create value; the digital value proposition puts it into one sentence that every digital touchpoint has to confirm.

## Explanation

Sachdev defines the digital value proposition as a unique, convincing proposal about why a consumer within the target market should purchase the offering. The format (adapted from Kotler and Armstrong):

> To [target market], [organization name] is [the product or service] which/that [what it does for the consumer, what need it fulfills, what it does better than its competitors].

The proposition sits in section 4 of the plan, after the digital STP. Digital segmentation uses the same four families of variables, in their digital version: demographic (the age of the social media user), geographic (where the consumer is clicking from), psychographic (the lifestyle of the smart-product user), behavioral (search and purchase history).

The main risk: an overpromising or unclear proposition. If someone clicks on a paid search ad and the page does not convince them, the cost of the click is lost, the purchase stays incomplete and the brand image suffers.

## Application

- Write the proposition before any ad and put it next to the brief; it is the digital equivalent of the single-minded proposition.
- Check whether the landing page says the same thing on its first screen.
- Write one proposition for each important target, not one generic proposition for everyone.

## Example

Peloton, in the book's version: to consumers who need or want to exercise at home, Peloton is an at-home, "immersive, high energy" exercise experience that lets you reclaim your time and save money. Amazon shows that the same product family can have different targets: Echo for adults aged 18–44, Echo Dot Kids for children and parents, with the promise that Alexa helps with homework.

## Questions

- How does the value proposition differ from positioning and from the single-minded proposition in a creative brief?
- What do you lose when the formula becomes a template and every proposition sounds the same?
