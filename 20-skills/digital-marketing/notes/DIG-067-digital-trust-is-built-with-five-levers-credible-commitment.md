# Digital trust is built with five levers: credible commitment, consistency, transparency, third-party validation and symbols of trust

> **The idea in one sentence:** trust reduces the "friction" in an online transaction, and Sachdev treats it as a concrete set of levers, not as a vague brand effect.

## Explanation

Sachdev defines digital trust as a firm belief or confidence in an organization when a consumer is engaging in a digital transaction. It removes friction: lack of conviction about the brand, low perceived value, fear about the website's security or about privacy. More trust means more conversion, retention and loyalty. Interbrand sums it up as "Trust Always Wins."

The five levers in chapter 10:

1. **Commitment and credibility**: the cause has to be integrated into the business model. H&M has a climate circularity strategy that encompasses its products, packaging and supply chain. A warning: simply stating support for a popular movement, without action, may look inauthentic.
2. **Consistency** across channels and within the same channel.
3. **Design transparency**: pertinent information (commitment periods for a subscription, conditions) is not buried in the terms and conditions.
4. **Social and third-party validation**: links from reputable sources, mentions, reviews, testimonials.
5. **Symbols of trust**: a money-back guarantee (eBay), certifications, Verified by Visa, the HTTPS padlock, safety checks, ISO 9001, awards, full contact details; the free trial period, with the risk that people forget to cancel and feel tricked.

## Application

- Audit a website or a brand profile against the five levers and mark what is missing.
- In copy, prefer verifiable proof (guarantees, numbers, certifications) to adjectives.
- Put the inconvenient information in plain view: it costs a conversion today, but it saves trust.

## Example

Wells Fargo, after its "fake-account scandal," faces a double problem: in a world where fewer consumers physically visit a bank branch, digital trust becomes a key factor when choosing a bank, and it is extremely hard to regain.

## Questions

- When does a symbol of trust (a badge, a certificate) become mere decoration that no longer convinces anyone?
- Can a small brand make up for a lack of reviews through transparency?
