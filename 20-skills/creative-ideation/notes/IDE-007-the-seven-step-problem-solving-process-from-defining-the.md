# The seven-step problem-solving process: from defining the gap to reviewing the results

> **The idea in one sentence:** a problem is a gap between the current state and the desired state; to close it, you define it, gather information, generate many solutions, evaluate them, choose, implement and review.

## Explanation

Naveed Ahmed defines a problem as a **gap** between our current state and our desired state, caused by insufficient information, conflicting goals or unexpected obstacles. He distinguishes four types: **simple** (a clear solution, little effort), **complex** (many factors, no single clear solution), **emergent** (urgent ones that demand quick decision-making) and **chronic** (they persist and need long-term strategies).

The process has seven steps:

| # | Step | Tools proposed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify the problem | the 5 Whys technique (you ask "why" five times), a written problem statement, clarifying objectives |
| 2 | Gather information | research, experts, surveys, interviews, observation |
| 3 | Generate solutions | quantity before quality: free writing, group brainstorming, mind mapping |
| 4 | Evaluate solutions | pros and cons list, SWOT, decision matrix |
| 5 | Choose a solution | the decision matrix score, consulting those affected, gut feeling |
| 6 | Implement the solution | action plan, responsibilities, timeline |
| 7 | Review the results | feedback, comparison with the objectives, adjustments |

For recurring problems, the author recommends root cause analysis: you define the problem, look for the causes (5 Whys, fishbone diagram), determine the root cause and only then develop the solution.

## Application

For an agency project, the steps map onto the familiar workflow:

- steps 1–2 = analyzing and debriefing the brief (the naive questions, the insight);
- step 3 = ideation, kept strictly separate from step 4 (no judgment while generating);
- steps 4–5 = selecting concepts against the criteria in the brief;
- step 7 = the measurement set in advance ([[BRF-014-measurement-is-written-into-the-brief-before-the-campaign|BRF-014]]).

The 5 Whys work well on the barrier behind a sales objective (see [[BRF-007-the-objective-in-the-brief-is-a-communication-objective|BRF-007]]).

## Example

An exercise on the mayonnaise case in [[BRF-007-the-objective-in-the-brief-is-a-communication-objective|BRF-007]]: step 1 (the 5 Whys) goes down from "stagnant sales" to "people know only two sandwich recipes with mayonnaise"; step 3 generates new recipes and occasions; step 4 weighs them; step 7 checks whether the target now knows four recipes.
