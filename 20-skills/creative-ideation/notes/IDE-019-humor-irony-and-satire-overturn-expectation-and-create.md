# Humor, irony and satire overturn expectation and create complicity with the viewer

> **The idea in one sentence:** the joke sets off in one direction, then jerks the viewer into another; the moment they get the joke, a complicity forms between them and the creator that no argument can produce.

## Explanation

Ingledew quotes two authors. Victor Borge: humor is the shortest distance between two people. Edward de Bono: humor is the exact model of creativity, because in both you reach something from the far end of the pattern, not from the near end. The comic mechanism is constant: a setup takes you in one direction, the mind runs ahead, and an unexpected twist pulls you back. Laughter is an involuntary reflex, so, Ingledew says, it can override any defense; in exchange for the entertainment, the audience gives the message its attention.

Variants from the book:

| Form | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Visual humor | shared knowledge plus a twist | "No Wind" (Ogilvy & Mather): Marilyn with a motionless skirt, for an anti-flatulence product |
| Irony | you say one thing and imply the opposite | the Diesel brochure "How To Do Fashion Photography", with deliberately appalling tips; the student invitation "Same Shit Different Year" |
| Satire and caricature | you exaggerate the significant features and diminish the rest; the weak laugh at the powerful | Gillray, Spitting Image; Orwell: "every joke is a tiny revolution" |
| Kitsch ("so bad it's good") | bad taste embraced; the viewer wonders whether the author is joking | Jeff Koons, Tretchikoff |

Braun and Landa add two nuances. Eoin McLaughlin (Channel 4): in purpose-driven communication, humor humanizes and spares you the pity-me piano music; a serious subject does not mean you have to take yourself seriously. Valerie Cheng-Madon warns: do not try too hard to be funny if you are not a genuinely funny person; humor is best scripted by comedians and often does not cross the border of its culture.

The research cited by Fennis and Stroebe tempers the enthusiasm: humor attracts attention and liking, but it can steal the memory of the brand and works mainly for cheap products bought without much thought ([[CPY-031-humor-attracts-attention-and-liking-but-steals-brand-memory|CPY-031]]).

## Application

- Build the joke from lived truths (Ingledew: a joke based on a real experience rings true).
- Check what remains after the laugh: is the benefit linked to the brand, or only the joke? (see *linkage* in [[BRF-026-adplan-six-questions-for-an-execution-that-is-on-brief-but|BRF-026]])
- For satire, choose the target carefully: satire punches up, at the powerful, not down.

## Example

"No Wind" (Ogilvy & Mather), for an anti-flatulence product: Marilyn Monroe over the subway grating, with her skirt motionless. The joke needs knowledge of the original image plus a twist; the viewer solves it alone and immediately links the twist to the benefit.
