# Original visual inspiration is found outside the Pinterest and Dribbble bubble

> **The idea in one sentence:** when designers find inspiration only in each other, on the same platforms, all the work starts to look the same; old, regional and forgotten references offer fresh solutions to old problems.

## Explanation

Allan Peters notes that most modern-day designers never leave their desks: they find their inspiration in the work of other contemporary designers on Pinterest, Dribbble, Behance and Instagram. A small pool of people who find inspiration only in each other produces the same styles over and over. By widening the pool of references, everyone's work becomes more beautiful and more diverse.

He calls his practice *#badgehunting*: antique stores, estate sales, small-town museums. A few rules:

- **Location is key.** Before Amazon, what you bought in Minnesota was probably made in Minnesota; each region has different local brands. Small towns have shops that are less picked through.
- **What he looks for:** badges with interesting shapes or clever ways of dividing up the type; bold marks that are not trend driven (they feel designed today, though they are 50 years old); subtle custom typography, an alternative to the current trend for minimal and lifeless logos; plus animals, mascots, numbers, anything that catches the eye.
- **Why:** much of this work was made before Paul Rand and Saul Bass created standards for what a logo should be; it was the wild west of design, with unusual solutions.
- **Do not copy, do not hoard.** A good photograph is enough; the aim is to learn from the designers of the past, not to reproduce their work.

The same idea appears independently in Sarah Moffat (Turner Duckworth), in *Goddess Guide to Branding* (SRC-016): she looks for inspiration in exhibitions, but also in a grocery store in a rural town, because images found online have been seen by many and you do not know who created them: "Downloading is not designing." Creativity, she says, means putting together two things with no apparent connection (a medieval castle led her to the idea of a knife sharp enough to slay a dragon).

Peters's Americana collection drew on the patriotic packaging from the World War II era found in antique stores.

## Application

- Build an offline reference folder: photograph old packaging, signs, labels and emblems from markets, museums and attics.
- For a Romanian project, search local visual archives (communist-era packaging, old shop signs, embroidery, neighborhood typography) instead of international moodboards.
- For every moodboard, check the proportion: how many references come from designer platforms and how many from outside them?

## Example

Peters turned his hunt for old emblems into a passion project (#badgehunting club crests for his designer friends' cities) that later brought him clients.
