# Brainjack: a good idea captures the viewer's mind, not just the eye

> **The idea in one sentence:** no one queues up to look at your work; to communicate, you have to switch the viewer's mindset from off to on and make them complete the idea themselves.

## Explanation

Ingledew defines it this way: "An idea is a sudden mental picturing of possibility", the realization that there is a possible way of doing something. In commercial creativity, the idea communicates on behalf of someone else (the client) and has to answer a problem; art, by contrast, asks questions. In both cases the viewer is not a volunteer: they are a passer-by in a hurry.

To reach the passer-by, Ingledew describes three steps:

| Step | What it means |
|---|---|
| Switch the viewer's mindset | the viewer moves from passive to active, so they become receptive |
| Ambush | a visually striking image that captures the eye is not enough; you have to capture the brain |
| Brainjack | the idea seizes the viewer's imagination and triggers a mental reaction (an inner smile) or a physical one (a laugh, an exhalation) |

The underlying mechanism is a **meeting of minds**: the viewer remakes in their head the connection the creator made. At the moment they "get it", they feel they have done something clever, they smile and feel included (Ross Cooper). Ingledew compares it to children's transformation drawing game: one draws a random shape, the other transforms it into something recognizable. Inspiration is transformed by an idea into something new.

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott describe the same mechanism in the language of psychology: an incongruous element stops attention, and resolving the incongruity brings a small mental reward (Kroeber-Riel's "remote conveyor" model, [[IDE-004-the-creative-idea-must-first-dramatize-the-key-benefit|IDE-004]]).

The whole book is built on this concept: each entry ends with a "Brainjack" section that explains *why* the technique captures the mind.

## Application

- When you judge an idea, ask: what does the viewer do in their mind? If they do nothing (they do not complete, decode or compare), it is not a brainjack, only decoration.
- Leave a small gap for the viewer to fill; too much explanation means no "aha".
- Check that the connection the viewer makes is the one you intended (see amplification in [[BRF-026-adplan-six-questions-for-an-execution-that-is-on-brief-but|BRF-026]]).

## Example

Ingledew opens the book with the line that ideas are the content and execution is the style, and with the image of the tenpin bowling "strike" that a brilliant idea makes when it connects. The entries on juxtaposition ([[IDE-015-juxtaposition-two-images-placed-side-by-side-make-the|IDE-015]]) or silhouettes show the same mechanism: the viewer takes the last step.

## Questions

- How much "work" can a viewer do in a social media feed where they stay for two seconds?
- What happens when the connection is too easy? And when it is too hard (see the rebus in [[CPY-010-visual-wordplay-the-pun-the-visualized-expression-the-rebus|CPY-010]])?
