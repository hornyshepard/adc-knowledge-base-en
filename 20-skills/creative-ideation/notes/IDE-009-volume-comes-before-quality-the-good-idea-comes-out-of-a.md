# Volume comes before quality: the good idea comes out of a large number of attempts, most of them bad

> **The idea in one sentence:** almost no one hits the good idea on the first try; whoever produces many options and then sifts them has better odds, so volume is a method, not a waste.

## Explanation

The principle appears independently in authors who otherwise have little in common:

| Source | How it is put |
|---|---|
| Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott | the theory of "random creativity": the more ideas there are, the more likely you are to come up with a winner; since only about 1 in 6 campaigns is successful, they recommend at least six ideas to be tested |
| Osborn, via Braun and Landa | the first rule of brainstorming: as many ideas as possible |
| Ahmed | when generating solutions, quantity is more important than quality, with no judgment on the spot |
| Allan Peters | in the sketch phase of a logo, "Quantity is more important than quality": a minimum of 50 sketches, focused on the idea, not the details |
| Watkins | Carl Spier (Hamilton) tries 40 or 50 approaches over several days, then writes the ad in seven minutes; Nelson Metcalf reworks "The Kid in Upper 4" 25 or 30 times |
| McTigue and Rucker | great creative teams generate many ideas, most of them bad; what reaches the client has already been through the agency's internal filters |

The mechanism has two sides. Statistically, each idea has a small chance of success, so more attempts raise the probability (Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott). In Ingledew's practice, the reflex ideas are collected at the start, and at the reflection stage the obvious ones are the first to go ([[IDE-006-ingledew-s-16-stages-for-solving-a-creative-brief-simplify|IDE-006]]): volume takes you past what would occur to anyone.

Volume only makes sense if selection follows it. Without clear criteria, a hundred ideas are only a hundred options ([[IDE-023-idea-evaluation-grids-converge-on-three-questions-does-it|IDE-023]]).

## Application

- Set yourself a quota before judging, for example 30 headlines or 50 sketches for the same brief.
- Write everything down, weak ideas included; two weak ideas can combine into a good one ([[IDE-002-new-ideas-are-combinations-koestler-s-bisociation-and|IDE-002]]).
- Show the client few, selected ideas: volume belongs to the kitchen, not to the presentation.

## Example

Peters's logo process: at least 50 rough sketches, of which the best 15 are vectorized and three become complete systems ([[ART-016-the-brand-mark-process-fifty-sketches-before-any-polish|ART-016]]).

## Questions

- When does volume become an excuse for a lack of strategy ("we'll send ten more options")?
- What changes when generative AI produces a hundred options in a few seconds? Where does the work move?
