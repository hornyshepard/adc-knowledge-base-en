# Is the idea born alone, in a pair or in a meeting? The classics disagree

> **The idea in one sentence:** the stories behind the great ads support three different models of creation (the solitary writer who toils to exhaustion, the copy and art pair, and the group whose members challenge each other), and each one has its evidence.

## Explanation

**The solitary model.** Lennen & Mitchell opened their agency in 1924 with the manifesto ad "Find the Man": good advertising is not made by mass production methods but by "the solitary worker who shuts himself up with his problem" and lives with it until he solves it. The text explicitly criticized "conference copy". Watkins's stories seem to confirm it: Rubicam covers dozens of yellow sheets with headlines for Squibb until two in the morning, when two fragments come together. For Steinway, he puts the phrase away in a desk drawer for a few days so that he can judge it with a cool head. Carl Spier (Hamilton) tries 40 or 50 approaches over several days, then writes the ad in seven minutes. Nelson Metcalf reworks "The Kid in Upper 4" 25 or 30 times.

**The pair model.** Richards places the heart of the creative revolution in the copywriter and the art director shut in the same room (Bernbach). Many of Watkins's "solitary" writers in fact worked in pairs, like Rubicam with George Cecil, who gave the "University of the Night" ad its headline.

**The group model.** Alex Osborn (BBDO) introduced brainstorming in 1942, and it became a hallmark of the agency (his rules are in [[IDE-010-osborn-s-brainstorming-has-four-rules-quantity-deferred|IDE-010]]). Watkins, who usually praises the solitary genius, admits the exception: "Soup on the Rocks" was born in a meeting. Someone asked what would happen if you poured the soup over ice, someone else wanted to find out, someone else had the courage to try. Keith Reinhard, in 2020, warns that working exclusively from home removes exactly the spontaneous collision of ideas within a team.

What the three models have in common: a period of intense work on the problem, followed by a moment of recombination, often after a break.

## Application

- Alternate: individual work (you generate many variants), then a confrontation in a pair or a group (you combine and eliminate), then individual work again (you write).
- Put an idea "in the drawer" before you present it; if it holds up for a few days, it is probably good.
- Don't wait for inspiration: in almost all the stories, the "aha" moment comes after dozens of failed attempts.

## Example

The "Find the Man" ad brought the new agency two very important accounts and growth from practically zero to close to $12,000,000 in billing by 1948.

## Questions

- Which model suits your temperament, and which model suits an agency team today?
- Can generative AI play the role of the partner who challenges you?
