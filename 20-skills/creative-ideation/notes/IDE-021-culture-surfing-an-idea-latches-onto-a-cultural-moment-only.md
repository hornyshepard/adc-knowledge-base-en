# Culture surfing: an idea latches onto a cultural moment only if the brand's values allow it

> **The idea in one sentence:** cultural moments and the spirit of the time are a rich source of ideas, but only for brands whose tone and values fit the moment.

## Explanation

Braun and Landa call *culture surfing* the co-opting of a cultural moment ("Barbenheimer", the simultaneous release of two films; Wordle; a fan phenomenon) or of the zeitgeist (the collective feeling of a period: the generative AI craze, musical festival season). The creative mind asks: how does this relate to the brief, to the brand's values and larger story?

The book's examples:

- Burger King "Confusing Times" (David Madrid), for the Impossible Whopper: people caught in the absurdity of the times (Emma prefers dating Mark's profile more than dating Mark; a book about climate change arrives packed in styrofoam peanuts). The dark humor reaches a vegan audience who wouldn't think to visit Burger King.
- Magnum "Mozart by Magnum" (Lola MullenLowe): a 1778 letter in which Mozart tells how, after a successful concert, he treated himself to an ice cream becomes the starting point for a remix of the 25th Symphony in G Minor.
- Burberry and the TikTok account "Sylvaniandrama": the Lola bag, in miniature, appears in a soap opera played by animal figurines, and the creator does not change her style for it.

**The golden rule:** the brand's values guide the idea. "Confusing Times" would not work for Coca-Cola, which is built on sharing and friendship. Kate Nettleton (VCCP) says culture should be a compass, not a line you cross. Harsh Kapadia: culture always has something new to say, and purpose must extend organically from what happens naturally in it.

Ingledew reaches the same idea from design: an idea doesn't have to be timeless, it has to work *now*. He asks: "Why make the present seem second-hand by using the styles of the past?" The idea that captures the zeitgeist gives viewers the feeling that they have understood the spirit of the age.

## Application

- Keep a weekly "cultural radar": three moments, three phenomena, three conversations in the target's communities.
- Put each one through the values test: does the brand's tone allow this? Do we have a legitimate reason to join the conversation?
- Decide fast: cultural moments have a short window (Reddit had one week from idea to air).

## Example

On 4 May 2010 BlackBerry tweeted "May the 4th be with you" and grew its followers by 15%. The moment was "its own", because the brand offered an app that turned the phone into a lightsaber (Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott, [[IDE-004-the-creative-idea-must-first-dramatize-the-key-benefit|IDE-004]]).
