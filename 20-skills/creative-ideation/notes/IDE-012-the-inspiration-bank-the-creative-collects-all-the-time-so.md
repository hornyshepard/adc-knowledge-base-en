# The inspiration bank: the creative collects all the time, so as never to start from zero

> **The idea in one sentence:** ideas do not appear out of nothing; they transform material gathered beforehand, so a creative lives with eyes open, a camera in the pocket and a full sketchbook.

## Explanation

Ingledew calls the brain the storehouse of inspiration and the imagination its creative studio. The art director Liam Gibson says you should experience as much as you can, so that when a brief comes you already have inspiration gathered, from both high and low culture. The book proposes several sources, each with a concrete routine:

| Source | The routine (the "DIY" section) |
|---|---|
| Observation (*finding ideas*) | always carry a camera and photograph visual coincidences: the manhole cover that looks like a football, the oddly shaped shadow |
| Sketchbooks and scrapbooks | a cut and pasted storehouse of ideas; seen together, images found online suggest new combinations (Ross Cooper) |
| Museums | the permanent collections, the forgotten corners, the local museum; and your own home, as a museum of your life |
| Travel | new colors, shapes, solutions; Ken Garland says images he absorbed at 18 were digested only decades later |
| Knowledge | James Webb Young: good creatives get interested in any subject and browse in all fields |
| Art, movies, music | the bloodlines of art history; famous film scenes for almost any theme |
| You (*You! put yourself in it*) | your experience, your childhood, your obsessions; what you love, someone else may love too |

The mechanism ("Brainjack"): ideas from sketchbooks offer maximum-strength communication when they have been digested and refined for months or years.

## Application

- Keep a cheap sketchbook (Jon Gledstone: expensive ones can become too precious) and a digital archive of images "that make you double-take".
- When you receive a brief, search your own bank first, before Google.
- Weekly exercise: ten photographs of visual finds from the city.

## Example

Marcel Breuer designed his tubular chair after cycling on his new bicycle, and the Anglepoise lamp started from the springed suspension under cars: the inspiration came from everyday objects, not from design.
