# Genius is a team sport: three myths of creativity that block collaboration

> **The idea in one sentence:** the big idea does not come from a lone hero, in a moment of illumination, from an expert in the field. It comes from combining many little ideas, from different people, often from the edges of the domain.

## Explanation

In the final chapter, von Scheel fights three myths that, he says, turn people into lone wolves (pp. 305–307):

1. **The myth of the lone hero.** We want to believe there is a source-genius, but there is no such thing as a lone inventor. The author claims that "Edison" worked as a brand standing for a team of 14 people and 10,000 patents. He quotes Steve Jobs: "I never created anything." All Jobs did, by his own account, was notice patterns and put people together: without Wozniak there is no original Apple, without Tony Fadell there is no iPhone.
2. **The myth of the eureka moment.** There is no single big idea that changes the world. There are little ideas that combine with other little ideas and improve until they become decisive. The author describes Industry 4.0 as a thousand problems solved.
3. **The myth of the expert.** You need experts on the *problem space*, not on the *solution space*: good solutions come from the edges of a domain. A fish asked how the water is replies by asking what water is. Someone too steeped in a domain no longer sees the patterns. The mantra: ideas can come from anywhere.

The advertising classics do not agree with all three: the stories collected by Watkins also support the model of the lone writer who toils to exhaustion ([[IDE-024-is-the-idea-born-alone-in-a-pair-or-in-a-meeting-the|IDE-024]]).

The core capacity is recognizing patterns and convergence, that is, novel associations between ideas, not collecting facts. Opportunity matters too: luck is what happens when preparation meets opportunity (Seneca). Bach and Mozart were put in positions that allowed them to create (p. 308).

## Application

- In an ideation session, bring at least one person from outside the category (another client, another department, a consumer).
- Do not wait for the perfect idea: produce many little ideas and combine them. The final idea usually has several "parents".
- When you present the idea, credit the team. It is also a sales technique: the client buys a process more easily than a genius.

## Example

The Threadless case study shows the same mechanism at scale: the community proposes thousands of designs, and the value comes from selection and combination, not from a star designer.

## Questions

- The arguments are unsourced anecdotes (the figures about Edison, the claim that the great sell 20% more). What research would you look for to check them?
- The author calls pattern recognition "apophenia", although the term describes seeing patterns that do not exist. Where is the line between creative intuition and illusion?
- In agencies, the "creative star" is also a tool for selling the agency. Does that contradict the myth?
