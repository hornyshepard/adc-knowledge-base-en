# The only space worth buying is the space in the audience's head: messages in places and formats that do not look like advertising

> **The idea in one sentence:** when the message appears where you do not expect it, or takes a form that does not resemble an ad, it gets past the viewer's defenses and becomes a topic of conversation.

## Explanation

Ingledew starts from an observation: the classic spaces (the billboard, the 30-second spot, the magazine page) work less and less well. Someone who sees a message in its usual place knows you are trying to sell them something; a message that appears in a new venue "sneaks up on them" and has a better chance of sticking. His examples: the luggage carousel at Venice airport turned into a casino roulette wheel; Pirelli (1962), with the passengers on the top deck of the bus "completing" the bodies in the slipper ad; Evian, which paid for the renovation of the Brixton Lido and painted its logo on the bottom of the pool, visible from passing planes. The "Word of mouth" entry adds messages made to be gossiped about: the Converse bands' surprise tour of China, the illy van that handed out coffee around the world. The "Digitality" and "Sensations" entries widen the channel: interaction, sound, smell, touch.

Braun and Landa take the idea further: the next great brand story may look nothing like advertising. Sheba "built" an underwater billboard out of coral reef that reads "Hope", visible on Google Earth. Heineken moved money from street billboards to the closed shutters of bars during the pandemic. Coors Light painted roofs in Miami with messages in reflective white paint that cools the apartments. The Red Cross and HBO asked *Game of Thrones* fans to give blood ("Bleed for the Throne").

The difference between the two books: for Ingledew the novelty of the venue is enough; for Braun and Landa the unexpected venue must also do something useful for people or the planet. A reservation comes from the research cited in *Kellogg on Branding*: content goes viral mainly through mass broadcast, not from person to person ([[ADV-067-messages-go-viral-through-broadcast-not-from-person-to|ADV-067]]), so the gossip mechanism also needs a large channel to set it off.

## Application

- Draw the target's day and mark the objects and moments in which they never receive messages: that is where the new spaces are.
- Ask whether the format can be the benefit itself (the roof that cools, the shutters that pay the rent).
- Think about the gossip mechanism from the start: what will someone tell their friends?

## Example

Coors Light "Made to Chill" (DDB Chicago, Nord DDB, adam&eveDDB): more than 36,000 square feet of roofs in Miami, painted reflective white and written with messages, cooled down 96 apartments. The "billboard" cannot be seen from the street, but it can be felt at home.

## Questions

- When does an unexpected space become intrusive or illegal (public property, private spaces)?
- How do you measure an activation whose main effect is earned media?
