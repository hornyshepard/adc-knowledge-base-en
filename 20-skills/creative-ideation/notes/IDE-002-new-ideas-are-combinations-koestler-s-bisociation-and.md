# New ideas are combinations: Koestler's bisociation and combinatorial thinking

> **The idea in one sentence:** a creative idea joins two frames of reference that seemed unrelated; the more you know, the more material you have to combine.

## Explanation

Both books in the group invoke Arthur Koestler (*The Act of Creation*, 1964). Ingledew keeps two theses. The creative process consists in "the discovery of hidden similarities", in art, science and humor alike. The idea comes from a **linking jump** from one frame of reference to another: you understand the established patterns and types of logic, then break free from them. Hence the importance of the *art of forgetting* at the proper moment. His example: which letter is the odd one out in A F H K M N Y Z? Habit looks for a relationship between the letters; the answer is M, the only one made from four straight lines, while the others feature three. To see it, you have to forget you are looking at letters.

Braun and Landa call the mechanism **bisociation**: the problem is considered simultaneously in two *matrices of thought*, which fuse into a novel synthesis. They add a genealogy: James Webb Young (the idea as a new combination of old elements), Poincaré (ideas that collide until pairs interlock into a stable combination), Einstein (*combinatorial play*), Margaret Boden (improbabilist creativity, through improbable combinations of familiar ideas, versus impossibilist creativity, which changes the conventions of the domain) and Paul Thagard (one hundred breakthrough discoveries and one hundred historic inventions analyzed, all reducible to combinations). The common condition: the new combination must be *interesting*.

## Application

- Write two lists: the elements of the brief (product, insight, a ritual of the target) and elements from other fields (sport, history, technology, pop culture). Force pairs.
- Look for intersections of factors: technology, demographics, trends, different sectors, brand rivalries.
- Invest in general knowledge: Webb Young observed that good advertising people can get interested in any subject, from Egyptian burial customs to modern art.

## Example

Samsung "iTest" (DDB New Zealand) combined the car test drive with the phone. A web application mimicked the Samsung interface on the iPhone, and over 12 million iPhone users have test-driven a Samsung. "Sick Beats" (Area 23) combined a vibrating vest, 40Hz music from Spotify and airway-clearance therapy for children with cystic fibrosis.

## Questions

- If every idea is a combination, what makes the difference between a banal combination and a brilliant one?
- Can generative AI do bisociation? Which criterion (the "interesting") stays with the human?
