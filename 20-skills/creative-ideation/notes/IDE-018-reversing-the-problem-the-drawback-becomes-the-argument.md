# Reversing the problem: the drawback becomes the argument

> **The idea in one sentence:** take the most problematic element of the brief and ask what its opposite is; sometimes the problem is the answer itself.

## Explanation

Ingledew gives a list of reversals that show how "reverse thinking" works:

| The problem | The reverse |
|---|---|
| The car I have to advertise is small. | "It makes your house look bigger" (Volkswagen) |
| The resort is chillier than its competitors. | "Skegness is so bracing" |
| Rain may spoil the outdoor event. | Design the event so that rainfall makes the day (the Squidarella umbrella changes color when wet) |
| Tattoos deteriorate as they fade. | Tattoos that improve with age as they fade |
| Pickpocketing is a problem. | Put warning messages in people's pockets by stealth |
| I have no budget. | The things I already have are perfect for the job |
| Museums prevent people touching objects. | Show objects that visitors are encouraged to touch |

Two related entries extend the technique. **Counter-intuition** turns conventional wisdom on its head: Dyson made the dust visible, although the whole industry hid it in bags; Apple made see-through, colored computers when everyone else sold drab grey boxes; Paul Smith used lined, lived-in faces instead of perfect models. **Reinvention** breaks the convention of a format: Droog showed its design in a down-on-its-luck hotel, with staff playing hotel employees; Alexander McQueen had two industrial paint-spraying robots decorate a model's dress on the runway.

Braun and Landa offer an example of strategy, not just execution: young drinkers avoided Canadian Club because it was what their dads drank, and the campaign made the dad cool. Kevin Koller (R/GA) starts every problem by asking "How do you do it opposite from the way that it's always been done before": the five-second Super Bowl spot became a text too long to read. The classic of the genre is still Volkswagen: "Lemon" and "Think Small" turned small size, seen as a flaw, into the very reason to buy ([[IDE-003-the-big-idea-the-concept-that-makes-an-ad-different-from|IDE-003]]).

## Application

- Ingledew: ask "what is the reverse?" at every stage, from the brief to the final execution.
- List the category's conventions ("all X ads look like…") and try the opposite of each.
- Check that the reversal stays true for the product; otherwise it is only a trick.

## Example

Dyson made the dust visible: a transparent container instead of the hidden bag. What the whole industry thought was unsightly became visible proof that the vacuum cleaner works.

## Questions

- What is the difference between reversing a problem and denying it? Where does reversal become dishonest?
- Does reversal work as well in heavily regulated categories (pharma, finance)?
