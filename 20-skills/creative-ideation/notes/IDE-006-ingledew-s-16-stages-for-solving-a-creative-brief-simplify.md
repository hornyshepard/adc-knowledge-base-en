# Ingledew's 16 stages for solving a creative brief: simplify at every stage

> **The idea in one sentence:** the road from brief to published idea has 16 stages, from naive questions to dissemination, and one rule runs through them all: cut, simplify, get to the essence.

## Explanation

Ingledew breaks commercial creative work down into 16 stages:

| # | Stage | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The briefing | clarify what you are asked to do and what you deliver; ask any question, however naive |
| 2 | Analyze the challenge | reread the brief and reduce it to its essence ("A problem well stated is half-solved", Dewey) |
| 3 | First ideas | collate the reflex, intuitive ideas |
| 4 | Visualization | draw right away; drawing sparks new connections |
| 5 | Know the subject / know the product | research, interviews, using the product; go off at a tangent too |
| 6 | Know your audience | what makes them laugh, what shocks them, their rituals, music, films |
| 7 | Assimilate | bring everything together and break it down into its simplest elements |
| 8 | Play with all you have learnt | make links, associate freely, use the A–Z |
| 9 | Visualize further | refine and simplify the developed ideas |
| 10 | Incubation / reflection | judge: will the audience get it? Get rid of the obvious |
| 11 | Final visuals | the most potent form: images should talk from 100 metres away |
| 12 | Presentation / the pitch | tell the story of how the idea evolved, with clarity and energy |
| 13 | Consensus | agree with the client which idea has cracked the brief |
| 14 | Implementing the idea | choose the visual tone; the delivery is key, as in telling a joke (Hegarty) |
| 15 | Deadline | hand over the work |
| 16 | Publication and dissemination | print, web, billboards |

**The rule that runs through every stage:** simplify at every stage, from analyzing the brief to the final image (Hegarty: the French Revolution got its message down to three words). An art director quoted in the book cut a ten-page brief down to 15 words.

## Application

- Stages 1–2 are detailed, with sources from both the client and the agency side, in [[BRF-024-receiving-the-brief-you-reduce-it-to-its-essence-check-it|BRF-024]].
- Stage 1: ask what seems stupid. Kenneth Grange learned that the buffers on a locomotive were an unnecessary hangover and could design a streamlined train.
- Stage 5: go off at a tangent. For a skin cream, talk to the person at the zoo who looks after the skin of the elephants.
- Stage 10: eliminate the obvious ideas first.

## Example

Edwin Land invented the Polaroid camera after his three-year-old daughter asked why she could not see the pictures now. One naive question in stage 1 can turn the brief on its head.

## Questions

- Which stages drop out when you work to a 24-hour deadline? Which ones cannot be sacrificed?
- Ingledew puts "first ideas" before research. Why? What is the risk of the reverse order?
