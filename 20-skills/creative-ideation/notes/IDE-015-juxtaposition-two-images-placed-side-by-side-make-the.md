# Juxtaposition: two images placed side by side make the viewer build the connection themselves

> **The idea in one sentence:** when two things are placed together, the brain compares them automatically; the imagination makes the journey from one to the other, and that is where the message appears.

## Explanation

For Ingledew, to juxtapose means to put side by side. The mechanism ("Brainjack") is simple: the brain instantly attempts to link what it sees, and the imagination fills in the journey from one image to the other. The message is in neither image but in the space between them, which the viewer completes.

The best-known form is **before and after**: the image implies that the product will make you thinner, less balding or give you clearer skin. The examples in the book show how far the technique can go:

| Work | What is placed side by side | What the viewer understands |
|---|---|---|
| johnson banks for the British Council (1998) | a nostalgic playground photograph and a multiracial class of today; an old master painting and Damien Hirst's sheep in formaldehyde | Britain, a place where tradition and modernity meet |
| Nicolai Howalt, "Battered Boxer" | the faces of young boxers just before and just after the fight, with the result in the caption | the brutality of each contest; the viewer imagines what happened |
| Cesar (2007) | dogs and the owners who look like them | the smile of recognition: we all know someone who looks like their pet |
| David Pearson, the *Austerlitz* book cover | the profile of a young man and of the same man old, in two lines | a whole life, from innocence to experience |

A related variant is the **mirror**: the viewer expects a symmetrical reflection and gets an unexpected comparison (the Paul Smith photograph that shows the front and back of a suit at the same time).

## Application

- Choose two images that are banal on their own. The message must come *only* from placing them together.
- Leave one possible connection, or one dominant one; if the viewer can link them in five ways, the message is lost.
- Test at small scale (a thumbnail in the feed): can you see both elements and the difference between them?

## Example

johnson banks for the British Council (1998): a nostalgic playground photograph next to a multiracial class of today. Neither image says "tradition and modernity"; the message appears only in the space between them, where the viewer puts it.

## Questions

- When does "before and after" become a misleading promise? Which regulations limit the technique in cosmetics and weight loss?
- How can juxtaposition work in time (video), not in space?
