# The generation and selection toolkit: SCAMPER, the Six Thinking Hats and the weighted decision matrix

> **The idea in one sentence:** SCAMPER produces variants of an existing idea, the Six Thinking Hats structure the discussion about them, and the weighted decision matrix chooses among them by criteria set in advance.

## Explanation

Ahmed gathers classic tools into three families.

**Generation: SCAMPER.** Seven operations on an existing idea or solution:

| Letter | Question |
|---|---|
| S: Substitute | what can I replace? |
| C: Combine | which elements can I join? |
| A: Adapt | how do I adapt it to another need? |
| M: Modify | what can I change, enlarge, shrink? |
| P: Put to another use | what else can it serve? |
| E: Eliminate | what can I remove to simplify it? |
| R: Rearrange | what can I reorder? |

Also for generation: **role playing** (you put yourself in the place of the customer, the competitor, the mentor) and the mind map.

**Discussion: the Six Thinking Hats** (Edward de Bono). Each hat is a way of thinking: white (facts), red (emotions), black (risks), yellow (benefits), green (new ideas), blue (managing the process, so that all the hats are used).

**Selection.** The **decision matrix**: you list the options and the criteria, weight the criteria, score each option, multiply and add up. The book also offers the pros and cons list, cost-benefit analysis, the **Pareto** principle (20% of actions produce 80% of the results) and the **Eisenhower** matrix (urgent/important) for prioritizing tasks.

## Application

Translated for a creative studio:

- SCAMPER on an execution: you have a good poster; run the seven operations to get the social, OOH and packaging versions.
- Six hats at the internal review: a quarter of an hour on the white hat (does it follow the brief?), then red, black, yellow; the green hat at the end, for improvements.
- A weighted matrix for concepts: the criteria come from the brief and from ADPLAN ([[BRF-026-adplan-six-questions-for-an-execution-that-is-on-brief-but|BRF-026]]), with weights agreed *before* the presentation, so that you don't adjust them to your preferences.

## Example

Read through SCAMPER, "Made to Chill" (Coors Light) is a P, "put to another use": the billboard becomes a roof that cools apartments ([[IDE-020-the-only-space-worth-buying-is-the-space-in-the-audience-s|IDE-020]]). The operations do not produce the idea on their own, but they show the path a team could have taken.

## Questions

- SCAMPER starts from something that exists. Can it produce a new idea or only variants? Compare with bisociation in [[IDE-002-new-ideas-are-combinations-koestler-s-bisociation-and|IDE-002]].
- A decision matrix can "kill" the boldest idea, which fails on the "risk" criterion. How do you protect strategic risk (see [[CLI-003-the-good-client-gets-the-good-work-alignment-from-the-start|CLI-003]])?
