# Ideation and creative thinking (IDE)

The skill answers three questions: what makes an idea an idea (not just a beautiful execution), how you reach it starting from a brief, and how you choose between several ideas. The repertoire of techniques comes mostly from *The A–Z of Visual Ideas* (Ingledew); the process and the campaign idea from *Shareworthy* (Braun and Landa) and from Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott; the historical perspective (the Big Idea, the creative team, brainstorming) from Richards and Watkins. The starting point is always the brief ([[20-skills/creative-brief/SKILL|BRF]]) and the insight ([[20-skills/consumer-insight/SKILL|INS]]); the visual and verbal execution continues in [[20-skills/art-direction/SKILL|ART]] and [[20-skills/copywriting/SKILL|CPY]].

## What mastering this skill means

Observable competencies ("you can…"):

- **C1** You can explain what sets a creative idea apart from a beautiful execution: the "brainjack" mechanism, the combination of two frames of reference and the requirement to dramatize the benefit from the brief.
- **C2** You can go through the path from brief to idea (reduction to the essence, research, play, incubation, selection) and reframe a badly posed problem.
- **C3** You can organize and lead a group ideation session with Osborn's rules, and unblock a team with random triggers or a round of questions.
- **C4** You can apply at least six idea techniques (juxtaposition, visual metaphor, the cliché turned on its head, reversal, humor, unexpected format) and say why each one captures the viewer's mind.
- **C5** You can tie an idea to a cultural moment only when the brand's values allow it, and weigh the risk of shock ideas.
- **C6** You can produce volume (dozens of variants) and then select with an explicit grid, chosen by the moment: Percy's requirements, A.L.T.E.R., the idea on paper, ADPLAN, the weighted matrix.
- **C7** You can argue a position, with sources, in the debates about the lone genius versus the team, and about shock and humor.

## How an AI agent uses this skill

1. **When you are asked to generate ideas for a brief**: reduce the brief to one sentence and check the problem ([[IDE-006-ingledew-s-16-stages-for-solving-a-creative-brief-simplify|IDE-006]], [[IDE-008-reframing-the-problem-in-the-brief-opens-up-solutions-from|IDE-008]], [[BRF-024-receiving-the-brief-you-reduce-it-to-its-essence-check-it|BRF-024]]); start from the insight ([[INS-092-an-insight-is-a-meaningful-human-truth-and-has-to-pass|INS-092]]); produce volume ([[IDE-009-volume-comes-before-quality-the-good-idea-comes-out-of-a|IDE-009]]) by going systematically through the techniques: combination ([[IDE-002-new-ideas-are-combinations-koestler-s-bisociation-and|IDE-002]]), juxtaposition ([[IDE-015-juxtaposition-two-images-placed-side-by-side-make-the|IDE-015]]), metaphor ([[IDE-016-visual-metaphor-and-formal-resemblance-one-thing-looks-like|IDE-016]]), the cliché turned on its head ([[IDE-017-cliche-convention-and-the-familiar-format-become-ideas-when|IDE-017]]), reversal ([[IDE-018-reversing-the-problem-the-drawback-becomes-the-argument|IDE-018]]), humor ([[IDE-019-humor-irony-and-satire-overturn-expectation-and-create|IDE-019]]), unexpected format ([[IDE-020-the-only-space-worth-buying-is-the-space-in-the-audience-s|IDE-020]]), cultural moment ([[IDE-021-culture-surfing-an-idea-latches-onto-a-cultural-moment-only|IDE-021]]). For each idea, say what the viewer does in their mind ([[IDE-001-brainjack-a-good-idea-captures-the-viewer-s-mind-not-just|IDE-001]]) and which technique you used. Present a few chosen ideas.
2. **When you are asked to evaluate or choose between concepts**: first check whether each one dramatizes the benefit ([[IDE-004-the-creative-idea-must-first-dramatize-the-key-benefit|IDE-004]]), then apply the grid that fits the moment from [[IDE-023-idea-evaluation-grids-converge-on-three-questions-does-it|IDE-023]]; for executions, use ADPLAN ([[BRF-026-adplan-six-questions-for-an-execution-that-is-on-brief-but|BRF-026]]). Point out the risk of shock ([[IDE-025-debate-is-shock-the-most-powerful-weapon-for-attention-or|IDE-025]]) and of humor that steals the brand ([[CPY-031-humor-attracts-attention-and-liking-but-steals-brand-memory|CPY-031]]).
3. **When a team is stuck**: propose random triggers and incubation ([[IDE-011-when-the-idea-won-t-come-incubation-creative-distraction|IDE-011]]), a round of questions instead of answers ([[IDE-008-reframing-the-problem-in-the-brief-opens-up-solutions-from|IDE-008]]) or SCAMPER on the best existing idea ([[IDE-022-the-generation-and-selection-toolkit-scamper-the-six|IDE-022]]).
4. **When you are asked to organize an ideation session**: structure it following [[IDE-010-osborn-s-brainstorming-has-four-rules-quantity-deferred|IDE-010]] (Osborn's rules, generating kept separate from evaluating), bring in people from outside the category ([[IDE-014-genius-is-a-team-sport-three-myths-of-creativity-that-block|IDE-014]]) and alternate individual work with group work ([[IDE-024-is-the-idea-born-alone-in-a-pair-or-in-a-meeting-the|IDE-024]]).
5. **When you need to explain what a Big Idea or a campaign idea is**: use [[IDE-003-the-big-idea-the-concept-that-makes-an-ad-different-from|IDE-003]], [[IDE-004-the-creative-idea-must-first-dramatize-the-key-benefit|IDE-004]], [[IDE-005-the-north-star-idea-is-crystallized-with-the-three-gs-goal|IDE-005]] and [[ADV-047-a-big-idea-is-not-enough-without-a-long-idea-the-audience|ADV-047]] (Long Idea), and show where the authors differ.
6. Cite the IDs of the notes you use. For visual examples and quotes, open in `90-library/text/` the files `a-to-z-visual-ideas.txt` (the A–Z entries), `shareworthy-robin-landa.txt` (ch. 6) or `audiobook/28.txt` (Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott), at the place given in the note.
