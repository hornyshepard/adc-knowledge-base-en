# AI in marketing and advertising (AIM)

The skill answers three practical questions for an agency: what AI can actually do in a marketing project, how you read an "AI-made" result critically, and where the human has to stay (insight, idea, ethical judgment).

**On the quality of the sources: read this first.** The library has a single rigorous book on AI and prediction: Steven Struhl (2017), which predates generative AI but is solid on methods, validation and reasoned skepticism. *Marketing 5.0* and *6.0* (Kotler et al.) give the strategic frame and the division of labor between human and machine. Richards & McLean (2014) describe decision engines and uplift modeling well, but they are self-published and pre-generative. The books on generative AI are thin: Najafov (ChatGPT) offers only five simple prompt examples, and Noil, James and Iroegbu are generic and repetitive, with no verifiable cases. The real cases of creative work with AI come from general books (*Shareworthy*, *Marketing 6.0*). So: the prompting techniques in this base are minimal, and where notes cite figures from the weak books, they are flagged as unverifiable.

## What mastering this skill means

Observable competencies ("you can…"):

- **C1** You can explain what AI concretely does in marketing (finds patterns, predicts, automates, generates) and what it does not do (insight, idea, positioning), using the division of labor between human and machine.
- **C2** You can choose the right method for a marketing question (classification trees, trade-off methods, Bayesian networks, simulators) and ask for the correct validation of a model.
- **C3** You can read an "AI" result critically: share of preference vs. market share, response model vs. uplift, correlation vs. cause, fluent explanation vs. proof.
- **C4** You can design personalization by registers and dynamic segments, with a decision engine and a contact strategy that do not tire the audience and do not cross the "creepy" threshold.
- **C5** You can write a prompt as a mini-brief, treat the output as a draft and bring generative AI into a creative workflow in which the idea and the selection stay with the team.
- **C6** You can apply an ethical checklist before launch: consent and minimization, labeling of generated content, bias checked by group, an easy way out to a human.

## How an AI agent uses this skill

1. **When a client or a vendor proposes "an AI model" or "insight from data":** translate the request into a row of [[AIM-008-you-choose-the-analysis-method-by-the-marketing-question|AIM-008]], ask what view of people the method holds ([[AIM-001-methods-for-predicting-choices-fall-into-four-classes-each|AIM-001]]) and ask for the result on unseen data ([[AIM-007-validation-on-a-hold-out-sample-separates-real-patterns|AIM-007]]). Check whether novelty is being asked of historical data ([[AIM-005-historical-data-predict-repetition-not-novelty-they-do-not|AIM-005]]), whether the result also counts sales that would have come anyway ([[AIM-021-uplift-modeling-looks-for-the-persuadable-a-response-model|AIM-021]]) and whether the model can be explained ([[AIM-010-accuracy-or-transparency-ensembles-and-neural-networks|AIM-010]]).
2. **When you have to choose between offers, prices, benefits or messages:** recommend the method from [[AIM-012-trade-off-methods-discrete-choice-conjoint-maxdiff-q-sort|AIM-012]], build the study with [[AIM-013-a-good-choice-study-is-built-before-the-questionnaire-a-ten|AIM-013]], adjust the result with [[AIM-014-share-of-preference-is-not-market-share-the-forecast-has-to|AIM-014]] and, for price or competitive launches, use the simulator logic from [[AIM-015-a-market-simulation-shows-when-a-price-cut-raises-share-but|AIM-015]]. For messages, see also [[ADV-080-the-elements-of-an-ad-can-be-tested-as-an-experiment|ADV-080]].
3. **When you plan personalization, CRM or a chatbot:** set the register ([[AIM-016-ai-personalization-works-in-four-registers-real-time|AIM-016]]) and the segment rules ([[AIM-017-ai-built-segments-are-dynamic-customers-move-between-them|AIM-017]]), place the client on the [[AIM-018-the-ice-maturity-model-ten-principles-and-five-levels-from|AIM-018]] scale, ask for the arbitration rule and the contact strategy ([[AIM-019-offer-what-the-customer-is-most-likely-to-accept-not-what|AIM-019]]), write the escalation map to a human ([[AIM-023-a-good-chatbot-knows-when-to-hand-the-conversation-over-to|AIM-023]], [[AIM-022-augmented-marketing-amplifies-people-instead-of-replacing|AIM-022]]) and check the "creepy" threshold ([[AIM-031-the-personalization-paradox-the-more-an-ad-knows-about-you|AIM-031]]) and the data rules ([[AIM-030-data-ethics-in-ai-marketing-has-four-basic-rules-consent|AIM-030]]).
4. **When you bring generative AI into a creative workflow:** decide what you leave to the machine and what stays with the team ([[AIM-004-in-the-new-cx-machines-are-cool-but-humans-are-warm-ai|AIM-004]]), write the prompt as a mini-brief ([[AIM-025-a-marketing-prompt-is-a-mini-brief-and-the-model-s-answer|AIM-025]]), build the voice cycle from [[CPY-029-brand-voice-does-not-come-from-a-prompt-but-from-a-cycle|CPY-029]], test the idea in one sentence without the word "AI" ([[AIM-026-in-award-winning-ai-campaigns-the-idea-comes-from-human|AIM-026]]) and do not delegate the identity decision ([[AIM-029-can-ai-create-a-brand-s-identity-the-promise-of-ai|AIM-029]]). Do not ask the model for causal explanations ([[AIM-027-a-language-model-summarizes-text-well-but-does-not-prove|AIM-027]]).
5. **Before launching a campaign with personal data or generated content:** go through [[AIM-030-data-ethics-in-ai-marketing-has-four-basic-rules-consent|AIM-030]], [[AIM-032-bias-in-ai-marketing-has-two-sources-the-data-the-model|AIM-032]] (results read by group) and [[AIM-033-disclose-when-you-use-ai-a-transparency-toolkit-and-an|AIM-033]] (labeling, consent for a synthetic face or voice), then through the practices in [[DIG-065-ten-privacy-practices-for-marketers-from-consent-that-shows|DIG-065]] and [[DIG-063-valid-consent-is-asked-for-actively-and-can-be-withdrawn|DIG-063]].
6. Cite the IDs of the notes you used and flag when a figure comes from a weak source (★☆ to ★★). Say explicitly what the base does not cover: advanced prompting techniques, current generative tools, AI-specific regulation (for example, the AI Act), copyright in generated content, measuring the effectiveness of generated creative work.
