# Disclose when you use AI: a transparency toolkit and an eight-point ethical checklist

> **The idea in one sentence:** trust in generated content is earned by saying openly where the AI worked and where a person checked, not by hiding it.

## Explanation

The most concrete part of Najafov's book is about **transparency in AI use**. The author proposes tools on three levels:

| Level | Tools |
|---|---|
| Clear communication | labels ("AI-Assisted Content"), disclaimers at the beginning or end of articles, AI usage **badges** in a consistent location (the footer, the email signature) |
| Educating the audience | campaigns that explain what AI can and cannot do, a **transparency page** on the website, behind-the-scenes demos that also show the human oversight |
| Accountability | feedback channels for errors, updates to the policy, emphasis on human review; in customer service, the first line announces that you are talking to an AI assistant |

On top of these comes an eight-point **ethical checklist**:

1. Disclose AI usage in interactions and content.
2. Obtain user consent for data.
3. Verify the accuracy of generated content.
4. Protect data privacy (GDPR, CCPA).
5. Audit regularly for bias.
6. Set up an ethical committee with people from diverse backgrounds.
7. Provide the option to opt out of AI in favor of a human.
8. Educate and train the staff.

In the chapter on video, the author names the **deepfake** risk explicitly: realistic but fabricated content that can deceive. He also mentions the social cost of automation: scriptwriters, editors and videographers could be replaced, which calls for retraining programs.

## Application

- Agree a **labeling policy** with the client before the campaign: what we disclose, where and with what wording.
- Do not use generated images or voices of real people without consent. Transparency does not fix a deception.
- Treat the checklist as an approval step, like the legal check of an ad.

## Example

The wording proposed for the support chat, "You are chatting with our AI assistant, ChatGPT. How can I assist you today?", sets expectations from the first second. The customer does not feel tricked on finding out later.

## Questions

- Does the "generated with AI" label lower the perceived value of creative content? When does it, and when does it not?
- Is transparency enough if the message itself is manipulative?
