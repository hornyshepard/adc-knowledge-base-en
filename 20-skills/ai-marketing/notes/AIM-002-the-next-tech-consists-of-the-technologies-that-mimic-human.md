# The next tech consists of the technologies that mimic human capabilities: AI, NLP, sensors, robotics, mixed reality, IoT and blockchain

> **The idea in one sentence:** the technologies behind Marketing 5.0 are not new (many date from the 1950s), but only now have the six enablers that make them usable matured, and each one mimics a human capability.

## Explanation

**Six enablers** (Fig. 6.1) took the next tech out of the lab: computing power (including GPUs), open-source software (Google, Microsoft, Facebook, Amazon and IBM opened up their AI research), the Internet (fiber and 5G), cloud computing, mobile devices and big data.

The authors describe the technologies as **bionics** (Fig. 6.2, "Bionics: Six Ways Technology Mimics Humans"): each one replicates a human way of learning or acting.

| Technology | What it mimics | Marketing uses in the book |
|---|---|---|
| **AI** (narrow, not AGI) | contextual learning, the brain | segmentation and targeting from unstructured data, recommendations, dynamic pricing; Ant Financial assesses car insurance claims from a smartphone photo |
| **NLP** | communication | sales and service chatbots (Lyft, Sephora, Starbucks), voice assistants; voice search changes how brands get chosen |
| **Sensor tech** | the senses | image recognition on social media, Tesco planograms, digital billboards that read the audience's demographic profile and emotional state |
| **Robotics** (physical and RPA) | movement | robot concierges (Hilton's Connie), coffee served by robots (Nestlé in Japan), programmatic advertising |
| **Mixed reality** (AR and VR) | imagination | IKEA's AR, the Louvre's VR tour, the TOMS VR experience of giving shoes |
| **IoT and blockchain** | the sociability of machines | Disney's MagicBand; the IBM and Unilever blockchain for transparency in digital ad placement |

The book insists that AI is "simply the brain of automation", but it needs the other technologies to deliver the experience. *Marketing 6.0* (2024) returns to the list and notes the leap brought by language models such as ChatGPT, which understand the user's context and intent, not only the words. The risks: biases from historical decisions, and widening income disparity if development is not inclusive. An example relevant to advertising: the Association of National Advertisers estimates that only 30 to 40 cents of every digital media dollar reaches publishers, and blockchain is proposed for tracking the intermediaries.

## Application

- For a creative concept that uses technology, ask: which human capability does it amplify (a sense, imagination, conversation)? The idea is stronger when the technology has a clear human role.
- Prepare brands for voice search: P&G created an Alexa skill for Tide that answers hundreds of questions about laundry.
- Do not confuse narrow AI with AGI in presentations to clients: promise specific tasks, not "intelligence".

## Example

At Disney's theme parks, the MagicBand works as a ticket, room key and payment method, and communicates with thousands of sensors. Staff can greet guests by their first name before the guests say anything.

## Questions

- Billboards that detect the emotions of passersby: where is the ethical limit of contextualization?
- Which technology in the table is already "hygiene" in your industry, and which still differentiates?
