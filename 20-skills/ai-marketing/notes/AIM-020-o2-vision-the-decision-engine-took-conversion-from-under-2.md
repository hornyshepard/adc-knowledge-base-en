# O2 Vision: the Decision Engine took conversion from under 2% to over 70%, then audience fatigue set in

> **The idea in one sentence:** saying the right thing to the person who is calling you right now works spectacularly, but the pool of people who call is finite and gets worn out.

## Explanation

The O2 case is the story the whole book rests on. Aly Richards led the project, and the foreword by Hugh Wilson (Cranfield) confirms it. At the start, O2 had no CRM system, only billing platforms, and a large department of analysts who sliced and diced the data into segments and targeting models. It was slow and not actionable: the campaigns reached more people than necessary.

The steps of the project:

1. **Organizing the customer data** into a manageable form, so that large volumes of predictions could be generated quickly. The first gain came in outbound campaigns, which were better targeted.
2. **A real-time decision environment**, branded *Vision*, used in the contact centers. The application concentrated the intelligence and the possible actions for the customer on the phone, kept a record of where the last conversation ended (so the same offer was not made twice) and was available across all channels.
3. **Monitoring** minute by minute, with daily, weekly and monthly reporting.

The reported results: the conversion rate of offers rose from **fewer than 2% to over 70%**, average revenue per user (ARPU) rose by **9%** (not including churn benefit), and the prediction process took **a seventh** of the time. O2 abandoned its average handling time measure: quality came before quantity.

The honest part of the case is the authors' note: the results **decrease over time**. Only some customers call regularly; after a while "the company runs out of things to say" and wears these customers out. Their example: paying by direct debit may convert 70% at the beginning, but once you have converted everyone who is going to convert, the rate drops. The conclusion: the Decision Engine has to be fed with people re-engaged through content. That is what the whole ICE method is about.

## Application

- When you see a case with spectacular results, ask: *at the beginning or after two years?* and *on which pool of customers?*
- Think of inbound channels (contact center, chat, app) as moments of high receptiveness, and invest in what people are told then.
- Plan from the start how you bring the audience back into the conversation, not only how you convert whoever comes.

## Example

The case itself is the example. For a brand in Romania, the analogue would be the telecom operator or the bank that proposes the right upgrade during the complaint call itself, not through a generic text message.

## Questions

- How much of the rise from 2% to 70% is due to the model, and how much to the fact that the customer made the call?
- What signals would show that an audience has reached "fatigue"?
