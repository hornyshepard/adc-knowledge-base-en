# The ICE maturity model: ten principles and five levels, from transactions to intelligent engagement

> **The idea in one sentence:** Intelligent Customer Engagement (ICE) is an organizational capability built in stages, not a tool you buy.

## Explanation

Richards and McLean measure maturity on two dimensions: **engagement complexity** (how individual and how adapted in real time the dialogue is) and **integration complexity** (how well data, channels and departments are connected around the audience). The ten principles:

| Engagement | Integration |
|---|---|
| 1. Individuality | 6. Holistic & actionable audience view |
| 2. Audience-centricity: "match actions/messages to audiences, not audiences to actions/messages" | 7. Consistency & unification |
| 3. Dynamic interactions, in real time | 8. Process orchestration (front office to back office) |
| 4. Interactive product/service configurability (negotiation in real time) | 9. Relationship management |
| 5. Customer selectivity by the customer's long-term value | 10. Command & control (planning, simulation, adjustment in real time) |

The five levels:

1. **Product Transactional**: processing systems, a company organized around products.
2. **Information Access**: a data warehouse for reporting, crude campaign targeting.
3. **Process Efficiency**: CRM, a single customer view, but the goal of the interaction is efficiency, not the relationship.
4. **One-to-One Decisions**: a **Decision Engine** uses predictive models in every interaction.
5. **Intelligent Engagement**: all channels, inbound and outbound, coordinated from a "command centre".

The benchmark cited (450 companies, 7 sectors, 12 countries): 3% at level 1, 22% at level 2, **51% at level 3**, 24% at level 4, none at level 5. The principle most correlated with performance: **dynamic interaction**. The lowest scored: negotiating with customers in real time.

The authors insist that not every company needs to reach level 5. Where the product is a commodity, level 1 may be rational.

## Application

- Use the model as a diagnostic in CRM or CX pitches: where is the client now, and which level makes sense for their business?
- The test of principle 2: in the client's plan, do campaigns start from the product ("whom do we find for offer X?") or from the person ("what suits Y?")?
- Do not promise one-to-one personalization (level 4) to a client whose data is not yet organized around customers (level 2).

## Example

O2 jumped from level 2 to level 4 by implementing a Decision Engine without having had a CRM system.
