# A good choice study is built before the questionnaire: a ten-point checklist

> **The idea in one sentence:** most choice studies fail before data collection, through the wrong attributes, jargon and unrealistic marketplaces.

## Explanation

Struhl observes that the first step, deciding how your product and your competitors' products can vary, can take as much time as everything else in the project. At a major US insurer, the marketing team knew neither the details of their competitors' products nor those of their own offering. His list of best practices:

1. **Understand your market** before the study (secondary sources, qualitative work).
2. **Use the users' language**, not internal jargon; doctors who are not specialists do not understand the company's terms.
3. **Benefits, not specifications** (not "how many foot-pounds of torque it takes to open the jar").
4. **Keep the attribute levels in order** (prices from lowest to highest).
5. **Measure only what you need** (Ockham's razor): every extra attribute makes the experiment bigger.
6. **Cover the whole plausible range**: you can interpolate, but not extrapolate.
7. **Narrow the field** when there are too many choices. A printer maker brought 57 products down to about 12 per respondent, by price range.
8. **Realistic marketplaces**, with the main competitors; the context drives the choice.
9. Tell participants to **imagine they really need to buy** one of the choices.
10. Include a **"none of these"** option.

Two acknowledged limits: people **cannot make sensory trade-offs** in a questionnaire (nobody admits to buying something that tastes worse to save money, although they do it in real life). And forecasts are **short term**. The criticism that "people do not weigh all the attributes" (non-compensatory theories) is rejected: the method accommodates any decision strategy; it does not impose one.

## Application

- Use the list as a briefing tool with the research agency.
- Points 2 and 3 are, in fact, copywriting rules: the attribute descriptions are small pieces of advertising copy.

## Example

A wine company tested nine prices out of order, three bottle sizes and 12 screens with no competitors. The data could not be interpreted: too many prices, not in order, and too few screens (the minimum was 9 × 3 = 27). The project was saved only by interviewing more people and using HB analysis, with delays and extra costs.

## Questions

- Why can you not test "tastes better" as an attribute, while you can test "brand"?
