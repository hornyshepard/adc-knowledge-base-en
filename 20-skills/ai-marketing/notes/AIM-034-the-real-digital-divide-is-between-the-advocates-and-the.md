# The real digital divide is between the advocates and the critics of digitalization: technology earns trust when it is personal, social and experiential

> **The idea in one sentence:** internet access is no longer the main barrier; the barrier is the fear of technology, and marketers overcome it only by showing that technology makes life happier, not colder.

## Explanation

In 2020 there were close to 5 billion internet users. The barrier is no longer coverage, but the affordability of access and the simplicity of use cases. For the authors, the real digital divide is between the advocates and the critics of digitalization (Fig. 4.1):

| Five perils | Five promises |
|---|---|
| automation and loss of jobs (Brookings predicts 25% of US jobs threatened) | digital economy and wealth creation |
| trust and fear of the unknown (algorithms nobody understands) | big data and lifelong learning |
| privacy and security concerns | smart living and "augmented being" |
| filter bubble and the post-truth era (deepfakes) | improved wellness and life extension |
| digital lifestyle and behavioral side effects (screen addiction, automation bias) | sustainability and social inclusivity |

To close the divide, marketers have to make technology (Fig. 4.2, the "Technology Compass"):

- **personal**: AI replaces selective attention; it simplifies choices (Barry Schwartz, *The Paradox of Choice*) but leaves the customer a sense of control (automated personalization plus optional customization, co-creation of the experience);
- **social**: it makes connection between people easier (forums, crowdsourcing, social commerce) and supports aspirations through role models from the network, not through paternalistic recommendations;
- **experiential**: it combines high-tech with high-touch (people communicate eight emotions by touch, with an accuracy of up to 78%, according to Hertenstein) and offers constant engagement, as an antidote to the "hedonic treadmill", by which any satisfaction returns to the baseline level.

## Application

- When communicating an AI-based product, answer the target audience's dominant fear explicitly (jobs, privacy, control), not just the list of benefits.
- Personalization is accepted when the benefit outweighs the perceived privacy risk and when the customer keeps a control button.
- Plan the renewal of the experience: what was WOW last year is normal this year.

## Example

The argument between Elon Musk and Jack Ma at the World AI Conference (2019) illustrates the polarization: Musk holds that AI could end human civilization, and Ma that humans will stay superior because of their emotional capacity.

## Questions

- The book treats the perils as adoption barriers to be overcome. Is it right for the marketer to be the advocate of digitalization?
- Automation bias affects strategists too: how much do you rely on the algorithm's recommendation?
