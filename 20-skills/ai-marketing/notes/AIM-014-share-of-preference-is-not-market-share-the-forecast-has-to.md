# Share of preference is not market share: the forecast has to be adjusted for awareness, understanding and distribution

> **The idea in one sentence:** in a study everyone knows all the products and can "buy" them; in the market they cannot, so every research result has to be corrected before it becomes a sales figure.

## Explanation

Struhl makes two distinctions.

**Forecast vs. projection.** A model saying that 15 percent of customers will buy if they get a coupon does not mean that sales will go up by 15 percent. To project to the marketplace, you need to know:

- what percentage of the total marketplace your customers represent;
- how many are aware of the product and how many understand what it does;
- how well it is distributed (can people find it on the shelf or on the website?);
- whether production or the website can keep up with demand;
- how many non-customers will buy, and how big the market actually is.

**Share of preference vs. market share.** The experimental methods (discrete choice, conjoint) give a *share of preference*: participants are 100 percent aware of all the choices. To get to the real share, you adjust at least for awareness and distribution, then for how well the messages perform and how well the product is presented in stores.

## Application

- When you see "X% would buy" in a report, ask: "before or after adjusting for awareness and distribution?"
- For a communication plan, share of preference shows the product's potential; the gap to market share shows **how much work is left for media and creative**.
- Do not narrow the sample with screening so strict that you no longer know which population it represents.

## Example

In a pricing study for men's casual slacks, a brand that sold poorly in the market came out strong in the test. The explanation: it was neither well known nor widely available. Once awareness and distribution were factored in, the estimates matched the actual sales. Conversely, a major software maker screened its sample down to engineers with a set number of computers. The product was well configured, but demand was underestimated by a factor of about three, with product delays, angry customers and order cancellations.

- How do you explain to a client why the product that "won" the test is not selling?
- What role does advertising play in the move from share of preference to market share?
