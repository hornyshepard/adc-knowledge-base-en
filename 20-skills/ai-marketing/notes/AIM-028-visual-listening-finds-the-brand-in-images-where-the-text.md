# Visual listening finds the brand in images, where the text does not name it

> **The idea in one sentence:** a large part of a brand's online presence is visual (logos in photos, products in videos), and monitoring text alone misses it; computer vision extends social listening to images.

## Explanation

The books in this group treat brand monitoring almost exclusively as sentiment analysis of text: comments, reviews, mentions (Iroegbu, ch. 9). Elle James adds a useful dimension: **visual recognition** (computer vision) applied to the brand. She lists six uses; the five that concern monitoring and identity are:

| Use | What it does |
|---|---|
| Visual brand mentions | finds the logo, the product or other visual assets in photos and videos on websites and social media |
| Brand ambassadors and influencers | identifies the people who already showcase the brand in their content, without a partnership |
| Brand misuse and infringement | flags unauthorized logo usage or fake goods |
| Visual sentiment | estimates the emotional reaction from context, colors, facial expressions |
| Improving the visual branding strategy | shows which visual elements (colors, pictures) resonate more with the audience |

The author also mentions **emotion AI** (affective computing): reading facial expressions, voice tones and text emotion, to see how people react to messages.

The idea is sound, but the book presents it without a single real case, without concrete tools and without limits. It does not say how accurate these systems are, nor that analyzing facial expressions raises serious problems of consent and reliability.

## Application

- In a brand audit, ask for visual monitoring, not only text monitoring. Especially for brands with a strong logo (fashion, drinks, sports), where people post the product without naming it.
- Use visual mentions to find micro-influencers who already love the brand.
- Treat "visual sentiment" and "emotion AI" as weak signals to be checked by people, not as measurements.

## Example

A hypothetical example: through logo recognition, a beer brand discovers hundreds of festival photos in which the bottle appears without a hashtag. Text data would not have found this material.

## Questions

- Is it ethical to analyze automatically the faces of people in the photos where your product appears?
- What could visual monitoring tell the art director about the identity system?
