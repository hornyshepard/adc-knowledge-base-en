# A good chatbot knows when to hand the conversation over to a human

> **The idea in one sentence:** a chatbot's value lies not in how many conversations it closes on its own, but in how well it recognizes its limits and hands the customer over to a human without friction.

## Explanation

Noil distinguishes between rule-based chatbots, which follow pre-programmed scripts, and AI-powered chatbots, which use natural language processing (NLP) to recognize intent ("When will my package arrive?" and "What's the delivery time for my order?" are both requests for tracking information), machine learning to improve over time, and **contextual awareness**: they remember what was said earlier in the conversation and what the CRM knows about the customer.

The chapter's central argument is the **hybrid approach**: the bot handles the frequent, repetitive queries, and humans take the complex, sensitive or emotional cases. The recommended practices:

- define clear objectives (response time, volume, satisfaction);
- start with the most common customer queries;
- integrate the bot with the CRM and the e-commerce platform, otherwise it cannot personalize;
- tell users what the bot can and cannot do;
- offer an **easy exit to human support**, proactively, not hidden;
- keep the tone in line with the brand voice.

The limits named: complex problems, miscommunication and data privacy risks.

## Application

- In a chatbot project, write the **escalation map** first: which signals (frustration, a sensitive topic, a third rephrasing) send the conversation to a human.
- The copywriter writes the bot's personality like a tone guide: greeting, polite refusal, handover message.
- Measure the rate of successful escalations too, not only the automatic resolution rate.

## Example

Sephora's chatbot, cited by Noil, gives product recommendations, answers beauty-related questions and books in-store appointments. The banking example in the book shows the boundary: the bot handles account balances, a dispute over a transaction goes to a human agent.

## Questions

- What is lost when a brand hides the "talk to a person" option to cut costs?
- Should the bot state explicitly that it is not human? What does that say about trust?
