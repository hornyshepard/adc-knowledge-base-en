# Augmented marketing amplifies people instead of replacing them: tiered interfaces and digital tools for frontliners

> **The idea in one sentence:** in sales and in service, the best result comes from human-machine collaboration: the machine takes the simple tasks and the volume, people take the consultative moments and the valuable customers, helped by data.

## Explanation

The opening story: after Deep Blue beat Kasparov (1997), "advanced" chess appeared, in which players can consult computers. In 2005, a tournament with grandmasters and supercomputers was won by two amateurs assisted by three regular computers (Team ZackS), because they had taught their machines better than anyone else. Hence the concept of **intelligence amplification** (IA): unlike AI, which tries to replicate human intelligence, IA augments it, and the decision stays with humans.

**1. Tiered interfaces:**

- **In sales** (Fig. 11.1): you divide the process into the top of the funnel (awareness, lead generation and qualification), the middle (nurturing the leads) and the bottom (meeting, negotiation, closing). You list the possible interfaces (self-service website, AR-enabled app, AI chatbot, live chat, telesales, salesforce) and match each activity with the interface that balances efficiency and effectiveness best. The salesforce, the most expensive channel, is kept for the bottom.
- **In service** (Fig. 11.2): you build a knowledge base of frequently asked questions (with real customer stories and a search function), a customer tiering model (CLV, loyalty status, financial and nonfinancial criteria, with movement up and down between tiers) and multitier support options: self-service and forums for everyone, chatbot and virtual assistant, then human agents (email, chat, phone) for the high tiers.

Noil ([[AIM-023-a-good-chatbot-knows-when-to-hand-the-conversation-over-to|AIM-023]]) and Najafov ([[AIM-024-the-human-forms-the-hypothesis-the-ai-runs-it-the-human|AIM-024]]) reach the same hybrid model independently: the bot takes the volume, people take the complex moments and the valuable customers.

**2. Digital tools for frontliners.** Customers arrive well informed (webrooming) and expect equally knowledgeable staff. The steps: understand the employees' frustrations (the map of the **employee experience**, EX, as a companion to the CX map), choose the technology together with them, and invest in change management (digital skills and mindset).

The authors note that Generations Y and Z see no borders between the physical and digital worlds: they live in a "phygital" world.

## Application

- In a CX project, draw the funnel and put next to each stage the human or digital interface with the best cost-to-effect ratio.
- Do not design the customer experience without also designing the experience of the employee who delivers it.
- Make access to people a benefit of the loyalty tiers: it is also an incentive to climb.

## Example

Sephora Digital Makeover Guide: the customer books an appointment, picks an inspiration from the online lookbook, the artist captures her skin tone with the Color IQ scanner, finds the right products and emails her the steps and the products used, so she can buy them again.

## Questions

- When access to people is reserved for valuable customers, the others get a "second-class" service. How do you manage that perception?
- Can chatbots hurt the brand image if they are too limited?
