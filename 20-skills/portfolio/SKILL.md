# Portfolio and personal branding (PRT)

The skill treats the portfolio as a product designed for its reader (the person who hires), not as the author's archive. The main source is Ian Fenn (*UX Portfolio Design*), which transfers almost entirely to the advertising portfolio; Kubišová brings curation, pacing and the project page from the architecture portfolio, Morgan brings the visual resume and the online personal brand, and Peters, passion projects. Where the authors disagree (skill charts, design contests, the name on the cover), the notes say so explicitly.

## What mastering this skill means

Observable competencies ("you can…"):

- **C1** You can select two to four projects for a specific job, starting from the reader and from an analysis of the job ad, not from your own history.
- **C2** You can write a case study for a campaign (brief, what you did, results), with an accomplishment title and captions that say what the image does not show.
- **C3** You can order the projects to create pacing and choose the format that fits the moment (sample, full, leave-behind; PDF, website, print).
- **C4** You can lay out the portfolio with an invisible structure (grid, white space, hierarchy), readable in 30 seconds.
- **C5** You can build the application package: a text resume and, where it makes sense, a visual one, a cover letter, a creative statement, a personal value proposition.
- **C6** You can present the portfolio at an interview and answer "Tell me about yourself" with a short, relevant story.
- **C7** You can distribute the portfolio actively (target list, internal ally, online reputation), ask for useful critique and respect non-disclosure agreements.
