# A candidate needs three types of portfolio: sample, full and leave-behind

> **The idea in one sentence:** depending on the stage of the hiring process, the portfolio takes three forms (a short extract that piques interest, the complete version for whoever wants to go deeper and a single piece left after the interview), and a flexible layout system makes all of them possible.

## Explanation

Kubišová first distinguishes two categories by audience. The **academic portfolio** (admissions, scholarships) is shorter and shows potential: how you think, concepts, process, research interests. The **professional portfolio** shows projects and practice, for readers with little time: aesthetic appeal, but also technical rigor, proof that "you are not just a designer but a problem solver and a strategic thinker". They are never the same document.

Then, three types, valid in both cases:

| Type | Role | Rough size |
|---|---|---|
| **Sample portfolio** | a short, heavily curated version that piques interest before an interview; a few key projects with concise descriptions and impactful visuals | 5–15 pages, including a bio summary and resume; about two images per page |
| **Full portfolio** | the comprehensive presentation, with extensive narratives and complete sets of drawings and visuals; often presented after the sample has sparked interest | professional: within 50 pages; academic: 20–25 pages |
| **Leave-behind** | a single printed piece (one project or theme): a business card, a thank-you postcard, a flyer, a small poster; it prolongs your presence after the meeting, or sometimes is sent ahead of it | one page |

The types overlap and are not mutually exclusive: you will need all of them, and versions for different audiences. That is why the author recommends a layout based on a modular grid (which allows content to be added, removed and resized) and keeping previous versions. At the start of a career, the full portfolio shows diversity; after 1–3 years, projects at different stages plus one or two pieces of student work; after 3–8 years, your best work, while maintaining diversity.

## Application

- For an ADC student: an 8–10 page sample attached to the application email, a 25–30 page full portfolio for the interview and a leave-behind, for example a postcard with the key visual of your best campaign and a QR code to your website.
- The leave-behind is itself a small demonstration of craft: a copywriter can leave a memorable headline, an art director an image.

## Example

After an interview at an agency, a candidate leaves a small A4 poster with the idea of their favorite campaign and a handwritten thank-you note; the creative director puts it on the wall, and the candidate stays in the team's memory.

## Questions

- Is the leave-behind an appreciated touch or an outdated gesture in the digital age?
- How do you decide what goes in the sample when you have only four good projects?
