# The professional story is built from an inventory of quantified accomplishments and a value proposition in three questions

> **The idea in one sentence:** before any resume or portfolio, you take an inventory of your assets (traits, skills, industries, projects, accomplishments), document each accomplishment with its measured result and translate it into a value proposition for the reader.

## Explanation

Morgan proposes five lists:

| List | How you make it |
|---|---|
| Personality traits | 3–5, the ones that appear in your performance reviews, letters of recommendation or what your coworkers say about you |
| Technical skills | specific ("bank reconciliation", not "accounting"); check job descriptions for what is missing |
| Industry experience | the list of industries you can claim knowledge in; for newcomers, the ones that interest them |
| Projects | the purpose, your role, the outcome: from work, school or volunteering |
| Accomplishments | starting from questions: what were you proud of? what did you do better than others? were you ever recognized? did you solve a problem, save time or money, provide a service that did not exist before, develop an idea that was used? |

**The framework for documenting an accomplishment:** the situation (in one sentence), the task, your actions (step by step), the result, quantified, even estimated if necessary. Infographic resume designer Hagan Blount insists that any task can be quantified: a process taken from 20 percent to 40 percent success, 20 percent less time, 20 percent more callbacks. The underlying question: "what can you add to the bottom line?"

**The value proposition** connects the inventory to the reader's needs through three questions:
1. What problem do you solve? (from projects and accomplishments)
2. How do you do it uniquely? (from personality traits)
3. Whom do you do it for? (from industries and types of projects)

From the same material you then write versions of different lengths: the resume summary, the LinkedIn summary (up to 2,000 characters), the short 160-character bio.

## Application

- For each portfolio project, write the accomplishment in the situation–task–action–result format; if you have no figures, use verifiable results (selection at a student festival, feedback from the real client, a campaign that ran).
- Write your value proposition in three sentences and compare it with the introduction of your portfolio.

## Example

"I solve the problem of local brands that do not stand out on social media (what), through ideas that start from neighborhood culture (how), for small food and beverage brands (for whom)."

## Questions

- How do you quantify the work of a creative, when the result depends on the whole team and on the media?
- Does the pressure to quantify risk producing invented or inflated figures?
