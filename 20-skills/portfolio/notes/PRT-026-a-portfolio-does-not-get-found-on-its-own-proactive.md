# A portfolio does not get found on its own: proactive targeting, the two-step and measuring the response

> **The idea in one sentence:** "Build it and they will come" does not hold for portfolios; the candidate makes a list of target companies, applies *and* looks for an inside advocate, shares the material with a clear request and measures whether people get in touch.

## Explanation

Morgan treats the job search as a sales campaign. Only about 20% of jobs are filled through job boards, and the spray-and-pray approach (sending your resume to hundreds of postings) is close to useless.

**Proactive targeting.** Like salespeople with their prospect lists, you make a list of target companies (from "Best Employer" lists, from the industry and the competitors of your dream company) and focus on the top 10: you follow their careers page and social accounts, contact the employees you know, share news about them, comment on their articles and take part in their groups.

**The two-step:**
1. You apply formally, with a resume tailored exactly to the requirements and a cover letter that says why you want *that* company.
2. You find an ally inside the company to refer you. Employee referrals were the number one source of external hiring (24.5% in the 2013 CareerXroads study). Only now do you also send your visual resume, as supporting material.

**Company-specific campaigns.** Hanna Phan made a presentation resume in the software of the company she wanted to work for, named it "Iwanttoworkatsliderocket" and tweeted it directly to the CEO; an hour later she got the invitation to talk, and she landed the job. Alice Lee built the website "Dear Instagram"; she was not hired there, but she landed an internship with Path, a company in a similar market.

**Sharing and measuring.** You name your files with your name (and occupation); you give them a "home" on your own website; you share them with an explicit request to your network ("please share"); you track the views (including through shortened links) and, above all, the number of people who reach out to you. If views go up but nobody calls, you check whether you have oversold or undersold your qualifications.

## Application

- Make a "Dream 10" list of target agencies; for each one, a possible inside contact and a specific reason why you want to work there.
- A personal project made for a specific agency (an idea for one of its clients) can work as an "application campaign", if it is sent straight to the right person and if it is well made.

## Example

A student who sends the portfolio through the agency's form and, at the same time, asks an ADC graduate who works there to recommend them to the creative director has a much better chance than one who waits for the form to get a reply.

## Questions

- Where is the line between a bold application and one that breaks the company's process (Morgan cites a recruiter at AT&T who warns that, at large companies, going around the process may send the wrong message)?
- What does "measuring" mean when you get feedback from only a few agencies?
