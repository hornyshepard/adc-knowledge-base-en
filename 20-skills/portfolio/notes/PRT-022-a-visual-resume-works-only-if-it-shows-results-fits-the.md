# A visual resume works only if it shows results, fits the reader and always has a text version

> **The idea in one sentence:** the infographic resume is an additional tool, not a replacement: it is welcome in advertising, marketing, PR and start-ups, but it must answer the same questions as any resume and exist alongside the text version for applicant tracking systems.

## Explanation

**The initial cut.** Morgan cites the same figures as Fenn: about 6 seconds for the first review of a resume (TheLadders); in a CareerBuilder study, one in five HR managers said they spend less than 30 seconds on an application. Every resume, visual or not, must answer three questions:
1. Can you do the job?
2. Will you do the job?
3. Will I like working with you?

**When a visual resume fits.** The author observes that the industries that typically welcome out-of-the-ordinary resumes are marketing, advertising, communications, public relations and new technology start-ups; in conservative fields, a single well-chosen graphic (for example, a 5-quarter profit turnaround) can be enough. For designers, the resume is itself a work sample.

**Construction rules** (after infographic resume designer Hagan Blount):
- Is the metric shown substantial in the eyes of the reader? Can you speak about it in an interview?
- Does the graphic reduce the time the reader spends looking for the most important part?
- Do you want to do that thing for the next company? If not, do not include it.
- The story matters as much as the design: if you cannot boil your skills down to why you would be perfect for the job, the design will not save you.

**What does not work** (the CareerBuilder examples and the common pitfalls): creativity unrelated to the job, inappropriate humor, typos, blocks of text, inappropriate email addresses, large amounts of wording copied from the job posting. And a final, marketer's measure: "If your phone isn't ringing, then your resume isn't working."

**The text version remains mandatory**, for applicant tracking systems (ATS) and for recruiters searching by keyword.

## Application

- For an art director: the visual resume can be a sample of typography and hierarchy, but every graphic element must communicate a result or a piece of information, not decorate.
- Always prepare a plain text PDF with the same information as well.

## Example

An "Amazon-style resume" (Philippe Dubost, 2013) drew over a million unique visitors in three weeks and landed him a job; the author warns, however, that its success also depended on luck and that such an effect is hard to duplicate.

## Questions

- Does the role of the visual resume differ for a copywriter and for an art director?
- Do infographic resumes still make sense when the first reader is often an automated system or an AI?
