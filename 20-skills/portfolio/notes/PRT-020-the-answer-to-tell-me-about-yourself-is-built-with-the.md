# The answer to "Tell me about yourself" is built with the principles of explaining and the structure of the Pixar pitch

> **The idea in one sentence:** presenting yourself is an act of explaining for someone busy: empathy, context, a short story and an analogy; and the purpose of the personal pitch is not to be hired on the spot but to begin a conversation.

## Explanation

**The principles of explaining** (after Lee LeFever, founder of Common Craft and author of *The Art of Explanation*), applied by Morgan to the career story:
- *Empathy*: the reader is a recruiter with hundreds of resumes on top of their own emergencies.
- *Knowledge is a curse*: the more you know, the harder it is to imagine what it is like not to know, especially when the subject is you. Check every word.
- *Context*: a real, relatable problem makes people care about what comes next.
- *A simple story*: "Meet Bob. Bob has a problem. Bob finds the solution. Bob is happy."
- *An analogy*: George Lucas is said to have described *Star Wars* as "Jaws in space"; Jobs introduced the iPod as "One thousand songs in your pocket."
- *The agreement statement*: "I think we can all agree that time is precious…"

**The pitch.** Morgan takes from Daniel H. Pink (*To Sell Is Human*) the idea that the purpose of a pitch is not to move the other person immediately to adopt your idea, but to offer something compelling enough to begin a conversation and arrive at an outcome that appeals to both of you. One of Pink's formats is the **Pixar pitch** (from Emma Coats, a former story artist at Pixar): "Once upon a time… Every day… One day… Because of that… Because of that… Until finally…". The structure can be the skeleton of a presentation resume or of the answer to "What do you do?" / "Tell me about yourself".

The story stays the same, but the words adapt to each audience; that is why a single version of your resume no longer works.

## Application

- Write your professional story in the six Pixar sentences and read it aloud in under a minute; then cut all the jargon.
- Look for an analogy for the way you work ("I'm the translator between strategy and creative").

## Example

"Once upon a time there was an economics student who drew in the margins of his notebooks. Every day he analyzed budgets. One day he made the poster for the faculty festival. Because of that, he was asked to do the festival's identity. Because of that, he ended up at ADC. Until finally, he does strategy *and* art direction, and wants to do them at your agency."

## Questions

- When does the story structure become a predictable formula for a recruiter who has heard it hundreds of times?
- How personal can "Once upon a time…" be in a formal interview?
