# Portfolio selection is done in three groups (YES, NO, MAYBE) with five control questions

> **The idea in one sentence:** once all your material is organized, you sort it into YES, NO and MAYBE, judging each project and each image by completeness, clarity, variety, the effort of reworking and identity; and three great images beat seven good ones.

## Explanation

The biggest challenge, says Kubišová, is to evaluate your own work objectively; other eyes, such as classmates or coworkers, are valuable from the preliminary selection onward. The starting point: projects that have received recognition (higher grades, competition wins, positive feedback from clients). For each project or image you ask five questions:

1. Is the work complete, thoroughly done?
2. Does the project exhibit clarity?
3. Do I have a variety of project typologies?
4. How much reworking will a lesser project need?
5. Is the work distinct, part of my identity?

| Group | What goes in |
|---|---|
| **YES** | work that strongly presents your skills, technical and creative processes and design ideas; a mix of design potential, technical understanding, scope of experience and interests; plus a few creative works from outside the field that round out the portrait |
| **NO** | work that is not relevant to the portfolio's purpose, unfinished, weakly executed or outdated work; collaborative projects in which you had a minimal role: they reveal your weaknesses and "bring the guilt of using others' talents" |
| **MAYBE** | work with strong images but uncertain relevance, or with potential that needs reworking; the decision depends on your available time, the number of such projects and their diversity |

**Rules for images:** every image should bring new information; similar images take up space and dilute the presentation; image diversity shows depth; a single poor image can undermine a lot of good work. Early work does not have to be re-imagined, but the drawings and the presentation are brought up to your current standards (always preserving the original files).

**Collaborations:** include only one or two team projects (as proof of your teamwork abilities), state your exact contribution and credit everyone; ask permission for projects created in an office.

## Application

- Spread out on a table (or a digital board) everything you have made in three years and sort it into YES / NO / MAYBE together with a colleague; set a deadline for reworking the MAYBE projects.
- For team campaigns at ADC, write next to each one who did what: "concept: me and X; copy: me; art direction: Y".

## Example

From a campaign with 12 executions, an art director keeps the key visual, one outdoor adaptation in context and one social post that shows the system: three images that say different things, not 12 variations of the same layout.

## Questions

- How do you judge your own work objectively when you are emotionally attached to a project?
- What do you do when your best project is a team project in which you had a small role?
