# The resume, the design statement and the cover letter complete the portfolio: functional, short and tailored to each application

> **The idea in one sentence:** the portfolio does not go out alone: a one- or two-page resume in which function beats creativity, a design statement of four to eight sentences and a cover letter of a page at most, which does not repeat the resume, together form the application package.

## Explanation

**The resume** (Kubišová): a synopsis of one page or, at most, two, with only what is relevant to the position; concise, precise and, above all, truthful: your credibility vanishes at the first stretch of the truth that is found out. A layout in two or three columns, hierarchy through font size and weight, left alignment, white space, a style consistent with the portfolio. Sections: (optional photo), contact, short summary, education, experience (reverse chronological, with active verbs: "created", "collaborated", "led"), achievements and awards (with details: title, year, reason), skills (grouped by function, **without self-assessment ratings**: they are subjective and unreliable), hobbies only if relevant. Mistakes: decorative graphics, redundant icons, an Instagram profile on the resume.

On the photograph, the author notes that it is not a requirement: talent is not defined by race, gender or photogenic appeal. "Allow a reviewer to see your work before they see you."

**The design statement**: four to eight sentences in a professional portfolio, at the beginning. Three questions: What do you want to say as a designer? Where is your inspiration coming from? Where is your work headed? Do not use it to tell the firm what you expect from it.

**The cover letter**: addressed by name to the person reviewing applications (not "To whom it may concern"); never more than a page; it does not echo the resume but enhances it with examples and short stories. Four questions: Who are you and what is your current position? How are you aligned with the position or firm? What relevant projects showcase your skills? Why would you be a positive asset to them? The close restates what sets you apart, expresses your enthusiasm for the position and thanks the reader.

**Before sending:** proofreading (a single mistake can undermine all your hard work), a test send to a friend (files that open, resolution, colors), professionally named files, no Google Drive links; and rehearsing the portfolio presentation, without memorizing it, focused on the key points.

## Application

- Write the cover letter for each agency separately, starting from one of their campaigns that impressed you and from a project of yours that shows you can do something similar.
- Put your creative statement into 5 sentences and place it after the cover.

## Example

A weak opening: "I am writing to apply for the junior copywriter position." A good opening: "Mrs. Popescu, your campaign for X convinced me that an idea can make a dull category a topic of conversation; over the past year I tried the same thing for a local water brand, and I attach the project."
