# Portfolio stories are collected during projects, with an indexed work logbook

> **The idea in one sentence:** a portfolio assembled at the last minute sells you short, because you forget why the decisions were taken; "The best portfolios are living documents and regularly updated as you work", fed from a logbook kept along the way.

## Explanation

Fenn proposes a simple **indexed logbook** system: in a notebook, the first 15 pages stay blank (the index), and the work starts on page 16. For each project you record: the core team, the stakeholders, the goals (with success criteria), the constraints, the users, the activities, the deliverables. When you write something significant for your portfolio, you list it in the index with the page number. The back of the notebook holds quick templates and reminders.

Besides notes:
- **Photographs** of the process: the main activities, the "Aha!" moments, the collaboration, the workshops (the overall activity, a particular outcome, a close-up of a sticky note), with people's permission.
- **Screengrabs** of the implemented design and of the "before" state; exports or screencasts of prototypes before you lose access to the client's tools.
- **A consistent folder structure** per project (brief, content, design, research).
- **The end-of-project interview with the client**: what benefits they saw, what results, what you contributed, what surprised them, and a request for a LinkedIn recommendation.

For past projects, **the Stockwell Spreadsheet** (developed by consultant Amanda Stockwell, on the model of a content audit): a table with the kind of project, your role, the timeframe, the decision points and, most importantly, the results.

The secondary benefit is reflection: reread, the logbook shows patterns in your preferences and gaps in your skills, which can guide your next career choices.

Kubišová adds the discipline of files: organization by generic categories (chronology, type of material, software), descriptive labels that start with the date (for example "2024.10.17_Miami Mall.ElevationB.Line Drawing"), archiving at least once every semester (or every six months, for professionals) and separate backups. For physical work (models, packaging, installations) she asks for photographs taken with intent: a steady camera, two light sources (direct and diffuse), a neutral background, angles that explain the idea and a recognizable object that conveys scale.

## Application

- ADC student: open a logbook (or a Notion page) for each course project: the brief, the insight, the rejected ideas and why, the teacher's feedback, the final version.
- Photograph the idea wall and the sketches: a visible process is portfolio material.

## Example

A copywriter who noted at the time that "the client rejected route A because testing showed confusion among 40% of respondents" can write a credible case study a year later; one without notes can only write "we proposed several routes".
