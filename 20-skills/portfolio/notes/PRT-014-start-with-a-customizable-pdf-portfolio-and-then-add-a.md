# Start with a customizable PDF portfolio and then add a website; portfolio communities are risky

> **The idea in one sentence:** the PDF is the easiest to tailor for each application and to share internally, the website makes you publicly findable, and Behance/Dribbble or video as the only format introduce risks for the reader.

## Explanation

Fenn compares the platforms by the needs of the people reviewing them:

| Platform | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| PDF | preferred by many hiring managers; simple to navigate, to share internally, to print and annotate; **customizable** for each job application (you hide case studies, change the cover and the introduction); works without the Internet | reads poorly on a smartphone; video and audio are hard to add |
| Website | indexed by search engines, ongoing public promotion; an address that is easy to share; responsive | hard to tailor for each application; requires Internet access; you are judged on the experience the website offers; hosting costs money |
| Communities (Behance, Dribbble) | visibility | they favor eye candy; competitors are one click away; senior executives do not understand the platform |
| Video | novelty value | requires time and engagement; a 2013 study found that job seekers seen on video are rated as less likeable |

The recommendation: the PDF first, then a companion website with project summaries and an invitation to get in touch; when someone contacts you, you send them a customized PDF.

Practical tips for the PDF: export to actual PDF (not unusual formats); the same page orientation throughout; numbered pages, each case study on a new page; under 10 megabytes (ideally 5); your name in the filename; no password. For the website: no "Coming soon!" pages, links checked, a redirect when you change the address, no password.

The cautionary example: a recruiter impressed by a Dribbble profile realized that, by a wrong turn, he was looking at another designer's portfolio: good for the recruiter, bad for the candidate.

**Convergence and nuance (Kubišová).** For her too, a strong digital portfolio is "a baseline", and platforms like Behance bring exposure in a market where recruiters search actively, but they take away your control over the presentation and favor final results over process. She adds, however, an argument for the **physical portfolio** at the interview: the tactile experience is more memorable, it invites the viewer through every page, you have full control over how the work is seen and it facilitates conversation. Shared warnings: do not send Google Drive links, do not send PowerPoint files, compress the PDF without sacrificing quality.

## Application

- For ADC students: a 10–15 page PDF, tailored to each agency, plus a simple website with the main projects.
- For art directors, Behance can be an additional showcase (the creative industry uses it more than UX does), but it does not replace a PDF or a website of your own.

## Example

A student applies to two agencies: for the digital agency, the social campaign and the prototype go first; for the branding agency, the visual identity goes first. The same source file, two exported PDFs.
