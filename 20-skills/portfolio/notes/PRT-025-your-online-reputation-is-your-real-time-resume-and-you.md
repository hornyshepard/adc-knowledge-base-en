# Your online reputation is your real-time resume, and you build it on purpose

> **The idea in one sentence:** employers search for you on Google and on social networks before the interview, so everything you have published becomes a real-time resume; instead of hoping they find nothing bad, you deliberately build "the right digital dirt".

## Explanation

Morgan cites Jobvite's 2013 Social Recruiting Survey: 93% of recruiters are likely to look at a candidate's social profile. They use LinkedIn to assess professional experience and hard skills, and the other networks to evaluate cultural fit and industry-related posts. News mentioning you, your comments, reviews, presentations and updates make up your "real-time resume".

**Four types of updates** that keep your stream in balance:
1. showcase your expertise (self-promotion in measured doses: a case study, a campaign);
2. responsible and emotionally intelligent communication;
3. staying up to date on industry trends and news (news, events, a compliment to the presenter);
4. personality and style.

**What to avoid** (according to Jobvite): illegal drugs, sexual updates, profanity, spelling and grammar errors, guns, alcohol; arguments and negative comments, especially about current or past employers; extreme views on politics and religion. The argument "it's my private life" does not hold: what you have put out publicly is fair game for judgment.

**LinkedIn as a portfolio.** The profile should complement the resume, not copy it: a headline with the role you want and keywords, a professional photo (in an eye-tracking study, recruiters spent 19% of their time on the picture), a vanity URL, a summary in the first person, embedded media (presentations, samples, video), and recommendations requested with specific pointers. For students: courses, projects, honors and awards, organizations.

**The right "digital dirt":** you regularly Google yourself, collect screenshots and testimonials, and publish content that supports the brand you want to have.

## Application

- Search for your name on Google and on social networks; write down what a creative director would see in the first ten results and what you want them to see.
- For creatives, social networks are also a portfolio: a well-curated Instagram or TikTok account of personal projects can be worth as much as a case study.

## Example

A junior strategist who posts a short analysis of a local campaign every month and comments constructively on industry articles becomes "visible" to recruiters before applying anywhere.

## Questions

- Is it fair for employers to judge candidates by their personal life online?
- How do you handle an old, embarrassing digital trail (from when you were 16)?
