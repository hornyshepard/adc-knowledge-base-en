# The stories in a pitch are structured with SELL or SOLVE, and the hero is the client

> **The idea in one sentence:** cases and anecdotes are the presentation's "sub-plots"; SELL serves personal stories, SOLVE data-driven cases, and in both the audience (not you) has to be the hero.

## Explanation

Many people believe they cannot tell stories, yet we tell them all the time (even announcing that someone will never guess what happened this afternoon starts one). Colenso's advice: "Stop thinking fairytale. Start thinking news report." Stories are branches off the main narrative, with their own beginning, middle and end, and they bring the numbers to life.

| SELL (personal stories) | SOLVE (cases, business cases, data) |
|---|---|
| **Setting**: the scene, through the senses: what it looked, sounded, smelled like | **Situation**: the starting point, the need, why it mattered |
| **Event**: the climax, the crossroads or the realization | **Obstacle**: what got in the way, the constraints, the impact |
| **Link**: what you felt ("I felt…") or what those involved felt | **Logic**: what changed in the thinking: the approach, the tool, the insight, the decisions |
| **Learning**: the moral, the explicit "so what" | **Victory**: the moment the obstacle is overcome, and the knock-on effects |
| | **Evidence**: proof: data, quotes, testimonials; how it can be replicated |

Four rules: keep stories short (they are sub-plots, not the main event); focus on emotion (the logic comes easily to us anyway); "show, don't tell", with vivid detail; and do not make yourself the hero. Colenso cites Donald Miller's point (*Building a StoryBrand*): when the customer is the hero and you are the guide, you become a trusted resource. The rule also applies to internal stakeholders. Richards and McLean reach it by another route, reading the hero's journey as the buying cycle seen by the customer ([[STO-032-applied-to-content-the-hero-s-journey-becomes-the-buying|STO-032]]).

## Application

- Rewrite every case study in the agency's portfolio in SOLVE format, with an "Evidence" section with real figures.
- For a pitch's opening story, use SELL and keep it under a minute.

## Example

A SOLVE case study: Situation: a regional brand with falling sales; Obstacle: a small budget, an ordinary category; Logic: an insight from research and an activation idea; Victory: the campaign becomes a national talking point; Evidence: sales, awards, a quote from the client.

## Questions

- Does SOLVE differ from the classic awards case study structure (challenge–idea–execution–results)?
- How personal can the "Link" be in a corporate pitch?
