# A strong logo is sold with the designer leading, not with the client dictating

> **The idea in one sentence:** "The secret to producing great work is your ability to sell great work"; the designer leads the presentation as the expert, structures the feedback and translates the client's requests into the real problem behind them.

## Explanation

Early in his career, Allan Peters worked at agencies where the culture was to fear the client: everything the client asked for was done, and the work suffered. When the client takes control, they take on the role of the creative director; Peters believes everyone should stay in their own role, the client as the expert in marketing, the designer as the expert in art.

**Presentation techniques:**
- Present live rather than sending a PDF by email, so you can answer questions and defend the work on the spot.
- State the goal of the meeting at the start (picking three marks).
- Place your own top three strategically in the deck.
- Ask the client to say what they like, not what they dislike.
- In large rooms, present all 15 options without client commentary, then ask each person to write down their top three quietly on paper; go through the votes person by person, starting the reveal with your own top three. That way the person in charge does not dominate the discussion, and people lower in the hierarchy give their opinion.

**Reframing feedback.** "Make the logo bigger" usually means the branding in the layout is not strong enough: a bigger logo would make things worse. At Kwikbit, the client asked for italic typography for "speed"; Peters found out that the real wish was for the name to feel fast and solved it with diagonal cuts and triangular dots on the "i"s, keeping the high-tech look. The request to combine two sketches ("Frankensteining Ideas") became a successful blend of the essence of one with the outer shape of the other.

Peters ties all of this to a personal lesson, in the section "Fearless": most bad design comes from fear-based decisions. The creative worries about the client, the client about the boss, the boss about the customer.

## Application

- Practice presenting each option in two or three sentences: the idea, the combined brand nouns, why it works.
- When you get execution feedback ("bigger", "a different font"), ask "what do you want people to feel?" and solve the cause.
- Use the silent vote in any meeting with many decision makers.

## Example

At FM (Featherstone Media), Peters put the winning mark on the last slide; the client pointed at it immediately. The time saved on revisions went into brand patterns and mock-ups for different media.

## Questions

- Where is the line between "leading the client" and creative arrogance?
- Does the silent vote still work when the paying client wants to decide alone?
