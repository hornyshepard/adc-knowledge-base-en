# A presentation's intention answers four questions: Know, Feel, Do and Why now

> **The idea in one sentence:** the audience acts only at the intersection of logic, emotion and timing, so every pitch has to know what it wants people to know, to feel, to do, and why now.

## Explanation

Colenso starts from the premise that every good presentation aims to produce an action; if it is information for information's sake, you might as well send an email. From his first book (*IMPACT*) he takes the three parts of a well-formed intention and adds a fourth, specific to the pitch:

| Intention | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| intellectual | What do I want my audience to *know*? | "The campaign raised awareness by 12 points." |
| emotional | What do I want them to *feel*? | reassured, excited, challenged |
| behavioral | What do I want them to *do*? | approve the budget for phase 2 |
| temporal | Why *now*? | the season starts in 8 weeks |

"Action is at the intersection of logic, emotion and timing": if any of them is missing, nothing happens. Human beings are designed for homeostasis (the status quo): change happens only when the benefits clearly outweigh the drawbacks of keeping things the same, so urgency has to be built explicitly.

The answers to the four questions form the "essence" of the presentation, a filter: anything you gathered in the Discover phase makes the cut only if it supports one of the answers. Every word and every visual should reinforce the essence.

## Application

- Write the four answers on a single page before any slide; give the page to your colleagues and ask them whether the final presentation respects it.
- For a creative concept presentation: Know = the idea; Feel = confidence and excitement; Do = approval of the production; Now = the launch window.

## Example

The formula resembles the classic models of advertising effect (cognitive-affective-behavioral), but it adds the time dimension explicitly, which Colenso considers decisive in selling. Fennis and Stroebe warn, however, that hierarchies of effects do not describe how people process ads ([[ADV-005-hierarchy-of-effects-models-aida-lavidge-steiner-dagmar|ADV-005]]); Know-Feel-Do stays useful as a checklist for planning a presentation, not as a theory of persuasion.

## Questions

- Does Know-Feel-Do resemble the hierarchy of effects in advertising (think-feel-do)? What does "Why now" add?
- Are there legitimate presentations without a "Do"?
