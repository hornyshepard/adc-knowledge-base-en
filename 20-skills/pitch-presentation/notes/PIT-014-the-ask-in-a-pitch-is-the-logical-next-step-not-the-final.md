# The ask in a pitch is the "logical next step", not the final "yes"

> **The idea in one sentence:** if you do not make what you are asking for explicit, you cannot expect a "yes"; and the most effective ask is not signing the contract but a small, concrete next step.

## Explanation

The behavioral intention (what I want the audience to *do*) is, in marketing language, a *call to action* (CTA). Colenso notes that most people avoid asking, for fear of coming across as pushy or "salesy". Yet the ask has to be stated in simple terms and placed "front and centre", not buried.

The distinctive idea: instead of a broad ask ("to get them to say yes", "sign the contract"), think in terms of the **"logical next step"**: another meeting, an introduction to the real decision-maker, approval of a pilot stage. Breaking the intention down into bite-sized chunks forces you to be specific, and specificity helps people act. Even a "project update" has an ask: for example, that your boss trusts you to keep going without further intervention.

The natural home of the ask is at the end, but the author prefers a dual approach: you set it out early (which lets you handle objections upfront) and then reinforce it subtly along the way. A clear ask also reduces the "off topic" questions that can derail the presentation.

## Application

- The author's exercise: write the ask in fewer than 30 words, then reduce it to fewer than 15 with the structure "I want my audience to…" (e.g. "…agree to a meeting next week", "…appoint our firm as their advisor").
- In a creative pitch, the logical next step can be "approval of two routes for testing", not "buying the campaign".

## Example

A sales pitch that asks directly for a $1 million budget is built differently from one that asks for a follow-up call with the finance director; the second often has a better chance of moving the relationship forward.

## Questions

- When is a step that is too small a mistake (the client was ready to decide)?
- How do you announce the ask at the start without seeming pushy?
