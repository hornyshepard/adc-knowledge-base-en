# The middle of a presentation is organized in three parts: POP, PPF and ten more formulas

> **The idea in one sentence:** the 70% in the middle has to be divided into three logical "buckets"; for persuasion use Problems–Options–Proposal, for a story Past–Present–Future.

## Explanation

Colenso calls the three-part structure a **liberating constraint**: limiting your options sharpens your thinking, like the invisible rules behind theatrical improvisation. It gives a logical flow and flexibility (the parts do not have to be equally weighted), and it avoids the "27 examples" that overwhelm the audience. If the audience has already dictated a structure ("progress, threats and opportunities"; "three experiences that have shaped your career"), you follow it.

**POP: Problems, Options, Proposal** (a persuasive argument). *Problems*: do not shy away from pain; if the audience identifies with what is wrong and with the cost of inaction, they listen to the solutions. *Options*: if you move too quickly to a single solution, people become suspicious; you lay out a few approaches (including attempted solutions that failed), but not too many. *Proposal*: the recommendation, with evidence; this is where you sell the benefits.

**PPF: Past, Present, Future** (a narrative). *Past*: how things used to be; the story needs contrast between the "bad old days" and the "brave new world". *Present*: today's reality, engaging the senses, not only facts. *Future*: the vision: what will happen in two weeks or two years if the audience acts.

Appendix A adds ten formulas for specific scenarios:

| Formula | Scenario |
|---|---|
| FIT: Frustration, Improvement, Transformation | sales pitch |
| FAB: Features, Advantages, Benefits | product demo |
| ACE: Accomplishments, Challenges, Expectations | team update |
| PRO: Progress, Roadblocks, Outlook | project review |
| ROI: Risk, Opportunity, Impact | business case |
| AIM: Aspiration, Impact, Motivation | town hall |
| SET: Scope, Execution, Timeline | project kick off |
| MAP: Mission, Action, Progress | strategy presentation |
| GRO: Goals, Reflections, Opportunities | performance review |
| LED: Learn, Explore, Do | educational presentation |

## Application

- In an agency pitch, POP maps naturally onto business problem → strategic / creative routes → the agency's recommendation.
- Collect your own three-part structures; the author encourages you to build a repertoire.

## Example

A campaign case study in PPF: the category before (an invisible brand), the situation now (insight, idea), the future (what the client will gain by extending the campaign).

## Questions

- When is POP better than PPF for presenting a creative idea?
- Is "Options" a trap in agencies, where the client may pick the weak route?
