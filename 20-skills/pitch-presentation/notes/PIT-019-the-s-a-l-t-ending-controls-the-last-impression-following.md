# The S.A.L.T. ending controls the last impression, following the peak-end rule

> **The idea in one sentence:** because we remember an experience by its emotional peak and its end, the close cannot be improvised: summarize, ask, link back to the beginning and thank.

## Explanation

Colenso starts from the peak-end rule described by Daniel Kahneman: the memory of an experience is dictated by its emotional peak and its final moments. You can be brilliant for 29 minutes and lose your impact in the last one. Three common mistakes:

1. **Falling off a cliff**: the presentation comes to an abrupt end after the last topic, with no synthesis.
2. **The fade to black**: energy and volume drop, the "umms" and "errs" appear, and everything fizzles out like a pop song with no ending.
3. **Death by Q&A**: you conclude nicely, then hand control to the audience, and the last thing people remember is an off-message question.

The fix for the third: invite questions throughout. If the organizer insists on Q&A at the end, you deliver your conclusion, take the questions and then redeliver a "micro version of the conclusion", so that you have the last word.

The **S.A.L.T.** formula (about 10% of the time):

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| **S**ummarize | restate the 3–5 key messages; "there are no prizes for subtlety" |
| **A**sk | spell out the ask in its simplest terms: if you would love to work with the client, say so |
| **L**ink | close the loop: return to the story, image or idea from the beginning (like the hero who returns home changed) |
| **T**hank | thank the audience; it is courtesy, it creates a bond and it clearly marks the end (sometimes it triggers the applause) |

## Application

- Rehearse the ending more times than the middle; in rehearsals, do not let the opening eat into the ending's time.
- Plan in the opening the "loop" you will close at the end.

## Example

A pitch that opens with a photo of a shelf where the client's product cannot be seen can end with the same shelf, remade with the agency's idea, then the ask ("We want to build this with you from March") and the thank you.

## Questions

- How do you apply S.A.L.T. when the client ends the meeting abruptly ("we've got it, thank you")?
- Is the peak-end rule an argument for putting the strongest idea at the end rather than at the start?
