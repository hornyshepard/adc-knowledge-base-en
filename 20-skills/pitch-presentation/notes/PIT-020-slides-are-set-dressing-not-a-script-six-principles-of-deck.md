# Slides are set dressing, not a script: six principles of deck design

> **The idea in one sentence:** slides exist so that the audience can understand and retain, not so that you can read; a good deck has one idea per slide, passes the mobile phone test and differs from the document left with the client.

## Explanation

First, Colenso reminds you that slides are not compulsory: a well-rehearsed presentation with no support is impressive; a prop (Bill Gates released mosquitoes into the room in his TED talk on malaria) can make the message "sticky"; a printed, double-sided A3 "placemat" can replace the deck in a pitch. If you do use slides, here are the principles:

| # | Principle | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your slides are not your script | they are "set dressing"; you read from the screen only a quote; if the tech fails, you must still be able to deliver |
| 2 | We don't know your data as well as you do | "As you can see from the slide" is banned; build graphs up step by step: axis, key, then line by line |
| 3 | If you include it, include it deliberately | a visual either adds or subtracts; there is no neutral; no apologizing that the slide can't be read, no skipped slides |
| 4 | Less is not always more | the "no more than ten slides" myth leads to crammed slides; one idea per slide, as many slides as you need |
| 5 | It needs to pass the mobile phone test | if someone follows you on a phone, in portrait, walking down the street, can they read the slide? People who read do not listen |
| 6 | Your presentation deck might not be their takeaway deck | you make the detailed document first, then delete: one graph per slide, no text to read; you can add "stickers" that point to the full document |

For the details, the author refers to Garr Reynolds (*Presentation Zen Design*) and Lee Jackson (*Get Good at Slides*), from whom he takes the idea that bullet points kill presentations.

## Application

- The author's flow test: reading only the slide titles, does the narrative make sense? Does each slide have a "so what?"
- For a campaign presentation, separate the pitch deck (big images, one message per screen) from the strategy document sent afterwards.

## Example

Colenso used 67 slides in a 45-minute slot (he finished at 44:32), roughly one and a half slides a minute, each illustrating a single idea.

## Questions

- How do the principles apply to a creative deck, where the image is the product itself?
- Is the takeaway deck a risk (the idea travels without you) or a necessity?
