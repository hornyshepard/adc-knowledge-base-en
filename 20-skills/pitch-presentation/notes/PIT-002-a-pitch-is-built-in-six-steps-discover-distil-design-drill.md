# A pitch is built in six steps: Discover, Distil, Design, Drill, Deliver, Debrief

> **The idea in one sentence:** a successful pitch follows a repeatable six-stage process, and most failures come from skipping the thinking stages and going straight to the slides.

## Explanation

For Colenso, a pitch and a presentation are the same thing: both try to get the listeners to buy or buy-in to an idea. The method of "the six Ds" carries the theatrical process (research, rehearsals, performance, analysis) over into business:

| Step | What you do | The key question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Discover | you look at the big picture: the question, the audience, the objective, the team, your uniqueness, the competition | What is being asked of me, and whom am I speaking to? |
| 2 Distil | you choose what stays: headline, key messages, Know-Feel-Do-Now, evidence, structure | What does this audience need to hear, now? |
| 3 Design | you build the narrative and the visuals | How do I package it all up? |
| 4 Drill | you rehearse, prepare the Q&A and backup plans | Where are the gaps? |
| 5 Deliver | you manage nerves, energy, technique, engagement, objections | How do I deliver on the day? |
| 6 Debrief | you analyze, ask for feedback, keep what worked | What do I take with me next time? |

The process is a loop: the lessons from the Debrief go into the Discover of the next pitch. The author insists that "in order to go fast we must first go slow": with practice, the steps become automatic. The typical mistake is to open a new file (or copy an old deck) and start with the slides. He recommends staying first in divergent thinking (sticky notes, brainstorming) and only then structuring ("go big to go small").

## Application

- In an agency pitch, plan backward from the presentation date and set aside time for each D, including rehearsals and the debrief.
- Do not open PowerPoint until you have gone through Discover and Distil.

## Example

In the theater, the author says, the most successful shows started with research and discussion, not with the actors jumping straight to their feet; in the same way, the teams that win pitches invest time in research at the start.

## Questions

- In a pitch with a 48-hour deadline, which D gets compressed and which one cannot be skipped?
- Is the Debrief "the most important" step, as the author claims?
