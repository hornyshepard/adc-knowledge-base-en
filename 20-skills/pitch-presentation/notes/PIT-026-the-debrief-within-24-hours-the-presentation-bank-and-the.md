# The debrief within 24 hours, the presentation bank and the learning log turn every pitch into progress

> **The idea in one sentence:** the difference between the amateur and the pro is the review afterwards: a session booked within 24 hours, feedback requested in advance, and a "bank" of the elements that worked.

## Explanation

Top athletes treat video analysis as non-negotiable; in business, people breathe a sigh of relief and forget. Colenso proposes:

**The wash-up session.** You book it when you book the rehearsals, within 24 hours at most (after that, memories fade). One advantage: you usually do not yet know the outcome of the pitch, so the analysis is not blurred by winning or losing. If you have a recording, note your first impressions before watching it: on video you become hypercritical.

**Three review tools:**

| Tool | Questions |
|---|---|
| WWWEBI | *What went well? Even better if?* |
| What? So what? Now what? (Rolfe, Freshwater, Jasper) | what was delivered → what mattered → what you change |
| SWOT | what resonated; what did not go smoothly; what areas for follow-up; what objections and what lessons from the competition |

The review covers the whole process, not just the delivery: did you research the audience enough (Discover)? was the headline clear (Distil)? did the opening work (Design)? did you practise the transitions (Drill)? did you handle the objections (Deliver)? The principle from NLP: "there is no failure, only feedback".

**Feedback.** Ask for it *before* the presentation, so that the person watches carefully; choose relevant people (including a sceptic); be specific (structure? visuals? stories? rapport?) and split the aspects among several observers; do not justify yourself, ask open questions, thank them, then decide what you take on board.

**The presentation bank and the learning log.** *Presentation bank*: a document with reusable script elements (introductions, stories, case studies, labeled explanations) and a *Master Deck* with the slides that worked. *Learning log*: the lessons from each presentation and a rolling action plan that you tick off.

## Application

- After every agency pitch, 30 minutes of WWWEBI with the team, whatever the outcome; also note what the client says in the formal feedback.
- For students: a learning log throughout the year, with one working goal per presentation.

## Example

An agency that loses a pitch and learns at the debrief that its opening took 12 of 30 minutes writes "opening under 5 minutes" in its learning log and checks the rule at the next pitch.

- How do you get real feedback from a client who has just chosen another agency?
- Is booking the debrief before the pitch a strength or a sign of pessimism?
