# Present from frameworks and key words, not from a memorized script

> **The idea in one sentence:** a full script makes you either read or fall apart at the first slip; it is safer to work with a scaffolding structure and strip the text back gradually to key words.

## Explanation

As a former actor, Colenso knows how hard it is to learn lines and speak them "as if for the first time"; it is a skill business people do not have and should not need. Fully scripted presentations carry two risks: if you have not learned them, you read and lose contact with the audience; if you have, an unexpected question or a couple of missed words breaks your flow. The pandemic made the problem worse: people got used to having their notes on the screen.

The author's solution:

1. Structure the big picture first, with colored sticky notes, not in PowerPoint.
2. Use frameworks as invisible scaffolding: the same opening (S.T.A.R.), a three-part middle, the same ending (S.A.L.T.). Everything fits into a "presentation on a page", which can even be your only support if you are short of time.
3. If you like to write everything out, do so, but then strip it back in rehearsal: key sentences → phrases → key words.
4. Then comes the day you put down the script: instead of the text, you lean on your emotional intentions and key messages; the words vary slightly from one run to the next, but they converge. We speak differently from how we write, so the result sounds authentic.

A checking tool: the **speed run**: you go quickly through each slide or section, saying only "for this section I want the audience to know… / to feel…".

Nishad proposes a minimalist version, the "ethical" cheat sheet: a single sheet, in large font, with only two or three words for each message and a couple for the example or story that goes with it, no full sentences. A single sheet cannot be shuffled or dropped on the floor; full sentences pull you back into reading. He goes further than Colenso: reading text off a slide implicitly tells the audience you think they cannot read for themselves.

## Application

- Fill in an A4 page: the exam question, the audience, the goal, Know–Feel–Do–Now, the three messages, S.T.A.R., the 1.2.3. middle, S.A.L.T.
- Legitimate exceptions to the rule: exact quotes, texts that must be spoken word for word for compliance purposes, speeches published in advance.

## Example

A creative presenting a concept does not learn the text by heart but remembers the order: insight → idea → three executions → why it works; the rest comes from conviction.

## Questions

- For a speaker using a foreign language, or a very anxious one, isn't the script a necessary safety net?
- How do you combine improvisation with a team pitch where the transitions are fixed?
