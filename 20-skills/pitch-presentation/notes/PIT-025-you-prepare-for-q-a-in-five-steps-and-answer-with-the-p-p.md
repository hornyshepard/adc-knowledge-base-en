# You prepare for Q&A in five steps and answer with the P + + – P formula

> **The idea in one sentence:** you rehearse the questions in advance, starting from a list of the ones you dread most, and in the room you listen calmly, paraphrase, answer briefly and get back to your message.

## Explanation

Colenso proposes five steps, practised in the public rehearsals:

1. **Planning and preparation.** A list of all the likely questions, plus a *worst-case list*: the hardest questions, the topics most likely to drag the conversation off track, the areas you are least confident about. Ask people you trust to add to it. Prepare data and appendix slides; in a team, decide who answers what.
2. **State choice and relaxation.** A question is not an interrogation: the questioner's "highest intention" is usually to understand something from their own perspective. Breathe; pausing before you answer increases your gravitas, while rushing makes you look defensive.
3. **Repeat or paraphrase.** Say back what you understand they are asking: you gain precision and time. If you suspect a deeper agenda, dig: "Is there anything else that you're concerned about?"
4. **Answer succinctly.** Don't bluff: say you don't have the figure to hand and will get back to them by Thursday. Don't over-answer, and don't add an unsolicited build to a colleague's reply. The **P + + – P** formula: your position; a first reason; a second reason; the acknowledged counter argument ("others prefer X"); your position restated. It is an assertive way of responding when there are differing opinions in the audience. The psychology of persuasion gives it a basis: a two-sided message that acknowledges a plausible counterargument makes the position more credible (Fennis and Stroebe, [[CPY-016-a-two-sided-message-gains-credibility-if-it-admits-a-minor|CPY-016]]).
5. **Get back on message.** Once the question is settled, do not put the ball back in the audience's court ("any other questions?"); link the answer to one of your key messages, again because of the peak-end rule.

## Application

- In the public rehearsal, ask someone to interrupt you at random and play an aggressive client; better to be caught off guard there than in the pitch.
- Prepare an appendix with the slides you hope not to show.

## Example

Question: "Why not TV?" A P + + – P answer: "We recommend social and OOH; your under-30 audience watches little linear TV, and this way the budget covers the frequency we need; we understand that TV gives scale and we can test it in phase 2, but for the launch we are staying with social and OOH."
