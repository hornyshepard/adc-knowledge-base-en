# Saying why you are the right person is an act of generosity towards the audience

> **The idea in one sentence:** people buy people; if you do not explicitly connect your experience to the audience's need, they will not join the dots themselves, and doing it for them is not boasting but a service.

## Explanation

"People buy people": a winning pitch makes you memorable, so you have to be able to say what makes you or your team unique. Colenso admits he found this uncomfortable (the fear of being seen as bragging), but notes that if you do not know why people should listen to you, the audience will not work it out. The trick is connecting your story to their need, as specifically as possible: if someone else could give the same reason, you do not have cut-through.

The difference between generic and specific, in the author's example: saying you have spent the last five years working in the industry (forgotten at once) versus saying you have spent five years with clients very similar to them, that six months ago one of them faced the same challenge they face, and that you helped them through it (compelling). Often the decisive piece of information is hiding in plain sight.

Uniqueness does not sit only in the introduction; it is reinforced subtly throughout. The test: if you asked the audience at the end what makes you different, would they all give the same answer?

In the S.T.A.R. opening, the "Authority" step comes only after the surprise and the takeaways, when the audience is open to finding out who you are. Recommendations: steer clear of job titles (they mean nothing outside your own organization) and say how what you do is relevant to them; you can "borrow" credibility (thanking Ali for asking you to lead on the topic). For imposter syndrome: if you are even one page ahead of everyone else in the textbook, you have something to offer.

The reframe: stating your value clearly puts the listeners' minds at ease. They stop worrying that they are in the wrong room and can start listening.

## Application

- Write three "why me / why us" sentences for the specific client, each with a verifiable fact (a project, a result, a similar client).
- In an interview or a portfolio presentation, replace "I'm passionate about branding" with a concrete project that answers the employer's need.

## Example

A junior presenting to a CMO can say: "I worked on the social media launches of the last three products in your category; let me show you what I learned about the under-25 audience."
