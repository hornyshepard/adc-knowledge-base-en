# Nerves are energy: reframe them, break them into "quick wins" and calm them with your breath

> **The idea in one sentence:** the symptoms of nerves and of excitement are practically identical; you do not have to get rid of your nerves but use their energy, lower the stakes and control your breathing before the first sentence.

## Explanation

Colenso, himself a very nervous performer when he arrived at drama school, says the symptoms never fully go away even for top speakers; what changes is the attitude towards them. Increased heart rate, sweating, a mind focused on future events: without your label, a doctor could not tell fear from exhilaration. The tools:

- **Reframing.** Your audience wants you to succeed: nobody walks into a meeting hoping it will be a complete waste of their time. Lower the stakes: it is rarely life or death, and the audience usually notices mistakes only if you point them out or apologize.
- **"Quick wins".** Instead of delaying gratification until the end, set yourself micro goals that are easy to achieve: eye contact as you shake hands, getting up the steps onto the stage without falling over, delivering the S.T.A.R. opening. Each success gives a hit of dopamine.
- **Move the spotlight.** If you focus on the audience's needs, you have no time to wonder what they think of you.
- **"Stop before you start".** Adrenaline works like an accelerator; compelling speakers appear to have time. Pause for a deep breath before the first word.
- **Breathing.** Under stress, breathing becomes shallow and high in the chest. *Diaphragmatic breathing*: both feet on the ground, abdominal muscles relaxed; notice your natural calm pattern and replicate it in the five to ten minutes before the presentation. *Box breathing* (used by U.S. Navy SEALs): inhale for four, hold for four, exhale for four, hold for four, at least six rounds; a quick reset.

The author draws on James Nestor (*Breath*): breathing is an autonomic function we can consciously control, and slowed down, it relaxes us.

## Application

- Before a pitch, write down three "quick wins" for the first five minutes.
- Practise diaphragmatic breathing every day, so that you have it to hand when it counts.

## Example

At conferences, the author spends the ten minutes before he speaks focused on his breath, even while sitting next to the CEO and listening to the other presenters, then pauses for one final breath before he opens his mouth.
