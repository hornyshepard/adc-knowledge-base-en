# An audience communicates from four energy centers: head, heart, gut and feet

> **The idea in one sentence:** every person in the audience has a dominant "energy" (logic, compassion, creation or action), and an effective presenter flexes their style to speak the language of each one.

## Explanation

Colenso built the **In Flow Energy Centres** model from Yat Malmgren's Movement Psychology class (a former soloist dancer who worked with the psychologist William Carpenter, on a Jungian basis) and tested it with thousands of delegates. People use all four energies, but usually one dominates:

| Center | The energy | The attitude | What they ask for / how you approach them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Head | logic | "Show me the detail." | numbers, process, other options looked at; a precise structure, an appendix of data, material sent in advance (they are reflectors) |
| Heart | compassion | "Show me the impact." | the purpose, the effect on people, consensus; ask them questions and listen, do not lecture them |
| Gut | creation | "Show me the options." | story, energy, possibilities; set ground rules, otherwise they hijack the conversation |
| Feet | action | "Show me the outcome." | ROI, timeline, cost; get to the point quickly, a clear plan, a decision |

You recognize the center from the type of questions and from the language ("The evidence suggests that…" vs. "It feels like…" vs. "There are several possibilities…" vs. "Our recommendation is…"). The key is flexibility: if you prepared a "feet" pitch but get questions about the detail, switch to "head". When you do not know who is in the room, plan something for everyone: actionable takeaways, well-researched data, human connection, story and energy.

## Application

- Make an "audience map": a sheet divided into four quadrants; write each participant's name in their dominant quadrant and notice which quadrant you default to yourself.
- Use the map when choosing the pitch team too: a storyteller (gut), a detail person (head), a direct one (feet), an empathetic one (heart).

## Example

A "head" finance director gets a document with the numbers before the meeting; a "gut" CMO gets the story of the campaign and a picture of success, with the details only on request.

## Questions

- How does the model differ from other typologies (DISC, MBTI)? Is it validated, or is it a heuristic?
- The risk of the "boxes": how do you avoid mislabeling a client after a single meeting?
