# Cut-through happens at the intersection of emotion, simplicity and energy

> **The idea in one sentence:** a presentation manages to capture attention and influence people (cut-through) only when it combines emotion, simplicity and the speaker's energy; none of them is enough on its own.

## Explanation

Dominic Colenso starts from the idea that we live in an "AHA world" (AI focused, Hyperconnected and Always-On): audiences are bombarded with messages, and a 2023 Microsoft survey (31,000 people) shows that 68% did not have enough uninterrupted focus time during the workday. A well-made deck, or even a good story, is no longer enough. The author takes the Cambridge English Dictionary definition, in which cut-through is "success in getting people's attention and influencing them", and says that this is exactly the aim of any pitch.

| Ingredient | Role in the presentation |
|---|---|
| Emotion | "the currency of attention"; if the audience leaves in a different emotional state from the one they came in with, you have earned the right to be remembered |
| Simplicity | complex ideas delivered in a way that is easy to understand; the audience wants the high-level concept first, the nuance comes later |
| Energy | you must be seen to believe in what you are saying, otherwise you cannot expect others to |

The intersection of the three is the sweet spot. Colenso warns that cut-through is not clickbait: without substance, attention moves on to the next shiny thing. Cut-through is only the Trojan Horse that opens the door to the content. He also sees an opportunity in the wave of AI-generated text: in a sea of sameness, a distinct human voice stands out.

## Application

- Check any presentation on the three axes: what will they *feel*? Can it be said in a few words? How will I deliver it with conviction?
- In an agency pitch, do not rely only on the quality of the idea: you also need a simple message and a delivery with energy.

## Example

The author's first presentation (at nine, about France) was boring, slides on an overhead projector read out verbatim, until he pulled out a box of Roquefort. Thirty-five years on, his classmates still remember it: surprise and emotion created cut-through.

## Questions

- Can a very technical presentation (a media plan) have emotion without losing credibility?
- Where is the line between energy and manipulation, when substance is missing?
