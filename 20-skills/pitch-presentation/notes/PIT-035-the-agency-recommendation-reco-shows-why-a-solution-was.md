# The agency recommendation (reco) shows why a solution was chosen, not the winding path to it

> **The idea in one sentence:** a good recommendation shows that the agency understood the request and offers the client something more ambitious than the client could have imagined, with a clear stance (*parti pris*) backed by a few rational arguments.

## Explanation

In the French system, the **agency recommendation** (*recommandation d'agence*, or *reco*) is the document that serves as the basis of the presentation to the client, in a competition or on an existing account. *La boîte à outils de la publicité* gives it a 14-step outline:

1. presentation of the agency and of the team's strengths;
2. a reminder of the context and of the client's request;
3. presentation of the market watch (*veille*) and contextualization of the problem;
4. the SWOT;
5. the avenues chosen for the rest of the strategic approach;
6. the communication problem (*problématique de communication*);
7. positioning;
8. objectives;
9. targets;
10. creative strategy;
11. the creative work;
12. the 360° plan (*dispositif 360°*) and the choice of media;
13. calendar;
14. budget.

The quality criteria are clear. A **good reco** is realistic in understanding the request and ambitious in its answer, surprising, with a solution that breaks with habit: "C'est vraiment cela la plus-value d'une agence." [That is really an agency's added value.] A **bad reco** makes no choices, has no stance, is weak or egocentric (the kind that says, in effect, look how well we thought this through). The tone adapts to the client: to a new client you show the agency's experience and successful campaigns, to a long-standing one you show what has changed.

## Application

- Cut the thinking process from the deck: keep only the elements that explain why you arrived at the solution.
- Back the intuition with a few key figures; intuition alone does not convince.
- Choose the angle of attack and stick to it. Do not corner the client to force their hand: if they do not want something, they will say no anyway.
- Check the thread: each step must follow from the one before (diagnosis → problem → positioning → objectives → creative work).

## Example

The order of the 14 steps in fact reproduces the chain of the strategic communication plan. That is why a recommendation in which the creative work does not follow from the problem stated in step 6 is immediately seen as incoherent.

## Questions

- Which of the 14 steps would you shorten in a 20-minute pitch? Which would you never remove?
- Is it always good to surprise the client? When is it wiser to confirm their expectations?
