# The speaker leads the room's energy at "level 8" and warms up body and voice like an instrument

> **The idea in one sentence:** the right energy is slightly above the audience's, "level 8" out of 10, and you get there through a physical and vocal warm-up, as athletes, musicians and actors do.

## Explanation

**The energy scale.** On a scale from 1 ("man-flu") to 10 (two bars of chocolate and a can of your favorite energy drink), Colenso places the performance sweet spot at 8: you lead the energy in the room without overpowering it. Level 8 is also practised in rehearsal; otherwise you have no muscle memory to fall back on. Most people are bad at calibrating themselves: what feels over the top to you often comes across as pitch perfect from the room, so ask for feedback and film yourself. On camera, the target is 8.5, because the camera sucks energy from your performance.

**Meet the audience where they are.** If you walk in at 8 into a room that is at 3, it can feel like a slap round the face. Go in a few points above them and lift the room gradually. You do not want the audience to reach your level (you would compete for the spotlight), but you do not want too big a gap either.

**Warming up the instrument.** Stress shows as tension: slumped shoulders, crossed arms, a tense face; millimetres with a big impact. The goal is an open, expansive posture that takes up space without dominating.

| Physical | Vocal |
|---|---|
| shoulder rolls, squeezing them up and dropping them with a sigh, a neck massage | humming on "mmmm" for resonance |
| shaking out each limb | pitch glides (a siren) from the lowest to the highest note |
| full body twists | face stretches (a "silent scream"), tongue exercises, tongue twisters for clarity |

Resonance carries emotion; clarity (the 43 muscles of the face) matters especially online, where only your face is seen, and for people who speak quickly or in a second language. The author recommends three prepared versions: 90 seconds, five minutes and 10–20 minutes, plus a pre-presentation checklist, as pilots use.

## Application

- Build your own warm-up routine and a checklist (tech + rituals) for in-person and online pitches.
- On entering: eye contact, a moment of stillness before you begin, a smile.

## Example

A 9 a.m. pitch with a tired client starts with a warm conversation at level 5 and rises towards 8 after the opening, not with a "Good morning!" at full volume.

## Questions

- How does an introvert adapt "level 8" without seeming fake?
- Does the right energy level differ between cultures (for example, a Nordic client vs. a Latin one)?
