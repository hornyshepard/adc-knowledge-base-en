# Is stage fright treated with calming techniques or by removing the unknown? Two approaches in tension

> **The idea in one sentence:** Colenso treats nerves as energy to be managed (reframing, breathing, visualization), whereas Nishad argues that nerves come from not knowing and disappear only when, through video, you know exactly how you come across: "the solution is not to meditate or visualise the audience in their underwear".

## Explanation

| | Colenso (*Cut-Through*) | Nishad (*The Art of Presentation*) |
|---|---|---|
| Cause of stage fright | a normal physiological reaction; the symptoms of fear and of excitement are identical | fear of the unknown: you do not know how you come across, whether what you say makes sense, whether you look stupid |
| Treatment | reframing (the audience wants you to succeed), lowering the stakes, "quick wins", diaphragmatic breathing and box breathing, visualizing success | filmed rehearsal until you like what you see; explicitly rejects meditation and picturing the audience in their underwear |
| Status of nerves | they never fully go away, even for professionals; what changes is your relationship with them | promises you will not be nervous at all if you follow the loop |
| Common ground | do not announce your nerves and do not apologize: the audience notices mistakes only if you point them out | the same rule: if you do not say "I'm nervous", nobody notices |

The tension is real, but the approaches are more complementary than opposed. Colenso works on your *state* on the day of the presentation (body, breath, attention moved onto the audience); Nishad works on the *confidence* built beforehand (competence checked on video). Colenso also includes filming rehearsals, while Nishad offers nothing for the moment when adrenaline rises before you walk into the room. Nishad's promise ("you won't be nervous any more") is stronger than the experience of most speakers would support, including Colenso's, a former professional actor.

## Application

- Combine them: the week before, the video loop (confidence in the content and in how you look); on the day, breathing, warm-up, "quick wins".
- For very anxious students, the video loop can be the first step, because it gives concrete evidence that "I look fine"; breathing techniques help with in-the-moment panic.

## Example

A student who trembles at their first project pitch can see on video that the trembling does not show (Nishad's approach) and then use box breathing in the five minutes before (Colenso's approach).

## Questions

- For whom might watching their own recording increase anxiety instead of reducing it?
- Which approach has more support in research on performance anxiety?
