# Rehearsing on video works when you correct a single flaw per round, until you like what you see

> **The idea in one sentence:** you film yourself giving the whole presentation, grade yourself fairly (what works and what does not), pick a single flaw to reduce, film again, and repeat the loop until you watch the recording and say "wow, I wish I was half as good as that guy".

## Explanation

Nishad's guide comes down, in essence, to a practice loop. The steps:

1. **Film the whole presentation**, with your phone or webcam, speaking as if to a real audience. Not in a mirror (you look at your hair and wrinkles, not at the message), and without criticizing yourself while you speak: presenting, researching and critiquing are separate activities, like writing and editing an essay.
2. **Grade yourself as a harsh but fair critic**: note precisely what you do well (passion, volume, speed, no "ums") and what you do not (boredom, a monotone voice, speaking too fast or too softly, eyes on your notes). If you find nothing good, ask a colleague to find it.
3. **Pick just one flaw** and redo the whole presentation, trying to do more of what worked and less of that one flaw. Ten corrections at once fluster you; that is not how any skill is learned.
4. **Repeat** until the final criterion, which is yours, not the teacher's: you watch the recording and admire yourself.

Do not waste rounds on what cannot be changed (the pitch of your voice, your accent): almost everyone hates their own voice the first time they hear it, and the author says he has met only one person with a genuinely irritating voice. Focus on what matters: do you look interested, comfortable, memorable, do you give good examples?

Colenso also recommends filming rehearsals, with a complementary warning: when you watch the recording you become hypercritical and see only the negatives, so note your first impressions beforehand.

## Application

- For a seminar presentation: at least three filmed rounds, each with a single goal written on paper.
- For the portfolio interview: film your presentation of three projects and apply the same loop.

## Example

The author tells of a billionaire CEO rehearsing a speech for a thousand sales reps: on video, he could be seen with his hands clasped, his head down and pacing nervously back and forth. After a few rounds, each focused on one flaw, the problems disappeared.
