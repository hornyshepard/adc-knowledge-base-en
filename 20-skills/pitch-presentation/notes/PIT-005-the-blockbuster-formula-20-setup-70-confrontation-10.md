# The blockbuster formula: 20% setup, 70% confrontation, 10% resolution

> **The idea in one sentence:** structure any presentation like a three-act blockbuster film and split the time roughly 20:70:10 between context, argument and conclusion.

## Explanation

Colenso distinguishes two types of film: **blockbuster** (a logical narrative, easy to follow, a wide audience) and **art-house** (an unconventional timeline, multiple plots, the audience works harder). In a pitch you want to be blockbuster 99% of the time: you do the hard work so the audience does not have to. The franchises (Bond, Harry Potter, Star Wars) show that audiences pay again and again for the same three-act structure (setup, confrontation, resolution), which the author links to Joseph Campbell.

| Act | In the film (Bond) | In the pitch | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup | starts with a car chase, then we learn the place, the villain, the mission | the context: the problem you are answering, who you are, what comes next | ~20% |
| Confrontation | trials, setbacks, steps toward the goal | the argument: options, pros and cons, data, stories, case studies | ~70% |
| Resolution | the villain is defeated, quickly | the verdict, the call to action, the next steps; in a formal pitch, you ask for the business | ~10% |

"You've never seen James Bond take his suit to the dry cleaners": the ending is quick. The ratio is invisible to the audience, but it makes the story easier to follow.

Rules for the calculation: first take off the Q&A time (even if you will take questions along the way), then apply the ratio. Example: 60 minutes with 10 minutes of Q&A → 50 minutes planned = 10 setup, 35 confrontation, 5 resolution. When the client (procurement) sets questions in a specific order, you follow them, but weave in a setup and a resolution at the beginning and at the end.

## Application

- Write the timings down and allocate the material you have gathered; you will almost always find that not everything fits, and then you cut.
- The acts are filled with: S.T.A.R. (setup), a 1.2.3. structure (middle), S.A.L.T. (ending).

## Example

For a 20-minute pitch: 4 minutes of context and insight, 14 minutes of strategy and creative idea with evidence, 2 minutes of recommendation and next steps.

## Questions

- When would an "art-house" structure be appropriate (a talk at a creative festival, a manifesto)?
- The ratio is a rule of thumb: what happens to a 3-minute pitch?
