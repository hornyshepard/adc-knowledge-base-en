# You visualize a pitch's goal, feel it, and only then say it in fewer than ten words

> **The idea in one sentence:** to know whether you have "hit the target", define the presentation's goal through an embodied sequence: see the moment of success, feel its emotion, then put it into 3–10 words.

## Explanation

At drama school, the question asked most often was "What do you want?", the character's motivation in a scene. Colenso applies it to the pitch: without a precise goal you cannot know whether you reached it, and typical goals are too vague ("for it to go well").

The method has three steps, in this order (you do not start with the wording):

1. **See it**: picture the moment just after success: how the audience reacts, whose faces you see, what is said, what happens concretely (a shake of hands? applause?). An "HD movie" in your mind.
2. **Feel it**: add the emotion (relief, excitement, contentment) and turn it up in your mind to level ten. The author draws on the idea that the brain has little ability to distinguish between what is real and what is imagined; athletes use visualization routinely (the kayak champion Anna Hemmings).
3. **Say it**: only now put it into words: three to five words ideally, fewer than ten always ("There's a reason that bumper stickers have cut-through!"). Say it aloud, check whether it sounds authentic, and write it on a sticky note that you keep with you while you work.

Specificity matters: is the real goal of the meeting the final "yes", or only the next meeting, or bringing a decision-maker into the conversation? If you want a follow-up call rather than sign-off on a $1 million budget, the whole presentation is built differently.

Nishad describes the same mistake from the opposite direction: most people start preparing by filling "a wheelbarrow" with facts found on the internet, without knowing what case they are making. The right order is the reverse: first write down the exact outcome you want (the vote, the donation, the job offer, the high grade), and only then ask what will motivate the listeners to produce it. The basic facts can be found on Wikipedia anyway; what the speaker adds is perspective and critical thinking.

## Application

- Before an agency pitch, each team member writes the goal in fewer than ten words; compare the wordings. If they diverge, the team is not aligned.
- Go back to the sticky note at every content decision.

## Example

"The client approves testing concept B" is a usable goal; "the client loves the campaign" is not.

## Questions

- Is visualization a clarity tool or a motivation ritual? Does the difference matter?
- What do you do when your goal and the client's "exam question" do not overlap?
