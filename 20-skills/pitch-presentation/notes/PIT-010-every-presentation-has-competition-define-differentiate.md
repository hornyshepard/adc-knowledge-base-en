# Every presentation has competition: Define, Differentiate, Destroy

> **The idea in one sentence:** because every presentation is an act of selling, there is always a rival for the audience's attention (another agency, another project, the phone), and preparing means defining it, differentiating yourself and neutralizing its pull.

## Explanation

Colenso cites Dan Pink's point (*To Sell is Human*) that although only 1 in 9 Americans works in sales, the other 8 in 9 also spend their days "moving others". Even a proposal to your boss is selling, so it has competition. In product pitches the competitors are obvious; in internal presentations the competition is more hidden: the other asks your boss gets that same week, the parallel session at the conference, the emails landing while you speak.

The three-column exercise:

| Define | Differentiate | Destroy |
|---|---|---|
| *who* and *what* is competing for the audience's attention; what the competitors will say about you, what they offer that you cannot, where you overlap | what you offer that they cannot; how your uniqueness builds into the narrative | all the information that neutralizes the competition's claims and attractiveness |

"Destroy" does not mean running others down. The author warns that explicitly talking down the competition makes you sound desperate or defensive. It means having at your fingertips the arguments that keep the audience focused on you: for example, prepared answers to the comparisons the client will make anyway.

## Application

- In an agency pitch, find out (or work out) who else is on the list and what their style is; anticipate the "safe" idea they will bring and build your differentiation around what they cannot offer.
- In an internal presentation, ask what other priorities your proposal competes with in your boss's budget.

## Example

If a competitor is known for big, expensive productions, the small agency does not attack it; it prepares evidence of efficiency and speed ("three campaigns launched in six weeks") that makes the difference obvious without naming the rival.

## Questions

- Where is the line between "neutralizing" and running others down in agency pitches?
- How do you differentiate yourself when the client explicitly asks every agency for the same things?
