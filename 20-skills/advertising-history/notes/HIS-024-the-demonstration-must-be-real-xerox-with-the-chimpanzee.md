# The demonstration must be real: Xerox with the chimpanzee versus Volvo and the monster truck

> **The idea in one sentence:** a demonstration is an ad's strongest argument only if it is true; when it is challenged, a real one can come out stronger, while a rigged one destroys the account and the reputation.

## Explanation

Richards presents, without comparing them explicitly, two cases that shed light on each other.

**Xerox (Papert Koenig Lois, 1960s).** Copying was seen as messy and complicated. George Lois filmed a little girl who visits her dad's office and makes a copy for him. The competitor, A. B. Dick, complained to the FTC that no kid could run such a machine, and the FTC ordered the spot stopped. Lois re-shot it with a chimpanzee, inviting FTC investigators to the shoot. The chimp succeeded, and both commercials were shown on a CBS news show. The free publicity was immense.

**Volvo (Scali, McCabe, Sloves, 1990).** For years Volvo had been promoted as strong and safe. The agency heard about a monster truck rally in Vermont where trucks had crushed car after car, but not a Volvo, and decided to re-create it. At the shoot, the truck crushed the Volvo as well. A second car, fitted with a heavy wooden cage, collapsed too. A third got a welded steel cage, and the roof supports of the other cars were cut. An extra in the rally crowd photographed everything. The ads did not disclose that this was a re-creation. The State of Texas sued Volvo, the company paid $316,250, and the agency resigned its oldest client.

Watkins shows the honest and monumental version: Ford's "The Longest Left Turn in History" (1957), a 50,000-mile endurance test at Bonneville, supervised around the clock by USAC and FIA officials. The ad told the story of the test like a piece of reporting.

## Application

- If the product does not pass the demonstration, change the demonstration or the idea, not the car.
- Any re-creation must be disclosed as a re-creation; the omission is as deceptive as a lie (see the FTC standard).
- Invite credible referees (officials, witnesses, public data); a challenge then becomes publicity.

## Example

In the Volvo case, all the evidence came from a single man with no role in the production and a camera. There are no secrets on a set.

## Questions

- Where is the line between "adjusting" a shoot (light, angle) and rigging the result?
- What does a real demonstration mean in the age of AI-generated images?
