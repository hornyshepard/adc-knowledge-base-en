# Subliminal advertising is a myth that survives the evidence

> **The idea in one sentence:** the idea that advertising manipulates us through messages below the threshold of awareness was born from an unproven claim in 1957 and survived for decades, although serious research found effects close to zero.

## Explanation

Richards (drawing on the study by Richards & Zakia, 1981) reconstructs the episode. In 1957, the same year as Vance Packard's best seller *The Hidden Persuaders* (advertising as psychological manipulation), the market researcher James Vicary announced that during a film he had flashed, too fast for conscious recognition, the messages "Drink Coca-Cola" and "Hungry? Eat Popcorn". He claimed Coke sales jumped 57.5 percent and popcorn sales 18.1 percent. The reaction was hysterical: an editorial suggested placing the invention at the center of the next nuclear test, congressmen submitted bills, Britain banned the technique and the FCC declared it contrary to the public interest. New Zealand concluded that it did not work.

The myth was reborn in 1973 with Wilson Bryan Key's *Subliminal Seduction*, another best seller about an industry engaged in mind control. In 1974 even the United Nations Human Rights Commission expressed concern. College textbooks added sections on subliminal advertising. Meanwhile, the British psychologist Norman Dixon published serious research in 1971 and 1981: subliminal effects are so small as to be almost nonexistent. His books got no noticeable public attention.

Richards's lesson: conspiracy theories about advertising sell better than evidence.

Two other sources in the library do not fully agree. Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott (SRC-049) go further: Vicary admitted he had made the study up, and subliminal processing cannot form attitudes. Fennis and Stroebe (SRC-012) allow a narrow effect: a brand shown below the threshold can increase choice only among people who already have the goal (they are thirsty), and even this effect does not always replicate. Even Vicary's numbers appear swapped from one book to another.

## Application

- When a client, a teacher or a journalist invokes "subliminal manipulation", ask for the source: it is usually Vicary or Key.
- The real power of advertising comes from repetition, association and relevance, mechanisms that are visible, not hidden.
- Ethics remains a legitimate question, but it concerns conscious influence (fear, social pressure), not invisible messages.

## Example

Richards observes that although the idea was "forgotten" after the 1957 scandal, it returns periodically in popular culture, and the advertising industry is still presented as a machine for mind control.

## Questions

- Why do we believe in hidden manipulation more readily than in open manipulation?
- Are behavioral targeting and recommendation algorithms the new "subliminal"?
