# Every new medium first refuses advertising, then lives on it, until the clutter breeds the technology to avoid it: radio, TV, internet

> **The idea in one sentence:** the history of 20th-century media repeats itself in three phases: the new medium declares that it will not be polluted by ads, then discovers it cannot live without them, then the audience invents a way to get around them.

## Explanation

Richards follows the cycle medium by medium:

| Phase | Radio and TV | Internet and social media |
|---|---|---|
| **Refusal** | Herbert Hoover found it inconceivable that radio should be drowned in advertising clatter. *Printers' Ink* and newspaper publishers attacked the idea, and the British considered radio commercials offensive. | Friendster and Twitter at first shunned advertising, and Facebook started with simple campus flyers. |
| **Adoption** | Stations without funding failed. WEAF (AT&T) sold airtime from 1922–1923, then sponsored programs appeared. | The first clickable web ad appeared in 1993, and the first banner on October 27, 1994: AT&T, "You will", clicked by 44 percent of visitors on HotWired. Then came AdSense (2003), YouTube brand channels (2006), Promoted Tweets (2010). |
| **Clutter and avoidance** | More ads call for more "share of voice", hence even more clutter. The remote control brings *zapping*, the home recorder *zipping*, then come ad-free subscriptions (HBO, Netflix). | The pop-up (1997) was created so as not to associate the brand with the page, and its author later publicly apologized. AdBlock (2002) is "the internet equivalent of a remote control". |

Richards's conclusion: the internet is not a new medium but a collection of new media, just as Gutenberg's press turned print into newspapers, magazines and handbills. In 2018, global internet ad spending had reached $404 billion, almost three times television's.

## Application

- When a new platform appears, ask which phase it is in: is it still looking for its money model, or is it already cluttered?
- A format that interrupts and annoys breeds the tool that blocks it. Design ads that people do not want to avoid.
- "Native" formats (Promoted Tweets, the sponsored post) are each medium's answer to avoidance; follow the disclosure rules.

## Example

The 1994 AT&T banner cost $30,000 for three months and was clicked by 44 percent of visitors. Compared with the average click-through rate of today's banners, under 1% according to Schwarz-Musch and colleagues (SRC-032), it shows how quickly the novelty of a format wears off.

## Questions

- Which phase are podcasts, messaging apps or AI assistants in today?
- Can a medium stay ad-free and still be free?
