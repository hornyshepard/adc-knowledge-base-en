# Advertising is any promotional communication, which is why its history starts long before print

> **The idea in one sentence:** if you define advertising broadly, as any communication with the intent or effect of promoting something, you find that its basic techniques (the sign, the promise, the offer, the sample) are thousands of years old.

## Explanation

Jef Richards starts from the fact that there is no agreed-upon definition. The academic definition he himself proposed (Richards & Curran, 2002) sets four conditions: a **paid**, **mediated** form of communication, from an **identifiable source**, designed to persuade the receiver to take some action. For a history, the author says, such a definition is too narrow and goes out of date as media change. He adopts a broad one: any communication that promotes a product, service, idea or entity. The consequences: an ad can exist without a medium (a man shouting "Fine fresh fish!"), without intent (Picasso's signature promotes the painting) and can even be negative (a spokesperson who gets into trouble).

Through this lens, the history begins with owners' marks, the trade seals of the Indus Valley, the pharaohs' cartouches and the Greek vase from around 520 BCE that urges "Buy me and you'll get a bargain", a very early example of point-of-purchase advertising. This is also where the simple definition of the brand comes from: a brand is whatever consumers think it is, built in large part by advertising.

The second gain is methodological. Richards checks claims repeated by other histories and shows that some are myths: the papyrus about the runaway slave Shem, cited as "the first ad", does not seem ever to have been in the British Museum's collection.

## Application

- When you analyze a marketing "innovation", ask: what old form does it repackage? (the sample, the crier, the emblem).
- Use the narrow definition when you discuss media strategy and budget; use the broad one when you are looking for ideas: any contact surface can communicate.
- Check historical "facts" before you put them in a pitch; a false quote undermines credibility.

## Example

Medieval innkeepers who offered "A taste for free!" (the poem *Piers Plowman*, 14th century) were practicing sampling, the same technique supermarkets use today.

## Questions

- Is a monument (the pyramids, Machu Picchu) an ad from the moment it is built or from the moment it becomes the symbol of a country?
- What do you lose if you call PR, packaging and family names all "advertising"?
