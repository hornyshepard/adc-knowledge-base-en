# Advertising awards follow two philosophies: aesthetics (ADC, Cannes, One Show) and effectiveness (Effie)

> **The idea in one sentence:** from the first Art Directors Club exhibition (1921), awards judged mostly creative quality; only in 1968 did the Effie bring in the criterion of demonstrated effectiveness, and the tension between the two has stayed.

## Explanation

Richards traces the chronology:

| Year | Award | Criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1921 | The Art Directors Club exhibition in New York (created by Earnest Elmo Calkins) | visual aesthetics alone |
| 1923–1930 | Harvard Advertising Awards, funded by Edward Bok | to raise the standards of advertising, a jury of experts; BDO won the most |
| 1942 | OBIE (outdoor) | excellence in outdoor advertising |
| 1954 → | International Advertising Film Festival (Venice, then Cannes; since 2011 Cannes Lions) | creativity |
| 1959 | Clio | "creative excellence", the equivalent of the Oscars |
| 1960 | Creative Hall of Fame (Burnett, Gribbin, Ogilvy, Bernbach) | career |
| 1963 | D&AD | design and art direction |
| 1968 | **Effie** (New York American Marketing Association) | **demonstrated effectiveness** |
| 1973 | The One Show (Art Directors' Club + Copy Club) | creativity, to cut down on the proliferation of awards |

In 1949 Watkins faces the same dilemma when he picks "the 100 greatest advertisements": is an ad great because it sold, or because it is admired? He names two juries, the people who read the ads and time. In his foreword, Raymond Rubicam adds that phenomenal results alone are not enough: the ad must also be made memorable by originality, wit or conviction, and it must be honest.

Richards notes that some people in the industry criticize these events, but he cannot imagine the industry without awards. An extreme case: the 1991 Clio show, where the emcee never arrived, a caterer handed out the awards, and the audience stormed the stage and stole trophies.

## Application

- For the portfolio, pick work that can stand up to both criteria: the idea (ADC, D&AD) and the result (Effie).
- When you write a case study, keep what was creative clearly apart from what was measured.
- Be skeptical of awards for work that never ran on real money.

## Example

Fearless Girl (State Street Global Advisors, 2017), the statue of a girl facing the Charging Bull on Wall Street, won eighteen Cannes Lions. It had a one-week permit; the statue stayed for more than a year and a half.

## Questions

- Can creative awards encourage work made for the jury rather than for the client?
- Why did it take almost half a century to get an effectiveness award?
