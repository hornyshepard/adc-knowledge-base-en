# Leo Burnett: the inherent drama of the product and the characters that carry it

> **The idea in one sentence:** Burnett believed the story has to be drawn from the product itself, not borrowed from elsewhere, and that a strong character can carry that drama for years.

## Explanation

Richards describes Burnett (agency opened in Chicago in 1935) as a copywriter who built his agency around creatives and let research take a back seat. His principle: writers should find the drama in a product and tell the story inherent in it. The agency's second hallmark is its characters: Tony the Tiger, the Jolly Green Giant, the Keebler Elves and the Marlboro Man. Draper Daniels, creative director at Burnett and the inspiration for the name Don Draper, is credited with creating the Marlboro Man.

Watkins (who worked at Burnett in 1959) gives two case studies written by the agency's own people:

- **Pillsbury (1950).** The cake mix market was built on convenience. Burnett changed the strategy to **quality**: the manufacturer who best demonstrated quality would sell the most. The photographer set out to capture "the inherent drama of a cake in its purest form", stripped of embellishment and borrowed interest. Burnett asked for the picture to go on a page as big as possible, and the copy stayed as conversational as the picture.
- Marlboro (1955), covered in [[ART-026-marlboro-repositioning-a-feminine-brand-through-a-single|ART-026]]: the drama is not in the filter or the pack but in the smoker's masculine personality.

Richards also adds an advantage of the invented character: it never ages, never dies and never ends up in court, unlike celebrities (see [[HIS-018-invented-characters-never-misbehave-celebrities-do|HIS-018]]).

## Application

- Before you look for an outside metaphor, take stock of the product: what can you see, hear and feel when it is used? That is where the drama is.
- Borrowed interest (celebrities, trends, unrelated jokes) can get attention, but it does not build the brand.
- A brand character is an investment over decades; define its personality before the first spot.

## Example

Pillsbury "Who... Me?" (1950): a huge cake over almost the whole page and a few lines telling the housewife she can make exactly that just by adding milk.

## Questions

- How do you find the inherent drama of a digital product or a financial service?
- When does a brand character become an obstacle to repositioning?
