# The Creative Revolution of 1959 put the copywriter and the art director in the same room

> **The idea in one sentence:** the decisive change of the "Creative Revolution" was not a style but a way of working: the copywriter and art director team that creates together, instead of the assembly line in which the copy was passed on to the artist.

## Explanation

Richards dates it to 1959, with Doyle Dane Bernbach's (DDB) Volkswagen campaign. In a time of big cars with fins, Bill Bernbach and art director Helmut Krone did not hide the Beetle's size; they turned it into the argument: *Think small*, *Lemon*. The ad industry had never seen anything like it.

The author asked George Lois directly what really happened. His answer: before, the "creative" people sat waiting for the marketing people to give them some facts and usually a terrible idea; Bernbach "put a copywriter and art director in the same room and locked the door" (figuratively) until they came out with a campaign. Richards also cites Samuel's analysis: before Bernbach, the art director received the copy and added a complementary visual. The team produced humor, metaphor, irony, surprise and even absurdity.

The revolution spread through people: Lois founded Papert Koenig Lois (1960), the second "creative" agency; Mary Wells, who had worked at DDB, founded Wells Rich Greene (1966) and reworked Braniff's image. The context helped: the social revolution of the 1960s pushed the whole culture past its limits. John Hegarty says Bernbach created a generation that wanted to work in advertising rather than escape from it into art or the novel. Watkins, in the 1959 edition, already catches the phenomenon as it happens, with DDB's campaigns for El Al and Polaroid. At Polaroid, sales doubled in three years without any new products.

## Application

- Work as a team from the start: the picture and the words must be the same idea, not an illustration of the copy.
- Look for the product "weakness" that can become an argument through honesty (the Beetle case).
- A good brief gives the team the problem and the facts, not a ready-made creative solution.

## Example

When the FTC ordered Xerox to stop running the commercial in which a little girl made copies (the competitor claimed no kid could run the machine), Lois re-shot it with a chimpanzee, in front of FTC investigators (see [[HIS-024-the-demonstration-must-be-real-xerox-with-the-chimpanzee|HIS-024]]).
