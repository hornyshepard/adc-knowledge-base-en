# Advertising became the financial engine of the press, and the press became advertising's second master

> **The idea in one sentence:** from the moment newspapers lived on ads, advertising served two masters (the advertiser and the medium), and the price, the content and even the freedom of the press depended on it.

## Explanation

Richards describes the first newspapers (Strasbourg 1609, the *Weekly Newes* 1622, the *Boston News-Letter* 1704) as publications in which ads were rare and set in the same type as the news. The important step is economic: London's *General Advertiser* (c. 1745) was designed from the start to live on ads. A historian the author quotes concludes that the periodical press flourished only once it was shown that the "financial foundation of every successful paper was to be found in its advertising columns".

Two men changed the practice. John Houghton (c. 1680) opened the newspaper to every category and included the advertiser's name and address, which made it clear that the ad was paid for. Benjamin Franklin (1729) gave ads headlines and white space (see [[ART-024-the-advertising-layout-was-invented-under-constraints|ART-024]]).

The relationship also had a political side. The British stamp duties on newspapers and on every advertisement (1712–1855) were aimed at stifling press criticism. They led to a press much smaller than the American one; in 1829, the four hundred newspapers in Britain contained less advertising than just twelve American papers. The attempt to extend the duties to the colonies was one of the triggers of the American Revolution. At the opposite pole, advertising made possible the penny newspaper (*The Sun*, 1833) and Munsey's ten-cent magazines (1893), that is, the mass press.

The cycle closed with the internet: in 2015, worldwide, subscription revenue passed advertising as newspapers' income for the first time; classified ads migrated to Craigslist, coupons to websites and apps.

## Application

- When you choose a medium, understand its revenue model: a medium that lives on advertising optimizes for audience, one that lives on subscriptions optimizes for loyalty.
- The tension between editorial and advertising (the advertorial, placement) is three centuries old; respect the line, or you lose the credibility of the medium that hosts you.

## Example

In 1666, the *London Gazette* declared that it would not mix news and ads and published a separate supplement of advertisements, the first advertising supplement. By 1669 it was already accepting ads on its pages.
