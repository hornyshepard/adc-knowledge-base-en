# Political advertising learned from commercial advertising: from the Eisenhower spots to "Daisy"

> **The idea in one sentence:** election campaigns took over, one after another, the techniques of product advertising (the slogan, the short repeated spot, emotion, data), and some of the best-known ads of the 20th century are political.

## Explanation

The political slogan comes before the commercial one: Richards cites "Tippecanoe and Tyler, too" (1840) and Lincoln's slogans of 1860. Radio took the campaigns into people's homes (1924–1932). The turning point, though, is 1952. Rosser Reeves, the man behind the USP, produced the "Eisenhower Answers America" campaign for Eisenhower: thirty-second spots, when sixty-second spots were common, because his research indicated that repeated short messages were more effective than a single long one. Adlai Stevenson, who did little with short TV commercials, lost in a landslide.

Three TV commercials then became canonical:

- **"Daisy" (Lyndon Johnson, 1964).** A little girl counts as she pulls petals off a flower, her count becomes a countdown, the camera zooms in on her eye and we see a nuclear explosion. It ran only once and is still held up as the quintessence of negative political advertising.
- **"Morning in America" (Reagan, 1984, Hal Riney).** The positive equivalent: Americans going to work, a strong economy, the question of why you would change direction.
- **"Willie Horton" (G. H. W. Bush, 1988).** Two spots that implied disaster if the opponent won.

Watkins adds an older and surprising case: the farewell ad of Wendell Willkie, president of Commonwealth & Southern, when its Tennessee properties were handed over to the TVA (1939). The witnesses Watkins cites say the boom of Willkie's presidential candidacy started with it.

Richards's last examples show two directions: Shepard Fairey's poster for Obama (2008), made in one day and placed in the National Portrait Gallery, and the Cambridge Analytica scandal (2016), which pulled data on fifty million people from Facebook in breach of its rules.

## Application

- The mechanisms are the same as for brands: a simple, repeated message, one dominant emotion (fear or hope), a symbolic image.
- Negative advertising works by implication, not by statement; that is exactly why it raises the hardest ethical questions.
- In civic campaigns, personal data is a responsibility, not only a tool.

## Example

The Willkie ad opened with the exact hour of the handover ("Tonight, at Midnight...") and turned a business loss into a public argument about competition subsidized by the state.

## Questions

- Should political advertising meet the same standards of truth as commercial advertising?
- Is political microtargeting a difference of degree or of kind compared with the TV spot?
