# Great campaigns invented consumption occasions: Coca-Cola, the coffee break, the diamond ring

> **The idea in one sentence:** some of the most profitable classic campaigns did not sell the product better; they created a new moment in people's lives in which the product becomes natural.

## Explanation

Watkins brings together several cases with the same structure:

| Problem | The invented occasion |
|---|---|
| Coca-Cola sold only in summer | "Thirst Knows No Season" (1922): thirst has no season; by 1930 Montreal was selling almost as much as New Orleans |
| Coca-Cola had to fit into everyday life | "The Pause That Refreshes" (1929): the company "stopped thinking entirely about the product and considered only the behavior of people", who get along better when they pause |
| Coffee needed more moments | the Joint Coffee Trade Publicity Committee campaign (Ayer, George Cecil, 1921–1923) proposed a coffee break at four o'clock in the afternoon; the idea took off in World War II |
| The diamond was losing ground as an engagement gift | De Beers (Ayer, from 1939) strengthened the diamond engagement ring **tradition**; Watkins cites surveys in which 85% of engaged young women had engagement rings |
| Soup was taken for granted | "Soup on the Rocks" (Campbell, BBDO): bouillon poured over ice, then 21 new ideas for 21 soups |
| The cigarette competed with sweets | Lucky Strike: a cigarette instead of a sweet (see [[HIS-028-tobacco-was-the-front-on-which-advertising-tested-its-power\|HIS-028]]) |

Richards adds a visual case: the 1931 Coca-Cola billboard painted by Haddon Sundblom fixed the modern, plump image of Santa Claus. Many believe it changed Christmas forever.

The common mechanism: instead of "why is our product better?", the question becomes "when, where and with whom would the product make sense?". The answer comes not from the spec sheet but from watching people.

## Application

- In the brief, add the question about the occasion: which moment of the day, the year or a life could "belong" to the product?
- Look for the barrier of season or habit and attack it directly, with a repeatable line.
- A new occasion needs consistency over years; Coca-Cola repeated "Thirst Knows No Season" until it became part of the public's attitude.

## Example

The "Soup on the Rocks" idea did not come from a lone genius but from a meeting in which someone asked what would happen if you poured soup over ice (see [[IDE-024-is-the-idea-born-alone-in-a-pair-or-in-a-meeting-the|IDE-024]]).

## Questions

- Where is the line between a useful occasion and a manufactured "tradition" (the diamond)?
- Which occasions have digital brands created (online Black Friday, "Spotify Wrapped")?
