# Advertising disguised as content is more than a century old: advertorial, placement, infomercial, native

> **The idea in one sentence:** every medium has had its own form of advertising that borrows the clothes of content; the argument for it (it is easier to process) and the argument against it (it steals the newsroom's credibility) have been the same from 1898 to today.

## Explanation

Richards follows a single family of techniques:

| Form | The book's first example |
|---|---|
| The advertorial | an 1898 "article" about visits to art studios, written to sell their artworks; the book dates the term either to 1917 or to 1946 |
| Product placement | the Lumière brothers' films of 1896, with boxes of Sunlight Soap placed in the frame; its birth is often attributed to the Reese's Pieces in *E.T.* (1982) |
| The sponsored program | *The Eveready Hour* (1923); the soap opera *Ma Perkins* (1933), produced by Procter & Gamble itself for Oxydol soap |
| The infomercial | the demonstration of the Vitamix blender (1949) |
| Native advertising | a term introduced by Fred Wilson in 2011, for online media |

The argument for: readers' minds process editorial content a certain way, and a classic ad disrupts that processing; an ad that resembles the surrounding material is easier to take in. The argument against: it is covert advertising that borrows the credibility of journalism; some call it "information pollution". Self-regulation tried to draw the line: since 1982 the American Society of Magazine Editors has required advertorials to be labeled clearly and conspicuously as ads, and since 2014 it has applied the rule to native ads too. The problem remains enforcement: in 2020 the labels varied enormously, and it is not known whether audiences notice them.

In the final chapter, Keith Reinhard predicts that native advertising will go the way of the old advertorials, because any attempt to deceive consumers breaches the integrity of both the brand and the medium.

## Application

- Brand content is legitimate when it is useful or entertaining **and** clearly labeled; hiding the source is an ethical and often a legal problem.
- For placement, check the context in which the product appears: Richards recalls an Infiniti driven by a drug lord's security chief.
- Think of the medium as a host: if you compromise its credibility, you compromise your message too.

## Example

When *Omni* (1990) cut a hole in its cover to show a Motorola hologram on the next page, two editors resigned, accusing the magazine of erasing the line between editorial and advertising.

## Questions

- Is an influencer who does not disclose a partnership a modern advertorial?
- Where does branded content that offers value end and camouflage begin?
