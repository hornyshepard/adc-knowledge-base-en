# The motive matrix: coherence, agency and communion, turned inward or outward

> **The idea in one sentence:** people use brands to confirm who they are, to show their competence or to belong to a group, either to themselves or to others, and a brand should target just one of these six paths.

## Explanation

Psychologists Neal Roese and Wendi Gardner treat the self as a knowledge structure in memory; self-brand connections are associations between the self-concept and the brand image. Three primary motives drive them:

- **Coherence**: the need for clarity about identity;
- **Agency**: the need for competence and achievement;
- **Communion**: the need for social acceptance and belonging.

Each motive can have an **inward** focus (the individual is the audience) or an **outward** focus (other people are the audience). The result is the motive matrix, a 3 × 2 grid:

| | Inward | Outward |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence | Connor buys Harley-Davidson to affirm his identity as a rebel | Laura buys Montblanc to project her new identity as a senior manager |
| Agency | Tom wears Omega because James Bond wore it | Patrick buys Speedo's premium swim goggles to signal his competitive swimming achievements |
| Communion | Martha adorns her Christmas tree with Coca-Cola souvenirs, just like her family did | Sherry wears a Chicago Cubs hat alongside the other fans |

**Principle 1: target one motive.** Several motives create complexity and confusion, especially for new brands. Dove's "Real Beauty Sketches" works through its focus on agency, with an inward focus. Only mature brands (Harley-Davidson, Chrysler's "Born of Fire") can afford several motives.

Brian Sternthal (ch. 10) proposes a convergent model, the **3C framework**: competence, community, contentment. His example: Halo Top (few calories, high protein) became the pint leader because it answered the competence motive (fitness), while Häagen-Dazs light failed, because a brand consumed for contentment cannot promise less pleasure; Häagen-Dazs Five (only five ingredients) reframed health in terms of contentment.

## Application

- Place the brand in a single cell of the matrix before writing the brief.
- Check whether the brand's audience is the person themselves (intimate messages) or other people (visible symbols, a big logo).
- When you launch a "light" or "healthy" variant, ask whether it contradicts the motive for which people love the brand.

## Example

ModCloth builds communion, with an outward focus: the brand community approves designs ("Be the buyer") before they go into production.

- Which cell would a software brand for freelancers fall into? And a natural cosmetics brand?
