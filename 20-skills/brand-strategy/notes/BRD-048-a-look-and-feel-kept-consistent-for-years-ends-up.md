# A look and feel kept consistent for years ends up triggering the brand without its name, and executions should vary within it, not be identical

> **The idea in one sentence:** visual consistency is one of the most important aspects of creative execution: the imagery is recognized faster than the name, and competitors' copies end up misattributed to the brand that owns the imagery.

## Explanation

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott require all the executions of a campaign (ads, promotions, materials, packaging) and, over time, all campaigns to have an immediately recognizable **look and feel**. The means are color, shape, recurring imagery and music (*audio branding*: AXA, which operates in 56 countries, has a mix-and-match library of specially composed music and an audio logo used by all executions).

**For brand awareness.** Once the look and feel is established, the execution triggers the brand on its own, and the name only confirms it. Silk Cut reached the point where its advertising showed only purple silk and a cut. At H&M in Sweden, well over half of the target market consistently recognizes the ads, and the retailers that copied the style were misattributed to H&M. In 2019 Mastercard dropped its name from the logo after almost 50 years of overlapping discs, and 80% of people recognized the brand without the name.

**For brand attitude.** Ideally, the consistent imagery is linked to the **key benefit** and is **unique**. Category clichés help no one: every truck travels easily over the roughest terrain. The imagery has to be checked with the target audience: a relaxed executive holding a cup of coffee was read by the target as a man in the morning before work, contemplating the stress of the day. OXO used the same family of actors around mealtimes for 16 years, evolving with the times.

**Consistency does not mean sameness.** If executions are too similar, the campaign wears out faster. A new logo is introduced only when the brand is repositioned, and messages with no logo and no name work only after years of consistency. The authors also separate the **slogan** (about the product, flexible, ubiquitous) from the **tagline** (about the company, durable, for brand building).

**Where the sources converge.** The same rule appears in identity design as the iconic hardware to keep and the dynamic software to change (*Goddess Guide to Branding*, *Logos That Last*) and in Watkins's collection of classic ads, where the lasting successes repeat the same idea year after year.

## Application

- Define 2–3 distinctive assets (color, character, sound) linked to the benefit and write them into the creative guidelines in the brief.
- Vary the stories, not the visual codes.
- Before dropping the name or the logo, measure how well the brand is recognized from its assets alone.

## Example

In 2014 Louis Vuitton asked six famous designers, from Karl Lagerfeld to Frank Gehry, to come up with new ways to use the 118-year-old LV logo. The brand was overexposed, but the answer was not to change the logo; it was to renew the products around it.
