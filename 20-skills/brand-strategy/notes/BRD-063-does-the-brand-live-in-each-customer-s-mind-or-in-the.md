# Does the brand live in each customer's mind or in the culture they share with others? The network of associations versus the collective myth

> **The idea in one sentence:** for the Kellogg school and consumer psychology, the brand is a set of associations in the individual's memory, which you choose and measure; for Holt and the brand culture theorists, its power comes from meanings shared collectively, and the two views call for different working methods.

## Explanation

| | The brand as a network of associations | The brand as a collective myth |
|---|---|---|
| Where it lives | in each consumer's memory | in the shared perception of a culture |
| Who supports it | Calkins and Tybout (Kellogg), cognitive psychology | Holt, Parsons and colleagues, Batat |
| What you build | strong, unique, favorable associations; a position | a myth that answers a cultural tension |
| How you research it | spontaneous associations, tracking, choice models | history, semiotics, subcultures |
| What stays stable | the position, sustained for decades | nothing for certain: the myth is reinvented at every cultural break |

Tim Calkins defines a brand as a set of associations linked to the name, mark or symbol, which the manager chooses through positioning and measures. Holt answers directly, as cited by Batat: marketers like to think of brands as psychological phenomena in each consumer, but "what gives the brand its power is the collective nature of these perceptions"; a brand's equity does not come from the richness of its mental associations but from the myth it delivers. Parsons, Maclaran and Chatzidakis say the same about brand culture.

The tension is real, but not total. Fernández Gómez places both views in the same paradigm, *consumer branding* (positioning comes from cognitive psychology, iconic brands from anthropology), and notes that Holt's idea is not radically new: it is indebted to semiotics and European myth criticism. Rosenbaum-Elliott and Percy order the theories of advertising on the same axis (individual versus cultural) and deliberately choose the cognitive approach as the most viable for management, while acknowledging that it does not see the social life of ads.

## Application

- In functional categories (detergent, insurance), work first with associations and positioning; in self-expressive ones (fashion, drinks, cars), add cultural analysis.
- Combine the methods: spontaneous associations show what the individual thinks, cultural analysis shows why it matters to the group.
- When the client asks for "an iconic brand," ask which collective tension it resolves; a set of pleasant associations is not enough.

## Example

The two schools read Mini differently: as a set of associations (small, fun, British) or as a myth that, after the Suez crisis, resolved the contradiction between size as status and the need for fuel economy.

## Questions

- Can a B2B brand have a collective myth, or does it stay at the level of associations?
- If the brand's power is collective, how do you measure the effect of a single campaign?
