# In the Nike case, "Just Do It" built the myth of the lonely hero who succeeds through willpower, on a cultural tension in America

> **The idea in one sentence:** according to Holt and Cameron, as Batat summarizes them, Nike's success comes not from better technology but from a myth of self-transcendence that answered the anxieties of Americans in an increasingly competitive economy.

## Explanation

Batat reconstructs the case along the steps of cultural strategy:
- **The orthodoxy.** In the 1970s, all sports brands highlighted the superhuman qualities of great champions.
- **The disruption.** The severe economic crisis of the late 1970s and globalization meant that the ease with which stars were shown winning no longer matched people's concerns. Rigor and mental strength now count; jogging takes sport out of the stadium and into daily life.
- **The ideological opportunity.** "Combative individual will".
- **The source material.** The cultural codes of the African American community and of the struggle against discrimination. Life in the ghetto becomes the analogy for the labor market: a permanent struggle.
- **The execution.** "Spike and Mike" (Spike Lee, as a fictional character from the ghetto, and Michael Jordan); "Hardrock Miner" (young men improving their shooting on a street court, a dark universe, a messianic tone); "If you let me play sports", for women; Tiger Woods, presented as an oppressed Black American who rose to the top through hard work and perfectionism in an elitist sport. In the football market, the ghetto is replaced by Brazilian favelas.

**The narrative template** shared by all the ads: the athlete in a situation of effort, a repetition of training, a modest success that has great value for the athlete, and a discreet voiceover that acts as the athlete's inner voice. This unity of style and form gives the brand an exclusive "ownership" of these cultural codes and the authority to tell stories about athletes' personal motivation.

## Application

- When you analyze an iconic brand, ask: what cultural tension does it resolve? What source material did it borrow? What narrative template does it repeat?
- For your own campaign: a strong myth needs consistency of form for years, not a single ad.

## Example

The same myth is adapted from one sport to another and from one market to another (basketball → golf → football; the American ghetto → the favela) without losing its meaning: proof that the brand's "story" is independent of the product.

## Questions

- Is it ethical to "divert" the codes of a community that faces discrimination in order to sell shoes? What would you say to a critic?
- What cultural tension today could support a new myth in a category in Romania?
