# A position is sustained through modern instantiation, a new kind of reason to believe and laddering

> **The idea in one sentence:** do not change the position when people get bored; change the way you show it: the context, the reason to believe or the level of abstraction of the benefit.

## Explanation

Once a brand's position gains traction, the goal is to sustain it for decades. Some positions are timeless (the Marlboro cowboy ran for about 50 years), but most need strategies to keep them fresh. Tybout describes four (laddering goes in two directions):

| Strategy | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Modern instantiation** | the same benefit, a contemporary context | Grape-Nuts, health and well-being: the naturalist Euell Gibbons (early 1970s), a country house (mid-1980s), Sir Edmund Hillary on a climb (2013) |
| **Switching from attribute to image reasons to believe** | when the attribute is no longer news, a story about people takes over | Extra: from long-lasting flavor to the story of Juan and Sarah, a long-lasting relationship |
| **Laddering down** | the abstract benefit keeps getting new concrete reasons to believe | Volvo: antilock brakes, collision warning, blind-spot warning, all for "protection" |
| **Laddering up** | attribute → functional benefit → emotional benefit → brand essence | Volvo: safety → enhanced protection → peace of mind → "Volvo is the brand for people who embrace life" |
| **Brand purpose** | the position is raised to the level of a cause (see the note on purpose) | Always, Patagonia, Honey Maid |

Laddering up relates the brand to increasingly abstract and enduring consumer goals. Volvo turned its commitment to life into a new product: LifePaint, a paint invisible during the day and reflective in the dark for cyclists (many luxury car owners also cycle as a hobby), sold at dealerships, with a sales increase of more than 1,000 cars.

## Application

- When the client asks for "a new campaign," first check whether the problem is the position or just a tired execution.
- Build the brand's ladder: attribute, functional benefit, emotional benefit, essence. Work creatively on the rung that fits the objective.
- Look for new "news" on the same position: a feature, a service, a gesture (LifePaint) that demonstrates the old benefit.

## Example

Extra moved its story from personal hygiene (protection against cavities) to community and sharing, without abandoning the idea of "long-lasting."

## Questions

- At which rung of the ladder do all brands become the same? (see the debate about purpose)
- How do you know that a modern instantiation has not, in fact, changed the position?
