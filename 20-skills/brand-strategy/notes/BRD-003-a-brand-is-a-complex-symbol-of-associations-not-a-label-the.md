# A brand is a complex symbol of associations, not a label: "The Product and the Brand" (Gardner & Levy, 1955)

> **The idea in one sentence:** the brand name tells consumers many things through the body of associations built up over time, and these ideas, feelings and attitudes decide choice and loyalty more than many technical facts.

## Explanation

Burleigh Gardner and Sidney Levy, motivation researchers (Social Research, Inc.), published in the *Harvard Business Review* (1955) the paper considered the seminal text of the idea of brand image. The thesis: a brand name is more than the label that differentiates among manufacturers; it is "a complex symbol that represents a variety of ideas and attributes". It communicates through the sound and literal meaning of the name, but above all through the associations it has acquired as a public object.

Key contributions:

- The image of a brand may be clear-cut or vague, varied or simple, intense or innocuous.
- The difference between brands often comes from subtle variations in feelings, not from product qualities.
- A brand cannot be all things to all people (an anticipation of segmentation).
- The image has to be coherent, an idea close to what would later be called identity and positioning.
- Reputation is built through time, frequently in ways that management is not aware of. The firm has to evaluate its current public image, the differences seen by important consumer groups and the images of competitive brands.

Four recommendations for advertising:

1. Copy should be thought about in terms of its symbolic and indirect meanings, not only its literal one.
2. Color and illustration have social and psychological implications, not only esthetic ones.
3. Media selection should be tied to the brand image plan, not only to circulation figures.
4. Research should seek out ideas and meanings, not only audience statistics.

## Application

- In a brand audit, gather the spontaneous associations (words, images, emotions) for your brand and for two or three competitors. The differences in feeling are often larger than the differences in product.
- Check a layout against the four recommendations: what does the color say about the user's status? What does the media context say about the brand?

## Example

Ogilvy took the ideas of the "complex symbol", segmentation and coherence from Gardner and Levy and publicly acknowledged the source in his 1955 talk.

## Questions

- The four recommendations were written in 1955. Which of them is most often ignored in today's digital advertising?
- How do you measure "subtle variations in feelings" without falling into arbitrary interpretation?
