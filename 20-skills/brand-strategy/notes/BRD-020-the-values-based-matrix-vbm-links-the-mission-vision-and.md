# The values-based matrix (VBM) links the mission, vision and values to the minds, hearts and spirits of stakeholders

> **The idea in one sentence:** the mission, the vision and the values are three "products" marketed to different audiences, not texts for the wall: the mission to consumers, the vision to shareholders, the values to employees, each at a rational, emotional and spiritual level.

## Explanation

Starting from their definitions (mission = *why*, vision = *what*, values = *how*), Kotler, Kartajaya and Setiawan cross the three elements with the mind–heart–spirit triad of Marketing 3.0. The result is the Values-Based Matrix (VBM) Model (Fig. 2.5):

| | Mission → consumers | Vision → shareholders | Values → employees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mind | deliver satisfaction | *ProfitAbility* | be better |
| Heart | realize aspiration | *ReturnAbility* | differentiate |
| Spirit | practice compassion | *SustainAbility* | make a difference |

Each cell is a promise that has to be proven. Part II of the book develops the columns: marketing the mission to the consumers (ch. 3), the values to the employees (ch. 4) and to the channel partners (ch. 5), the vision to the shareholders (ch. 6). The logic comes from Drucker: the performance of the mission brings financial returns as results. The shareholder column is the boldest: the authors argue that sustainability can also be defended with arguments of cost, revenue and brand value.

## Application

- In a brand platform workshop, fill in the 3×3 grid and ask for concrete proof for each cell (a program, a metric, a policy).
- Use the columns to adapt the message: the same brand idea is said differently to the consumer, the employee and the investor.
- Empty cells show where the brand is only declarative.

## Example

Timberland (Fig. 2.7): the mission "Make it better" becomes the tagline itself; the vision of being the twenty-first-century example of a socially responsible corporation is shown rationally by profit growth, emotionally by stock performance and spiritually by Sustainability Key Performance Indicators; the values (humanity, humility, integrity, excellence) are practiced through the Path of Service volunteering program. S.C. Johnson (Fig. 2.6) builds its values on the *triple bottom line*.

## Questions

- Is it realistic to believe that short-term shareholders will pay for *SustainAbility*?
- What happens to the matrix when the brand is bought by a group with different values (Ben & Jerry's, The Body Shop)?
