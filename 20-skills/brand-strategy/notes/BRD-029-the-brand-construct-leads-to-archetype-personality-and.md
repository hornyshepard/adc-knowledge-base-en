# The brand construct leads to archetype, personality and narrative, and an idea is on-brand when it falls in the same place on the same scales

> **The idea in one sentence:** the brand builds itself a "world", as a novelist does, and an ad idea is checked by placing the brand and the idea on the same scales from 1 to 5.

## Explanation

Braun and Landa define the **brand construct** as a theoretical assembly of three main points: what people want and might need in the near future; how the brand fits into what people want; and how the brand's attributes stand out as distinctive in a crowded commercial arena. In this way the brand stakes a claim to a position in people's minds: Ben & Jerry's owns interesting-tasting ice creams, Disney family fun, Jeep rugged, go-anywhere vehicles, Apple creativity. The construct is the armature of the North Star story and supports the **brand promise**. **Worldbuilding** then chooses the tone, themes, voice and visual elements. Burberry builds its films on the heritage of its founder, who made weatherproof outerwear for explorers: "Inherent in every Burberry garment is freedom."

The chain the authors propose:

**brand construct → brand archetype → brand personality → brand narrative**

The brand's self-identity (how the business wants to be perceived) is not the same as its brand image (how the audience actually perceives it). For the archetype, the book gives a list of 14 types, wider than the classic 12: the Hero, the Lover, the Magician, the Outlaw, the Sage, the Innocent, the Creator, the Ruler, the Caregiver, the Everyday Person, the Citizen, the Explorer, the Warrior, the Jester. Each comes with brand examples (Duolingo and Wendy's as Jesters, Patagonia as a Citizen).

**The on-brand alignment tool.** You place first the brand, then the ad idea, on the same scales from 1 to 5; they should very nearly match up. The scales: mainstream–unconventional; profit-driven–triple-bottom-line-driven (people, planet, profit); purpose-led–"sell, sell, sell"; having a sense of humor–humorless; disruptive thinking–same-old; individuals–family-oriented; plays hard–works hard; aspirational–grind. An *off-brand* idea confuses people and dilutes the brand's greater story.

The book adds a **brand narrative tool** with blanks to fill in: what the entity thinks the brand signifies and what the target audience thinks, the brand's core emotion now and the one it should have, what the two top competing brands signify, and which message and action align everything.

## Application

- Before the presentation, score the brand and each concept on the eight scales; a gap of more than two points needs a strategic justification.
- Fill in the narrative tool with the client's team; the gaps between "what we think" and "what the target audience thinks" are material for the brief.

## Example

Since 2004, Dove has built its world around "real beauty". When a large Canadian media company fired a 58-year-old anchor who had allowed her hair to go gray, Dove joined the debate about ageism: an on-brand idea, because it follows directly from the construct. The authors also quote the criticism: the righteous tone lets the brand keep its own beauty tips unspoken.

## Questions

- A slightly off-brand idea may be exactly the one that moves the brand where it wants to go. How do you tell drift from evolution?
- What do you gain and what do you lose with 14 archetypes instead of 12?
