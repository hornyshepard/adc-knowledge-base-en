# The frame of reference tells consumers the goal the brand serves

> **The idea in one sentence:** before you say why you are better, you have to say "better than what", and the frame you choose (the category, another category or the goal) also decides who you compete with.

## Explanation

In Alice Tybout's model, the frame of reference "informs consumers about the goal in using the brand." It can be conveyed in three ways:

1. **Category membership.** Stating that Lite is a beer tells most people it is an alcoholic beverage often consumed with friends and food. To be credible, the brand must share the category's key features, its *points of parity*: a new "beer" with no alcohol or carbonation would be suspect. When the iPhone launched in 2007, the brand name and the advertising announced its frame of reference, a phone, because Apple was then known for making computers.
2. **Comparison with a different category.** Pioneers explain a new concept through a familiar one: Blue Apron used grocery shopping as the frame, Uber used taxis. Once the new concept (meal kits, ride-hailing) is familiar, later entrants such as Plated and Lyft use that category directly. Coke Zero Sugar compares itself to Coke on taste, which implies it tastes better than other diet brands.
3. **Showing the goal.** In an eBay ad in Asia, a man breaks an antique vase, finds a similar one on eBay and opens champagne to celebrate. The cork flies off and destroys the new vase.

The frame has to be **orchestrated** with the point of difference. Bounty presented its new strength through scrubbing pots and pans and cleaning fish tanks. That moved it from the paper towel frame into the sponge frame, where brands such as Scotch-Brite compete on durability, and the absorbency advantage became unclear.

Bobby Calder adds that the category name is a brand design decision, not a given. 7 Up chose the "uncola" category. Chobani built its brand on a new category, Greek yogurt, and owned the association: Yoplait Greek failed, while "Oui by Yoplait", sold as "French-style yogurt", succeeded. vitaminwater gives the category name prominence on its label, and Motorola coined "Telematics".

Other sources describe the same step. For Ollé and Riu, positioning starts with "contextualization": you choose the reference brand that benefits you most. Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott call it the "what is it?" question of the general model of positioning (ADV-020).

## Application

- Ask what people would use instead of the brand. That list is the real frame of competition.
- Choose the frame that draws the most demand *and* leaves you a difference you can defend.
- In the creative work, decide whether the ad first has to explain the category (a launch) or can go straight to the difference.

## Example

Uber placed itself in the ride-sharing category, not the taxi category. Its original name, UberCab, pulled the wrong way: the associations of "cab" were at odds with the intended ones, and the word caused political problems with city governments, so the name was shortened to Uber.

- When is it risky to invent a new category instead of entering an existing one?
- Which points of parity does an electric car brand have to prove?
