# Attribute reasons to believe get copied, while image reasons to believe can be owned

> **The idea in one sentence:** a reason to believe based on a product feature persuades quickly but gets copied; one based on people and occasions of use lasts, because the brand can own it through repetition.

## Explanation

In Alice Tybout's chapter, the point of difference is an abstract benefit (empowering, convenient, safe). Its choice depends on the brand's rank in the category: the **leader** claims the benefit that reflects the main reason for using the category (Tide cleans best), and **followers** choose a niche benefit (Gain makes clothes smell fresh).

The benefit becomes credible through the reason to believe (RTB), which can be of two types:

| Type | What it is | Examples | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Attribute** | ingredients, technology, heritage, country of origin | Jif: made with fresh-roasted peanuts; Louis Vuitton: the brand's travel heritage; Perrier: the spring in Vergèze | it can be imitated; the window of advantage is short |
| **Image** | the people and occasions associated with the brand | Stephen Curry for Under Armour; "the most interesting man in the world" for Dos Equis | it requires repeated investment in communication |

Attributes that matter to consumers are generally considered first, because they are the most compelling evidence. The problem is imitability: Instant Pot grew through its programmable feature, but Ninja, All-Clad and Philips quickly entered the same category, and the point of difference became a point of parity. That is why, in most cases, brands end up with image reasons to believe, which they "own" through frequent advertising. The Dos Equis campaign grew sales by 34.8% between 2007 and 2015.

## Application

- When the client has a new technical advantage, use it quickly to achieve scale, but prepare from the start an image territory that will take over when it is copied.
- Check that the proof does not contradict the benefit: for Lite, "fewer calories" could undermine "great taste".
- For a follower brand, do not claim the leader's benefit; look for a credible niche.

## Example

Harry's bread demonstrates "nice and soft" with the image of a child napping with her head on the bread: a visual proof, not a technical one.

## Questions

- Does a branded ingredient (a registered trademark) turn a copyable attribute into a protected one?
- When does a celebrity ambassador become a risk to the reason to believe?
