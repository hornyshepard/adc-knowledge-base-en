# The positioning map is made in four steps, and the chosen positioning passes six tests: clear, pertinent, distinctive, coherent, attractive, durable

> **The idea in one sentence:** positioning is a way of choosing sides (*parti pris*) that becomes the red thread (*fil rouge*) of the communication plan; the perceptual map shows where competitors stand and where there is an empty space, and the six criteria check whether the chosen space is a good one.

## Explanation

*La boîte à outils de la publicité* defines positioning (*positionnement*) by the way we want the offering to be perceived by consumers relative to the competition. The advertising work consists in making the chosen positioning become the brand image that consumers perceive.

**The six criteria:**

| Criterion | Test |
|---|---|
| Clear (*clair*) | it rests on objective characteristics (the taste of Nutella) or symbolic ones (Apple's visionary innovation) that are easy to identify |
| Pertinent (*pertinent*) | it meets customers' expectations and is credible against the competition |
| Distinctive (*distinctif*) | it puts forward a quality the others do not have or do not talk about |
| Coherent (*cohérent*) | it seems logical to the consumer |
| Attractive (*attractif*) | it highlights the advantage capable of persuading |
| Durable (*durable*) | it lasts over time; a new positioning takes time to settle in |

The main pitfall: not choosing, and combining all the brand's strengths in order to persuade "everyone". Better to pick one axis, the most promising, and develop it until it becomes an element of differentiation.

**The perceptual map (*carte perceptuelle* or *mapping*) in four steps:**

1. define the universe of reference (which moves: new competitors arrive, habits change);
2. analyze the positioning and above all the brand image of competitors;
3. draw out the main consumer expectations (price, quality, reliability, range, accessibility, image);
4. choose two criteria as axes and place the brands.

The map does not change the marketing decisions taken upstream; it shows how the brand is perceived, helps with image adjustments and reveals possible niches. It also shows whether you risk a frontal attack on the leader.

Other sources point out the same pitfall: the brief with five benefits blocks the creative idea (BRF-012), and Grewal and Levy build the same perceptual map to find unserved demand (MKT-018).

## Application

- Make two or three maps with different pairs of axes. The pair that reveals an empty and relevant space is the useful one.
- Put the proposed positioning through the six tests before you write the promise in the copy strategy.

## Example

**Petit Bateau** (bought by the Yves Rocher group in 1988) moved from a conventional children's brand to a transgenerational brand, starting from the image of a Petit Bateau tee-shirt worn with a Chanel suit. In 2008, with BETC, the print campaign showed real people, young and old, with their age expressed in **months**, as for babies: "la marque nous habille de zéro à 924 mois" [the brand dresses us from zero to 924 months]. On the map (price × quality), the brand sets itself apart from Kiabi and from retailers' own brands.

## Questions

- "Durable" and "attractive" can conflict when tastes change quickly. How do you arbitrate?
- Which axes would you choose for a map of banks in Romania?
