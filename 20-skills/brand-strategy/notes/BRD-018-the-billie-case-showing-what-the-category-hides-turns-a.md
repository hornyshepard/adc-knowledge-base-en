# The Billie case: showing what the category hides turns a taboo into a positioning

> **The idea in one sentence:** Billie, a shaving brand for women, became the first in its category to show body hair on women, turning a silent convention of the industry (shame) into its very point of difference.

## Explanation

The interview with co-founder Georgina Gooley in *Goddess Guide to Branding* describes one of the most useful cases for an advertising school. Billie launched in 2017 as a direct-to-consumer brand; it raised $35 million in venture capital and was sold for $310 million in four years.

**The category insight.** Gooley noticed that most women's shaving brands had been spun out of men's brands and kept their lens, including the "pink tax" on price. What is more, the ads showed women shaving over already-shaved, perfectly smooth skin: the industry was not prepared to show the hair the product removes, because it treated it as a societal taboo. Legacy communication was shame-based.

**The positioning.** Billie presented shaving as a choice, not an expectation: "Hey, shave or don't shave … it's up to you". The message may sound counterintuitive for a company that sells razors, but it puts the audience ahead of the product.

**The execution.** Project Body Hair (underarm hair, a unibrow); Red, White & You-Do-You for the Fourth of July (women at the beach who didn't groom their bikini lines); a Movember campaign reminding people that women have mustaches too; photos of women's postpartum bodies placed literally on a pedestal for Mother's Day; a board game about society's double standards.

**The tone.** Although the message was rebellious (the Queen Persephone archetype, "the Fire", in the book's system), the aesthetic stayed bright, optimistic and playful: a brand *for* women, not *against* men. Hostile reactions on Instagram were accepted as part of the conversation; one of the company values is Take a Swing, because nobody remembers a wallflower.

## Application

- Take an inventory of the visual conventions of the client's category: what does everyone show and, above all, what does *no one* show?
- Check whether one of these omissions hides a shame or a lie the audience would be relieved to see named.
- When the message is provocative, balance it with a warm tone, so that it sparks conversation, not rejection.

## Example

The same "white space" mechanism appears with Liquid Death, which sells water with the dark energy of Hades in a category dominated by purity.

## Questions

- Which category in Romania has a false visual convention worth breaking?
- Where is the line between provocation that creates conversation and provocation that alienates the target audience?
