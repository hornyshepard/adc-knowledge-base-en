# Human-centric brands have six human attributes: physicality, intellectuality, sociability, emotionality, personability, morality

> **The idea in one sentence:** in a world of interactions with machines, the most attractive brands are those that behave like a mature person (approachable, likeable, even vulnerable) and influence customers as friends, not as an authority.

## Explanation

Marketing 4.0 asks brands to become less intimidating: to be authentic and honest, to admit their flaws and to stop trying to seem perfect. The authors borrow from Stephen Sampson (*Leaders without Titles*) the six human attributes through which horizontal leaders attract others, even though they have no authority over them:

| Attribute | For a brand it means | The book's example |
|---|---|---|
| **Physicality** | attraction through brand identity, product design, customer experience design; unique, albeit not perfect | the dynamic logo systems of Google and MTV; Apple's product, user-interface and store design |
| **Intellectuality** | the ability to think beyond the obvious, to innovate, to solve customers' problems | Tesla, whose name promises innovation; Uber and Airbnb, viewed as "smart" brands |
| **Sociability** | not afraid of conversation: listens, answers, shares interesting content | Denny's Diner's witty tone on Twitter; Zappos, with a customer-service call of 10 hours and 43 minutes |
| **Emotionality** | an emotional connection through inspirational messages or humor | Dove and women's self-esteem; the Doritos "Ultrasound" ad at Super Bowl 50, polarizing but the most emotionally engaging |
| **Personability** | self-awareness: knows what it stands for, shows its flaws, takes full responsibility | Patagonia's Footprint Chronicles; Domino's, which admitted in 2010 that its pizzas were not compelling and reinvented them |
| **Morality** | ethics and integrity; keeps its promises even though customers do not keep track | the Unilever Sustainable Living Plan: double the size of the business while halving its environmental footprint by 2020 |

The process has two steps: first uncover customers' latent anxieties and desires (through digital anthropology), then unveil the human side of the brand. The attributes are the remedy for weak **appeal** on the 5A path.

## Application

- Use the table as an audit grid for brand personality: score each attribute from 1 to 5, with evidence.
- For tone on social media, work first on sociability and emotionality. For reputation, on personability and morality.
- Consider admitting a flaw as a creative strategy (the Domino's case), but only if the product actually changes.

## Example

Domino's 2010 campaign publicly shared customers' criticism of its pizzas, then the new recipe, offered to the critics themselves. The vulnerability it owned up to made the brand more human.

## Questions

- Can "vulnerability" and "premium" coexist? Can a luxury brand show its flaws?
- Where does sociability turn into excessive familiarity or into jokes that do not suit the brand?
