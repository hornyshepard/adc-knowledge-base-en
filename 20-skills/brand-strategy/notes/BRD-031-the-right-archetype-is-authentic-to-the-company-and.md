# The right archetype is authentic to the company and attractive to the customer

> **The idea in one sentence:** four exercises bring the possible archetypes to the surface (the natural one for the category, the one that inspires the team, the one customers want and the unexpected one), and the final test keeps only the archetypes that are both authentic to the company and attractive to customers; from those you pick one.

## Explanation

McCarthy and McAndrew start from the idea that people decide based on feeling first and reason second. A powerful brand **conjures emotion** that customers want to experience and **fortifies it with rationale**. The right archetype therefore has to evoke the desired emotion and align with the product's tangible attributes.

**The four exercises:**
1. **The Natural**: the archetype connected in an obvious way to the category: beauty → Venus, outdoor apparel → Diana, education → Athena, cooking → Demeter. "First thought, best thought."
2. **The Inspirational**: the goddess who excites the team because of where she could take the brand.
3. **The Match**: the goddess customers would want to talk to first at a party; it requires knowing their motivations (the book offers a Customer Toolbox, with an emphasis on the question "why?").
4. **The Unexpected**: you list the top brands in the category, assign each an archetype and look for the "white space" (Liquid Death = Hades in the water category).

**The pressure test:** for each candidate, you ask whether she is authentic (does she have a natural connection to who you are and what you do?) and whether she is attractive (does she inspire a feeling your customers want to have?). If several pass, you pick the one you feel passionate about and who brings a refreshingly different energy to the category. And you **pick one**: focus makes communication quick and clear.

Special situations: if the founder is the face of the brand, the archetype has to resemble the founder; an established brand starts from what customers already love about it and avoids archetypes where it has historically been weak, because they would not be credible.

## Application

- Run the four exercises in a workshop with the client, noting every option before choosing.
- Map the competitors' archetypes: it is a visual competitive analysis that is easy to present.
- Use the "authentic + attractive" criteria as a filter for other brand decisions too (name, tone, partnerships).

## Example

In the fictional Soft Scrubs Co. case (medical scrubs for millennial women nurses), Hestia was the natural goddess (healing), Demeter inspired the team, interviews showed that nurses want calm (Hestia) and to be cared for themselves (Demeter), and the competitor map (Dickies = Mars, Figs = Venus, Jaanuu = Diana) showed that Demeter was missing from the category. The choice: Demeter.
