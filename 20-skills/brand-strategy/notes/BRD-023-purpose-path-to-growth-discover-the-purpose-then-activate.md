# Purpose Path to Growth: discover the purpose, then activate it inside first

> **The idea in one sentence:** a brand's purpose (why it exists, beyond profit) is discovered from heritage, archetype, customer, category and the needs of the world, articulated in a Who–Why–What–How framework, and lived by employees first, before it is taken to market.

## Explanation

Jim Stengel (former global head of marketing at P&G) and his colleagues refined the *purpose path to growth* model in more than 80 companies. Purpose answers the question of why the company or brand exists and is sometimes called a "brand ideal", because it will never be fully realized. How it differs from positioning: positioning targets a segment and the competitors; purpose speaks to all stakeholders, employees and investors included.

**Phase 1: Discovery.** Inputs: heritage, the archetype that governs the brand, customers' deep motivations, category and cultural trends, the competition and societal needs. The output is a **purpose framework** with four questions:

| Question | Choices |
|---|---|
| **Who** | audience and insight |
| **Why** | purpose and beliefs |
| **What** | promise and its pillars |
| **How** | personality and assets (tonality of advertising, packaging, logo) |

**Phase 2: Activation**, internal first, then external, through three activity systems: *captaining* (the behaviors required of leadership), *telegraphing* (demonstrative acts that show the organization is walking the talk) and *sustaining* (initiatives that perpetuate the focus on purpose). The process must be owned by the CEO. Principles: a common understanding of the case for purpose, a small multifunctional coalition of stewards, top-down and bottom-up engagement, integrating the purpose into metrics and policies, and accepting emotion.

## Application

- When the client asks for a "manifesto", ask whether there is also an internal activation plan; without one, the purpose stays a slogan.
- Look for a *telegraphing* gesture: an act that says more than an ad.
- Articulate the purpose as a redefinition of the business: not "we sell X", but "we are in the Y business".

## Example

Pharmavite (Nature Made) found that it isn't just in the vitamin business; it's in the vitality business: "We exist to bring the gift of health to life." Activation started with employees (fresh fruit, free vitamins and wellness screenings, a new headquarters with meditation rooms). Airbnb ("Belong Anywhere") telegraphed its purpose through meeting rooms designed as hosts' homes, its pledge against discrimination and the "We Accept" ad at the 2017 Super Bowl.

## Questions

- The figures the authors quote (purpose-driven companies growing three times faster) come partly from their own studies; how would you check them?
- What happens when an employee publicly contradicts the purpose (the 2018 Starbucks incident)?
