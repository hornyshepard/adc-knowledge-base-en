# The John Deere case: the brand essence stays, the company's orientation changes

> **The idea in one sentence:** an old brand stays relevant in a market transformed by technology if it defines its essence at the level of the customer's need, "helping farmers feed a growing world," and reorganizes the company around it.

## Explanation

The John Deere brand is almost two centuries old: the founder, a blacksmith in Grand Detour, Illinois, listened to farmers' frustrations and built a plow of highly polished steel that could shed the sticky soil of the Midwest; in 1837 he created the company that bears his name. The brand was built on quality and reliability ("Nothing Runs Like a Deere"), on the green and yellow colors and on local dealers involved in their communities.

Precision agriculture, based on data, sensors and GPS, changed what farmers needed: not just bigger and faster machines, but answers to questions that no single product division could solve alone (when is the best time to plant a given field?). What did not change: the focus on helping farmers succeed.

The company's response:
- **Intelligent Solutions Group (ISG)**: hundreds of system architects and developers, outside the five product platforms.
- **ECAP** (enterprise customer acquisition process): the move from a product-first orientation to a customer-first approach; cross-functional teams (product leaders, technology experts, agronomists) broke the season down into four phases (field preparation, plant or seed, apply, harvest) with the decisions and variables of each. The question was no longer how the planter performs, but how the tractor, the planter and the software work together.
- **Marketing involved from the beginning**, with design thinking and joint workshops with engineering.
- **Dealer transformation**: a shift to multilocation ownership (*Dealer of Tomorrow*), training for solution selling, value-selling guides on tablets, demonstrations in the field.

Result: 2018 revenues of nearly $37.4 billion and a brand that remains a leader.

## Application

- When revitalizing a heritage brand, state the essence at the level of the customer's permanent need, not the current product.
- Break the customer's "jobs" down into stages and decisions; communication and innovation are built on them.
- Do not forget the channel: dealers and salespeople are the face of the brand and must change along with it.

## Example

The chapter's contrast: Kodak, Blockbuster and BlackBerry were great brands that did not make it through the shift in their markets.
