# Patagonia's "Don't Buy This Jacket" is a Black Friday ad that asks you not to buy

> **The idea in one sentence:** on Black Friday 2011, Patagonia ran an ad in the *New York Times* that asked people not to buy its jacket. The paradox worked because years of consistent behavior stood behind it.

## Explanation

The facts, according to Viturino (ch. 1 and 4). The "Don't Buy This Jacket" campaign appeared in the *New York Times* on Black Friday 2011. It encouraged consumers to reconsider the real need to purchase a new product and to opt for alternatives: repairing old clothes or buying used products. The central message: instead of buying more, we should value what we already have and make conscious choices.

The ad rested on real practices:
- Patagonia pioneered the use of recycled polyester and uses organic cotton, responsible wool and recycled fabrics;
- the **Worn Wear** initiative offers repairs through a network of repair centers, repair kits and online tutorials;
- it donates 1% of its annual sales to environmental groups through "1% for the Planet", a program co-founded by Yvon Chouinard;
- its sustainability reports describe both its successes and the challenges it faced.

**Why it works** (the note's analysis, not the source's):
1. **The paradox grabs attention.** A request not to buy, published on the most commercial day of the year, contradicts the category and becomes a story for the press.
2. **Credibility comes from consistency.** The ad says what the company already does (repairs, durability). It does not promise a future change.
3. **Positioning on durability.** The implicit message is that the product lasts a long time. A brand that asks you to repair it declares its confidence in its quality.
4. **Narrative, not story.** The ending (you buy or you don't) is left to the reader.

## Application

- The "could a competitor sign it?" test. A fast-fashion retailer could not run the same ad without being accused of hypocrisy.
- When a client wants a sustainability campaign, first ask for the behavioral proof (the program, the figures, the report). If it does not exist, it is not the time for a campaign.

## Example

The idea can be turned into activations: a free repair stand instead of Black Friday discounts ties the message to a concrete act, as The Repair Cafe at Hassett Ace Hardware did (SRC-008, ch. 16).

## Questions

- The book claims that responsibility and commercial success "can coexist", but gives no sales data. How would you assess the campaign's effectiveness?
- An anti-consumption ad is still an ad. Where is the line between sincerity and strategy?
