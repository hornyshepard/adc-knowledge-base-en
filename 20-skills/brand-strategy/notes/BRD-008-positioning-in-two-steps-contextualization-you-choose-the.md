# Positioning in two steps: contextualization (you choose the reference brand) and conceptualization (you say it simply)

> **The idea in one sentence:** a brand cannot position itself in a vacuum; first you choose the competitive context that benefits you, then you find the simplest expression that places you in it.

## Explanation

Starting from the principle of the reference brand, Fernández Gómez uses the method of the Spanish planners Ramon Ollé and David Riu. The logic: in perception everything is relative, so an absolute judgment exists only in relation to a reference. And the reference is not given by the market; it is **chosen by the brand**.

**Step 1: Contextualization.** You think about the reference brand that benefits you most and place yourself next to it. The strategic challenge is not to "play better" than competitors but to establish the rules of the game, which the others then follow at a distance. This is what leading brands do: they "paint the axes" of the perceptual map. They educate consumers to buy according to the attributes that benefit them and force rivals to play on their ground.

**Step 2: Conceptualization.** The correct communication of the chosen context: the simplest and most popular expression of the offering, a word, a value proposition, a verbalized idea.

Ollé and Riu estimate that a good positioning strategy is 50% correct contextualization and 50% simple and efficient communication. They also warn that today products, in themselves, can no longer be positioned only on comparable attributes: a brand is a portfolio of meanings, and the consumer or the moment of use can themselves be sources of difference. Nilson adds two effects that are sometimes forgotten: a distinct position excludes some targets, and by positioning yourself you also reposition the competition.

## Application

- List three or four possible reference brands or categories (not only the direct competitor). For each one, ask: on which attribute would we win?
- Draw the perceptual map with the axes you want, not those of the current leader.
- Only after you have chosen the context, look for the word or expression that communicates it.

## Example

Kapferer, cited in the same chapter, explains the pioneer advantage: the first brand in a new market becomes the prototype that defines the criteria of choice. The brands that come after it are judged by its rules, so they are handicapped.

## Questions

- What risks arise when you choose a foreign category as the reference (for example, a bank that compares itself to a messaging app)?
- How do you test whether the audience accepts the context you have chosen?
