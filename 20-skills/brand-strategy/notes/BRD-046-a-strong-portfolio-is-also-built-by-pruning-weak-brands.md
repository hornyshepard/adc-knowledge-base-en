# A strong portfolio is also built by pruning weak brands

> **The idea in one sentence:** it isn't enough to optimize each brand on its own; the portfolio has to be managed as a whole: you build the core, add brands only for major opportunities and prune weak and redundant brands without mercy.

## Explanation

Calkins describes a strong portfolio by three characteristics: each brand has clear associations and a distinct customer advantage, there is little overlap, and the number of brands is manageable. The typical problems are the opposite: weak brands, redundant brands, too many brands.

The General Motors case: at first each brand had a clear segment (Chevrolet for young people starting out, Pontiac for people making progress in their careers, Buick for families, Oldsmobile for more mature buyers, Cadillac for luxury). Then each manager was told to drive growth, so they all introduced bigger and snazzier cars; a shared platform meant the brands were selling variations of essentially the same car. To make up for it, GM added Saab, Hummer and Saturn. The result: ten overlapping brands, inefficient, confusing, weak, and one reason GM went bankrupt in 2009.

**Calkins's five keys to success:**

1. Build and extend core brands (a new idea that fits the existing brand goes there).
2. Add brands only to address major opportunities: distinct, profitable and with the resources to manage them (PepsiCo launched Bubbly instead of extending Pepsi into carbonated water).
3. Proactively prune weak and redundant brands.
4. Keep things simple (a sub-brand that is meaningless to customers creates needless complexity).
5. Involve senior management, the only level that can make trade-offs among brands.

**Four ways to prune a brand:** divest it (maximizes its value, but could create a new competitor), harvest it (manage it solely for short-term profits), discontinue it (watch out for the abandoned trademark: P&G dropped White Cloud in 1993, an enterprising marketer claimed the mark and relaunched it as a premium brand at Walmart) and combine it gradually (Nestlé folded Contadina into Buitoni: first an endorser, then a sub-brand, then the name was dropped).

## Application

- Take an inventory of the client's portfolio and mark the overlaps in target and benefit.
- When you propose a new brand, also show which existing brand could be combined or discontinued.
- Acknowledge the emotional side: teams defend their brands as an identity.

## Example

Macy's brought dozens of iconic regional department stores (Marshall Field's, Dayton's, Lazarus) together under just two brands, Macy's and Bloomingdale's, and increased efficiency and built scale.

## Questions

- Why do the incentive systems in companies reward launching brands, not pruning them?
- How do you tell loyal customers that a brand they love is going away?
