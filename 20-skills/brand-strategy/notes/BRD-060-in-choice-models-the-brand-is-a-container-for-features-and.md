# In choice models the brand is a container for features, and its value shows in what remains after the features

> **The idea in one sentence:** a brand's strength can be measured through real choices: how much more often people choose the same set of features, at the same price, when it carries that name.

## Explanation

Struhl contrasts two ways of putting the brand into a model:

- In **traditional conjoint analysis**, the brand is just another attribute that combines freely with all the others. The result is impossible combinations (a luxury brand with "no annual fee") and the assumption that all brands respond to price in the same way.
- In **discrete choice modelling**, the brand is a **container**: each one has its own attributes and its own prices. The term called the **constant** measures the residual value of the brand, what remains after you account for the attributes being tested.

The practical consequence is the **brand × price interaction**. In the golden days of Sony, Sony televisions sold more than competitors' sets at any given price; RCA had to lower prices to sell as much; the store's own brand and an unknown brand ("Nonameo") fared considerably worse. An average price curve shared by all is wrong for each brand. A weak brand that uses it overestimates its sales and sits on unsold inventory.

The limit, which the author acknowledges: choice models show **that** the brand allows a price premium, not **why**. For the "why" (associations, perceptions) you need traditional research.

## Application

- When the client asks "what is our brand worth?", propose a behavioral measure: the price premium or the extra share for identical features.
- Use the difference as an argument for brand investment: a strong brand can afford not to compete on price alone.
- Combine it with qualitative research to explain where the premium comes from.

## Example

The Sony case is the book's example: an apparently amorphous "brand identity" becomes measurable "by the hard-edged metric of what people choose," as the extra price people pay for the same features.

## Questions

- Which components of brand equity escape a measure based only on choice?
- Can the brand constant grow through advertising? How would you test that?
