# The value equation: brands compete in the numerator or in the denominator

> **The idea in one sentence:** perceived value = (physical + emotional benefits) / (monetary + time costs), and each brand chooses where it differentiates and where it only keeps pace.

## Explanation

Tybout proposes representing the point of difference as a conceptual value equation:

**Value = (Physical benefits ± Emotional benefits) / (Monetary costs + Time costs)**

The numerator covers physical quality and the emotional response; the denominator, the money and time needed to acquire and use the brand. Brands usually choose one side of the fraction:

- **Starbucks** competes in the numerator: superior-tasting coffee (physical) and a comfortable destination (emotional).
- **McDonald's and Dunkin'** compete in the denominator: coffee quickly and inexpensively for people on the go.

The elements you do not differentiate on become points of parity: Starbucks cannot afford waiting times perceived as inordinately long or prices perceived as too high, and McDonald's has to have "good enough" coffee and "pleasant enough" stores.

Some brands claim the whole equation: GEICO, superior quality you can trust *and* savings in as little as 15 minutes. The advantage: you make the others look deficient. The risk: consumers who want to maximize a single element choose the specialist, and the "does it all" promise runs afoul of the lay theory "You get what you pay for".

The great merit of the equation is that it links positioning to the marketing mix: physical quality comes from the product, the emotional benefit from the experience and the image, the time cost from distribution, the monetary cost from price.

## Application

- Draw the equation for your brand and for two competitors; mark where the difference is and where the parity is.
- Check whether price, channel and experience support the same side of the fraction as the advertising.
- In copywriting, do not promise "premium" and "cheapest" at the same time without very strong proof.

## Example

A campaign that praises a coffee shop's atmosphere (numerator) is undermined if ordering in the app takes 20 minutes (the denominator becomes a point of disadvantage).

## Questions

- Where does a luxury brand sit in the equation? Can a high price actually increase the emotional benefit?
- What does the equation look like for a free digital service paid for with personal data?
