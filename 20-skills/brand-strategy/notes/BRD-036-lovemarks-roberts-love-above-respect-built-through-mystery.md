# Lovemarks (Roberts): love above respect, built through mystery, sensuality and intimacy

> **The idea in one sentence:** Kevin Roberts proposes that tired brands move from respect to love, that is, to Loyalty Beyond Reason; Fernández Gómez reads the model as a commercial repackaging of the emotional branding of the 1950s.

## Explanation

Roberts, former worldwide CEO of Saatchi & Saatchi (*Lovemarks: The Future Beyond Brands*, 2004), starts from a diagnosis: brands are out of juice. The causes: ever heavier brand manuals, the loss of mystery before a consumer who understands how brands work, a new consumer (better informed, more critical, less loyal), too many brands to be noticed as individuals, differentiation pursued by everyone in the same way, and conservatism.

The model has two axes, **respect** (performance, reputation, trust) and **love**, and three pillars of love:

| Pillar | Built through |
|---|---|
| **Mystery** | great stories; past, present and future; dreams; myths and icons; inspiration |
| **Sensuality** | the five senses: sound, sight, smell, touch, taste |
| **Intimacy** | commitment, empathy, passion |

Without respect, love does not last; respect alone is not enough. Jim Stengel (P&G) offers the most useful test: how would the consumer feel if you took the brand away?

**The author's critique.** The core (brands are about emotion and personality) is already in Dichter, Martineau, Levy and Ogilvy. The typology of emotions is stereotyped, the critique of quantitative research gives a feeling of déjà vu, and the book is more commercial than conceptual. Still, "mystery" anticipates storytelling, "sensuality" links motivational psychology to neuromarketing, and "intimacy" criticizes the brands that moved away from people to speak to shareholders. The commercial success is plain: the press linked the concept to winning the JCPenney account (estimated at US$430 million).

## Application

- Use the respect/love grid as a diagnosis: where is your brand today? Without respect (product, trust) there is no point investing in "love".
- For each pillar, list the existing assets: what story, what sensory experience, what gesture of intimacy does the brand already have?

## Example

Ordering a Coca-Cola and turning down the Pepsi offered instead (the example the book uses for emotional loyalty): the customer is demanding a brand, not a generic cola drink.

## Questions

- If "Great brands have always been Lovemarks" (Stengel), what does the model actually add?
- Can a B2B brand or a public utility be loved? Is it worth trying?
