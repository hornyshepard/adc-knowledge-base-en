# Implementing a brand strategy takes six elements, not just a positioning

> **The idea in one sentence:** a strategy that is 80 percent correct and executed 100 percent beats a perfect strategy executed at 80 percent, and brand execution depends on company strategy, brand platform, culture, governance, tools and momentum.

## Explanation

Eric Leininger (former senior vice president at McDonald's and Kraft Foods) interviewed senior marketing leaders at 15 companies and drew out a model with six elements:

| Element | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Company strategy** | the brand has to be seen as a prime economic driver; a firm that wins on a purely low-price strategy will not invest in brand building |
| **Brand platform** | positioning, character and purpose; it has to be *implementation friendly*: easily understood by everyone, everyone sees how their job relates to it, it provides a rallying cry |
| **Culture** | "culture feeds strategy" when the two are aligned; leaders model behavior (A. G. Lafley and the three moments of truth: at the point of purchase, at the moment of use, at the moment of social media) |
| **Brand governance system** | a brand council with a clear leader, which gives local markets "freedom within the framework" |
| **Tools** | guidelines that are easy to find (often a web site) and a scorecard that balances brand metrics with financial ones; Damgaard details what the guidelines contain: visual identity, messaging and tone, application across mediums, photography and imagery, mission and values |
| **Momentum** | quick, visible wins that justify the long-term commitment |

Leininger also lists the pitfalls: no commitment from senior management, platforms too elaborate for frontline employees, silos (even within marketing), inside-out process metrics instead of outside-in brand health metrics. The Starbucks example (2018): a brand built on inclusiveness was hit by a store manager's decision, because whether noncustomers could use the restrooms without making a purchase was left up to store managers.

The Ulta Beauty case (ch. 18) shows the model at work: a brand book for the corporate and store teams, the brand personality "fresh, fun, and real" carried online as well, the brands it sells aligned with the Ulta brand (an "all play"). Unaided awareness rose from 28 percent to 52 percent in five years.

## Application

- When you hand over a brand platform, check the three implementation-friendly guidelines with a frontline employee.
- Identify the moments of truth in the client's category and build KPIs around them (AbbVie called them "moments of meaning").
- Plan an explicit quick win in the first months of any brand campaign.

## Example

Richemont bought back watch inventory from retailers to prevent discounting and gray-market sales, giving up short-term profit for brand value.

## Questions

- Who should have anticipated the Starbucks crisis: marketing, operations or social responsibility?
- How detailed can the guidelines be without stifling local creativity?
