# Star strategy (Séguéla): the brand-person has physique, character and style

> **The idea in one sentence:** a brand without a physique does not sell, without a character it does not last, and without a style it is invisible; a brand that has all three becomes a "star".

## Explanation

Jacques Séguéla (RSCG) named his method *star-stratégie* in opposition to its forerunner, P&G's copy strategy, and in tribute to the Hollywood star system (*Hollywood lave plus blanc*). The copy strategy demonstrates the efficiency of an object; the star system appropriates the supremacy of dreams. Advertising should be "the dream factory": the public buys the product's image and desirability, not only its effect.

The contribution Fernández Gómez values most is the triad of the **brand-person**:

| Attribute | What it is | Without it… |
|---|---|---|
| **Physique** | the product, the benefit, what it does, its objective qualities, its differentiation | the brand does not sell |
| **Character** | the psychological universe, the invisible part that advertising reveals; what cultivates confidence and fosters loyalty | the brand does not last |
| **Style** | the language, the mise en scène, the symbols by which it is immediately recognized; "The style is the expression of the character" | the brand is invisible |

A brand that keeps changing its character runs the risk of not being understood. Séguéla also applies the self-concept: no consumer feels attracted by a product in which he does not see himself portrayed.

**The Chevron variant** ("Give your brand in marriage", 1985) takes the same three elements as stages of a relationship: seduction through the physique (packaging, colors, logo: enough for a sporadic relationship), then the style or *look* (difficult to copy, the first step toward emotional involvement), and finally the character (what wins hearts; it is felt over time, not shown). The author believes Chevron's order fits human logic better.

## Application

- Describe the brand in three paragraphs: physique (what it does, proof), character (three or four traits), style (visual code, tone, recurring gestures). Check what is missing.
- For the art director: style is the most visible attribute, but it has to express the character, not replace it.

## Example

*La boîte à outils de la publicité* applies the triad to Evian: **physique** (*le physique*) = purity and youth, the pure water of the Alps; **character** (*le caractère*) = balanced, cheerful, communicative, convivial; **style** (*le style*) = the child's soul (*l'âme d'enfant*), the behaviors of childhood, but across generations (hence the babies on roller skates). Vespa (Piaggio) shows style in Chevron's sense: an aesthetic and an attitude associated with the 1960s mod movement.

## Questions

- Does the star metaphor suit small or niche brands? Séguéla says all brands have to be stars.
- What happens to a brand with a strong style but a weak physique?
