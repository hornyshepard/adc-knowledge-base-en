# The awareness/liking/market share model points to the brand strategy

> **The idea in one sentence:** high awareness is not automatically an asset; by combining unaided awareness, liking and market share, you find out whether the brand needs extension, a new name, awareness building, repositioning or relationships.

## Explanation

Julie Hennessy criticizes the "more awareness is better" thinking. Awareness is a necessary condition, but it can also be an obstacle: if everyone knows you and dislikes you, changing their attitude is extremely hard. She recommends **unaided** awareness, a more honest measure than aided awareness, and combines it with **liking** and **market share**:

| Profile (A-L-S) | The situation | The strategy | Case |
|---|---|---|---|
| High-High-High | everyone knows you, likes you, buys you, but the category is flat | **expansion** into new categories, regions or audiences | Lego (movies, video games, girls, adult men who are former Lego players) |
| High-Low-Low | well known, but with negative associations that block any good product | **rebranding** (a new name) | Samsung in North America (1980s–1990s) did not rebrand and needed nearly 30 years of repositioning; Lucky Goldstar → LG succeeded quickly |
| Low-High-Low | few know you, but those who know you love you | **awareness building** (distribution, advertising) | Barilla in the United States, carried by the associations with Italy |
| Low-Low-Low | few know you, and those who know you don't like you | **repositioning**, preferably toward a new audience | Old Spice ("The Man Your Man Could Smell Like") |
| High-Low-High | everyone knows you, no one likes you, but they buy you anyway because they feel they have no choice | **building relationships**, away from the act of selling | Microsoft, helped by the Gates Foundation |

Two counterintuitive points: in the Low-Low-Low profile, low awareness is the good news (you have a clean slate); in the High-Low-High profile, any attempt to change perceptions while selling is met with suspicion, so the relationship is built through actions with no direct commercial interest.

## Application

- Ask the client for the three data points (unaided awareness, associations/liking, share) before you accept a brief to "build awareness."
- If the profile is High-Low-Low, discuss openly the option of a new brand; it can be ten times faster.
- For brands people must use and dislike (utilities, operators), look for projects that help people without selling.

## Example

Toyota launched Lexus, and Facebook focused its youth efforts under a different name, Instagram, precisely so as not to force their brands toward meanings they could not take on.

- Where would you place a well-known Romanian brand that is seen as old-fashioned?
