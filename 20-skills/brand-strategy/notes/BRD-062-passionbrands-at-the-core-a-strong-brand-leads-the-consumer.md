# Passionbrands: at the core, a strong brand leads the consumer instead of being led by them

> **The idea in one sentence:** brands that take their direction from consumer research end up looking alike and losing their identity; a brand with a belief of its own serves people better than one that only does what they ask.

## Explanation

Helen Edwards and Derek Day (*Creating Passionbrands*, 2005) published, almost at the same time as Lovemarks, a similar model that is, according to Fernández Gómez, on more solid conceptual foundations. They describe five "symptoms of malaise of consumer-led brands": a growing similarity between brands, an inconsistency between brand image and the product, a dearth of innovation and surprise, a distancing between the product and brand management capacity, and the lack of a "heart" in brands.

A **Passionbrand** has three traits: an **active belief**; trust based on its internal capacity (its "roots"); vibrancy in an ever-changing world. It is a brand that people don't just buy, but join. The driving force is the **idealized self**, who people want to become, not the fantasy self of escape.

The method has an analytical phase and a creative phase and uses the metaphor of a trampoline with four legs: **ideology** and **capability** (internal, controllable), **consumer** and **environment** (external). Their intersection is the *Passionpoint*, where the brand's identity is built. The practical recommendation: three values, simply stated, strongly felt, held even when the world moves on. Passion implies focus: better to do one thing superbly well than many things tolerably well.

The thesis worth keeping, according to the author: the objective is not to seek direction from consumers but to find better ways to serve them, combining that understanding with the company's own culture and capability. In the authors' words, "a brand must be consumer-leading not consumer-led." Here the model comes into tension with the consumer branding paradigm.

## Application

- When research asks for "more of what competitors have," ask what your brand believes and what it would refuse to do.
- Write the brand belief as a sentence of the "We believe that…" type, then check whether the product and the organization can support it.

## Example

The book does not analyze cases in detail; the author only notes that Passionbrands remained known almost only in the Anglo-Saxon world, while Lovemarks was translated into more than twelve languages: a difference in commercial drive, not in the quality of the idea.

## Questions

- Where is the line between a brand that leads and an arrogant brand that does not listen?
- How does the thesis fit with consumer-centered methods (design thinking, test and learn)?
