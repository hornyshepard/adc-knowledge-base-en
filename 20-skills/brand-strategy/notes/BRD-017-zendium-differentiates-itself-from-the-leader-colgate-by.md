# Zendium differentiates itself from the leader Colgate by what it leaves out and by a story of harmony instead of a war on bacteria

> **The idea in one sentence:** when the leader owns the main benefit (fluoride, cavities) and every competitor uses the same ingredient at the maximum legal level, a challenger can build its differentiation on what is absent and on a different narrative frame.

## Explanation

The case study in chapter 8, written by Lars Pynt Andersen, describes the toothpaste market. Colgate (Colgate-Palmolive) has over 40% of the global market and built its brand on fluoride. Today all major competitors use fluoride, almost all at the maximum legal amount, so "more fluoride" is no longer a possible benefit. Colgate adds strong flavors, the foaming agent SLS (sodium lauryl sulfate) and sub-brands with new active ingredients.

Zendium (Unilever), strong in Scandinavia, chooses a **differentiated positioning**:

- **Distinctive attribute**: three probiotic enzymes, also produced naturally in saliva, that support the "good" bacteria in the mouth, based on the research of a Dutch scientist.
- **Benefit claim**: "protect your mouth naturally", "born of science and powered by nature".
- **"Free-from" claims**: 0% SLS, no harsh chemicals. SLS is the main active component in detergents and washing-up liquid, and the case invites the unpleasant image of brushing your teeth with washing-up liquid.
- **An opposite narrative frame**: a TV ad compares the mouth to the Milky Way. The human body is home to as many microorganisms as there are stars in the sky, "a friendly little universe of bacteria". The story is about symbiosis, not about killing off germs.
- **Sensory experience**: soft brushing, mild taste, and you can actually taste your breakfast after brushing.

## Application

- When the leader owns the category's main benefit, look for an important benefit the leader cannot claim without contradicting itself (here: no SLS).
- Use "free-from" claims as attributes (A) that support a subjective characteristic (C): gentle, natural.
- Change the category's metaphor. If everyone talks about "war", you can talk about "harmony".

## Example

In the benefit focus options, "0% SLS" is an attribute. "Gentle and natural" is the subjective characteristic it supports (A → C). The image of the "mouth as a friendly universe" adds a positive emotion, useful if part of the target audience also buys for pleasure, and not only to avoid cavities.

## Questions

- Is Zendium's positioning product-oriented or user-oriented?
- What does a "natural" brand risk if the leader responds with its own "SLS-free" variant?
