# Bloom & Wild: letting customers opt out of Mother's Day campaigns is still marketing, thoughtful marketing

> **The idea in one sentence:** the online florist Bloom & Wild lets customers stop receiving messages about occasions that are painful for them (Mother's Day, Father's Day). A gesture of giving up a sale became a brand statement.

## Explanation

Ejeke presents the case under the trend "content segmentation". Classic segmentation groups *people* by demographics or common interests. Content segmentation groups *messages* by type or theme. The subscriber can opt out of a particular type of content, instead of all or nothing (trend 29).

Bloom & Wild applied the idea to sensitive occasions. Customers can ask not to receive the messages about Mother's Day or Father's Day. The source does not go into the reasons, but it is easy to see for whom these days are painful (for example, after the loss of a parent). In 2019, the company launched the **Thoughtful Marketing Movement**, based on the idea of "serving clients with the same care you would offer your own friends and family." Ejeke also gives a counterexample: many people who don't celebrate Christmas are bombarded with festive marketing every December, which, in a personalized digital environment, makes a bad impression.

Why it works (analysis): (1) the brand shows that it knows what its audience feels, not only what it buys. This is an emotional insight turned into a product feature. (2) The option itself is a message: even those who do not use it learn that the brand cares. (3) The behavior is easy to copy, but the *first* brand to do it owns it as a story.

## Application

- In any CRM plan or campaign calendar, add the question: "Who could this message hurt?" Then offer an opt-out by theme, not only a full unsubscribe.
- In the brief, treat the opt-out as a copy opportunity: the tone of the opt-out request says a lot about the brand.
- Occasions worth thinking about: Mother's Day and Father's Day, Valentine's Day, religious holidays, Back to School.

## Example

For a florist, Mother's Day is one of the biggest sales occasions of the year, so not pushing it has a real commercial cost. That cost is exactly what makes the gesture credible: it is hard to read as a mere calculation.

## Questions

- Ejeke gives no results data. How would you measure the effect of such a gesture: retention, mentions, NPS?
- When does "thoughtful marketing" become just another PR technique that everyone copies?
