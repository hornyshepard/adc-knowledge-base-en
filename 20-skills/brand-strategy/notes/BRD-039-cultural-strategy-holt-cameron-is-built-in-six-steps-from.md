# Cultural strategy (Holt & Cameron) is built in six steps, from the category's orthodoxy to a new myth

> **The idea in one sentence:** the myth of an iconic brand is not guessed but deduced: you start from what the whole category says, look for the social change that makes that discourse outdated, and build a new ideology from real cultural materials.

## Explanation

Batat presents the method from Douglas Holt's second book, written with Douglas Cameron (*Cultural Strategy*, 2012). It starts from two premises of the theory of iconic brands:

- **The market is segmented by cultural tensions**, not by socio-demographic criteria. Myth markets arise from contradictions between a dominant ideology and the "identity project" of individuals.
- **The brand's power is collective.** Holt says that marketers like to think of brands as psychological phenomena that frame the perceptions of each consumer, while their power comes from the collective nature of these perceptions. The brand is a "symphony" of actors (media, communities, other brands, popular culture), and the manager becomes a **cultural narrator** who borrows and enriches stories already told by more powerful cultural actors: films, social movements, political speeches.

**The six steps** (Table 7.2):

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1. Examine the cultural orthodoxy | the conventional cultural expression (ideology, myths, cultural codes) of the category and the competitors |
| 2. Identify the social disruption | the change (technological, economic, media) that weakens the orthodoxy |
| 3. Uncover the ideological opportunity | the emerging ideology toward which people's values gravitate |
| 4. Collect the source material | cultural expressions from subcultures, social movements or the brand's own history |
| 5. Apply cultural tactics | provoke ideological fighting, mythologize the brand or revive a dormant ideology |
| 6. Shape the cultural strategy | answer a precise occasion, in a given social setting, with a specific cultural manifestation |

The first three steps are analysis: what is conventional, what has changed, where values are moving. The last three are construction: from what materials, with what tactic, at what moment.

## Application

- Use the steps as the structure of a strategy workshop: one slide for the category's orthodoxy, one for the disruption, one for the opportunity.
- When all competitors say the same thing, that is where the space for a new myth lies.
- Look for the source material in real cultures (music, street sport, trades), not in stock moodboards.

## Example

Nike: the orthodoxy of the 1970s celebrated superhuman champions; the economic crisis at the end of the decade made it outdated; the opportunity was "combative individual will", built from the cultural codes of the African American community.

- Batat sums up the method in a few pages. What would be lost if you applied it without reading the original source?
- How do you tell a real social disruption from a social media trend?
