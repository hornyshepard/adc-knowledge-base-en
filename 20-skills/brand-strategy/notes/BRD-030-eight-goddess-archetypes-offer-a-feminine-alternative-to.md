# Eight goddess archetypes offer a feminine alternative to the 12 Jungian archetypes

> **The idea in one sentence:** instead of Jung's "Big Twelve" archetypes used in advertising, Jane McCarthy and Kate McAndrew propose eight Greco-Roman goddesses (each with a counterpart god), with their own values, gifts, words, symbols and colors, which give the brand a recognizable character.

## Explanation

An archetype is a character who is deeply familiar, who has existed throughout time (from Shakespeare to Hollywood) and embodies an essential human energy. The authors distinguish archetypes from stereotypes: a stereotype is like a cartoon that offers a simplistic experience, while an archetype is lush and layered. Brands that "hire" an archetype connect with ideas and emotions bigger than the product's function and can say a lot in an instant (*bullseye brand transmissions*).

Jane McCarthy recounts that she used Jung's twelve archetypes for years in advertising, but found them lacking for brands with a feminine identity (the example of a Betty Boop style "Seductress" profile at a multinational corporation). Her system starts from Jean Shinoda Bolen's *Goddesses in Everywoman*, layered with the Enneagram, archetypal astrology and mythology:

| Goddess | Essence | Feelings offered | Counterpart god | Brands cited |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Diana**, the Free | adventure, courage, independence | courageous, strong, free | Mars, the Rugged | Nike, Always, Rivian, The North Face |
| **Hestia**, the Sacred | peace, purity, harmony, healing | calm, balanced, tranquil | Apollo, the Shining | Goop |
| **Demeter**, the Love | compassion, care, abundance | nurtured, loved, secure | Poseidon, the Deep | Subaru |
| **Maiden Persephone**, the Dream | imagination, sweetness, magic | optimistic, youthful, enchanted | Pan, the Mischief | Kin Euphorics |
| **Queen Persephone**, the Fire | transformation, rebellion, truth | passionate, provocative, bold | Hades, the Mystery | Savage X Fenty, Liquid Death (Hades) |
| **Hera**, the Regal | tradition, partnership, hospitality | elegant, classic, connected | Hermes, the Eloquent | Southern Living |
| **Venus**, the Beauty | joy, pleasure, creativity | inspired, indulgent, radiant | Hephaestus, the Genius | Vogue, Beautiful by Drew |
| **Athena**, the Wise | wisdom, strategy, excellence | empowered, clear, in command | Zeus, the Great | Chief, Ellevest |

Each profile has the same structure: who she is, what is in her heart, what gifts she brings, her style (personality, words, symbols, colors), cultural icons, the "constellation" of related goddesses from other cultures (Durga, Baba Yaga, Berehynia) and the complementary god. The authors state that they chose the highest, lightest interpretations of the myths and that feminine and masculine energies mix.

## Application

- Use the table as a working vocabulary: each goddess's lists of words, symbols and colors are a starting point for the moodboard and the tone of voice.
- Analyze the competitors in a category by the archetype they embody; the gaps become opportunities.

## Example

Liquid Death sells water with the energy of Hades (mysterious, dark, ironic) in a category dominated by purity and health (Hestia); that is exactly why it is noticed instantly on the shelf.

## Questions

- What does a system of eight archetypes gain and lose compared with the system of 12 (Mark & Pearson)?
- Can Greco-Roman archetypes work as well for a Romanian audience?
