# The MACE framework (Collis & Collis): a digital brand lasts if it offers mastery, accessibility, cadence and ensnarement

> **The idea in one sentence:** in digital, brands rise and fall fast; MACE describes four factors a brand can use to prolong its life.

## Explanation

Sachdev takes the MACE framework for enduring brands in the digital age from David and William Collis (Harvard Business School, 2020). The objective it serves is called *digital durability*, the ability to make a brand last in an online world: Coca-Cola has done this well; AOL, once a pioneering Internet brand, has not.

| Factor | What it means | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| **Mastery** | rewards and incentives to stay with the brand, an ecosystem that grows more valuable the more you use it | Apple's set of products and services; Samsung's wide range |
| **Accessibility** | the product is easy to obtain | Canva's free basic version |
| **Cadence** | constant content, including micro-content, user contributions, constant communication even when things are going badly | Apple's customer service on Twitter; its product launch events |
| **Ensnarement** | the brand becomes "sticky" and people stay with it | the quality of Apple's products and the anticipation of new versions; the Harley-Davidson communities |

Cadence and ensnarement are the most closely tied to communication: the first calls for a permanent editorial calendar, the second for community and rituals.

## Application

- Score the client's brand from 1 to 5 on each factor; the weakest one becomes the priority of the digital strategy.
- For cadence: plan "crisis" communication too, not just launches.
- Build ensnarement out of value (community, status, usefulness), not only out of switching costs.

## Example

Apple ticks all four: an ecosystem that rewards loyalty, easy ordering from anywhere, constant communication (including public customer service on social media), plus the anticipation of every new version.

## Questions

- "Ensnarement" literally means catching in a trap. Where does it turn into abusive *lock-in* or *dark patterns*?
- Can MACE be applied to a small brand with no product ecosystem?
