# Brand culture is a universe of myths, rituals and values that the customer enters, not a stream of content about the brand (Emphasis)

> **The idea in one sentence:** Batat's fourth E replaces "push" promotion with brand culture: a system of myths, values and rituals co-constructed with people, which goes beyond any medium that carries it.

## Explanation

Batat notes that a message pushed through a medium loses its effectiveness amid a multiplicity of platforms. The "Emphasis" component of the experiential marketing mix humanizes the brand through personality, myth and values, with two levers: **brand culture** and **storytelling**. Three changes explain the shift from *brand content* (the brand's short films, documentaries, books) to *brand culture*: fragmented audiences, who choose the medium themselves and produce content; the demand for responsible communication after the 2008 crisis; and the move from modernity (the brand as an agent of progress) to postmodernity (self-deprecation) and to "alter-modernity", in which communication becomes a service for the customer's experience.

| Brand content | Brand culture |
|---|---|
| messages translated through a speech | a place in which consumers can immerse themselves |
| brand reality is affected by the media that carries it | reality goes beyond the media that expresses it |
| affirms the positioning and the expertise of the brand | creates a universe of the brand in which values are captured, created and shared |

**Where the sources converge.** Parsons, Maclaran and Chatzidakis define brand culture the same way (following Schroeder): the system of symbols and meanings that makes the brand part of everyday life. It is continually recreated by the company, employees (Innocent and its "Fruit Towers"), the culture industries, intermediaries and customers, and its power lies in collective perception, not in an individual's psychological response. Both sources warn that, because it is co-constructed, brand culture can escape the company's control.

## Application

- Before a communication platform, write down the brand's culture: what myths, rituals, norms and beliefs does it have? Which are its own and which are borrowed?
- Check whether a campaign only affirms the positioning or creates a space in which people can live the values.
- Watch who uses the brand and what meanings they bring: users can change the culture.

## Example

Three personal care brands, three cultures (mini-case 7.2): Nivea, protection and natural beauty, a cream whose recipe has stayed unchanged since 1911; Dove, the fight for women's self-esteem, models in photos that have not been retouched; L'Oréal, beauty as a cultural fact, backed by research.

## Questions

- Can a new brand, with no history, have a culture, or only content?
- What does the company do when the community builds a brand culture different from the one it wants?
