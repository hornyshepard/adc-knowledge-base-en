# The 3i model: a brand is credible only when identity, integrity and image are aligned

> **The idea in one sentence:** in a world where consumers believe one another more than they believe companies, positioning without verifiable differentiation is a mere claim, and a brand becomes credible only when the brand–positioning–differentiation triangle is complete.

## Explanation

Kotler and his coauthors start from the triangle of brand, positioning and differentiation and add the "3i" to it:

| Side | What it means | What it reaches |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand identity** | positioning in the consumer's mind: unique, so that it is noticed in the cluttered marketplace, and relevant to rational needs | the mind |
| **Brand integrity** | fulfilling the promise through real differentiation, the brand's "DNA", the proof | the spirit |
| **Brand image** | a share of the consumer's emotions, beyond functions | the heart |

The logic of the decision: positioning triggers the mind to consider a purchase, authentic differentiation lets the spirit confirm the decision, and the heart leads to the act. Positioning alone makes consumers wary; differentiation that is synergetic with it automatically creates a good image.

On social media, a brand behaves like a member of the community: its identity is its "avatar", rated by accumulated experience, and one bad experience spoils its integrity and its image. Hence the authors' conclusion: vertical control no longer works; honesty, originality and authenticity are what count.

## Application

- In the brief, write the positioning on one line and, below it, the list of verifiable proofs (policies, products, behaviors). Without proofs, do not communicate the claim.
- Test the campaign: a creative idea that promises more than the existing proofs erodes integrity.
- Image is not "made" directly in the ads; it results from aligning the first two sides.

## Example

Timberland positioned itself as "the good outdoor-inspired footwear and apparel company", with the differentiation proven by its Path of Service volunteer program. When net profits fell in 1994 from $22.5 million to $17.7 million, the program was not cut, because it was considered part of the brand's DNA (pp. 55–57). S.C. Johnson supports its positioning as "the sustainable five-generation family company" through bottom of the pyramid projects in Kenya.

## Questions

- What do you do when the real differentiation is invisible to the consumer (for example, an ethical supply chain)?
- How long can a brand with a strong image but weak integrity survive (greenwashing)?
