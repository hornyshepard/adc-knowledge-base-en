# A digital brand has three components that work together: identity, visibility and credibility

> **The idea in one sentence:** a strong digital brand is known for what it stands for, is seen where people look and is believed.

## Explanation

Sachdev starts from the Digital Branding Institute's definition: the identity, visibility and credibility of a brand among the consumers who discover, relate to and interact with it on digital platforms.

- **Digital identity** is the story of the brand: what it stands for, how it got where it is, what that does for the consumer. Social media profiles, the website and other digital assets build it.
- **Digital visibility** is the extent to which consumers are exposed to the brand on search engines, in social media, on blogs.
- **Digital credibility** is the trust placed in the brand online: mobile app rankings, reviews, voice and tone.

The three work hand in hand: content marketing works on identity and visibility, social media marketing on identity and credibility.

The book's digital branding strategies: the value proposition and positioning (including the niche, where consumers may cherish the brand because it is so tailored to them), the brand story, design (the same logo, colors, fonts and style on every platform), content that supports the product (recipes for a grocery brand, health tips for a gym) and connectedness, that is, how often and in how many ways people interact with the brand.

## Application

- Diagnose which of the three components is weak and choose the tactic accordingly: content for identity, search and paid media for visibility, reviews and transparency for credibility.
- Check that the logo and colors look the same on every platform; otherwise people do not recognize the brand.

## Example

BlackBerry used its old reputation for secure smartphones to reposition itself as a cybersecurity provider. It is a digital rebranding that keeps the credibility and changes the identity.

## Questions

- Can a brand have high visibility and low credibility? What happens to paid campaigns then?
- Is the digital brand different from the brand as such, or is it the same construct seen through digital channels?
