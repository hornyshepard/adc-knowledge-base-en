# A brand helps customers choose quickly and protects the firm from price competition, through elements that are easy to recognize

> **The idea in one sentence:** a brand is an asset built from memorable elements (name, logo, characters, slogan, sound, packaging) that shortens the customer's decision and brings the firm loyalty, protection and market value.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy start from a blunt statement: a company lives or dies based on brand awareness, because consumers cannot buy products they don't know exist. **Brand elements** (Exhibit 11.4, adapted from Keller):

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| Brand name | Comfort Inn (suggests product characteristics), Apple (no association with the product), Zillow (invented term) |
| URL / domain name | toyota.com |
| Logos and symbols | the McDonald's arches; the Nike swoosh, recognized even without the word |
| Characters | Tony the Tiger, the Energizer Bunny |
| Slogans | "Like A Good Neighbor" (State Farm), "America Runs on Dunkin'" |
| Jingles and sounds | Intel's four-note sound signature; Mastercard's new "sonic brand", which plays at checkout |

Packaging is also treated as a brand element, with tangible benefits: it attracts attention, stands out on the shelf, carries promotions ("NEW") and allows sizes for different markets.

Policarpio (SRC-002) makes the legal distinctions: the **brand name** (of the product), the **trade name** (of the company), the **brand mark** (the symbol, coloring, lettering, such as Apple's apple or Coca-Cola's cursive typeface), the **trademark** (the legal protection) and **trade characters** (the Quaker Man, used for over 100 years).

**The value of branding**:

- for the customer: **brands facilitate purchases** (you recognize the Pepsi logo before you read the label) and **establish loyalty** through trust;
- for the firm: **brands protect from competition and price competition** (Lacoste commands a premium price), **brands are assets** that can be legally protected (McDonald's won the "MACCOFFEE" case) and **brands affect market value** (the decline of the RadioShack brand led to bankruptcy).

## Application

- For a rebranding or a new brand, check each element: is it easy to recognize and remember? Does it work without the others (logo without name, sound without image)?
- Think of the system, not the piece: the elements must tell the same story at every touchpoint.
- Sound is an underused territory. An authentic sonic brand can work as an audio logo.

## Example

Mastercard created a combination of notes that "translates" its logo into sound. It plays in all its advertising and is heard at checkout when the payment is accepted. A counterexample on authenticity: Lay's collaboration with Bebe Rexha on music inspired by chip flavors left fans cold.

## Questions

- Which brand element of a local brand would a Romanian consumer recognize without seeing the name?
- When does a brand element become too strong and freeze the brand (impossible to change)?
