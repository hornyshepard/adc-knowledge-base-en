# Life transitions and threats to the self-concept open people up to new brands

> **The idea in one sentence:** brands enter the self-concept most easily when it is unstable: after a major life change, in a conflict between two identities, or after a blow to the self-image.

## Explanation

Roese and Gardner set out seven principles for self-brand connections. Four of them describe the moments and mechanisms through which a brand becomes "mine":

**Life transitions (principle 2).** Moving, marrying, divorcing, losing or gaining a job, a first child, retirement: all require incorporating a new identity or de-emphasizing an old one. The freshman arriving at college buys college-branded T-shirts but also brings the stuffed animals of childhood. In these periods people are particularly open to new brands, and the opportunity peaks within **six months to a year** after the transition. Social media data (posts about weddings, births, retirements) allow *hyper-targeting*, beyond the classic diaper offer for first-time parents.

**Self-concept conflicts (principle 3).** A software engineer who also sees herself as feminine and stylish lives with a tension between two identities; a rose gold paperclip from See Jane Work quietly bridges them. A Patagonia briefcase unites the identity of the outdoor enthusiast with that of the office worker.

**Threats to the self-concept (principle 4).** When the self-image is threatened, people compensate: those whose intelligence was questioned choose a fine writing instrument, and someone who made an embarrassing purchase adds *Scientific American*. Lean Cuisine ("Weigh This") moved the conversation from weight to personal accomplishments. Strong connections also protect the brand: Samsung's fans defended it after the 2016 exploding battery crisis.

**Usage (principle 7).** The self also grows through observing one's own behavior (Joan regularly drinks Brisk iced tea and so infers that she is a "Brisk drinker"). Mere ownership creates the endowment effect. That is why incentivizing usage (the Starbucks mobile app, free trials, auto-enrollment with opt-out) matters more than incentivizing purchase.

## Application

- Map the life transitions relevant to the category and build offers and messages for the first months after them.
- Look in the target audience for conflicts between identities that the brand can bridge.
- Design activation campaigns around repeated usage, not only around the first purchase.

## Example

Trading in the sports sedan for a minivan upon the birth of a child is practical, but it also announces a new identity, fatherhood, to oneself and to the world.

## Questions

- Where does targeting based on life transitions cross the ethical line (illness, divorce)?
- Does compensation also work for B2B brands?
