# Global brands with soul: the common thread, the glocal balance, the agile rollout

> **The idea in one sentence:** a global brand starts from a universal desire, keeps its essence at the center and leaves the local context to the people on the ground: neither "hopelessly local" nor "mindlessly global."

## Explanation

Sanjay Khosla (former president of developing markets at Kraft Foods) proposes three keys:

**1. The common thread.** Global brands grow from universal desires, "desires that have passports": nearly every teenage boy has his mind on sex (Axe), everyone wants to be an athlete (Nike). Segment by habits and needs, not by borders: not South Koreans, but new South Korean mothers. Involve local managers (Corn Flakes failed in India, where milk tends to be warm) and test on a small scale (Tang single-serving sachets were tested in Brazil alone first).

**2. The glocal balance.** Global owns what crosses borders: technology, values, safety and above all the overall positioning and the essence of the brand; local owns the context: tastes, pricing, distribution, competitors. Lines of responsibility must be clearly drawn from the start. The two emblematic failures: Gillette launched low-cost razors in rural India without knowing that the lack of running water made shaving cream clog them ("mindlessly global"); Pond's let each country draw its own tulip for the logo and ended up with more than 50 different tulips ("hopelessly local").

**3. The orchestrated rollout.** Agility, educating new markets (the North American team for years had little interest in taking on Axe) and gradual growth. Best Buy and Uber show the cost of expanding too fast, values included.

The central example is Oreo. The product was adapted locally (packs of seven cookies in China, green tea flavor, a less sweet version), and the Chinese ads featured Yao Ming. But the "twist, lick, dunk" ritual was kept everywhere, despite French protests, because it expresses the essence: the bond between parent and child. Revenues in developing markets increased fivefold in six years.

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott reach a similar conclusion from another direction: standardization works for low-involvement, low-emotion categories in close cultures, and the more emotion and cultural distance grow, the more the execution has to be adapted (ADV-049). Khosla adds the governance rule: even then, the essence stays global.

## Application

- When you adapt an international campaign, list explicitly what is "essence" (does not change) and what is "context" (is adapted).
- Phrase the global insight as a human desire, not as a product feature.
- Test the local execution in a pilot market before the rollout.

## Example

In India McDonald's sells the Chicken Maharaja Mac and the McAloo Tikki, but keeps the signature McDonald's naming devices, the fast and clean in-store presentation and the theme that a McDonald's meal is fun.

## Questions

- How did Axe stay the same brand when it moved from sexually charged ads to the "Is it okay for guys to…" campaign?
- When does the global essence become a brake (Apple and the price of the iPhone in India)?
