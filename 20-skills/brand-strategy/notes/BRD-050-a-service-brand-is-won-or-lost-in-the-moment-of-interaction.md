# A service brand is won or lost in the moment of interaction

> **The idea in one sentence:** with services, production and consumption happen at the same time, through people, so brand value is created or destroyed in every interaction; that is why it needs precise standards, and also latitude for employees.

## Explanation

Tom O'Toole (former chief marketing officer at United Airlines and Hyatt) starts from five particular traits of services:

1. **Intangibility**: you cannot try out a dentist or a cruise beforehand, so the perceived risk is high and the brand has to build trust.
2. **Inseparability**: the service and the employee who delivers it are one.
3. **Variability and perishability**: a hair stylist has good and bad days; an unsold seat on a plane remains unsold forever.
4. **Supply that is hard to adjust in the short term**: a hotel has 500 rooms, however many people want to come.
5. **No ownership**: the customer owns nothing; on platforms (Uber, Airbnb) the provider does not own the assets either, which increases the variation.

Digital brings self-service, "informed contact" (the flight attendant knows the passenger had two flights cancelled in the previous week) and the shift of manufacturers toward services (GE sells "power by the hour," not engines).

To build a service brand, the author recommends:
- **clarity about the value proposition** and how it is delivered, checked in person on the ground;
- very detailed **service standards** (what time is quoted for room service, where the food is placed, should the coffee be poured?), but with **latitude** for employees: rigidity destroys value as surely as chaos (see "the Hyatt Touch");
- **internal communication before external**: employees should never see the new television campaign for the first time when it airs;
- **simple metrics** that frontline staff understand ("The coffee was hot when delivered");
- increasingly personalized **loyalty programs** ("nonprogram loyalty programs").

Marketing textbooks describe the same particular traits as four characteristics (intangible, inseparable, heterogeneous, perishable; MKT-027). O'Toole adds a fifth, no ownership, and turns them into brand decisions.

## Application

- Before you promise "care" or "professionalism" in an ad, check what the frontline employee actually experiences.
- Propose an internal launch of the campaign to the client, with employees as ambassadors.
- Turn the brand promise into a few observable standards.

## Example

Amm's Limousine Service always arrives 15 minutes before the scheduled pickup time; the driver opens the door and asks what route you prefer. The repeated standard creates differentiation, reduces risk and makes the customer willing to pay a premium.

## Questions

- How do you adapt service standards when the interaction becomes fully digital?
- Where does standardization end and turning the employee into a robot begin?
