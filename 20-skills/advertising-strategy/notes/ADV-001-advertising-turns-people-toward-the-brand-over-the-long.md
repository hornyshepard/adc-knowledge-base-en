# Advertising turns people toward the brand over the long term, promotion moves the sale forward now, and promotion does not need an incentive

> **The idea in one sentence:** the difference between advertising and promotion lies not in the channel or in the presence of a coupon, but in the objective: positive attitude built over time versus purchase intention triggered right away.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy define advertising by four traits: it is paid, it is delivered through media, it comes from an identifiable source and it is persuasive, that is, it aims at some action now or in the future. Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott draw a finer line, starting from etymology, after Daniel Starch: *advertere* means "to turn towards," *promovere* "to move forward."

| | Advertising | Promotion |
|---|---|---|
| Horizon | long term | short term, tactical |
| Main effect | positive brand attitude | purchase intention |
| Mechanism | indirect: information or emotion that forms a favorable impression | a reason to act **now** |

Two counterintuitive consequences. First: "a promotion does not require an incentive." It is enough that the primary communication objective is brand purchase intention and that the message encourages immediate action. When retailers feature a brand, or a marketer features one item from a brand's line, it is almost always a promotion. Second: promotion also always has brand awareness and brand attitude as objectives, so it has to contribute to the brand (*consumer franchise building*, Prentice).

The authors insist that the role of advertising has not changed with digital: it sells more of the brand or achieves a higher price, through brand awareness and brand attitude. McTigue and Rucker reach the same separation from the side of the brief: they cite the Binet and Field heuristic, roughly 60% long-term brand building and 40% short-term product-focused support, but treat it as a benchmark, not a rule.

## Application

- When the client asks for "a campaign that sells this month," call it by its name: it is a promotion, and the brief has to contain a reason to act now.
- Check every promotion against the two "great commandments" the authors cite (Simmons): it must relate directly to the objectives and be compatible with the total brand proposition.
- Do not "stick" a coupon on an image ad; the promotion is created separately, with the same key benefit.

## Example

The Natuzzi ad for the Re-vive recliner does not ask for a purchase. It links the brand to comfort and relaxation, so that it will be chosen when the reader enters the market. A store poster with the same recliner "this weekend only" would be a promotion, even without a discount.

## Questions

- By this criterion, is a performance ad on social media advertising or promotion?
- What is lost if a brand runs only promotions for several years in a row?
