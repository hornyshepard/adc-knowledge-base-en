# The message must be framed to fit a promotion or a prevention orientation

> **The idea in one sentence:** the same benefit persuades better when it speaks the language of the person's goal: gain and progress for those with a promotion orientation, safety and avoiding loss for those with a prevention orientation.

## Explanation

Brian Sternthal shows that the impact of an ad depends not only on its content but also on how that content is framed. Persuasion often involves **self-persuasion**: an ad works to the extent that its information resonates with what people already know. Those with significant prior knowledge of the category weigh the claims against their experience and may become skeptical (is mesh underwear really comfortable?).

One form of framing is matching the person's **regulatory orientation** (*regulatory fit*). The same motive (competence related to control) can be experienced in two ways:

| Orientation | What the person seeks | Fitting frame | Tampax example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prevention** | safety, avoiding problems | "it protects you", "it stops leaks" | a woman plugs the hole in a leaking boat with a tampon |
| **Promotion** | accomplishment, progress, confidence | "it lets you move ahead" | a woman in a white dress walks rapidly to work and confidently deals with her skirt mishap herself |

The fit between orientation and frame creates a positive feeling (it feels right), which is transferred to the brand. Sternthal specifies when this matters more: people usually base their evaluations on content and prior opinions, but when they become uncertain they rely on their feelings about how they reached the judgment, which, he suggests, happens more with online advertising than with advertising vetted by traditional media.

The same logic applies to amplifying the benefit: Rent the Runway spoke the language of promotion (the closet becomes the office for launching a startup), Dollar Shave Club the language of prevention (you save time and money).

## Application

- In the insight, note not only the motivation but also the target audience's orientation: does it want to gain something or to avoid losing something?
- Write two headline versions for the same benefit, one framed for promotion and one for prevention, and test them.
- For self-persuasion, let the audience formulate the argument itself (see the example below).

## Example

The anti-smoking campaign in Thailand: kids asked adult smokers for a light; the adults refused and explained why smoking is dangerous; the kids then handed them a note asking why they did not worry about themselves. Inquiries about how to stop smoking increased 40%.

## Questions

- Is orientation a stable trait of the person, or does it depend on the situation and the category?
- What would an insurance campaign framed for promotion look like?
