# The high-involvement transformational strategy requires the target audience to identify personally with the brand as portrayed, and liking must extend from the ad to the brand

> **The idea in one sentence:** with a sports car, luxury fashion or an expensive holiday, people buy a feeling; but because they risk money or status, they must believe that this exact brand, as it is shown, will give them that feeling.

## Explanation

The quadrant covers risky (financially or psychologically), positively motivated decisions: sports cars, haute couture, jewelry, vacations, premium drinks for connoisseurs, corporate image. **The principles** (Table 9.7):

1. Be alert to possible **dual motivations** and the need to provide information. The risk may call for some proof.
2. Elicit the correct emotional response and link it to the brand.
3. Make sure it is **emotionally authentic**.
4. The target audience must **personally identify** with the brand as portrayed in the execution.
5. Liking must go **beyond the execution** and extend to the product and the brand.

**The creative tactics** (Box 13.2):

- authenticity is tailored to the lifestyle group to which the target audience belongs or aspires;
- a unique execution, a strong visual, a C → E⁺ or E⁺ benefit focus;
- tangible information where the risk calls for it, possibly with a slight over-claim, but without exaggeration. The Indian Ocean example: an emotional image, plus a lot of information in the small print;
- the emotional sequence for social approval: from apprehension to feeling flattered, from shame to pride ("Think how your friends will feel when you serve them our reserve cognac");
- **source**: attractiveness through **similarity**. Not someone like the target audience, but someone like the person the target audience *imagines or wants* to be.

**Media**: visuals are required. **High frequency is not needed**, unlike in the low-involvement transformational quadrant, because purchase cycles are long. Television, digital and most print media work, but not radio. If detailed information is needed, processing time also matters.

## Application

- Define the target audience's aspirational group and check whether the characters, places and photographic style belong to it.
- Test not only whether the ad is liked but whether the *brand* becomes desired after exposure.
- Separate the layers: the emotion in the image and the headline, the proof (warranty, specifications, awards) in the secondary copy.

## Example

In 2015 Puma took on pop star Rihanna as global ambassador for its women's products. She was not an athlete, but she was an icon who had worn athletic-looking costumes as part of her stage and street persona for years. She met the requirement of attractiveness and aspirational similarity, and the company had not found a female athlete with global appeal. Michter's Bourbon (for a whiskey drinker, it is all about the whiskey), Peugeot RCZ (the excitement of driving) and Heal's (an inverted headline, the dog inside the store) are the book's examples.

## Questions

- When does "aspirational identification" become a source of self-dissatisfaction (the feminist critique in ch. 2)?
- Why does corporate advertising after a serious crisis also end up in this quadrant?
