# Numerical goals are set per ad for exposure and processing, and per campaign for communication effects and action

> **The idea in one sentence:** objectives (general directions) are set at every level, but goals (measurable figures) only where they make sense: you do not ask a single ad to raise brand attitude, and you cannot fix a percentage of attention for a whole campaign.

## Explanation

The authors distinguish **objectives**, the general ends sought ("increase share in segment X"), from **goals**, objectives with very specific definitions ("from 15% to 18% in the next year"). Written objectives help coordinate production and finance with the campaign. They align the agency (account service, creative, media, planning) and provide the basis for evaluation.

The question "goals or objectives?" has a different answer for each step of the communication response sequence:

| Step | Individual ad | Campaign |
|---|---|---|
| Exposure | **goal** (e.g., % of the audience reached by insertion X) | objective (maximizing reach or frequency) |
| Processing | **goal** (e.g., 45% of the target learn the main benefit) | objective (more attention to the brand's advertising) |
| Communication effects | objective ("contributes to brand attitude") | **goal** (e.g., brand attitude from 4.0 to 4.5 on a 5-point scale) |
| Target audience action | objective ("contributes to usage") | **goal** (e.g., usage from 50% to 56%) |

The logic: effects and action come from the cumulative work of ads, promotions and other forms of communication, not from a single execution. Specific promotions are an exception: they get action goals of their own.

## Application

- In the brief, write the KPIs at the right level: the pretest of an ad measures attention and learning, and campaign tracking measures attitude and behavior.
- When a client judges a single post by sales, explain that you asked something else of that post.
- In digital reporting, separate exposure and processing metrics (views, watch time) from campaign metrics (brand lift, sales).

## Example

A campaign for a yogurt has the campaign goal "brand preference from 30% to 35% in 6 months". Each spot has the pretest goal "at least 50% of the target retain the *no added sugar* benefit".

## Questions

- How would the table apply to an influencer campaign in which each creator makes a single post?
- What does a brand risk by setting sales goals for every creative in performance marketing?
