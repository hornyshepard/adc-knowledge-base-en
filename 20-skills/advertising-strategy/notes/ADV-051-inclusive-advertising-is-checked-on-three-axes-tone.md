# Inclusive advertising is checked on three axes (tone, language, representation) before it is published

> **The idea in one sentence:** for Ejeke, diversity is no longer only an HR topic but also a condition of effectiveness. An ad's messaging, pictures, voices and beliefs must reflect the whole spectrum of possible consumers, and this is checked on three concrete axes.

## Explanation

Ejeke asks marketers to value diversity in all its forms, to represent underrepresented groups and to eradicate long-standing cultural prejudices. He warns that agencies and brands can exclude people because of their own *unconscious prejudice* (trend 21).

The arguments backed by data (taken from surveys):
- Adobe, 2019, over 2,000 US consumers: 61% said diversity in advertising was essential. 38% are more inclined to trust brands that use more diverse advertising.
- Also according to Adobe, about 120 million Americans do not see themselves depicted in ads.
- Nielsen: 43% of the 75 million US millennials identify as African American, Hispanic or Asian. A company without a multicultural strategy, the report says, has no growth plan.

The proposed checklist:

| Axis | The check question |
|---|---|
| **Tone** | How do we portray the "typical person" in the target audience? A caricature, a stereotype or a real person? |
| **Language** | What words, symbols and phrases do we use to describe people and their problems? |
| **Representation** | Does the material reflect society? Are we promoting a wide range of viewpoints? |

## Application

- Build the three questions into the internal approval process, before the client presentation: in casting, in the copy and in image selection.
- Put into the brief not only the target audience but also who must *not* feel excluded or ridiculed.
- Diversity behind the camera (the team that makes the ad) reduces the risk of blind spots.

## Example

A working illustration: an ad for a skin care product shows only light skin. The representation test stops it before production. The language test checks whether "problem skin" or "normal skin" implies a norm that excludes.

## Questions

- Where is the line between genuine inclusion and "diversity-washing", that is, representation in ads without any change inside the company?
- The figures are American. What does "representation" look like in the Romanian market (ethnic minorities, rural areas, age, disability)?
