# The response of sales to the budget follows a concave or an S-shaped curve, with a wear-in threshold and a wear-out point

> **The idea in one sentence:** each extra leu spent on advertising brings less and less, and in the S-shaped model the first investments seem to bring nothing until a threshold is passed.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe (following Tellis) distinguish two approaches to effectiveness: the **modeling approach** (aggregate: markets, segments, budget versus sales) and the **behavioral approach** (individual: what a specific ad does to a person). Sales-response models belong to the first approach and have two basic shapes:

- **The concave model**: the law of diminishing returns: once the campaign has reached all the non-buyers (of whom only a subsample convert), additional expenditure adds little.
- **The S-shaped model**: at first the impact is low (the *wear-in* phase), then sales increase exponentially with the budget, up to a saturation point; beyond it, added investment can lead to adverse results through *wear-out*, when positive evaluations are replaced by negative ones.

The limitations the authors point out: at the aggregate level you cannot isolate the effect of advertising from the rest of the marketing mix, from the competition and from the economy. Above all, the models are input-output representations and say nothing about the psychological processes that produce the relationship (or its absence). That is why they must be complemented by the behavioral approach.

## Application

- When planning the budget, ask whether you are below the threshold (the budget is too small to matter) or in the saturation zone (the money would bring more elsewhere).
- An "underfunded" campaign can look like a creative failure when in fact it has not got past the wear-in phase.
- Watch for wearout signals (falling liking, irritation) before raising frequency.

## Example

A new brand that airs a spot a few times a week may see no movement; the same spot with twice the media weight can pass the threshold of the S-curve.

- How would you explain to a client why doubling the budget does not double sales?
