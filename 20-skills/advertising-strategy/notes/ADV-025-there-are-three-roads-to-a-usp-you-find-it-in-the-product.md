# There are three roads to a USP: you find it in the product, you build it into the product, or you claim it first

> **The idea in one sentence:** the uniqueness does not have to exist in the product; it can be discovered, created together with the client, or gained by being the first to state it.

## Explanation

Reeves describes three broad roads that lead to Rome:

| Road | What it means | The risk |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Discovery** | You study the product and its use until you find a real, unsuspected difference, "very much like finding a pearl lying in an oyster". Reeves says that radical differences come to the surface surprisingly often. | The difference may be irrelevant to the consumer. |
| 2. **Building** | You persuade the client to improve or modify the product, so that the USP is tailored and built in, to the benefit of the manufacturer and of the public. | It takes time, budget and a client willing to change the product. |
| 3. **Claiming** | You make the public see something in the product that they had not noticed before. This is not a uniqueness of the product but of the claim: it cloaks itself in uniqueness. | A competitor can say the same thing; you must be first and persistent. |

*La boîte à outils de la publicité* sums up the same ladder for students: if the product has no exclusive quality, the agency can advise improving it; if even then nothing makes it stand out, the last solution is to say it differently, in a way the argument has never been put before. The manual specifies that "unique" here means exclusive (*exclusive*), not "one and only" (*une seule*).

The third road explains why Reeves spoke of **preemption**: his brand-history studies showed that the first big advertiser can "take" the category's USP and is then protected by the penetration gained. The idea anticipates the positioning principle of being first rather than being better.

## Application

- Start with road 1: a day in the plant, the lab or with the product team beats a day of brainstorming. Ask how it is made, from what, how long it takes, what is tested.
- If you find nothing, propose road 2 to the client: a service, a guarantee or a pack that creates the difference.
- On road 3, choose the generic category benefit that no one has claimed and say it first, for a long time.

## Example

Hopkins, admired by Reeves for exactly this, used in beer ads the fact that the bottles were sterilized with steam, a practice of the whole industry that no one had mentioned. (The example is a classic of the literature on Hopkins; the book describes the principle without giving the details of the case.)

## Questions

- Is road 3 ethical, when the "uniqueness" is only a claim everyone could make?
- In the age of online reviews and comparisons, how long does a preemption last?
