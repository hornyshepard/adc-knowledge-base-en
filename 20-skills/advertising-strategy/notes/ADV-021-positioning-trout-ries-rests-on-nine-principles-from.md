# Positioning (Trout & Ries) rests on nine principles, from information saturation to permanence

> **The idea in one sentence:** "Positioning is what you do to the mind of the prospect", not to the product; because Trout and Ries never theorized it rigorously, Fernández Gómez rebuilds it from nine principles.

## Explanation

The concept first appears in 1969, in a paper by Jack Trout in *Industrial Marketing* (on the failure of those trying to compete with IBM with a "me too" product). It was developed in the series "The positioning era" in *Advertising Age* (1972) and in the bestseller *Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind* (1981). The basic idea: marketing is not a battle between products but between perceptions. The theoretical construct proposed by the author:

| # | Principle | In short |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The problem of information saturation | too many messages, media "noise"; too much choice paralyzes |
| 2 | The problem of the mind's limited capacity | the mind as a computer with a slot for each category, which rejects what does not fit |
| 3 | The importance of being first | the easy way into the mind is to be first; if you cannot be first in a category, set up a new category you can be first in |
| 4 | The need to become generic in a category | Kleenex, aspirin: a mental monopoly, but with the risk of becoming a common noun |
| 5 | The importance of the reference brand | you position yourself only in relation to someone else |
| 6 | The principle of differentiation | Levitt: there is no such thing as a commodity; everything can be differentiated |
| 7 | The principle of difference beyond the product | psychological attributes, not only functional ones; added by later writers, not by Trout and Ries |
| 8 | The principle of simplicity | the differentiating idea must be simple, obvious and repeated on every channel |
| 9 | The principle of permanence | a position accumulates over the years; most successful companies rarely change a winning formula |

Trout and Ries say that positioning does not create something new but manipulates and reties connections that already exist in the mind. The author criticizes them for staying with product attributes and for favoring the verbal over the visual, a step back from emotional mechanisms.

## Application

- Start from the customer's mind, not from the product: which slot already exists, who occupies it, which slot is free?
- State the position in one word or a short phrase, then in a sentence that explains it.
- Check your proposal against all nine principles; most often it fails on simplicity or on permanence.

## Example

Ries and Ries: if the pigeonhole is named "safe cars", it is the hole for Volvo. Marlboro does not compete in a "market" but in the mental category of the "masculine" cigarette.

## Questions

- Principle 3 (the first one wins) is contradicted by many cases of followers who beat the pioneer. What conditions make it true?
- Is the "saturated" mind still a new argument, if Reeves said the same thing in 1961?
