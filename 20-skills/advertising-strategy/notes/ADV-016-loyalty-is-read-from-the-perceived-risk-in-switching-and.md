# Loyalty is read from the perceived risk in switching and from satisfaction, not only from purchase behavior

> **The idea in one sentence:** two people who buy the same brand may be one loyal and the other merely "stuck"; combining satisfaction with the perceived risk in switching gives four groups with different brand attitude strategies.

## Explanation

For Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott, brand loyalty is an **awareness-attitude-behavior** concept. Loyals have strong preferences for one, two or three brands. Switchers have only moderate preferences. As an alternative to "black box" models such as the conversion model, the authors propose their own model on two axes: the **perceived risk in switching** (involvement) and **satisfaction** with the current brand.

| | High risk in switching | Low risk in switching |
|---|---|---|
| **Satisfied** | Loyal: highly satisfied, unlikely to switch | Vulnerable: satisfied, but nothing stops them from switching |
| **Not satisfied** | Frustrated: not satisfied, but the risk in switching seems greater than the potential gain | Switchable: neither satisfied nor inhibited from switching |

The model shows what behavior hides. Our regular customers may be loyal or frustrated. We lose the frustrated ones at the first opportunity unless we find out why they are unsatisfied. Competitors' customers may also be frustrated, and therefore open, if we reduce the perceived risk in switching.

Attitude objectives by group:

| Group | Our customers | Competitors' customers |
|---|---|---|
| Loyal | maintain attitude | rarely in the target; modify or change attitude |
| Frustrated | build attitude and fix the cause | increase attitude and remove the barriers to switching |
| Switchable | build attitude | build or increase attitude (the most open) |
| Vulnerable | strengthen attitude, consolidate brand equity | modify attitude (hard to persuade) |

A figure for context: for personal use products (toothpaste, shampoo), about 40–50% of all customers are loyal to a single brand. But the vast majority of brands are bought more out of habit than from a strong commitment to the brand.

## Application

- Add two simple questions to target audience research: how satisfied are you? how complicated or risky would it be to switch?
- In services with a high risk in switching (banks, telecom, insurance), look for the competitors' frustrated customers and offer them a guarantee that lowers the risk.
- Do not treat all current customers with "thank you for staying with us" messages. The frustrated ones need a problem solved.

## Example

A bank has many long-standing customers who stay out of inertia (frustrated). A digital competitor targets exactly this group, offering to handle the whole account transfer itself, in 10 minutes. The attack lowers the perceived risk instead of only promising satisfaction.

## Questions

- How does the model relate to the observation by Sharp, Wright and Goodhardt that loyalty is either "repertoire" or "subscription" loyalty?
- Can communication alone move a customer from "frustrated" to "loyal"?
