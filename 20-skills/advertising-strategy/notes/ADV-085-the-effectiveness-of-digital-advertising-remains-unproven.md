# The effectiveness of digital advertising remains unproven, and the book sees social media as a "negotiated" medium, hit by fraud and poor visibility

> **The idea in one sentence:** digital is just another way of delivering words, sound and pictures, which are processed the same way as anywhere else; the difference lies in the context, the weak control over the message and the measurement problems.

## Explanation

Chapter 11 is the most skeptical in the book. The authors' conclusion: "there is still no real evidence of the effectiveness of digital media", despite the large audience numbers.

**Social media.** Don Schultz called it marketing pyrite, fool's gold. Social media was created as personal media, not as advertising vehicles: marketers must be *invited* in, so it is a negotiated, not persuasive, media form. On Google people are searching and are near a final decision; on Facebook they are there for social interaction and are rarely in the market for the advertised product. Burger King's Subservient Chicken was extremely popular but had little, if any, effect on sales.

**Measurement and market problems** (Box 11.1–11.3):

| Problem | Figures from the book |
|---|---|
| Unseen ads | 54% of the display ads studied in 2012–2013 were not seen by anyone |
| Fraud | 19 billion dollars in 2018; about 16% of clicks fraudulent |
| Programmatic buying | only 28% of marketers say they understand it |
| Processing time | ads on Snapchat average about three seconds |
| Ad blocking | over a quarter of U.S. internet users |
| Privacy | GDPR (2018), location data classified as personal information |

**Fine targeting** is now thought to have limited effectiveness: case studies show more sales when you reach a larger proportion of a platform's audience, and major brands are returning to broad reach.

The nuance: the authors do not reject digital. In the media selection table, it is the only medium compatible with all strategies, and integration with traditional media works: Monster.com more than doubled the YouTube views of its Super Bowl commercial through social media integrated with PR. They only ask that digital be judged by the same processing and effect criteria.

## Application

- Do not go into social media "so as not to be left behind". Write down which communication effect you are after and how you will measure it.
- Ask for viewability data, invalid traffic checks and transparency in the programmatic chain.
- Measure *carryover* effects: Mentos's popularity on social media probably benefited from the 13 million dollars spent on traditional media for Mentos chewing gum.

## Example

The book's counterexample is Gatorade, with the "Serena Match Point" game on Snapchat (2016). 16% of users chose to play, for 200 seconds on average, and 35% shared it. Digital can support a long brand experience if the idea deserves it. The cost, however, was high.

## Questions

- The book's data stop in 2019–2020. What has changed since then (retail media, TikTok, AI)? Does the thesis still hold?
- How would a *performance marketing* author answer the claim that fine targeting has limited effectiveness?
