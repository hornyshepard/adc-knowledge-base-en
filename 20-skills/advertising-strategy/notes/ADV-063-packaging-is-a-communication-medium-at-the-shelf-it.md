# Packaging is a communication medium: at the shelf it triggers recognition awareness and must carry the benefit from the advertising

> **The idea in one sentence:** many brand decisions are made in the store, and there the package is the only "ad" the person is sure to see; it must cut through the clutter of the shelf and reactivate the need and the benefit learned from the rest of the communication.

## Explanation

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott say packaging is often underestimated by managers, although it is a very important part of marketing communication. It has two roles: it attracts attention, so it supports **brand awareness**, and it expresses the brand's image, so it reinforces **brand attitude**. They cite studies by the Point-of-Purchase Advertising Institute: over 70% of brand decisions in supermarkets are made in the store. For products where the decision is triggered by recognition, the package brings the purchase if recognition has been linked to the right need by the rest of the communication, especially by advertising.

Three consequences:

1. **Visual differentiation.** The shelf has dozens of facings, so the visual elements of the package must be different from competitors'. Small wine producers use fun, creative labels, even artist series, to stand out and to make the wine seem more approachable.
2. **Continuity with the ad.** The package repeats the benefit: Bold shows the 2-in-1 benefit and the freshness from the ad on the pack, and Fairy reminds you that it is pure, without chemicals that can irritate a baby's skin. Fennis and Stroebe show the mechanism: a small reproduction of the ad's picture on a corner of the package reduced the effect of interference from competitors' ads (Keller, 1987).
3. **The risk of change.** After the 2009 redesign of Tropicana Pure Premium, sales dropped 20% in less than two months, and PepsiCo quickly went back to the old package.

The package can also become content or a status sign. The Macallan went back to elements from an 1861 bottle for a premium expression and launched limited editions with photographers' work on the label.

## Application

- In a recognition awareness strategy, treat the package as a medium: show it in the ad exactly as it will be seen at the shelf.
- Put a cue from the campaign (a character, a picture, a benefit) on the package, especially when the executions in the category look alike.
- Test any redesign at the shelf, with short exposure, before the launch.

## Example

Share a Coke made the package the campaign's message itself: the names on the bottles became content photographed and shared by people.

## Questions

- What role does the package still have when the purchase is made online, from a photo a few pixels wide?
- How much of the campaign's look and feel fits on the package without cluttering it?
