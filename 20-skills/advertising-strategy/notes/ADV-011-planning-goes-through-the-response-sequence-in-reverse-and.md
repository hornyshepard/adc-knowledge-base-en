# Planning goes through the response sequence in reverse, and a mistake at one step does not cancel the steps before it

> **The idea in one sentence:** the consumer goes from exposure to action, but the strategist starts from action and ends at exposure; unlike the consumer, the strategist can correct one step without redoing everything.

## Explanation

Once the marketing objectives are set, the authors propose that planning follow the reverse order of the response sequence:

| Planning step | Response sequence step | The decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Target audience action | 4 | who can try or repurchase the brand |
| 2. Communication effects | 3 | positioning and communication objectives |
| 3. Processing | 2 | creative strategy, tested before it runs |
| 4. Exposure | 1 | media selection and scheduling (how often) |

Planning also has **compounding probabilities**: each shortcoming at one step is enlarged at the next ones. But it has an advantage that the response sequence lacks: **sequential planning**. In the consumer's sequence, if one step fails, everything fails. In planning, a wrong decision at one step does not invalidate the sound decisions made before it. You only replace the unsuccessful strategy.

The authors' example: the marketing plan calls for secondary demand (more usage from existing customers), and brand loyalists are chosen as the target audience. If they already buy what they see as their limit, the secondary demand strategy is still valid. Only the target audience changes, for example to brand switchers. In the same way, a wrong communication strategy does not mean that the target audience was wrong.

## Application

- When a campaign underperforms, look for the step that failed, from the bottom up, before you throw away the whole plan.
- Present the plan to the client in planning order (action → effects → creative → media), so that each decision follows from the previous one.
- Do not start from the media ("we'll do TikTok") before you know the action and the effects you want.

## Example

For Nescafé: the action (trial among non-users), the effects (category need for soluble coffee, brand awareness, an attitude based on taste), processing (a positioning on taste or on freshness, chosen by testing) and only then exposure (TV as the primary medium, print and coupons as secondary media).
