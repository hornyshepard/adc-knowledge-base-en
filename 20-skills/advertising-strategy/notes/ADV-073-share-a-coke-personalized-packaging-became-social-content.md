# Share a Coke: personalized packaging became social content, backed by integrated communication

> **The idea in one sentence:** Coca-Cola put names on bottles, and people turned the product into a message and into content; the success comes from the integration of packaging, outdoor, TV and social media, not from any single channel.

## Explanation

The chapter on acquisition ends with a rule: no single medium or channel is enough to reach a market segment; it takes integrated marketing communications (IMC), online and offline, in coherent, sequenced campaigns. Coca-Cola is the authors' example, a company whose advertising is mostly reminder advertising and which runs individual campaigns under the umbrella of a brand theme ("Taste the Feeling").

**Share a Coke** started in Australia (in 2011, Connors specifies), with a digital outdoor billboard in a major square in Sydney showing consumers' names, and was rolled out to other countries based on its initial success; in the United States it was supported by television advertising. The stated goal: to connect with young people personally and emotionally. Social media did the rest: more than 500,000 photos shared with #shareacoke, more than 6 million virtual bottles created and shared by September 2014, 25 million new Facebook followers; the campaign is credited with increasing soft drink sales by 2% in the United States (figures the textbook cites from a Coca-Cola executive).

Connors (SRC-030) explains the mechanism through three factors: **personalization** (the name creates a sense of ownership), **the social nature** of the campaign (people are invited to share the bottle and the photo) and **the emotional connection** (memories associated with their own names or the names of loved ones).

## Application

- When you look for an IMC idea, ask: what can the consumer do with the product, so that the product becomes a medium?
- Design the owned → paid → earned route from the start: the package (owned) is amplified by TV and outdoor (paid) and becomes people's content (earned).
- Check scalability: a simple personalization (common names) can be produced industrially; a complex one cannot.

## Example

The mechanism is related to the 5Cs: people consume (look for their name), connect (give the bottle to someone), create (post the photo): a simple idea that touches several motivations at once.

## Questions

- What happened to people whose names did not appear on the bottles? How could a campaign handle exclusion?
- Is +2% in sales a success for a global campaign of this scale? How would you separate its effect from other factors?
