# PAR and BAR measure marketing productivity: how much brand awareness turns into purchase and advocacy

> **The idea in one sentence:** high brand awareness is worth nothing if it does not turn into purchase and recommendation; the purchase action ratio (PAR) and the brand advocacy ratio (BAR) show where customers drop out along the path and where you need to intervene.

## Explanation

Brand managers track awareness, service managers track loyalty, and their reports rarely meet. *Marketing 4.0* proposes two ratios, patterned after the ratios finance executives use, such as return on equity (ROE):

- **PAR** (purchase action ratio) = how many of the people aware of the brand buy it (from the aware stage to act);
- **BAR** (brand advocacy ratio) = how many of the people aware of the brand recommend it (from aware to advocate).

The book's example: out of 100 people, 90 spontaneously recall Brand X, 18 buy it and 9 recommend it. PAR = 0.2 and BAR = 0.1, so 80% of the awareness is lost before the sale. PAR can also be calculated by dividing market share by brand awareness. With a PAR of 0.5, a 1% increase in awareness brings roughly a 0.5% increase in market share.

PAR and BAR break down into four conversion rates, and the lowest one reveals the **bottleneck**. Each bottleneck has a remedy, matching chapters 8–11 of the book:

| Conversion | The problem when it is low | Intervention |
|---|---|---|
| aware → appeal = **attraction** | poor positioning or execution | human-centric marketing |
| appeal → ask = **curiosity** | fails to trigger conversation | content marketing (the only rate that should not be close to 1, otherwise the message is unclear) |
| ask → act = **commitment** | flaws in product, price, distribution, selling | omnichannel marketing |
| act → advocate = **affinity** | poor post-sales experience | engagement marketing (apps, social CRM, gamification) |

A useful metaphor: **customer conversation is the "debt", advertising is the "equity"**. Conversation provides leverage (it builds awareness cheaply), but it amplifies both profits and losses. That is why even the best brands cannot give up advertising entirely.

## Application

- Ask the client for data along the whole path (awareness, preference, purchase, recommendation) and calculate the conversions. Do not propose an awareness campaign if the bottleneck is commitment (distribution).
- Use PAR as a ROMI argument: how much of the awareness money is lost.
- Fix one bottleneck first, not the whole path.

## Example

A brand that invests heavily in awareness but is hard to find on the shelf or online will have good attraction and curiosity but low commitment. The remedy is distribution and omnichannel, not a new TV spot.

## Questions

- Does BAR measure stated recommendation or actual recommendation? How do you avoid the overestimates of surveys?
- The debt metaphor suggests an "optimal level of debt" in conversation. How would you estimate it?
