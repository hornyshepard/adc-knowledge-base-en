# A Big Idea is not enough without a "Long Idea": the audience must be met at the next step (Volvo Trucks, "Epic Split")

> **The idea in one sentence:** an excellent ad that leads nowhere loses exactly the audience it has won over; the idea must be thought of as a journey, not a moment.

## Explanation

Richards and McLean set the **Big Idea** (the great campaign idea) against **"Long Ideas"**: ideas that any ad has to carry through the next steps of the audience's journey. Their favorite example is the film of Jean-Claude Van Damme doing the splits between two Volvo trucks (2013). The authors stress that this is not a criticism of the agency and that the stunt is brilliant. The video achieved millions of views and was a superb demonstration of Volvo Trucks' *dynamic steering*. But when it ended there was no link and no follow-through. Anyone who wanted more had to Google, find the website and find their own way. Their verdict, as a fleet buyer's reaction: "I crossed the threshold … but Volvo were not there to meet me."

Several principles follow from the case:

- **The threshold**: the moment the audience moves from interest to purchase intent. Most content strategies stay in "Act 1", with soft audience goals, because the brand brief is not connected to below-the-line campaigns and sales.
- **Different journeys per persona**: the same film could have opened separate journeys for fleet managers, truck drivers and Volvo brand advocates.
- **Sustain, not just campaign**: the campaign becomes a stepping stone. Campaigns end up being either **start** (getting someone started on the journey) or **nudge** (moving along someone who is stuck).
- **Likes are not engagement.** In the 2014 World Cup, very few social campaigns had a structure that took the audience toward a sale.

## Application

- For every big execution, map "what comes next": where people land after the last frame, what they find there, what is asked of them, what they get.
- Write the threshold goal into the brief: what the audience must do right afterward (a visit, a sign-up, a meeting, a test).
- Put viral videos and ATL at the service of a content destination (a microsite, a hub), not as isolated events.

## Example

The Volvo case is the central example. As an alternative, the book proposes three different entry points for an airline: a brochure, a viral video and the press. All of them lead to a site about holidays with children under 10, which becomes a must-see resource for the family whether or not an immediate sale follows.

## Questions

- Would "Epic Split" have gone just as viral with a visible call to action at the end?
- Where does the creative agency's responsibility end and the CRM or digital team's begin?
