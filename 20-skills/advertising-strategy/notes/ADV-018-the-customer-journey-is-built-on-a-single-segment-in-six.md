# The customer journey is built on a single segment, in six stages, with three questions at each: only then come objectives and KPIs

> **The idea in one sentence:** instead of looking for a list of the "right channels", which can change monthly, build a map of one target segment's journey and ask at each stage what people want, think and do; only then set objectives and KPIs.

## Explanation

Kevin McTigue calls a brand touchpoint any point where the brand intersects with the consumer, from the ad you remember from 10 years ago to the coffee served at the dealership. A study by BCG and IRI found over 50 potential touchpoints even for an inexpensive consumer packaged good. Expectations also transfer across categories: people used to Uber and Amazon expect the same ease from every brand (the *transference of experience expectations*).

The model builds on the AIDA funnel and on McKinsey's loyalty loop (2009), but it is **consumer-centric**, not centered on the company's goals. It has six stages arranged in a circle:

1. **Pre-need**: the stage where 99% of customers are; it is where companies direct most advertising dollars (Starwood posts vacation images on Instagram that are barely branded).
2. **Trigger**: a car lease comes due, a machine breaks down, a child is born; Google calls this ZMOT. The brand's relevance is at its highest.
3. **Consideration and evaluation**: the domain of content marketing; 68% of B2B buyers prefer to research online on their own.
4. **Buying**: omnichannel (75% of Warby Parker's store customers had been to its website first).
5. **Usage**: the floral-delivery company that understood people do not buy flowers but a moment of surprise.
6. **Post-usage evaluation**: the paths diverge: rejecters, price-driven switchers, loyalists, evangelists.

The method: choose the most valuable segment; identify its **most well-worn path** (like the trails of Hyde Park seen from above); ask three questions at each stage (*what are the needs and the goal?*, *what are customers thinking: attitudes, friction points, what they think of the brand?*, *what do they do, and where?*); then overlay the business objectives and KPIs on the stages (awareness at 45%, top 3 in consideration, 3.5% conversion, NPS, repeat booking rate). Do not wait for perfect data: an internal team can sketch a first usable map in a few hours.

## Application

- When the client asks "should we be on TikTok?", answer with the map: at which stage, and for which need, would the platform serve?
- Build one map per segment; a map for "everyone" waters down the insights.
- Budget in proportion to the opportunity at each stage, not out of habit.

## Example

For a vacation company, the map shows that in pre-need people dream on Instagram and Facebook, in consideration they read reviews on Google and TripAdvisor, and after the vacation they share their photos, with a different objective and KPI at each stage.
