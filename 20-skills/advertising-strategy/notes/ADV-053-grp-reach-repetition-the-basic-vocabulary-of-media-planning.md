# GRP = reach × repetition: the basic vocabulary of media planning

> **The idea in one sentence:** a campaign's effectiveness depends on the quality of the message and on the number of times it reaches people in the target audience; audience measurement quantifies the second part with a few standard indicators.

## Explanation

Definitions from *La boîte à outils de la publicité*:

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Contact** | one exposure of a person to the message: an "opportunity to see" (*occasion de voir*: TV, press, cinema, outdoor) or "to hear" (*occasion d'entendre*: radio) |
| **Potential / actual / useful audience** (*audience potentielle / réelle / utile*) | those who can be reached / those actually reached / those actually reached **who belong to the target audience** |
| **Duplication** (*duplication*) | the useful audience shared by two media vehicles |
| **Reach** (*couverture*) | the number of people in the target audience reached at least once during the campaign; the **reach rate** (*taux de couverture*) is that number as a percentage of the target audience |
| **Repetition** (*répétition*) | the average number of contacts per person reached |
| **GRP** (*Gross Rating Point*) | useful contacts per 100 people in the target audience; GRP = reach rate × repetition |
| **C/GRP** | the cost of one GRP point (*coût au point GRP*) |
| **CPM** | the cost per 1,000 people in the target audience (*coût pour mille*, used mainly in the press and online) |

Media vehicles are ranked on three scales: **power** (*puissance*: the size of the useful audience), **affinity** (*affinité*: the useful audience relative to the vehicle's total audience) and **economy** (*économie*: C/GRP or CPM). A good plan is the best compromise among the three. Qualitative criteria also count: the fit between message and vehicle (glossy women's magazines for a luxury perfume) and the risk that the message gets lost among others.

The authors point out that any audience measurement is an extrapolated survey, so it shows a trend. Their example: the households that accept a TV audience meter at home are probably avid viewers, which may favor "mainstream" choices.

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott challenge this very vocabulary: the same GRP can hide an actual frequency below the threshold, and average repetition is an abstraction. They judge a plan by its reach at the minimum effective frequency ([[ADV-054-a-media-plan-is-judged-by-reach-at-the-minimum-effective|ADV-054]]).

## Application

- When you compare two plans, compare the GRPs for the same target audience and the same period, then the C/GRP.
- The same GRP can mean high reach with low repetition, or the reverse. For awareness at launch you want reach; for a complex message or an impulse product, repetition.
- Ask for the **useful** audience, not the vehicle's total audience.

## Example

A campaign with 60% reach and an average repetition of 5 has 300 GRPs. One with 30% reach and a repetition of 10 also has 300 GRPs, but it reaches half as many people, more times each.

## Questions

- What are the equivalents of reach and repetition on social platforms, where an "impression" does not mean attention?
- When is it worth paying a higher CPM for affinity?
