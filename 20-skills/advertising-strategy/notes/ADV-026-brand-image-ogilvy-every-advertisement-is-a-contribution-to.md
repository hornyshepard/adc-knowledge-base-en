# Brand image (Ogilvy): every advertisement is a contribution to the complex symbol that is the brand image

> **The idea in one sentence:** instead of the advertisement that must pay for itself, Ogilvy proposes the advertisement as a long-term investment in a coherent brand personality, kept up year after year.

## Explanation

On 14 October 1955, in a talk ("The image and the brand") given at a lunch organized by the American Association of Advertising (AAA), David Ogilvy stated the principle that made him famous: every advertisement should be thought of as "a contribution to the complex symbol which is the brand image". He acknowledged that he had borrowed the idea from the article by Gardner and Levy published the same year, and that he had changed his mind radically: until then, as a follower of Hopkins, he had believed that every advertisement must sell at a profit and had derided long-term effect as an alibi.

The contributions credited to Ogilvy:

- **the long term**: every commercial is an investment in the total personality of the brand;
- **coherence**: the same style, year after year; manufacturers want their brand to be all things to all people and end up with a brand that has no personality;
- **segmentation**: a brand cannot be both a male and a female brand, both upper-crust and plebeian;
- **the supremacy of the picture**: the only function of the words is to explain what the pictures are showing; *story appeal* (the term coined by Harold Rudolph): a photograph that tells a story draws the eye and gets people to read the copy.

Fernández Gómez is critical: Ogilvy hardly theorized the concept, he popularized it commercially, and the move helped him win clients who were calling for less explicit communication. More important, Ogilvy's brand image is a concept of **emission** (what the firm wants to convey), not today's brand image, understood as **reception** in the public's mind. Rom and Sabaté sum it up simply: if the brand image is positive, advertising reinforces it; if it is negative, advertising changes it.

## Application

- Before approving an execution, ask: what does it add to the brand's personality? What would it damage?
- Write the brand's personality in a few traits and use it as a coherence filter for all campaigns.
- Use story appeal in the key visual: an image that suggests "what happened before" or "what comes next".

## Example

Ogilvy said that 95% of the campaigns of his day were created ad hoc, without any long-term considerations, and called it a "miracle" when a manufacturer managed to sustain a coherent style over a period of years.

## Questions

- Ogilvy also recommended repeating an ad until it stops selling, a principle of Reeves's. Is this an internal contradiction?
- What practical difference does the distinction between emission and reception make when you measure a brand's image?
