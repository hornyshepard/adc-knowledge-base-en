# Strategic planning turns a compilation of information into a point of view: monitoring, weak signals, synthesis

> **The idea in one sentence:** today the problem is not a lack of information but an excess of it; the strategic planner must sort, rank and connect information from seemingly unrelated fields until finding a consumer truth (*vérité consommateur*) that no one has seen before.

## Explanation

*La boîte à outils de la publicité* describes planning as the agency's laboratory of ideas (*laboratoire d'idées*). The planner steps in at several moments: upstream (monitoring, or *veille*, and benchmarking), at the brief (understanding the context), then in defining the brand territory and the communication axis; for the creatives, the planner marks out a field of search, so that the team does not wear itself out in too many directions. The planner is the link between client service and the creative team.

The process of working with information:

1. **Organization**: define the information needs and the best sources (internal: sales data, after-sales service, networks; external: official statistics, research institutes, trade fairs, the press, the web).
2. **Collection**: gather, select, synthesize.
3. **Use**: analyze, cross-check, validate, pass arguments on to the teams.
4. **Choice**: set the axes on which the strategy will be built.

The skills required: curiosity, general culture, the ability to synthesize and above all spotting **weak signals** (*signaux faibles*): a banal piece of information linked to another, which reveals a change in habits or the beginning or end of a cycle. The authors also recommend carrying ideas over from one sector to another, as a way to make the creative leap easier. Very good campaigns, they say, say something new and do it in an original form.

## Application

- Keep a permanent monitoring file, tagged by category; do not rebuild it from scratch for each brief.
- At the end, the synthesis must fit into a few key ideas. If you hand the team 40 slides of data, you have not done planning.
- Look explicitly for links between distant fields (art, technology, sport) and the client's category.

## Example

**Social TV** (2013): by studying young people in front of the TV, the experiments from across the Atlantic and the conversations on social networks, planners saw before 2009 that young people are multi-screen, comment in real time and reject the linearity of TV. The conclusion for channels and brands: programs that bring viewers together, replay, active communities, bonuses and making-of content, a digital ecosystem that extends the show.

## Questions

- How do you tell a weak signal from mere noise or a one-month fad?
- Should the planner also work after launch, on measuring the campaign?
