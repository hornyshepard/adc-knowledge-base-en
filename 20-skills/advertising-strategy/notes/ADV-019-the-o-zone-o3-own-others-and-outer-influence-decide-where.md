# The O Zone (O3): own, others' and outer influence decide where you invest your marketing effort

> **The idea in one sentence:** every decision along the 5A path is a mix of outer influence (advertising), others' influence (word of mouth) and own influence (experience), and their weight tells you whether to invest in communication, in community or in the post-purchase experience.

## Explanation

*Marketing 4.0* calls the three sources of influence the **O Zone** (O3):

| Source | What it is | How controllable | Where to invest |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Outer** | advertising, marketing communications, the sales force, customer service | manageable: the message, the media and the frequency can be planned | marketing communications |
| **Others'** | friends, family, communities, communal rating systems (TripAdvisor, Yelp); the strongest comes from youth, women and netizens (YWN) | difficult to control; it can only be facilitated with the help of loyal customers | community marketing |
| **Own** | past experience, personal judgment, preference | built over time | the post-purchase experience |

The three are always intertwined. Outer influence usually reaches customers first. If it triggers conversation, others' influence follows, and the way the two interact shapes, over time, the customers' own influence. Today customers rely more on others: the book cites a 2015 Nielsen study (60 countries) in which 83% of respondents named friends and family as the most trusted source of "advertising", and 66% paid attention to opinions posted online.

Along the path, customers are most open to influence in the **ask** stage (they seek advice, so it is the window in which brand favorability can grow) and the **act** stage (they shape their perception through use). Experience matters too: first-time buyers of a category rely on outer influence, more experienced customers on others', and the most experienced on their own judgment.

## Application

- Before allocating the budget, estimate the weight of the three sources in the client's category (research, social listening, sales data).
- Outer influence dominates → invest in campaigns. Others' influence dominates → build community and advocates. Own influence dominates → invest in the product, the service and the usage experience.
- In the brief, write explicitly "what we want others to say" about the brand, not only "what we want to say".

## Example

A new category bought for the first time (for example, a first streaming subscription) is dominated by outer influence. A routine category, such as a favorite detergent, is dominated by own influence. There, a big campaign changes behavior little if the usage experience is not convincing.

## Questions

- How do you separate outer influence from others' influence in the data, when the ad itself generates the conversation?
- Where do paid influencers belong: outer influence or others' influence?
