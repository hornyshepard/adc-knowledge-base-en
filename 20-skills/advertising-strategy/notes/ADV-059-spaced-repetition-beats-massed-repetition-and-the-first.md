# Spaced repetition beats massed repetition, and the first commercial in a block is remembered best

> **The idea in one sentence:** for memory, the same exposures work better spread over time than back to back, and in a commercial break the first positions have an advantage.

## Explanation

The relationship between the number of exposures and recall is monotonic and positive (unlike the one with attitude, which is curvilinear). But **spacing** matters: Ebbinghaus had already found that spreading his study sessions over three days nearly halved the time he had to spend studying. Fennis and Stroebe present three explanations:

1. **the attention hypothesis**: with an immediate repetition, people realize it is the same ad and ignore it;
2. **the encoding variability hypothesis**: context and mental state change between exposures, so more retrieval cues are formed;
3. **the retrieval hypothesis**: each difficult (but successful) retrieval strengthens the trace.

Appleton-Knapp et al. (2005): spaced ads were remembered better. A change of layout improved recall at short intervals but impaired it at longer intervals, where the identical ad worked better as a retrieval cue. The result supports the retrieval explanation. The limit: short-term laboratory studies with novel materials.

**Serial position.** Field studies in the Netherlands (over 39,000 television viewers, 2,677 commercials) and at the Super Bowl show a primacy effect: commercials early in a block are recalled better. Recency appears only with immediate testing and disappears with a delay. The more commercials in a block, the lower the recall.

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott still recommend, for FMCG, the impact pattern (3+ exposures concentrated in one day, every four weeks), based on sales data, not memory data ([[ADV-056-the-reach-pattern-is-chosen-by-purchase-cycle-and-decision|ADV-056]]).

## Application

- In media planning, prefer a continuous schedule (or closely spaced waves) over a short, very heavy flight, if the objective is memory.
- Vary the execution when exposures are close together; keep the execution identical when the gaps are long.
- Negotiate the first position in the block, and short blocks.

## Example

The Dutch study by Pieters and Bijmolt (1997, surveys from 1975 to 1992): a weak but consistent primacy effect; the duration of the commercial and the number of competing commercials in the block influenced recall.

## Questions

- When is a heavy flight still justified (a launch, an event)?
- What does spacing mean for frequency on social media?
