# For Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott, the new jargon ("platforms", "metrics", "brand ideals") does not change how advertising works

> **The idea in one sentence:** the book takes a polemical stand against the idea that digital or "purpose" marketing has changed the rules; the mechanism is still linking the brand to a need and a benefit in the mind of a target audience.

## Explanation

The authors return to this thesis three times.

1. **The role of advertising has not changed** (ch. 1). Digital brought new ways of delivery, not a new role. They deplore the descent into ever-changing jargon: advertisers no longer place adverts in media, they use multiple "platforms", and advertising measurement has become "metrics". A more substantial-sounding name does not bring more reliable answers.
2. **Strategic planning has not become useless in social media** (ch. 5). Those who argue that it has are wrong. Every message, however it is delivered, needs a target audience, a positioning and a brand awareness and brand attitude strategy. The brain processes words, pictures and sounds the same way in any medium (Bavelier and Green, cited in ch. 1): technological change does not change the fundamental abilities of the brain.
3. **"Brand ideals" cannot replace positioning** (ch. 8). The authors cite a 2012 *Advertising Age* article in which a former global marketing head of Procter & Gamble announced that we no longer deal with benefit positioning strategies but with brand ideals: Pampers helps parents care for babies' and toddlers' development, Starbucks creates connections for self-discovery and inspiration, Heineken helps men be worldly. Their verdict: "more vacuous jargon." Positioning must address the target audience, the category need and the key benefit of the brand.

**The tension.** Many authors in the library (for example those who put *purpose* at the center of the brand, or those who call for new models for the digital era) would say that ideals give meaning and coherence over the long term, and that interactive platforms change the relationship between brand and audience. Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott do not deny that the context is different (user-generated content, privacy, fraud). They deny only that the mechanism of persuasion has changed.

## Application

- When you receive a "brand ideal" as a brief, translate it: for which target audience, which need and which benefit? If it cannot be translated, it is not a positioning.
- Use the authors' thesis as a critical test for the "proprietary methodologies" that agencies present.
- Do not reject *purpose* automatically: check whether it can work as a benefit (positive emotion, social approval) for a concrete target audience.

## Example

"Pampers helps parents care for babies' development" can become a positioning: the brand for parents of babies (target audience), in the disposable nappy category (need), that keeps skin dry all night so the baby sleeps and grows (benefit).

## Questions

- Is the book right that "purpose" is only a new label? Give a case where the ideal worked without a product benefit.
- What has changed in digital all the same, even by the authors' logic (context, the source of the message, fraud)?
