# Media strategy has four objectives (the 4Ms: matching, monopolizing, moment, mindset), not only reaching the target cheaply

> **The idea in one sentence:** media is not only about reaching the target cheaply (matching), but also about being the dominant voice somewhere (monopolizing), showing up when the category is salient (moment) and showing a shared interest with the audience (mindset).

## Explanation

Brian Sternthal sums up the objectives of media strategy as the 4Ms:

**1. Matching**: selecting media that reach the brand's target efficiently. Historically: first the medium that addresses the communication objective (TV for a demonstration, social media for approval by friends), then the vehicles with the lowest cost per thousand target individuals reached. Programmatic: the ad follows the person to whatever site they visit (Tennessee tourism showed clips of the Grand Ole Opry to consumers interested in music and hiking scenes to those interested in outdoor activities). Precautions: exclude web sites that do not fit the brand's equity; programmatic works better for attribute information than for insights about consumer emotions.

Matching includes **reach and frequency**: reach builds awareness and reminds people of brands bought infrequently (Tide); frequency is needed to get into the consideration set, for frequent repeat purchases (McDonald's) and for unplanned decisions (Goodyear tires). Repeated exposures increase the effect, then **wearout** sets in: people have learned what they can, and their own thoughts ("I know this already") are less favorable than the message. Over the year, **continuity** suits products bought regularly, seasonal weighting suits seasonal products, and **flighting** (periods of advertising alternating with a hiatus) suits limited budgets. Continuity with too small a budget often promotes category rather than brand demand.

**2. Monopolizing**: select media where the brand can outshout its competition: a small car dealership on radio, when the large ones are on TV.

**3. Moment**: reach people when the category is salient: Oscar Mayer's Wienermobile visiting schools at lunchtime; *The Economist* on the tops of buses, seen by executives from their office windows. The right moment reduces the need for frequency.

**4. Mindset**: media that suggest a shared interest beyond the product: "Absolut Warhol" in *ARTnews*.

Cindy Halvorsen (ch. 22) warns that TV and digital **impressions** are not comparable: on TV you pay for the audience of a show, in digital often for a "qualified audience" selected by behavior. Media outcomes must be linked to marketing and business outcomes, not evaluated in isolation.

## Application

- For a small budget, first look for a channel you can dominate and the moment of greatest relevance.
- Monitor wearout: change the execution before frequency turns into tedium.
- Ask media partners for insight data, not only media plans.

## Example

Google search data showed a bourbon brand that the top questions were "What is bourbon?" and "How do I drink bourbon?", and that searches peaked between 5:00 and 7:00 p.m.: a moment and a message ready to use.

## Questions

- How does monopolizing work on social platforms, where everyone is present?
- Which indicator would you use to detect wearout in real time?
