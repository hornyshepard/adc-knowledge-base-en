# Category need becomes an objective in only two cases, when people must be reminded of it or when it must be sold, and selling it takes a whole campaign

> **The idea in one sentence:** category need is a perception, so it can be created; but you create it for the whole category, so it pays only for the leader or for the brand that launches the category.

## Explanation

Category need is the target audience's feeling that it needs a product to satisfy a particular need. Because it is a **perception**, the advertiser can establish it. But it produces **primary demand**, which benefits every brand in the category. Selective demand needs brand effects. Most brands live in categories where the need is already established, so this effect becomes an objective in only two situations:

| Situation | When | Execution requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Reminding** of the need | a latent or forgotten need, slackening demand, products bought or used infrequently (pain remedies, traveler's checks) | little dedicated copy; there is plenty of room for the brand. Suits the market share leader, which reaps most of the renewed demand. |
| **Selling** the need | new products; established products aimed at new category users | category-level effects must be created (awareness of the category, attitude and intention toward it) in addition to brand effects. Almost impossible in a single execution: it takes a campaign. |

The same category can be "sold" differently to different segments. With DVDs, some people wanted to be first with something new, others wanted picture quality, and others could no longer find videotapes. These are three perceptions of the need and three strategies.

In a new category the brand must still be present from the start: you first communicate what the category is, but you position the brand as the one that satisfies the new need.

## Application

- Before setting "educating the market" as an objective, ask: are we the leader that will reap the demand? If not, we are paying for the competition's growth.
- When reminding, link the need to a moment ("when you feel the first signs of a cold") and then to the brand.
- In a new category, plan in stages: first the category and the brand as its exemplar, then differentiation.

## Example

Since the 1980s, whenever sales have begun to soften, Campbell's has run a campaign built around "Soup is good food". It reminds people of a category benefit, and because the brand dominates the category, its sales grow along with interest in soup. Planitherm ("Keep the energy in") reminds people of the need to conserve energy, then offers the most energy-efficient window glass.

## Questions

- Should a new electric car brand sell the category or leave that to the leader?
- What does "reminding people of the category need" look like for a subscription service that people forget about?
