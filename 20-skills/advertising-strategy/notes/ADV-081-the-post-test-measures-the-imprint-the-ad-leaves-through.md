# The post-test measures the imprint the ad leaves through five scores; the campaign review measures the whole campaign

> **The idea in one sentence:** after the campaign you check two different things: what the target remembers of the message (post-test) and whether the communication as a whole reached its objectives (campaign review).

## Explanation

*La boîte à outils de la publicité* notes that companies spend about 3% of the communication budget on evaluation. The condition: precise, measurable objectives, with indicators set **before** the campaign.

The **post-test** is a short quantitative survey, face to face or by telephone, often with partial or full re-exposure to the message. The five scores:

| Score | % of the target who… |
|---|---|
| Spontaneous memorization (*mémorisation spontanée*) | say they remember seeing or hearing the ad |
| Proven recognition (*reconnaissance prouvée*) | can describe the message precisely |
| Attribution (*attribution*) | can name the brand or product in the ad |
| Liking and understanding (*agrément et compréhension*) | liked the message (understanding, credibility, adherence, consistency with the brand image) |
| Incitement (*incitation*) | say they are ready to act in the direction the campaign seeks |

For non-media actions (*hors-média*), specific indicators are used: the response rate to a mailing, the coverage generated by a press kit, visitors and orders at a trade show stand.

The **campaign review** (*bilan de campagne*) evaluates the communication as a whole: the direct effect on sales or, if it cannot be isolated, the influence on the prospect (cognitive, affective, conative). Methods: a before-after study (for example, the change in awareness) or **tracking**, a continuous survey that follows the effects over time and in relation to competitors' actions.

In digital, evaluation has two approaches: *site centric* (unique visitors, visit duration, bounce rate, entry and exit pages, traffic sources) and *user centric* (what visitors look for, what difficulties they meet, through surveys and focus groups).

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott would reject some of these scores: for them, ad recall does not predict effectiveness, and brand awareness is measured separately and on a delayed basis ([[ADV-079-the-pretest-is-built-on-the-communication-objectives-with|ADV-079]]). The Jell-O case proves them right ([[ADV-083-jell-o-pudding-with-bill-cosby-the-campaign-with-a-weak|ADV-083]]).

## Application

- Write the KPIs in the brief and ask for the "before" measurement (baseline); without a zero point, "after" says nothing.
- Keep memorization scores separate from attribution scores: a memorable ad attributed to a competitor is a loss.
- Use the campaign review as a tool for progress (adjustments, corrections), not only as a verdict.

## Example

The book's KPI example: on the item "je suis une marque qui propose des véhicules plus respectueux de l'environnement" [I am a brand that offers more environmentally friendly vehicles], the campaign must gain 5 points in the post-test or the brand tracking, measured by an outside consulting firm.
