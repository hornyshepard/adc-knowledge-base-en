# Continuous tracking measures the campaign on the same four steps of the response sequence, and wearout is diagnosed from the plan down to the execution

> **The idea in one sentence:** the pretest only says whether an execution can communicate; only tracking shows whether the campaign actually works in the market, at which step it gives way, and whether the effect comes from advertising or from something else.

## Explanation

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott ask for the campaign to be tracked continuously after launch, together with competitors' activity. Otherwise you cannot separate the effect of advertising from that of price, the trade or rival promotions. Four methods:

| Method | Problem |
|---|---|
| Correlating sales with spending | causality remains unknown |
| Consumer panel | it sensitizes the participants |
| Periodic waves (before-after) | you do not know what happens between measurements; you can miss the effect completely |
| **Continuous tracking** | small, ongoing samples, read as moving averages; the recommended method |

The measures follow the response sequence: **exposure** (GRP, or better, effective reach), **processing** (recognition or recall of the campaign, with a verified description; the rigorous version starts from the category, not from the brand), **communication effects** (the same as in the pretest, but brand awareness is measured first, because the brand must be recognized or recalled before it is bought) and **action** (purchases, scanner data).

**Diagnosing wearout** is done in order, from the plan down to the execution:

1. external factors and the marketing plan: your mix, competitors' mix, what consumers are looking for;
2. the media plan: does it still deliver reach at the minimum effective frequency?
3. the executions. **Processing wearout** occurs when attention drops after repeated exposures (especially in print; the remedy is variations within the same look and feel) or through interference from competitors' executions or from your own new executions. **Attitudinal wearout** comes from overexposure: counterarguing increases until the message is rejected.

## Application

- Ask for continuous tracking for large campaigns, with questions on all four steps, and a benchmark measurement before launch.
- Before declaring "the ad has worn out", check the market and the media first. Only then change the execution.
- Read the effects over time: the result of a burst can appear in the weeks after it airs.

## Example

A launch campaign seems to have no effect at the measurement taken at the end of the flight. Continuous tracking would have shown that the effect grew in the following weeks. Broadbent observes that most of the sales effect of a one-week burst occurs in the first week or two.
