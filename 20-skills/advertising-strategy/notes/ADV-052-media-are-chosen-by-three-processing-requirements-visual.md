# Media are chosen by three processing requirements (visual content, time to process, frequency potential), matched to the brand awareness and brand attitude strategy

> **The idea in one sentence:** you do not choose a medium because "the target audience is there" but because it allows the message to be processed: radio cannot show the package, and television leaves no time for long arguments.

## Explanation

Media must be selected according to the communication objectives. The authors check three characteristics:

1. **Visual content**: required for recognition brand awareness (the brand shown as it will be seen on the shelf) and for transformational strategies (emotional authenticity).
2. **Time to process the message**: required for the high-involvement informational strategy, where the message must be accepted (durable goods, B2B decisions).
3. **Frequency potential**: needed for recall brand awareness (the need → brand link is built through repetition) and for the low-involvement transformational strategy (attitude builds slowly, and the purchase cycle is short).

The media ratings (Tables 10.3–10.4, simplified):

| Medium | Visual | Time to process | Frequency | Not suited to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TV | yes | short | high | high-involvement informational |
| Radio | no | short | high | recognition, any transformational strategy, high-involvement informational |
| Newspapers | limited (color) | long | high | recognition only with good color reproduction |
| Magazines | yes | long | limited | recall and low-involvement transformational, with limitations |
| Posters | yes | long | limited (a stationary medium) | recall, low-involvement transformational, high-involvement informational: with limitations |
| Digital | yes | long | high | suits every strategy |
| Direct mail | yes | long | low | recall and low-involvement transformational: with limitations |

By quadrant: low-involvement informational works in almost any medium (the benefit must be learned in one or two exposures), except when the benefit has to be demonstrated. Low-involvement transformational needs visuals and frequency, so TV is ideal. High-involvement informational needs print or digital. High-involvement transformational needs visuals but not high frequency, because purchase cycles are long.

The media chosen must satisfy **both** objectives. With recognition, the brand attitude strategy decides. With recall, you check compatibility with both.

## Application

- Put the type of brand awareness and the grid quadrant into the media brief, before the list of channels.
- Use one primary medium that can deliver all the objectives, with most of the budget, and one or two secondary media that reinforce a specific objective.
- If you only have radio, you can support a recall strategy. But radio that really makes you see the product is the exception, not the rule.

## Example

Twix (Mars) used TV and online for the "Twix Meltdown" video and social media for the competition in which you could win the tool. The book's question: why not radio? The answer comes from the grid: package recognition and a transformational strategy need visuals.

## Questions

- Where would you place TikTok or podcasts in the table above?
- Is the claim that digital "works for everything" compatible with the authors' skepticism about digital effectiveness (ch. 11)?
