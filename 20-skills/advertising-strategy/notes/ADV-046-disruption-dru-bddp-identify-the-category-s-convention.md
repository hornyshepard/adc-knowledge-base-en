# Disruption (Dru, BDDP): identify the category's convention, break it and propose a vision

> **The idea in one sentence:** a category's conventions are opinions, not facts; that is exactly why they can be challenged, and challenging them coherently gives the brand a creative leap (*saut créatif*) and a new direction.

## Explanation

Disruption was developed by the BDDP agency (Boulet Dru Dupuy Petit) in the second half of the 1980s as a "mode de pensée" [way of thinking], and it got its definitive name at the very start of the 1990s, popularized by Jean-Marie Dru. *La boîte à outils de la publicité* places it in the line of creative strategy methods, after the USP, the copy strategy, the Y&R creative work plan (*Plan de travail créatif*) and the star strategy. The method has three stages:

| Stage | What you do | Who leads |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Convention** | list the received ideas, stereotypes and commonplaces of the category that nobody questions anymore | strategic planning, together with the client |
| **2. Disruption** | find the idea that breaks the convention: the opposite tack (*contre-pied*), the new angle | the creative team |
| **3. Vision** | define the future direction of the brand (and sometimes of the market) and put it into practice, including in the 360° media plan | the agency and the client |

The break is not an end in itself. The authors warn that the point is not to break the codes just to get talked about: without a long-term vision, disruption is gratuitous. The break must be coherent and even seem natural, obvious, without the work behind it showing. The gain: a serious competitive advantage and free media coverage.

## Application

- List 10–15 conventions of the category (what the ads look like, what everyone promises, how they talk, which colors they use). Mark the ones that are merely habits.
- For each convention, ask: what would happen if the brand did the opposite? Would it be credible, given the product?
- Write the vision as a sentence about the future before looking for the execution.

## Example

The book gives no detailed disruption case. A useful seminar exercise: the convention of the detergent category ("whiter than white", demonstration, the satisfied housewife) and where a credible break could begin.

## Questions

- When every brand in the category "disrupts", the break itself becomes a convention. What do you do then?
- How does disruption differ from the "create a new category" positioning of Ries and Trout?
