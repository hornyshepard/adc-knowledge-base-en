# Whopper Detour turned McDonald's restaurants into ordering points for Burger King to get the app downloaded

> **The idea in one sentence:** a promotion with many steps can work if the reward is big, the idea is provocative and earned media replaces the paid budget.

## Explanation

The case study in chapter 4 (Lars Pynt Andersen) is used to discuss the communication response sequence and compound probabilities.

**The business problem.** Mobile apps give fast-food brands a direct channel of communication, ordering and payment. They can track the customer through GPS and tailor promotions to previous consumption patterns. The first and hardest step is getting people to download the app, then to create a profile and log in. The Burger King app was lagging behind its competitors. After a year of redesign (geofencing, reliable ordering and payment), the brand kicked off the campaign.

**The mechanism.** The offer: a Whopper for one penny, ordered from the app *only when you are at a McDonald's*. Four steps were explained in the ads: download the app, drive to McDonald's, order a Whopper, pick it up at the nearest Burger King. A print ad showed "the King" changing the McDonald's sign from "Billions Served" to "Billions Swerved". Mobile arrow-shaped frames with "The Whopper Detour Available Here" were placed in front of McDonald's restaurants, so that bystanders would photograph them and post the pictures.

**The media.** The paid media spending was minuscule: a few print ads, some mobile out-of-home, the frames, a PR video on YouTube, no TV.

**Results.** The app jumped from number 686 to number 1 on the list of most downloaded apps, on both Android and iOS. 4.5 million downloads followed. Sales through the app tripled during the promotion and doubled after it. Burger King reported a return on investment of 37 to 1. The idea also solved a structural problem: McDonald's has twice as many restaurants in the US, and now each of them became a starting point toward Burger King.

## Application

- As an exercise, estimate the probability of each step of your mechanism. If the product is very small, raise the reward or cut steps.
- Look for an idea that turns the competitor's strength (the large network) into an advantage for you.
- Think from the start about what the public will photograph and post: here, the arrow frame in front of McDonald's.
