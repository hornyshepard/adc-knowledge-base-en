# Product placement and native advertising bypass resistance, but their effect is disputed, and they backfire on the brand when they are too visible or linked to something negative

> **The idea in one sentence:** integrating the brand into content avoids channel switching and lowers the perception of persuasive intent, but the effect depends on centrality, the character, the context and disclosure, and Percy doubts there is any effect on attitude.

## Explanation

**Product placement** is the paid inclusion of a brand in media content. Fennis and Stroebe classify it by modality (seen, or also mentioned) and centrality to the plot (background, used by a character, relevant to the story). The results they cite:

- central placement increases **explicit memory**; implicit memory and choice increase through mere placement (Yang & Roskos-Ewoldsen, 2007);
- **the character matters**: a branded T-shirt worn by a liked rap artist improved brand attitude, worn by a disliked one it worsened it (Schemer et al., 2008);
- **the context matters**: Mercedes asked for its cars to be erased from the slum scenes of *Slumdog Millionaire*; blatant placement in *Seinfeld* irritated the sitcom's fans;
- compared with a commercial, subtle placement is weaker for memory, and prominent placement stronger.

**Native advertising** is the online equivalent: the ad looks like the editorial article, and fewer than one in ten readers recognize it. Disclosure activates persuasion knowledge, which produces **cognitive** resistance (counterarguments) and **affective** resistance (annoyance with the brand).

**The opposite position.** Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott are much more skeptical. The few empirical studies do not find placement effective; the successes, such as the Ray-Ban sunglasses in *Top Gun*, are anecdotal. The most likely effect is a rise in brand awareness, but even this requires conscious attention. Implicit processing, they say, has no effect on brand attitude or behavior, which contradicts the results on implicit memory above. They also mention Rossiter and Bellman's ethical criticism: the audience does not always know it is being persuaded. Practitioners believe placement works better when accompanied by advertising.

## Application

- Negotiate centrality and the character, not only the "appearance": who uses the product and in which scene.
- Avoid blatant placement in programs with devoted fans, and use placement only in a context consistent with the positioning.
- In branded content, annoyance is the main risk: be transparent and useful, not disguised.

## Example

James Bond switching from Aston Martin to BMW is the example with which Fennis and Stroebe open the discussion (p. 239).

## Questions

- Is native advertising that nine out of ten readers do not recognize ethical?
- What should a test measure to settle the dispute between implicit memory and the absence of an effect on attitude?
