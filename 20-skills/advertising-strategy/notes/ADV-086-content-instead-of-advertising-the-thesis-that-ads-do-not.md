# Content instead of advertising? The thesis that ads do not build relationships, and its limits

> **The idea in one sentence:** Wuebben argues that content marketing has replaced advertising as the way to build relationships; yet his own data, Ejeke's and Percy's show that content also needs distribution money and that the opposition is a false one.

## Explanation

**The thesis (Wuebben, SRC-008).** Content marketing *is* marketing, and it will still be the standard in 2030: valuable, relevant and consistent content that attracts and acquires a clearly defined audience (the CMI definition). An ad has never made the author feel closer to a company, only interrupted: "It builds the relationship. Ads don't do that." Brands become publishers (Red Bull Media House, PepsiCo, Mondelez).

**The data that support it (Ejeke, SRC-010).** 763.5 million people use ad blockers. Native ads have an average CTR of 0.80%, against 0.09% for display ads (AppNexus).

**The limits, from the sources themselves.**
1. Wuebben cites CMI: five years ago, enterprises spent 80% on content creation and 20% on content promotion, and the ratio has switched. Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott confirm it: brands spend almost as much on the creation and management of branded content as they do on paid online advertising.
2. Native advertising, the "gateway drug" to content marketing (Joe Pulizzi), is still paid advertising, only harder to recognize.
3. Wuebben admits that the ads that work are experiential campaigns on camera and that ads need to get better. The author sells content marketing software, an interest he declares in the text itself.

**The third voice (Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott).** Content marketing differs from advertising in two ways: it is found on owned or earned media (if there is a media buy, it is advertising), and it relies on a *pull*, not a *push*, strategy. The ideal content is not about the product but about what interests the target audience, with a clear link to the brand. Subway gained share among teenagers with a web series about the first job, filmed in a Subway restaurant. But there is still no good way to measure it, and in digital, content is rarely flagged as advertising. McGuire had shown that forewarning of the intent to persuade does not reduce effectiveness; it actually increases it.

## Application

- When a client says "we don't do ads anymore, we do content", ask how the content will be distributed and with what budget.
- Label sponsored content clearly: whatever you gain from confusion you pay for in trust.

## Example

Red Bull's filming of a skydive from the edge of space created a great deal of word of mouth without visibly promoting the product. Seminar exercise: how much of the success comes from the content, and how much from paid distribution and the sponsorships around it?

## Questions

- Does advertising build a relationship? What would the authors who link effectiveness to memory and emotion say?
- The CMI statistics are produced by an organization that promotes content marketing. How do you weigh them?
