# A media plan is judged by reach at the minimum effective frequency, not by GRPs or average frequency, and when in doubt it is better to choose frequency

> **The idea in one sentence:** it is better to communicate successfully with part of the target audience than to reach a lot of people without the message being processed.

## Explanation

The basic vocabulary (Table 10.1):

- **reach**: the percentage of the target audience exposed at least once in a given period, usually four weeks;
- **frequency**: how many times an individual is exposed;
- **OTS** (*opportunities to see*): cumulative exposures;
- **GRP**: reach × frequency;
- **effective frequency**: the number of OTS needed for the ad to have an effect.

Scheduling can be done in **flights** (2–3 weeks in the market, then a few weeks out), in **bursts** (heavy advertising a few times a year) or **continuously**.

The authors' criticism is harsh. Reach and cumulative reach, in the conventional sense, bear no relationship to a plan's effectiveness. They coincide with it only if the required frequency is 1, which almost never happens. Average frequency figures are, as McDonald puts it, "strictly meaningless" abstractions. An average of 3 can mean that everyone saw the ad three times, or that half saw it three times, a quarter once and another quarter five times. Rossiter and Bellman point out that the average almost always exaggerates the frequency with which the typical member of the target audience is reached. And 180 GRPs can be 90% × 2 or 45% × 4.

The right concept is **minimum effective frequency** (MEF): the minimum number of exposures within an advertising cycle for the message to be fully processed at least once. It is almost always higher than 1. It is higher for groups that are not loyal and highest for new category users. It rises with competitors' advertising pressure and depends on the execution's ability to gain attention. **Effective reach** is the number of people in the target audience reached at least at the MEF.

Because the budget never allows maximum reach, high frequency and a long duration at the same time, the plan makes trade-offs among reach, frequency and timing. Below the MEF, advertising probably does not work at all. Hence the rule: **in the trade-off between reach and frequency, err on the side of frequency**. High frequency is needed above all for recall and for strategies based on positive motives.

## Application

- Ask the media agency for the frequency distribution (how many people at 1, 2, 3+ exposures), not just GRPs and average frequency.
- Set the MEF by target group: higher for a launch aimed at new users than for a maintenance campaign aimed at loyals.
- When the budget is small, narrow the target audience or the period instead of "thinning out" frequency below the threshold.

## Example

A new energy drink brand has a budget for 300 GRPs. The wrong option: 75% reach × 4 OTS on average, spread over three months. The recommended option: concentrate on one city or one segment, with 3+ OTS per cycle for most of the people reached.
