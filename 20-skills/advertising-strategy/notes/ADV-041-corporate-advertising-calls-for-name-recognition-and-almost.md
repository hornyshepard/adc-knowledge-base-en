# Corporate advertising calls for name recognition and almost always a transformational strategy, with high involvement when the target audience must be convinced

> **The idea in one sentence:** people who pay attention to an ad about a company are not solving a problem but want to learn something; learning for its own sake is a positive motive, so the strategy is transformational.

## Explanation

The authors also apply the Rossiter-Percy grid to the "brand" that is the company itself.

**Brand awareness:** companies want their name to be recognized, so the awareness strategy is **recognition**.

**Brand attitude:** the aim is a general positive attitude, that is, people should like the company. There is no immediate purchase decision. Whoever pays attention to the ad wants to learn something about the company, and learning simply to acquire knowledge is almost always positively motivated. The result is a **transformational** strategy. The level of involvement remains to be decided:

| Situation | Strategy |
|---|---|
| General goodwill, maintaining a positive attitude | transformational, low involvement |
| Negative publicity with a weak effect | transformational, low involvement |
| A seriously damaged image | transformational, high involvement: the target audience must be convinced |
| Investors who "feel" a company before analyzing it | transformational, high involvement |
| Regulatory agencies | transformational, high involvement: the company wants any interaction to start from a strongly positive association |

If the ad targets a **specific decision** (buy shares, recommend the bond issue, approve the project), the strategy follows the risk and motive of that decision, as with any brand. For example, the investment decision itself is negatively motivated.

**Retail advertising** works in a similar way: the retailer becomes the brand. For image, the strategy is transformational. For information about the store, it is informational. Brand awareness is **recall**, because the retailer wants to be included in the consideration set.

## Application

- When you write for a company, do not try to "sell" products in the image ad. Build affect and personalize (people, faces, few words).
- After a serious crisis, do not rely on liking alone. The target audience must *believe* that the company has changed, so the execution needs proof built into the emotional experience.
- Keep the reputation campaign separate from a campaign for a specific decision (a share offering, for example).

## Example

The AbbVie ad in the book has only four words in the headline ("Takes all of us"), a personalized image and the tagline "People. Passion. Possibilities." It does not sell a drug; it builds affect for the company. IKEA, as retailer and brand, uses an informational strategy instead (the headline underscores the benefit of shopping at IKEA: finding new wonders) and the blue of the shopping bag as the link to the brand.

## Questions

- What strategy would an employer branding campaign call for: low or high involvement?
- What happens when a company uses corporate advertising on a controversial social issue?
