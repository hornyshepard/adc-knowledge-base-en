# Strategic communication planning has five steps, from selecting the target audience to media strategy, and each step follows from the one before

> **The idea in one sentence:** after reviewing the marketing plan, the strategist selects the target audience, understands how it decides, positions the brand, sets the communication strategy and only then chooses the media; all the authors require the same order, even if they count the steps differently.

## Explanation

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott's process is the backbone of their book (ch. 6–11) and of this skill. The other textbooks fit onto the same steps:

| Percy & Rosenbaum-Elliott | The question | *La boîte à outils* | Grewal & Levy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Review the marketing plan | what must communication support? | SWOT → diagnosis → a single problem | (none) |
| 1. Select the target audience | where will sales come from: trial or repeat purchase? | targets: main, core (*cœur de cible*), influence | 1. identify target audience |
| 2. Understand how the target audience makes decisions | who takes part, in which roles and stages? | (none) | (none) |
| 3. Determine the positioning | "what is it?" and "what does it offer?" | positioning | 4. convey the message (USP, appeal) |
| 4. Develop a communication strategy | which brand awareness and brand attitude objectives? | objectives, creative strategy | 2. set advertising objectives; 6. create advertisements |
| 5. Set media strategy | how does the message reach people so that it is processed? | media and non-media (*hors média*) | 5. evaluate and select media |
| campaign: budget, tracking | | budget and calendar | 3. determine the advertising budget; 7. assess impact |

**Where they converge.** All of them put objectives before the message and the message before the media. Barre and Gayrard-Carrera state the rule of coherence: nothing appears in the solution unless it appeared in the diagnosis. In Percy, the same rule means that each step follows from the one before.

**Where they differ.** Percy adds the step that general textbooks skip, understanding the decision (who, where, when, how), and defines the target audience by behavior toward the brand, not by demographics. Grewal and Levy put the budget and the evaluation in their list; Percy deals with them at the level of the campaign. Percy explicitly rejects the idea that social media makes planning unnecessary: any message, however it is delivered, needs a target audience, a positioning and a strategy.

Two things are often forgotten. The trade (distributors, retailers) is almost always part of the target audience, as initiator or influencer. The calendar is built by backward planning (*rétroplanning*), from the key date back.

## Application

- Use the five steps as the structure of the strategy document behind a brief and of the presentation to the client.
- The "why?" test: every channel, tone or target in the plan must point back to the link that justifies it.
- Do not choose a channel (step 5) before you know the type of brand awareness and the brand attitude strategy (step 4).

## Example

Aperol Spritz in the US: the target audience was the urban elites of New York (step 1). The decision was made in bars and at events, in front of others (step 2). Awareness relied on recognizing the orange glass (step 4). The media were booths at festivals, an orange bus and moments designed for Instagram (step 5).

## Questions

- Which step is most often skipped in performance agencies? What are the consequences?
- Where can the creative idea change the positioning or the target audience after the fact?
