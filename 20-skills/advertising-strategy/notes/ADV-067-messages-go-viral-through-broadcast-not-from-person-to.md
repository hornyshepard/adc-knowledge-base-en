# Messages go viral through broadcast, not from person to person

> **The idea in one sentence:** people share information only with a few people close to them, so a piece of content goes "viral" almost always because a mass channel exposed it first; the exception is communities that already exist.

## Explanation

Brian Sternthal takes apart the myth of spontaneous virality. The research he cites shows that most sharing of information, online and offline, occurs among a few groups: family members, co-workers, college friends, neighbors. Most people have close ties with 5 to 10 people, who receive about 80% of their communications. On Yahoo, Facebook and other social media sites, **96% of "cascades"** (instances in which one person shares with another) involve no sharing or sharing with only one or two people, and 99% involve fewer than seven.

So how did "Gangnam Style" get over three billion views? The explanation: a message is first exposed **one-to-many** (broadcast), on YouTube, talk shows and news programs, and then each person exposed passes it on to a few others. The Dollar Shave Club launch video (over 25 million views) had a message that resonated (less costly blades and razors) in a form entertaining enough to get noticed on Reddit, with its 400 million unique visitors.

**The exception:** when a close-knit **community** is already in place (political parties, online gamers, runners), cascades are numerous, and social media is enough. Tough Mudder grew participation for six years using only Facebook, because its target (military personnel, firefighters and police who engaged in extreme physical activity) communicated intensely within their own networks. Once the core communities had been tapped, it moved to TV programs and commercials. To get beyond the community you need broadcast or a voice with many followers.

## Application

- When the client asks for "a viral", include the source of initial exposure in the plan: paid media, PR, big influencers, aggregator platforms.
- Find out whether the target forms a real community; if it does, you can start from inside it.
- Design the content so that it is easy to pass on to the 5 to 10 close ties (personal relevance), not "for everyone".

## Example

Tough Mudder is the textbook case of both phases: first diffusion from inside an already formed community, then, once the community was tapped, classic broadcast to attract people outside it. Similarly, "#LikeAGirl" (Always, ch. 11) moved from online views to the biggest broadcast possible, the Super Bowl.

## Questions

- Do recommendation algorithms (TikTok) change Sternthal's conclusion? Is the algorithm also a broadcast?
- What does this mean for small budgets that hope for free virality?
