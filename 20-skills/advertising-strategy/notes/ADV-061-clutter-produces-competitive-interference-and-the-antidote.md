# Clutter produces competitive interference, and the antidote is a distinctive retrieval cue at the point of purchase

> **The idea in one sentence:** competitors' ads in the same category make us forget what our brand said, especially when the executions look alike; a visual cue from the ad placed on the package brings the memory back.

## Explanation

We forget because of the passage of time and also because of **interference**: new, similar information covers the old (retroactive interference), and old information gets in the way of the new (proactive interference). With nearly one in every four minutes of US prime-time television taken by commercials or other non-programming messages, advertising clutter makes interference everywhere.

Results presented by Fennis and Stroebe:

- **Burke and Srull (1988):** ads for other brands in the same product class reduced recall of the target brand's claims, but not among those who processed the ad while thinking about a purchase (deeper processing).
- **Kumar and Krishnan (2004):** similar executions interfere too: a suntan lotion and an ice tea, both with a beach scene, were confused when the picture was the retrieval cue.
- **Kent and Allen (1993):** familiar brands are protected: the new information is linked to an existing schema. Unfamiliar brands suffer the most.
- **Keller (1987):** a small reproduction of the ad's photo and headline on the package partly made up for the effects of interference.

Moderators: the processing goal (a purchase versus mere interest) and brand familiarity.

## Application

- Avoid the category's visual codes (the same scene, the same palette) if you want to be remembered; distinctiveness protects against interference.
- Link the ad to the shelf: a key element from the spot (a character, a picture, a slogan) on the package, at the point of sale or in the app.
- For new brands, clutter is more dangerous: choose moments and media with fewer direct competitors.

## Example

The Keller study: the visual cue from the ad on a corner of the package worked as a "reminder" at the purchase.

## Questions

- Which visual codes are so common in a category that they interfere (for example, bank = blue)?
- How does the idea of the retrieval cue translate into the digital environment?
