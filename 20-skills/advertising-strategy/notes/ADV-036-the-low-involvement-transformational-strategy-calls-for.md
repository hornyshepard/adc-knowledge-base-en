# The low-involvement transformational strategy calls for emotional authenticity linked to the brand, and since the benefit is the execution itself, the ad must be liked

> **The idea in one sentence:** with ice cream, chocolate, beer or soft drinks, people buy a pleasure; the ad must make them feel it now and link that feeling to the brand, not give them arguments.

## Explanation

The quadrant covers low-risk, positively motivated decisions (sensory gratification, sometimes social approval). **The principles** (Table 9.5):

1. Elicit the **correct emotional response**.
2. The ad must ring true: **emotional authenticity**. This is not literal realism; the target audience must see itself emotionally in the role of the person using the product (the ice cream bar looks so good that you want one right now).
3. The emotion must be **linked to the brand**.
4. The ad **must be liked**, because "the benefit is in the execution."

**The creative tactics** (Box 13.2):

- an execution **unique** to the brand, with a strong visual component, used consistently over time. An emotional ad that is not linked to the brand is wasted;
- a C → E⁺ or pure E⁺ benefit focus. The emotional sequence runs from dull or neutral to joy (sensory gratification);
- humor and pleasant music increase liking for the ad;
- **source**: attractiveness, more precisely *likeability*;
- music: tempo and pitch matched to the emotion (happiness means a fast tempo, medium volume, high pitch). The music must not resemble another brand's. Avoid popular songs, because people sing the lyrics to themselves instead of hearing the brand.

**Media**: visuals and **high frequency**, because attitude forms slowly and the purchase cycle is short. Television is ideal, digital works, radio is excluded (only highly creative radio can make you "see" the product).

A common trap is the temptation to "strengthen" the ad with a tangible benefit or an offer. A price offer addresses a negative motive and can interfere with the emotion.

## Application

- In pretests, measure liking for the ad and the intensity of the emotion, not just message recall.
- Build a distinctive visual asset (a color, a gesture, a character) and use it for years. It will trigger the emotion from nondeclarative memory.
- Do not tack a promotion onto the end of an emotional commercial. Run the promotion separately.

## Example

In Sweden, Toblerone used the AdBox pretesting technique to test two versions of the same emotional commercial. The second added a *loading device* at the end (10% more chocolate for the normal price). The original version consistently outperformed it, including on buying interest: 65% versus 55%. Kinder Bueno (mostly white space, relaxed contentment, the package clearly shown) and Häagen-Dazs (from neutral to intense pleasure, reinforced by the copy) are the book's positive examples.

## Questions

- How do you measure "emotional authenticity" without confusing it with realism?
- What does "the advertising must be liked" mean for brands that deliberately make provocative ads (Liquid Death)?
