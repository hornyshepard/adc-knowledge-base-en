# The target audience is chosen from five buyer groups, and each group calls for trial or repeat purchase, at different costs

> **The idea in one sentence:** the target audience is not "women 25–45" but a group defined by its behavior toward the brand: our brand loyals, those who switch between us and other brands, those who switch among other brands, the competitors' loyals, or new users of the category.

## Explanation

Every action objective is, at bottom, **trial** or **repeat purchase**. This holds for consumers, retailers (listing vs. bigger orders), voters, employees or regulators. Rossiter and Percy divide the market into five buyer groups, and Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott add the brand awareness and brand attitude considerations:

| Group | Objective | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Brand loyals (BL) | repeat purchase | highest awareness, most positive brand attitude, "immunized" (McGuire's inoculation theory). Not always the right target for growth: people do not "use more" toothpaste or cold remedies. |
| Favorable brand switchers (FBS) | repeat purchase | the bulk of the business and the main source of growth. Awareness must be maintained. Check how strongly promotions hold them. |
| Other brand switchers (OBS) | trial | the best potential for new customers, if they know us and do not see us as too expensive. |
| Other brand loyals (OBL) | trial | the hardest to persuade, immunized by their attitude toward their own brand. |
| New category users (NCU) | trial | awareness of and attitude toward *the category* matter. Find out why they do not use the category. |

A special case is evolving category users: a new mother suddenly enters dozens of new categories. They must be reached *before* the need arises.

**Cost** follows a simple rule: it costs less to maintain your business than to build on it, and less to build on it than to attract new business. The most expensive is going after the competitors' loyals. A group enters the target audience only if the expected return outweighs the cost. For FMCG products, the authors estimate that about 60% of sales growth comes from new customers who continue to buy and 40% from existing customers who buy more.

The French school describes the target differently, in layers (main, core, influence), with socio-demographic data ([[ADV-017-the-communication-target-has-layers-main-target-core-target|ADV-017]]); Percy keeps demographics only for the profile.

## Application

- In the brief, replace the "demographic target" with the buyer group, then use demographics only for the profile.
- For trial, choose promotions such as samples, coupons and refunds. For repeat purchase, loyalty programs, contests and premiums.
- Estimate the cost per group before you promise growth from "winning the competitors' customers".

## Example

A coffee bean brand with a 10% share finds that most of its buyers switch among three brands. The right strategy is not a conquest campaign. It must strengthen brand attitude and keep the brand present in the minds of this group, which already buys it sometimes.

## Questions

- What would the Ehrenberg-Bass school (Byron Sharp) say about the emphasis on loyalty here?
- How do you apply the groups to a subscription service, where almost no one "switches"?
