# The brand attitude objective takes five forms (create, increase, maintain, modify, change) and is chosen only once you know what the target audience already believes

> **The idea in one sentence:** "improving the image" is not an objective; the brand attitude objective depends on where the target audience starts from, and a single negative belief can cancel all the others.

## Explanation

Brand attitude is always a communication objective, but the authors distinguish five options. The first step is always to understand the target audience's attitudes toward the category and toward the brands it sees as competing in it.

| Objective | When | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| **Create** | new category users or a new brand | young mothers form brand attitudes toward baby food before their babies are born |
| **Increase** | a moderately favorable attitude: almost anyone who occasionally buys the brand | most cases |
| **Maintain** | a strong positive attitude, a mature market, loyal customers, stable competition | watch for positive shifts in attitudes toward competitors |
| **Modify** | repositioning: you appeal to the target audience through a different motivation or a different benefit, especially when you can no longer increase the attitude | diet products repositioned toward "watching your weight" (Lean Cuisine), then toward "healthier"; Arm & Hammer in the refrigerator, to absorb odors; Vaseline as a lip balm |
| **Change** | a significant part of the target audience holds a negative belief | VW Polo L |

The "change" case is the most delicate. A significant negative belief **dominates** the positive beliefs. The consumer thinks "yes, but…". The negative link between the brand and the purchase motive must be removed without harming the positive beliefs. Sometimes the change is not feasible, and that segment must be dropped from the target audience.

## Application

- Measure the starting attitude in each buyer group before the brief. The same brand can need "maintain" among loyals and "change" in a segment that thinks it is expensive.
- For "change", pin down that exact "but" and build the message around it, while explicitly keeping the positives.
- For "modify", change the motive or the benefit, not just the tone.

## Example

In the mid-1990s, Volkswagen had significantly reduced its prices in the UK, yet people still saw it as unaffordable: quality (a positive belief) but expensive (a negative belief). Posters in London Underground stations imitated the Underground's danger warnings, with humor. The eye was drawn to the price and then to the tagline "Surprisingly ordinary prices." The negative belief was dealt with without jeopardizing the quality image.

## Questions

- Why is it harder to change a negative attitude about price than one about taste?
- When is it more profitable to give up a segment than to change its attitude?
