# Recognition brand awareness (brand → need, at the shelf) and recall brand awareness (need → brand, before purchase) call for opposite creative and media tactics

> **The idea in one sentence:** first find out from the BSM how the brand is used in the decision: if it is seen at the shelf and reminds people of the need, you need recognition; if the need comes first and the brand must come to mind, you need recall.

## Explanation

Brand awareness is the ability to identify the brand well enough to buy it. It does not necessarily require the name. Silk Cut advertised with nothing but a piece of purple silk with a cut. Yorkshire Tea is recognized by the red stripe over the countryside picture, McDonald's by its Golden Arches. The authors say brand awareness is "widely misunderstood, even among advertising professionals." A recall objective means recall of the **brand** in response to the need, not recall of the advertising. Day-after recall tests measure something else.

The theoretical basis is the principle of **encoding specificity** (Endel Tulving): retrieval succeeds when the cue at the moment of recall matches what was encoded at learning, and in the same order.

| | Recognition | Recall |
|---|---|---|
| Order in memory | brand → category need | category need → brand |
| Where it is activated | at the point of purchase | before purchase |
| Creative tactics (Table 13.7) | show the package exactly as it will be seen on the shelf; the category need must be obvious. Some FMCG ads look like the package itself. | present the need **first**, then the brand; repeat the association within the same execution; personalize ("I", "you") |
| Media | need visuals: radio is ruled out | need frequency: any medium, but more repetition |

Both objectives at once are rare and difficult. Rossiter and Percy call this option *brand-recall-boosted brand recognition* and warn against using it casually in the hope of covering both bases. It is justified only when the actual decision requires it. The example is beer: you recognize it in the supermarket, but in a bar you must recall the name.

A prior condition: the ad must be perceived as belonging to the brand. Otherwise no association forms. We all remember funny ads without knowing which brand they were for.

## Application

- In the brief, write "brand awareness: recognition" or "brand awareness: recall" and justify it from the BSM.
- For recall, check the script: does the need appear before the name? Is the need → brand pair repeated?
- For recognition, check that the package in the ad is identical to the one on the shelf (color, angle, format).

## Example

Little Greene (paints) puts the need first: you want to redecorate a room, then the brand appears, and the green used throughout the execution reinforces the name. Nobody sees a paint can on the shelf and decides there and then to redecorate. Panasonic Viera does the same: "Enjoy wildlife as nature intended" (the need), "thanks to Panasonic" (the brand). Warner Breaks personalizes: when you need a break, *you* are invited to one of its 13 hotels.

## Questions

- On an online marketplace (Amazon, eMAG), is brand awareness recognition or recall? And with a voice assistant?
- Why are abstract or new names more exposed to *blocking* (Schacter) when the link to the need is missing?
