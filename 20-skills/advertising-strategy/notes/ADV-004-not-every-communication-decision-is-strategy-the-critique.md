# Not every communication decision is strategy: the critique of strategic advertising monism

> **The idea in one sentence:** "strategy is central and unique" for a brand; the media plan, the creative idea or the digital activation are most often tactical decisions that apply it, not separate "strategies."

## Explanation

Fernández Gómez uses the term **strategic advertising monism** for the industry's habit of sticking the word "strategy" on everything: "creative strategy," "media strategy," "sponsorship strategy," "PR strategy," "digital strategy." Under this all-encompassing prism every decision becomes strategic (a plan, a stage, an action, a medium) and, as a result, none is.

The author grants that tactical decisions take part in a strategy and can sometimes change its course. But most of them are adaptations of language, process stages or logical continuations of a decision already taken higher up. His explanation for the abuse of the term is commercial: specialized firms, media companies and purchasing networks talk about "strategy" to give their offering intellectual force. Almost every agency highlights its expertise in strategic planning, even when it has no people trained for it.

The same critique applies to books: many titles with "strategy" on the cover are in fact about technology, creativity or business.

## Application

- When you write a deck, keep the word "strategy" for the central choice: what change we are after, in whose mind, and which mechanism we rely on. The rest are "plans," "tactics" or "executions."
- The quick test: if the decision can change without changing what the brand says and to whom, it is tactical.
- With the client, one clearly stated strategy is more convincing than five parallel "strategies" (creative, media, social, PR, CRM).

## Example

A plan with a "TikTok strategy," an "influencer strategy" and an "OOH strategy," but no sentence saying which brand problem all three solve, shows exactly the monism the author describes: many labels, no strategy.

## Questions

- Where is the line? Can the choice to launch a brand only through creators be strategic, not just tactical?
- Would a media planner agree that media planning is "only" tactical?
