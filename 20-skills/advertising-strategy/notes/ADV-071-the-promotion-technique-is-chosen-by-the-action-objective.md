# The promotion technique is chosen by the action objective (trial or repeat purchase), but it must also build the brand, carrying the same benefit and look

> **The idea in one sentence:** promotion speeds up purchase intention, but it is still communication: a coupon that is kept or the package of a sample goes on carrying the name and the benefit, so promotions must be built strategically rather than used to "buy" sales.

## Explanation

There are three levels of promotion. Consumers do not tell them apart (to them it is all "advertising"), but for the brand the difference is large.

- **Trade promotion**: discounts, free goods, display material, incentives for staff. The trade profits from the category, not from the brand, so these promotions must win and keep distribution and cooperation.
- **Retail promotion**: almost always price-based, independent of the brand's strategy, the "wild card" of integration. An explicit comparison between regular price and sale price beats the "now only" formula, and "today only" triggers the scarcity effect.
- **Consumer promotion**: the six basic techniques (Table 9.8, 15.1).

| Technique | Strength | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Coupons | trial; low cost; redemption rate 2–5% | calculate the redemption rate precisely; online fraud |
| Refunds | trial for expensive goods | delayed reward; the process must be simple |
| Sampling | **the most effective technique for trial** | expensive; ideal when the difference can be demonstrated |
| Loyalty / *loading* | repeat purchase: continuity, bonus packs | price cuts subsidize regular users |
| Sweepstakes, games, contests | repeat purchase, excitement, low cost | low conversion; legal requirements |
| Premiums | both objectives | perceived value; shelf space |

The two "great commandments" (Simmons): the promotion directly serves the objectives and is compatible with the brand's total proposition. Prentice called for *consumer franchise building* promotions, which build awareness and attitude. Promotion comes after advertising has created awareness and an at least tentatively favorable attitude, and it must be **created as a promotion**, not added to an ad: in the Toblerone test, the added offer weakened the emotional commercial. It also has defensive roles: a *loading* before a competitor's launch temporarily takes brand switchers out of the market.

## Application

- Start from the target group: trial for other-brand switchers or new category users (sampling, coupons), repeat purchase for loyal buyers and for those who switch between us and others (loyalty, contests).
- Choose prizes consistent with the image: an exotic island for Bounty, not for a ski wear brand.
- Calculate the scenario of excessive success. A promotion that succeeds too well can be a disaster.

## Example

In the Philippines, Pepsi offered the equivalent of 26,000 pounds to anyone finding a bottle top with a specific number. Thousands of winning bottle tops had been distributed by mistake. Pepsi paid out 8 million pounds, then stopped paying, and what followed were public demonstrations, grenade attacks on bottling plants and executives who fled the country with bodyguards. Hoover (free flights to the US) is the similar British case.

## Questions

- Is Whopper Detour a promotion that builds the brand? What effect did it have on attitude?
- How does the rule "same story, same look" apply to online discount codes and to influencers?
