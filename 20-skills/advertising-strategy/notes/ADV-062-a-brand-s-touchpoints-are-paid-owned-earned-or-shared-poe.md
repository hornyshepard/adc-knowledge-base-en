# A brand's touchpoints are paid, owned, earned or shared (POE and PESO), and they must be combined

> **The idea in one sentence:** every encounter between a person and the brand is a chance to communicate; a good plan lists the touchpoints, finds out which ones influence the purchase and combines paid, owned and earned media, to which the PESO model adds shared media.

## Explanation

*La boîte à outils de la publicité* cites Médiamétrie: 44 media and non-media contacts per day and per person in 2013, against 33.9 in 2005. The new screens do not replace the old touchpoints, such as the store. The authors define POE this way: "Les Paid Owned et Earned sont la version digitale du marketing mix" [Paid, Owned and Earned are the digital version of the marketing mix].

| Type | Examples | Advantage | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paid** (*acheté*) | TV, radio, press, posters, banners, sponsored posts | control over the message and the schedule | high cost, ad avoidance |
| **Owned** (*détenu*) | website, stores, sales force, newsletter, social pages | the customer relationship, lower cost | needs close follow-up |
| **Earned** (*gagné*) | word of mouth, reviews, press coverage, natural search ranking | visibility, credibility, speed | hard to control and measure; risk of *bad buzz* |
| **Shared** (PESO only) | content about the brand posted and reshared on social networks | community and sharing | control belongs to the platforms and to people |

The **PESO** model (Gini Dietrich), used by Glenister, separates *shared* from *earned*. Glenister's conclusion: influencer marketing doesn't fit into a pre-determined box. A sponsored post is paid, a spontaneous review is earned, the creator's content on the brand's blog is owned. The best campaigns are integrated (influencers on billboards and in paid ads), not mass product send-outs made in the hope that some will stick.

The older **media / non-media** distinction (*média / hors-média*), ATL / BTL, is still useful: according to the authors, non-media (sales promotion, direct marketing, PR, sponsorship) accounted for about 65% of investment and rarely works without media support. The brand's own website is non-media; space bought on other sites is media.

## Application

- Draw the purchase journey and note the touchpoints at each step. Put the emphasis on those that influence the decision most (recommendation from people close to you, the salesperson).
- Map each activity onto P, E, S, O and check which area is missing. Ask what in the paid campaign deserves to be reshared by people.
- Negotiate usage rights from the start if you want to move creators' content from earned or shared into paid or owned.

## Example

Lipton Iced Tea launched its biggest-ever creator-led campaign in Australia. A comic creator performed an original song for the brand, shown on a digital billboard at Bourke Street and amplified on social media and through an experiential activation.

## Questions

- Are paid influencers Paid or Earned? Where do you put a creator who is also a loyal customer?
- Is the "earned" distinction still useful when almost any visibility on social media can be bought?
