# Online targeting helps, but an ad that is both matched and obtrusive lowers purchase intention

> **The idea in one sentence:** a match with the person's interests and the ad's obtrusiveness each raise purchase intention, but combined they become suspect: the person senses manipulation and reacts the opposite way.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe (following Goldfarb, 2014) distinguish three forms of online targeting, all cheaper than offline: **demographic** (the profile of a website's visitors: cosmetic products on Vogue.com), **contextual** (a match with the content: Volkswagen on a used-car site) and **behavioral** (based on each user's digital footprint). The types of online advertising: search, classified and display.

Key findings:

- **Goldfarb and Tucker (2011),** a large-scale field experiment: ads matched to the content and obtrusive ads (video, pop-up, taking over the web page) each improved purchase intention, but **the combination** lowered it, probably through reactance.
- **Naive retargeting:** one of the authors, after buying a winter coat online, was "drowned" in ads for yet another winter coat, a waste rate of probably 100%. It would have been more useful to show him complementary products (gloves, a warm wool beanie), as online booksellers do.
- **Cambridge Analytica:** the data of about 87 million Facebook users, inferred from a sample of 270,000 who had completed a personality inventory. The authors warn against overestimating the effect: personality traits predict behavior weakly (median correlation .19, less than 4% of the variance: Gignac & Szodorai, 2016).
- **The privacy paradox:** 92% say they should have control over their online data, but only a minority update their settings; German users would accept data sharing for about $8 a month. The Baruh et al. meta-analysis (2017, 166 studies): privacy concerns do not reduce use of social networks, only the disclosure of information.
- **Spam** is thoroughly disliked, but it sometimes sells, especially to older consumers.

## Application

- Do not combine fine targeting with the most aggressive format; choose one of them.
- After a purchase, move to cross-selling or to usage content, not to the same offer.
- For micro-targeting on personality, expectations should be modest; the message and the offer matter more.

## Example

The winter coat (p. 349): "perfect" behavioral targeting, zero effect, because the need had already been met.
