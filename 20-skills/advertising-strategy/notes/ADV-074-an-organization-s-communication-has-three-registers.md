# An organization's communication has three registers (external, internal, institutional), with different targets, objectives and tools

> **The idea in one sentence:** advertising is only one part of an organization's communication; it coexists with internal communication (to employees) and institutional communication (about the company, to stakeholders), and all three must tell the same story.

## Explanation

*La bible de la communication* distinguishes:

| Register | Target | Objective | Tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| **External** (*communication externe*: commercial, brand-product) | customers and prospects | to make known, to sell, to stand out | advertising, social media, newsletter, press relations, events, website, SEO |
| **Internal** (*communication interne*) | employees | equal information, cohesion, belonging, retention; employees are the first ambassadors of the brand | intranet, newsletter, onboarding, internal events, notice boards |
| **Institutional** (*communication institutionnelle*, or *corporate*) | stakeholders: press, investors, suppliers, partners, decision-makers, the local community | to inform about the company as an entity with values and a mission | annual reports, press releases, trade fairs and shows |

Stakeholders (*parties prenantes*) are **contractual** (*contractuelles*: shareholders, employees, customers, suppliers, unions) or **diffuse** (*diffuses*: authorities, local communities, associations, NGOs). The relationship with them has four modes, from the weakest to the most involved: **information**, **consultation** (debate, survey), **design** (*conception*: co-construction, for example, of the corporate social responsibility strategy), **association** in drafting important documents.

The book's warning for the institutional register: do not claim to be what you are not. Companies that tried *greenwashing* ended up with bad publicity; if you use environmental arguments, you must have proof that the approach is authentic.

## Application

- With a campaign brief, ask what internal and corporate communication are saying at the same time. A campaign about "caring for people" launched in the middle of a wave of layoffs backfires on the brand.
- Use employees as media: an internal campaign that precedes the external launch turns the team into ambassadors.
- Measure in the long term (loyalty, satisfaction, audience), not only short-term turnover.

## Example

**Michel & Augustin** (biscuits), cited in the book: every package shows the "tribe" of employees, each with a name and a photo. The same gesture is external communication and employer brand communication (*marque employeur*), supported internally through rituals, for example the pastry diploma (*CAP en pâtisserie*) offered to every employee.
