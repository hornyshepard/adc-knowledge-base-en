# Strategic advertising mechanisms fall into three paradigms: product, personality and consumer branding

> **The idea in one sentence:** every strategic "recipe" (USP, brand image, positioning and so on) makes sense only within a view of the brand, and choosing the wrong mechanism for the brand's paradigm produces incoherent communication.

## Explanation

Jorge David Fernández Gómez proposes a hierarchy with three levels: **paradigms** (the long-term view of brand management), **theories** (the psychological, economic and marketing foundations that flesh them out) and **strategic advertising mechanisms**, the applied methods with which advertising is actually made. The term "mechanism" is chosen deliberately, in place of the vaguer "philosophies," "methods" or "techniques."

| Paradigm | Theoretical foundation | Mechanisms |
|---|---|---|
| *Product branding* | the rational "economic man," behaviorism, AIDA/DAGMAR, the marketing mix | copy strategy, "reason-why" copy, the USP, the dominant idea |
| *Personality branding* | motivation research, psychoanalysis, semiotics, self-concept | brand image, the psychological axis, the star strategy, Lovemarks, Passionbrands |
| *Consumer branding* | cognitive psychology, then the anthropology and sociology of consumption | positioning, iconic brands (Holt) |

The paradigms do not follow one another neatly: they coexist. The USP, over 60 years old, is still used today alongside Lovemarks. The author considers consumer branding the most advanced paradigm and notes that it has produced the fewest strictly advertising mechanisms, because it belongs to the web, content and co-creation.

## Application

- Before you choose a tool (USP, emotional manifesto, cultural story), establish in which paradigm the brand is conceived: product, personality, or relationship with the consumer and their culture.
- Does a brief ask for a technical reason-why while the brand platform talks about "passion"? The tension comes from mixing paradigms, not from the execution.
- Use the table as a reading map: read each note about a mechanism together with its paradigm.

## Example

P&G built copy strategy for brands conceived as products (Ivory, Camay). The same tool applied to a fashion brand, where buyers express their identity, would miss the point: there, personality or cultural mechanisms work.

## Questions

- Is the consumer branding paradigm in fact "more advanced," or just more recent?
- Can a single brand use mechanisms from different paradigms on different product lines without losing coherence?
