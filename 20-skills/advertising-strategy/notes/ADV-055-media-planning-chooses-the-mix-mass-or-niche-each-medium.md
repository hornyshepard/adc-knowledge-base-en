# Media planning chooses the mix (mass or niche, each medium with its own strengths) and the schedule: continuous, flighting or pulsing

> **The idea in one sentence:** media planning evaluates and selects the combination of channels and the frequency in each, so that the message reaches the target audience clearly and consistently, and the media buy is usually the largest expense in the budget.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy define **media planning** as the process of evaluating and selecting the **media mix**: the combination of the media used and the frequency of advertising in each. The content of an ad and the characteristics of the media that carry it are closely tied. The **media buy** is generally the largest expense, and television is by far the most expensive medium.

**Mass vs. niche.** Mass media channels (outdoor/billboards, newspapers, magazines, radio, TV) reach large numbers of anonymous audience members. Niche media channels (specialty TV channels, specialty magazines, direct mail) reach narrower segments. The internet offers both.

**Advantages and disadvantages** (Exhibit 19.3, summary):

| Medium | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| TV | wide reach, sound and video | high cost; may also increase awareness of competitors' products |
| Radio | inexpensive, targeted, wide reach | no video, less focused attention, short exposure |
| Magazines | very targeted, passed along to others | inflexible, take time to become available |
| Newspapers | flexible, timely, local | short life span of the ad |
| Internet and mobile | linked to content, interactive, specific targeting | cluttered, blocked by software |
| Outdoor | inexpensive, repeat exposure | hard to target, very short exposure |
| Direct marketing | highly targeted, personalized | variable costs |

People use media for different purposes, and the message must fit: TV is used for escapism and entertainment, while radio in the car reaches consumers close to the moment of decision (groceries, fast food).

**Scheduling** (*advertising schedule*): **continuous** (steady throughout the year; Tide), **flighting** (periods of heavy advertising followed by periods of none; suntan lotion before the summer) and **pulsing** (a base level plus spikes; airlines and hotels in low-demand periods).

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott do not compare media by general advantages but by what the strategy requires: visuals, time to process or frequency ([[ADV-052-media-are-chosen-by-three-processing-requirements-visual|ADV-052]]).

## Application

- Choose the schedule by the category's demand: steady demand calls for a continuous schedule, seasonal demand for flighting, and base demand with peaks for pulsing.
- Match the execution to the medium: the same message, but the format must respect the way each medium is used.
- Calculate the plan's total GRP to compare options within the same medium.

## Example

Macy's may decide that a heavy dose of TV, radio, print and billboards is right for the holiday selling season between Thanksgiving and the end of the year: a flight concentrated on the shopping season.

## Questions

- The table of advantages was written for a world dominated by TV. What would need rewriting for TikTok, CTV and retail media?
- Is flighting risky for brand awareness, given delayed effects and forgetting?
