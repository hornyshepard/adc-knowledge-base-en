# The Reeves-Ogilvy dispute: USP versus brand image, reason versus emotion, research versus judgment

> **The idea in one sentence:** the most famous dispute in advertising pits a mechanism that sells a tangible benefit against one that builds a brand personality, and history shows that each side ended up borrowing from the other.

## Explanation

Rosser Reeves and David Ogilvy were mentor and protégé, brothers-in-law, friends and rivals. Ogilvy said that Reeves had taught him more about advertising than anybody he had known; Reeves replied that if he ever got out of packaged goods and into luxury items, he would be glad to go and sit at David's feet and listen. In substance, the dispute runs along three axes:

| Axis | Reeves (USP) | Ogilvy and the brand imagists |
|---|---|---|
| What it sells | a physical difference in the product | the total personality of the brand |
| Psychology | a rational consumer, stimulus-response | unconscious motives, symbols |
| Method | research and testing | judgment; research used "as a drunkard uses a lamp post, for support rather than for illumination" |

The main criticism of the USP: it lacks any affective component. Rom and Sabaté argue that Reeves did include emotion (he asked that the totality of the advertisement project a USP as well as a feeling). Fernández Gómez rejects this reading: Reeves used the word only twice and treated the image as mere background, with the words as the content and the image as the form. He did not understand brand image as a mental image. The practical proof: in 1995 the Bates group itself revamped the USP to include emotional benefits.

The dispute gave rise to variants: the **ESP** (emotional selling proposition, John Bartle, BBH: a uniqueness created by the campaign, not by the product, built around a "brand truth"), the **UBP** (unique buying proposition) and the **MSP** (multiple selling proposition).

## Application

- Do not treat the choice as dogma: ask whether your category has a relevant tangible difference. If it does, start from it; if not, the difference has to be created through image and emotion.
- The mature combination: a clear proposition, dressed in a feeling consistent with the brand's personality.

## Example

Ogilvy himself had a "pre-brand image" phase in which, as a follower of Hopkins, he loaded his ads with facts: the more he said, the more he sold. Only after the Gardner and Levy article (1955) did he change his discourse.

## Questions

- Is the "Poets vs. killers" dispute (*Advertising Age*, quoted by Trout) still current in the age of performance marketing?
- If the ESP is a uniqueness created by the campaign, how does it still differ from brand image?
