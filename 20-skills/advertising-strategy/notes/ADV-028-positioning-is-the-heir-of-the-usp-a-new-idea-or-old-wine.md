# Positioning is the heir of the USP: a new idea or "old wine in new bottles"?

> **The idea in one sentence:** almost all the foundations of positioning are already in Reeves, and Trout and Ries's real contribution is moving the logic from the advertisement to the brand, plus a brilliant name.

## Explanation

Fernández Gómez lists the points where the USP (1961) and positioning (1969–1981) overlap:

1. **The mind as a limited container.** Reeves described a "small box" in the consumer's head for each product category: when a new campaign pushes facts in, others fall out. Trout and Ries would later speak of slots, pigeonholes and a computer that rejects what does not fit.
2. **Information saturation.** Reeves calculated that $117,000,000 a day was spent to buy brands a bit of space in the memory of a consumer battered by TV, the press and radio.
3. **Duration.** For Reeves, a long campaign beats a series of short ones; for Trout and Ries, positioning is accumulative.
4. **The product and the verbal.** Both mechanisms put the product attribute at the center and favor the word over the image.

Moreover, Joe Sacco attributes to Reeves, from an alleged "lost chapter" of his book, a definition: positioning is the art of selecting, out of a number of USPs, the one that will get you maximum sales. Trout himself, in *Differentiate or Die* (2000), devotes whole chapters to Reeves and argues that the only difference is the intensity of today's competition. Maggard (*Journal of Marketing*, 1976) had already said that positioning is as old as market segmentation and product differentiation.

On the other hand, the author notes the dispute over paternity: Trout & Ries, and later Trout & Partners, attacked those who used the term (Porter, Peters, McKenna), and after the split in 1994 Ries reformulated the idea as *focus* and *divergence*. The conclusion: the value lies more in the word "positioning" than in a new theory; the big gain is that **brands** are positioned, rather than products merely being sold through advertisements.

## Application

- When you work on positioning, use Reeves as a test: do you have a promise that is unique, relevant, simply stated and kept for years?
- Do not confuse marketing positioning (Porter: activities different from the competitors') with communication positioning (Trout: the difference in the mind). Say clearly which one you mean.

## Example

In the same alleged chapter, Reeves warned that "positioning" can become a dangerous buzzword, a pseudo-marketing that sees segments existing only in the eye of the beholder.

## Questions

- If the idea is old, why did positioning have such a great impact? What role does the name of a concept play?
- What does positioning add to the USP for brands without product differences?
