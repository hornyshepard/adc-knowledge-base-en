# Every ad informs, persuades or reminds: the objective is chosen by the product's stage and by its focus (product, institution, cause)

> **The idea in one sentence:** advertising objectives derive from the marketing plan and can be described on three axes: pull or push strategy, role (inform, persuade, remind) and focus (product, institution or public service).

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy say that the campaign's objectives are written in the **advertising plan**, a subsection of the marketing plan, which then becomes the benchmark of success.

**Pull versus push.** Advertising to consumers usually follows a *pull* strategy: consumers "pull" the product into the marketing channel by demanding it. *Push* strategies focus on wholesalers, retailers and salespeople, to "push" the product to consumers.

**The role**, aligned with the product life cycle:

| Role | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Informative | early on, when people know little; also for retail events | Arby's: the smallest ad in the world (on a sesame seed) and the largest (nearly five acres), to announce that it serves Coke, not Pepsi |
| Persuasive | growth and early maturity, when competition is at its peak; also for repositioning | Lancôme: try the product, switch brands, continue to buy |
| Reminder | maturity, accepted products | Kleenex: top-of-mind awareness, plus an end-of-aisle display, make you pick up the box without thinking |

**The focus**:

- **product-focused**: sells a specific product or service;
- **institutional**: promotes the organization, not a product (Lockheed Martin, which does not sell to the public: a virtual reality "Field Trip to Mars" for schoolchildren);
- **public service advertising** (*PSA*): public welfare, sponsored by nonprofits, civic groups or trade associations. It is a form of social marketing, often created by top agencies, quite creative and sometimes controversial.

Whatever the type, the objective must be specific and measurable (for example, to persuade 10 percent of a competitor's customers to switch to your brand).

## Application

- In the brief, write a single dominant role. An ad that wants to inform and remind at the same time has no criterion of success.
- Check whether the problem is a *pull* problem (the consumer does not ask for the product) or a *push* problem (retail does not list it). The answer changes who the communication is addressed to.
- With PSAs, keep the risk of ambiguity in mind. South Dakota's anti-meth campaign "Meth. We're on it." stirred debate precisely because the tagline had more than one meaning.

## Example

Arby's turned a mundane piece of news (a change of soft drink supplier) into a memorable informative campaign that entered the Guinness World Records. It also got consumers laughing rather than complaining, so the negative reactions of Pepsi fans were avoided.

## Questions

- Is long-term brand advertising (building associations) a fourth role, distinct from the three?
- When does a PSA campaign become too creative and lose its message?
