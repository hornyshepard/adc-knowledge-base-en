# Four genres of social profile: personal, community, brand, journalist

> **The idea in one sentence:** a social profile is the digital business card of an identity, and although successful profiles mix the personal with the professional, it helps to choose a dominant genre that focuses your content.

## Explanation

Mueller and Rajaram propose a typology of profile stories:

| Genre | What it reflects | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| **Personal (*profersonal*)** | an individual who posts under their own name about their profession and expertise; strategic ID marketing, useful to the employer too | the climate scientist Stefan Rahmstorf, who creates native content adapted to each platform, in German and English |
| **Community** | the values and actions of a non-commercial network; it has a claim and a central hashtag | Youth Against AIDS: "Do what you want. Do it with love, respect & condoms", #faqyou |
| **Brand** | the benefits, expertise and creative fun associated with the brand; it changes as the brand changes | National Geographic (@natgeo), a single claim on all platforms, different native content on each |
| **Journalist** | expertise and credibility; research and verification techniques useful to any storyteller | Mai Thi Nguyen-Kim (MaiLab), science explained for everyone |

The authors add **liquid profiles**, based on a hashtag: #GretaThunberg, #1619Project or #BlackLivesMatter connect a person or a cause with its community without a central hub, and are searched, followed and ranked by algorithms like a profile.

Practical rules: a clear name, the same recognizable photo on all networks, an up-to-date bio, a link to the central hub (website, store, LinkedIn), a slightly different design and tone on each platform, a check on every device. Deana Mrkaja recommends not displaying your relationship status or your religious or political views, and controlling your privacy settings.

## Application

- For a new brand: choose the dominant genre, then define the claim and the central hashtag.
- For students (portfolio): the profersonal profile calls for native content, not just links to work.
- Audit the profile periodically: personalities and brands change.

## Example

Lego runs a classic brand profile but benefits greatly from hashtag-based UGC; when it posted #BlackLivesMatter in 2020, the gesture fitted years of diverse minifigures and was received as a credible signal.

## Questions

- Which genre does a CEO who posts on LinkedIn on behalf of the company belong to?
- What does a brand risk if its personal profile (the founder's) is stronger than its brand profile?
