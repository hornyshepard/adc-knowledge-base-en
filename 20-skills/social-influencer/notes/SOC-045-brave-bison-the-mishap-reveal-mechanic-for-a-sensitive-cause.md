# Brave Bison: the mishap-reveal mechanic for a sensitive cause

> **The idea in one sentence:** for a subject that is hard to address head-on, two influencers posted photos with a deliberate mishap, let their followers point it out in the comments and, after 24 hours, revealed the link to the campaign's message: if you see that something is not right, speak up.

## Explanation

The brief: the Internet Watch Foundation (IWF) and the Marie Collins Foundation (MCF) asked the social video company Brave Bison for a talent-led campaign on Instagram for their education campaign "Something's Not Right", which encourages young men to report the sexual images of children they come across online.

The mechanic in three beats:

1. **The mishap**: the influencers Arron Crascall and EmanSV2 staged and posted "mishap" photos, images in which something wasn't quite right.
2. **The participation**: followers did what they naturally do on social media: they left comments pointing out the mishap.
3. **The reveal**: after 24 hours, the influencers revealed their work with the IWF and the MCF and asked their combined 5 million followers to speak up and act in the same way if they ever stumble across sexual images or videos of under-18s. A paid-for behind-the-scenes Facebook video was also shared on the IWF's page.

Reported results: over 8.5 million user impressions and more than 5.4 million views; 23 times higher engagement, 15 times more impressions and 37 times more views than expected; an average engagement rate of 6.9 percent, nearly four times the average of the non-profit industry (1.75 percent) and of influencers on Instagram (1.67 percent).

Why it works, from the ADC point of view: the mechanic turns the behavior the campaign wants (noticing and reporting) into an action the audience already performs on the platform without effort. The sensitive message arrives only after the user has already "played" the desired role.

## Application

- When the subject is taboo, look for a harmless, platform-native behavior that is analogous to the desired behavior.
- Use the two-beat format (teaser + reveal) and plan the moment of the reveal.
- Choose creators whose style (humor, pranks) makes the mishap believable.

## Example

The mechanic resembles "The Arrivals" (SAS) only in the idea of choosing a single strong moment; the difference is that here the moment is created by the community, through its comments.

## Questions

- Is there a risk that the audience feels manipulated by the fake "mishap"? How do you manage it?
- Which other platform-native behaviors (duet, stitch, save, share) can serve as analogies for a cause?
