# Metaverse marketing starts from users' motivations and takes four forms: branded collectibles, experiential advertising, O2O commerce and gamified loyalty

> **The idea in one sentence:** for Generation Z and Generation Alpha, the metaverse is the immersive form of social media, and a brand belongs there only if its audience overlaps with the "metaverse natives" and if it offers an experience, not a logo on a virtual billboard.

## Explanation

**What the metaverse is.** The idea comes from the novel *Snow Crash* (1992). The metaverse is associated with **Web3**, the "human-centric", decentralized version of the Internet, after Web1 (consumption only) and Web2 (platforms and UGC). There are **centralized** metaverses (Roblox, Fortnite, Minecraft; the most popular, with an average user age of 12–13) and **decentralized** ones (The Sandbox, Decentraland; hit by the 2022 crypto and NFT crash).

**Why people come in** (Fig. 10.3, Oliver Wyman): a **fun escape** (41 percent), synchronous, 3D **connection** (26 percent), **convenient shopping** and **financial gain** (play-to-earn, virtual assets, services).

**Four ways to participate** (Fig. 10.4):

| Form | Motivation served | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Branded collectibles**: for Z and Alpha, about experience and belonging, not rarity (as for Y) | escape | Air Jordan sneakers in Fortnite; Nike's .SWOOSH |
| **Experiential advertising**: branded spaces, not placed logos | connection | the Vans skatepark on Roblox; Samsung and *The Tonight Show* in Fortnite; The Home Depot's "Virtual Kids Workshop" |
| **Online-to-offline (O2O) commerce**: the virtual experience continues in the real world | shopping | the American Girl virtual store, with reservations at the real café; Drest, with real Gucci and Prada products |
| **Gamified loyalty**: play-to-earn, adapted | gain | Chipotle's "Burrito Builder" (a virtual burrito exchanged for real food); Starbucks Odyssey |

**Success factors:** enter only if there is an audience overlap; work with the existing communities; keep things simple (no VR headsets); avoid the "NFT" label (Starbucks says "journey stamps" and accepts card payment); keep the brand positioning from the real world; test with limited-time campaigns (Gucci: the Gucci Garden for two weeks, then a permanent Gucci Town); measure participation, brand lift and incremental revenue.

## Application

- Before a metaverse proposal, check the data: what share of the client's target is on Roblox or Fortnite? Otherwise, it is FOMO.
- Build an experience that can exist only there (a 10-minute concert, instant customization), tied to the brand's story.
- Link the virtual to the real (O2O, physical rewards), because many categories cannot be consumed virtually.

## Example

Marshmello's concert in Fortnite had 10 million concurrent attendees, split into groups of 100 players so that each one experienced the same concert ambiance (ch. 7).

## Questions

- The book acknowledges the drop in hype in 2022 but stays optimistic. What data would you ask for to justify an investment today?
- Ethically: is it appropriate to market in an environment where over 83 percent of users are under 18?
