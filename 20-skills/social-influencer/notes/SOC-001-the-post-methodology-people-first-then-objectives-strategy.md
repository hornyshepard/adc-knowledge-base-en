# The POST methodology: people first, then objectives, strategy, and technology last

> **The idea in one sentence:** a social media strategy is built in the order People → Objectives → Strategy → Technology; jumping straight to the next new and shiny platform is the most common mistake.

## Explanation

The old advertising maxim says marketers should follow the eyeballs: where customers go for information and entertainment, the marketer must follow. So that this does not turn into a chase after platforms, Lauren Labrecque and her coauthors take up the **P.O.S.T.** methodology from *Groundswell* (Josh Bernoff and Charlene Li), which they consider still effective:

| Step | The question |
|---|---|
| **P**eople | Who is the target, what are their social activities, why do they use social media? ("What's in it for them?") |
| **O**bjectives | What do you want to achieve for your brand? With what KPI and what target? |
| **S**trategy | How will relationships with customers change? What content reconciles your objectives with their motivations? |
| **T**echnology | Only now: on which platforms, with which tools, with what content calendar? |

The steps are not strictly linear (strategy refers back to people), but the order of importance is fixed: people are at the forefront, and technology is the least important piece. At the people step, the authors recommend a persona and comparing platforms by demographics (fig. 9.3). Platforms also differ by geography: TikTok, Snapchat or Pinterest are much more popular in the United States than in the rest of the world, and messaging apps (WeChat, Messenger) are growing everywhere.

## Application

- In a social brief, fill in the people section first (motivations, not just demographics), and only then ask for format ideas.
- For each objective, write the KPI and the target before choosing the platform.
- When the client asks "to be on TikTok", go back to P: are their people there, and with what motivation?

## Example

Too many businesses, the authors say, push their business objectives onto social media and make the platform feel like just another sales channel; users do not engage. POST corrects this with the question of what is in it for them, which is where the 5Cs of motivation come from.

- Is POST too simple for today's multi-platform strategies? What would it be missing?
- How does POST apply to a B2B brand on LinkedIn?
