# Threadless: the community proposes, votes and buys, and the customer becomes the company

> **The idea in one sentence:** Threadless built its business on an online community that submits designs, votes on what gets produced and buys. The brand is not the shirts but the community experience.

## Explanation

Threadless was founded in 2000, in Chicago, by Jake Nickell and Jacob DeHart: an online community of artists plus an e-commerce website. The mechanism: members submit thousands of designs each week (for t-shirts, hoodies, home art), the community votes, and the winning ones are produced and sold ($15–80). Every product carries the artist's name (ch. 16).

The figures from the article "The Customer is the Company" (2008), cited by Wuebben: no advertising, no professional designers, no sales force and no retail distribution. Margins above 30%. Every product sells out, because the community has said in advance what it wants. The tagline: "Make friends. Then, make things with your friends." The headquarters is open to customers, and the founder describes the brand as a club.

The academic reading: Eric von Hippel calls the company a perfect example of user innovation: most innovations come from the people who use the products, not from labs. Karim Lakhani (Harvard) says Threadless blurs the line between producer and consumer, because idea generation, marketing and sales forecasting are distributed across the community. The contrast comes from Frank Piller: BMW gets a thousand good ideas a year through its portal and uses maybe one every two years. An ideas platform is worth nothing if the input is not used.

LEGO illustrates the same logic. The Ambassador Program (40 fans in more or less daily dialogue with marketing) and the LEGO Click community (2011) let fans share their creations, which amplifies the content.

## Application

Four conditions for a co-creation community (drawn from the case):
1. give the community a **real decision** (the vote decides production);
2. **credit** the creators publicly;
3. **close the loop**: what gets voted on actually gets produced;
4. offer a **place** or a ritual (an open headquarters, competitions, awards).

## Example

The competition turns artists into promoters. Each participant sends their friends to vote, so customer acquisition comes from word of mouth (the designer Tom Burns describes Threadless as something that spread mainly by word of mouth).

## Questions

- Does the model work in categories where design is not the main reason to buy?
- How do you keep a co-creation community from becoming free labor for the brand?
