# The strategic framework of an influencer campaign starts from research and SMART objectives

> **The idea in one sentence:** most influencer campaigns fail not because of the creators but because research, a measurable objective and a realistic budget are missing; Glenister's framework puts these three elements before any selection.

## Explanation

The steps of the strategic framework in the book:

1. **Research and social listening**: where the brand, its competitors and the category are discussed, which topics are hot, who leads the conversations. You monitor the brand name, handles, products, slogan, your own and competitors' hashtags, industry keywords (including slang, acronyms, other languages). *Monitoring* looks at the past, *listening* at future strategy. Do not forget "dark social" (private messages, closed communities).
2. **Competitor analysis and benchmarking**: which influencers competitors use, what engagement they get, which formats they post and at what times.
3. **SMART objectives**, chosen from: awareness, brand identity (values, purpose), subscriber base, sales, loyalty and repeat purchase, leads, engagement, backlinks.
4. **Budget**: indicative rates by platform and tier, target cost per engagement, production costs, exclusivity.
5. **Tactics**: gifting, sponsored content, co-created content, guest posting, mentions, competitions, giveaways (5 to 10 times more new followers than a normal creator post), flashmobs, affiliate links, polls, podcasts, panels, account takeovers, webinars, explainers.
6. **Brief, selection, content, monitoring** and adjustment along the way.
7. **Internal buy-in**: a six-point proposal (Onalytica): problem, current impact, solution, competition, return on investment, long-term partnerships.

Sedge Beswick (Seen Connects) adds five principles: archetypal alignment between the brand's persona and the creators, clear KPIs set up front, creative collaboration (let the creator pitch the platform), relationships instead of gifts sent at random, and the courage to take risks.

## Application

- A good objective, following the book's example: 20 food bloggers, stories tagged with the brand hashtag and a trial coupon for a new gin, with a target of 5,000 coupon redemptions and 20 percent conversion over 60 days. A poor objective: "1,000 likes" or "a viral video".
- First campaign: 5–10 micro-influencers, a discount code of about 20 percent, tracking through analytics.
- Do not choose the "trendy" platform if your audience is not there.

## Example

The mistakes the author lists: unclear goals, a message without a strong CTA, insufficient effort (mass emails), the wrong person leading the campaign, chasing the latest thing.
