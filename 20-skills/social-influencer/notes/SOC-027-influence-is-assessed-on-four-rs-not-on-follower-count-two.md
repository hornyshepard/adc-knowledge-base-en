# Influence is assessed on four Rs, not on follower count: two complementary models, Onalytica and Grewal-Levy

> **The idea in one sentence:** two "4R" models in the library share relevance and reach, but one (Onalytica, taken up by Glenister) judges influence *before* the contract, and the other (Grewal and Levy) judges the effect *after* the campaign.

## Explanation

Both start from the same observation: followers can be bought (click farms, bots), so their number does not prove influence.

| | **Onalytica** (Glenister, SRC-035) | **Grewal and Levy** (SRC-001) |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Relevance**: how often the influencer has posted on the topic in the last 12 months | **Relevance**: the link between influencer, followers and brand (Ronaldo can promote soccer cleats; his fans care less about which baby foods his children prefer) |
| R2 | **Resonance**: the average number of engagements on the topic | **Reach**: followers, the influencer's level of activity, engagement; an entertaining, hedonic post likely spreads further than a basic, informational one |
| R3 | **Reference**: how often relevant influencers reference them | **Response**: do followers act in ways that benefit the brand? The measure depends on the objective: likes for awareness, purchases for sales |
| R4 | **Reach**: the combined audience across social channels | **Return**: ROI = (revenue − cost) / cost |

**Where they converge.** Relevance is the first criterion in both models, and reach is no longer the deciding one. The practitioners in [[SOC-028-shared-values-matter-more-than-reach-in-choosing-a-creator|SOC-028]] reach the same order: shared values come before reach.

**Where they differ.** Onalytica is a *selection* tool, calculated on a specific topic. Reference, meaning recognition by peers, is the hardest to fake and weighs most in B2B and technical niches. Grewal and Levy give an *evaluation* tool: response and return show up only after the post, and attributing a sale to an influencer remains difficult. Glenister completes the selection with the media kit, the audience profile, collaborations with competitors, the number of sponsored posts per month and the creator's responsiveness.

## Application

- Before the contract: an Onalytica scoring sheet on the campaign topic, not on the general profile; ask for the media kit and check it independently.
- After the campaign: report the response tied to the objective and the return, with trackable codes or links.
- Direct purchase links measure the return, but they can spoil the impression of authenticity.

## Example

Grewal and Levy compare Cristiano Ronaldo for Jeep with the micro-influencer Sydney Loveleigh Nelson for NUX Active: much smaller reach, much more precise target. For Sage, the agency OST identified over 100 influencers in six countries by reach, topical relevance and resonance on X (Twitter) and LinkedIn, then evaluated the campaign with a monthly scorecard of over 20 KPIs.

## Questions

- How do you calculate the return when the main effect is on image, not on sales?
- How do you measure "reference" without a specialized tool?
