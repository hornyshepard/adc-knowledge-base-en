# A brand community is recognized by three markers and has four types of members, with different roles for marketers

> **The idea in one sentence:** a brand community works like any community (consciousness of kind, rituals, moral responsibility), but its members are not equally attached either to the brand or to the community.

## Explanation

Parsons, Maclaran and Chatzidakis take up the definition by Muniz and O'Guinn (2001): a brand community is a specialized, non-geographically bound community, based on the social relationships among admirers of a brand. It has three **markers**:

1. **Consciousness of kind**: shared values, in-group vs. out-group (fans of the classic Mini vs. owners of the new MINI);
2. **Shared rituals and traditions**: celebrating the brand's history;
3. **Moral responsibility**: helping other members, making newcomers feel at home.

More than half of the top 1,000 global brands have online brand communities, and engagement has seven dimensions (enthusiasm, enjoyment, attention, absorption, learning, endorsing, sharing). **Kozinets's typology (1999)**:

| Type | Tie to the brand | Tie to the community |
|---|---|---|
| *Insiders* | strong | strong: the most influential |
| *Devotees* | strong | weak |
| *Minglers* | weak (sometimes they do not even consume it) | strong |
| *Tourists* | weak | weak (often they only lurk) |

Communities can exist even without the brand: users of the Apple Newton (discontinued in 1998) still maintain their devices and hope for a "second coming". Fans can force relaunches: Wispa (Cadbury, 2007), the Fiat 500, the Raleigh Chopper.

***Seeding* campaigns with bloggers** (Kozinets et al., 2010): bloggers adapt the marketer's message so as not to damage the relationships in the community, through four narrative strategies: *evaluation* (no mention of the marketer's influence, a focus on product performance), *explanation* (they acknowledge the marketer's participation), *embracing* (a focus on personal needs) and *endorsement* (they act as quasi-marketers). The message is accepted only if the product fits the blog and the community's norms.

## Application

- Feed the *insiders* (access, information, recognition) and give *minglers* social reasons; do not treat the community as an advertising audience.
- In campaigns with creators, leave room for their narrative formula; a message that is too obviously commercial produces resistance.
- Listen to the community before major product changes (the case of the new MINI).

## Example

Wispa: a bar discontinued in 2003, brought back in 2007 after a campaign by fans on social media.

## Questions

- In which category in Romania is there a real brand community (not just a page with followers)?
- How ethical is it to seed products with bloggers without the audience knowing?
