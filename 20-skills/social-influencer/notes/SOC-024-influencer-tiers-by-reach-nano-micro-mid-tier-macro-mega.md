# Influencer tiers by reach (nano, micro, mid-tier, macro, mega): relative engagement falls as the audience grows

> **The idea in one sentence:** influencers are classified by number of followers into five tiers, and the working rule is that as reach grows, relative engagement falls, which is why effective campaigns combine several tiers.

## Explanation

Glenister uses an approximate guide (the thresholds vary between sources):

| Tier | Followers | Profile |
|---|---|---|
| **Nano** | 1,000–10,000 | high engagement, close to the audience; important for SMEs |
| **Micro** | 10,000–50,000 | clear niches, low cost, easy to manage |
| **Mid-tier** | 50,000–500,000 | a balance between reach and relationship |
| **Macro** | 500,000–1 million | large reach, less intimacy |
| **Mega** | over 1 million | more of a "celebrity" than an influencer |

Grewal and Levy (SRC-001) classify differently, by **type**, with average costs per post: celebrity ($3,000–$500,000), social media ($100–$500,000), blog ($400–$5,500), specialized, meaning experts and key opinion leaders ($500–$5,000), micro ($80–$500). The two typologies agree: the smaller the audience, the closer the relationship and the lower the cost. Glenister adds Onalytica's typology by activity (content creator, social amplifier, event speaker, industry practitioner, industry KOL) and special categories: virtual (CGI) influencers, pets, children.

Practical consequences from Glenister:

- **The mix**: the best results often come from 15–20 influencers, three to four macro-influencers and one or two mega-influencers, depending on the budget.
- **Ten with 100,000 beat one with 1 million**: higher average engagement, a more varied audience, ten pieces of content to repurpose.
- **Rates**: between 2015 and 2022, micro-influencers' earnings per post rose from $107 to $1,674 (IZEA), a sign that relatable influencers are increasingly valued.

## Application

- For a new brand, start with 5–10 micro-influencers, with tracking codes.
- Use macro and mega for fast awareness, nano and micro for credibility and conversion.
- Choose the tier by objective, not the other way around.

## Example

LaCroix (Grewal and Levy) deliberately chose micro-influencers with thousands of followers, because their posts felt more organic (a work break, a summer picnic). A London vegetarian restaurant (Glenister) offered vegetarian influencers a free meal during its opening phase; one influencer alone could be attributed to four table bookings.

## Questions

- Do the thresholds hold in every country? (In Brazil, a YouTube channel with 1 million subscribers is not necessarily considered big.)
- What do you lose by working only with nano-influencers?
