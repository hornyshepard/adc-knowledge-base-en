# Shared values matter more than reach in choosing a creator

> **The idea in one sentence:** a collaboration with a creator succeeds only if the brand's values and the creator's values match credibly, if the format respects the creator's style and if the platform is the one where the creator's community lives; reach comes only after that.

## Explanation

Three experts in the book converge on the same principle:

- **Adil Sbai (weCreate)**: the first priority is values. His test: would the creator consume the brand's products or build a relationship with the brand even without being paid? The agency does not even enter into negotiations if the match is missing; a values mismatch can turn into a *super-GAU* (a worst-case disaster) that no one gets out of without losing their image. Example: McDonald's asking Younes Zarou, who is extremely concerned about healthy eating, for a collaboration would be untrustworthy. The brand has to give creators freedom and not impose a *creative corset* on them.
- **Tim Hendrik Walter**: the creator should not bend to please a brand; authenticity is lost at once, the community notices, and the campaign suffers.
- **Maximilian Wolf**: influencers are not "living advertising boards" but creative people who can tell the organization's story better than it can itself. What matters is authenticity, a connection to the topic, the values and the commitment of the community. Formats are adapted to the creator; a creator who changes too much for a campaign is still one of the biggest factors in the failure of influencer communication. Creators are also platform-specific: a YouTube creator does not guarantee success on TikTok.

Glenister reaches the same conclusion from the industry side: Sedge Beswick asks for *archetypal alignment* between the brand's persona and the creators, David Robson asks for shared values in the Venn diagram of talent, brand and audience, and 70 percent of consumers consider a natural brand affinity and shared ethics between influencer and brand important. Mueller and Rajaram also note the reverse risk: a brand that links itself through a hashtag to an ambassador with unsuitable values can trigger calls for a boycott (#cancelculture).

## Application

- Before the metrics, write down three brand values and check them against the creator's last 30 posts.
- Ask the creator how they would tell the story; the brief gives the direction, not the script.
- In NGOs, brief the creator thoroughly on the cause: the community will ask them questions.

## Example

FAQ YOU (Youth Against AIDS) works only with influencers who share its values of diversity, equality and respectful sex, such as the entrepreneur Diana zur Loewen, who promoted the organization's book.

## Questions

- What do you do when the creator who matches on values has too little reach for the client's objectives?
- Can a beauty creator credibly support a political cause (Fridays for Future)? Wolf says yes, if the narrative is strong enough.
