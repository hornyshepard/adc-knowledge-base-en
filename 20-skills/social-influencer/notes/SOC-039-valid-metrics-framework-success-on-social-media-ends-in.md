# Valid Metrics Framework: success on social media ends in advocacy, not purchase

> **The idea in one sentence:** for social media, the authors propose five stages of measurement (exposure, engagement, influence, impact, advocacy), and the last stage is not the sale but the recommendation.

## Explanation

Measurement in social media is complicated because many of the things that matter are qualitative (the type and quality of engagement with the brand). After wrestling with these issues for years, marketers settled on the **Valid Metrics Framework**, which Zahay takes from her textbook *Social Media Marketing: A Strategic Approach*. The model is grounded in traditional advertising measurement theory:

| Stage | Definition | Example metric |
|---|---|---|
| **Exposure** | the opportunity to see and/or hear a piece of content; it does not guarantee attention | page view |
| **Engagement** | interaction with the content; its exact definition is hotly debated, most often measured as time spent | time, comments |
| **Influence** | the ability to affect attitudes and behavior; measured on each particular platform | audience reach |
| **Impact** | the effect on business results; ideally, the Return on Promotional Investment (ROPI) | purchase |
| **Advocacy** | the person recommends the action to someone else (opinion leaders, WOM) | recommendation |

Two observations: at every stage there are qualitative and quantitative metrics; and the framework **ends with advocacy**, not purchase, because purchase is not always the expected outcome of a social media campaign. Objectives have to be set with care, so the metrics measure what the campaign set out to do. And the definition of "engagement" should be checked every time you cite a statistic.

## Application

- Tell the client in the brief which stage the campaign is aiming at; do not promise sales impact for an exposure campaign.
- For each stage, choose one quantitative metric and one qualitative metric (what people say).
- With influencers, measure influence on the specific platform, not with a universal score (Klout has disappeared).

## Example

**#EmpowerMoves** (UN Women's Council, Australia, TikTok): the choreographer Karla Mura created a dance that hid self-defense moves, then videos that broke the moves down. Results: 130 million video views, 59 million unique viewers, a total reach of 62 million, third song on the viral TikTok chart, a reported ROI of 4,924 percent. Without selling anything, the campaign reached advocacy for women's rights.

## Questions

- An ROI of 4,924 percent for a campaign with no sales: what was actually put in the numerator?
- Is advocacy worth more than a sale? For what type of brand?
