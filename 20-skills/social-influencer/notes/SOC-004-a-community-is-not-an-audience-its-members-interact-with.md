# A community is not an audience: its members interact with each other

> **The idea in one sentence:** an audience passively consumes the content of a sender; a community forms when its members all follow the same account and converse with each other about a shared topic, and the value of a social channel lies in the community, not in the number of views.

## Explanation

Mueller and Rajaram translate *social* as "shared with others", peer-to-peer. Audiences belong to conventional linear media; a digital community lives on the interactions among its members, who become co-creators (for example through UGC). The authors distinguish **informal** communities (formed temporarily around a hashtag or a fundraising goal) and **formal** ones (which permanently follow an NGO, an influencer, a brand, or join a closed group with common rules).

Three ideas complete the concept:

- **Social+ companies** (a term coined by D'Arcy Coolican, Andreessen Horowitz): the community is integrated into the product, and product and community are mutually dependent. Spotify is not Social+ (it is a music player for individual users), Clubhouse is; so are Sleeper (fantasy sports) and TalkShop (live sales).
- **The 90-9-1 rule**: about 1% of members are active, 9% interact occasionally, over 90% remain silent. The silent ones are not lost: they can become multipliers at any time, including offline.
- **A community has to be built and maintained** (Maximilian Wolf): without it, a channel is left with a loose set of content. The biggest enemies are boredom and indifference; the more interactions you create, the more followers become ambassadors.

## Application

- Measure the community through conversations among members and through returns, not through reach.
- Start with listening: follow the conversations of your followers and of those who do not follow you yet.
- Use polls and questions from the comments as a source of new topics.

## Example

The FMD (Filmemacher Deutschland) Facebook group has more than 28,000 filmmakers and up to 12,000 interactions a day; the administrators make only three to six posts per day, and the rest is content created by members.

## Questions

- Can a mass-market brand have a real community, or only an audience with comments?
- If 90% stay silent, how do you know what your community thinks?
