# Latching onto a viral hashtag without a values match produces a shitstorm

> **The idea in one sentence:** hashtags and memes are storytelling templates that have already proven themselves, and a brand can use them, but if it joins a trend just for the reach, with no link to its values and history, it risks a wave of negative reactions.

## Explanation

Mueller and Rajaram describe the hashtag as probably the smallest and most versatile storytelling tool: invented by users (Chris Messina, Twitter, 2007; #SanDiegoFire), it organizes content, creates micro-stories and establishes its own genre. Memes grow out of popular hashtags: an idea published by someone, copied and varied by many (including in a political key, as when the #BlackLivesMatter movement adapted #MannequinChallenge). "Hacks" use a tool in a way its inventors did not intend (journalist Yusuf Omar used Snapchat's lenses to protect the identity of his sources).

The authors' rule: use only hashtags and memes that match your values, your brand and your followers. The cases in the book:

- **Lego and #BlackLivesMatter (2020)**: the gesture fitted years of diverse figures; the brand reaped positive resonance and UGC. Many brands sat on the trend, few as convincingly.
- **Snapchat and Rihanna (2018)**: an ad trivializing domestic violence ("Better to slap Rihanna or punch Chris Brown?") led Rihanna to call on her fans to delete the app; Snap's share price fell by 4%, and the company publicly apologized.
- **Lidl (2016)**: a poster with a grammatical error ("Die Wahl Du Hast") was mocked as "Yoda language"; Lidl responded self-ironically with "Viel Zu Lernen Du Noch Hast" and #LidlSichLohnt, online and offline, and won sympathy.

## Application

- Before joining a trend, ask: does the brand have the right (history, values, behavior) to speak here?
- Self-ironic humor and transparency turn a mistake into a favorable meme.
- Prepare a crisis plan for any campaign that touches sensitive topics.

## Example

During the 2020 lockdown, Porsche launched #GetCreativeWithPorsche, with replica Porsche models in Lego landscapes. The link was credible, because both brands live on a passion for building and collecting.

## Questions

- How do you tell opportunistic trend-jacking from legitimate real-time marketing?
- Is silence a safe option when the community expects the brand to take a stance?
