# Influencers can be used across the whole product launch cycle

> **The idea in one sentence:** influencer marketing is not just the star holding the product, but a tool that can step in at every stage of the launch: testing, aspiration, expert validation and confirmation from peers.

## Explanation

Glenister takes from Joe Sinkwitz (Intellifluence) a four-stage sequence:

| Stage | Who | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Pre-launch testing** | micro-influencers | test the packaging color, the size, the price, the target buying niche, even the right network, before millions are spent |
| **2. Aspiration** | a celebrity (the *aspirational influencer*) | the large placement, optimized for the audience validated in stage 1 |
| **3. Authoritative validation** | experts | in-depth videos and blog posts confirming that the interest in the product is warranted |
| **4. Confirmation from peers** | micro-influencers who mirror the buying audience | share the celebrity and expert content with their own commentary about buying or sampling the product |

The psychological argument: "we all want to be like our heroes but need to be like our peers". Repeated social proof from peers turns desire into action. Sinkwitz calls the result **compulsion marketing**, a term the book presents approvingly but which raises ethical questions.

The book also lists complementary uses: product development and co-creation (a *brand immersion approach*, common in China), audience education, important news (out of stock, crisis information), recruiting members for a program, events.

## Application

- Map the launch campaign onto the four stages and assign different tiers of creators to each.
- Use stage 1 as cheap research: the micro-influencers' reactions are qualitative data.
- Prepare operations: a celebrity can sell out the stock in minutes.

## Example

For the new sweetener Purecane, the agency MG Empower invited four nutritionists-turned-influencers to a three-day workshop to co-create the brand before launch; their Instagram content reached over 6.8 million followers and generated over 302,000 reactions.

## Questions

- Where does compulsion marketing cross from persuasion into manipulation? How does it relate to consumer autonomy?
- Which stage is missing from a typical agency campaign that works with a single large influencer?
