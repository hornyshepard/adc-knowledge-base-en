# Conversational vs. transactional: two kinds of relationship on platforms

> **The idea in one sentence:** a conversation on social media is either conversational (built on values, friendship and shared interest) or transactional (subordinated to a business exchange), and a storyteller has to say openly which one they are pursuing.

## Explanation

The distinction comes from Evan Spiegel, the founder of Snapchat, who describes the app as a conversation with a friend, not a transaction. Mueller and Rajaram generalize it:

- **Conversational conversations** revolve around social value and the relationship. Each exchange deepens the bond; the participants reveal a little more about themselves each time.
- **Transactional conversations** are tied to a business goal: talking to an Uber driver or an Airbnb host, a closed group that sells fitness products. They also produce information and networks, but the goal of the transaction determines their character and limits; members interact whether or not they share values.

The authors apply the distinction to platforms as well: Pinterest or Alexa are transactional digital shop windows (visual and voice search engines that push toward a purchase), where storytelling is secondary; LinkedIn is an ambiguous hybrid. Stories and toks are conversational by nature.

The practical rule: define your goal and make it clear whether you are going the conversational or the transactional way; this is part of your transparency and credibility. For conversational stories, you identify the shared values and interests and build the stories around them.

## Application

- In the editorial calendar, separate relationship content from conversion content, and balance the two.
- Do not disguise a transaction as a conversation (the "friend" who sells): the community senses it and reacts.
- In the all-in-one strategy, conversion comes on the timeline after the relationship has been built.

## Example

The FMD group of filmmakers is conversational (advice, showreels, discussions about the Oscars); the "Bucket List Japan" landing page, at the end of the campaign, is transactional (routes, flight bookings).

## Questions

- Does social commerce (live shopping, TikTok Shop) blur the line? How does a live sale stay conversational?
- What kind of relationship do different brands call for (a bank vs. an energy drink)?
