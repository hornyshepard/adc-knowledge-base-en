# Social media contests are chosen by the effort they ask for: easy participation brings many people, hard participation brings fewer, but more attached to the brand

> **The idea in one sentence:** the mechanics of a contest are a strategic decision: the more time and thought people invest, the more they think about the brand, but the fewer take part.

## Explanation

Nishad describes six types of contest, which we can order by effort:

| Type | Effort | Participation | What the brand gains |
|---|---|---|---|
| Like, comment and share to win | very low | high | the post's relevance, reach |
| Fill in the blanks | low | high, can be repeated often | entertainment, rhythm |
| Questions and answers, with funny or incorrect answers | low–medium | medium | mental effort put into a request from the brand |
| A caption or meme for an image | medium | medium | involvement, loyalty (according to the author) |
| A photo contest, with the brand tagged | high | low, but a lot of attention | reach among the participants' friends, new followers |
| User-generated content (logo, slogan, T-shirt) | very high | low | content, maximum involvement; three finalists, with surprise rewards for those who do not win |

For lead capture, the author recommends a giveaway platform (Gleam) with terms and conditions, a captcha against bots, a minimum age of 18 and entry by liking the page and giving an email.

## Application

- Choose the mechanics by objective: reach → low effort; content and involvement → UGC; leads → a form with consent.
- The prize has to attract the target audience, not prize hunters.
- Check the platform's current rules and the law on promotions; over time Meta has restricted mechanics such as "share on your profile to enter".

## Example

The author's UGC contest for designing T-shirts for in-store staff turns customers into co-creators and produces content the brand can then use on all its channels.

## Questions

- Contests also attract people interested only in the prize. How do you measure the quality of the participants, not just their number?
- Data collected through contests is often false (SRC-032). Is it worth using as the main source of leads?
