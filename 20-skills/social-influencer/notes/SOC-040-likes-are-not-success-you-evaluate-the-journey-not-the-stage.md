# Likes are not success: you evaluate the journey, not the stage

> **The idea in one sentence:** likes, follows and views are easy to give and easy to inflate; the success of a social campaign is judged by the user's whole journey to conversion, not by a viral peak on a single platform.

## Explanation

Sascha Gottschalk (FMD) starts with a question for the reader: how many likes do you give out every day, and how strict are your criteria? Likes and subscriptions are practically unlimited, so they say little about time, attention or intent. The algorithms themselves no longer give them priority. The value of each metric:

- **View**: counted once the first seconds have run; you do not know whether the whole video was watched or by how many people.
- **Reach and impressions**: how many people noticed the content, like a headline in a daily newspaper looked at briefly.
- **Comments**: explicit, qualitative feedback, but bots have to be filtered out.
- **Saves**: a valuable signal, motivation unknown.
- **Shares**: can multiply exponentially (10 → 100 → 1,000).
- **Completion rate**, return visits and relevance: the recommended qualitative indicators.

Two strategic principles:

1. **Do not confuse a stage goal with the overall goal.** Gottschalk says he has seen employees wow management with quickly achieved high metrics on one platform, although relevance and purchase numbers were what counted; he believes the mistake happens in the majority of companies.
2. **Paper or wood.** Viral attention (cute animal babies, funny moments, a big influencer with the product in the corner) burns like paper: it flares up quickly and fades quickly. A sustainable campaign is chess rather than roulette: you steer it at every stage.

Glenister confirms the convergence: the BCMA warns that vanity metrics include non-human responses; consultant William Bonaddio sums up that "likes don't pay the bills", and Phil Szomszor saw projects with vloggers where the discussion was about audience size, not about sign-ups or enquiries. Every post needs a call to action pointing to the next step; a post that does not serve the campaign plan is a wasted brick.

## Application

- Define the KPIs starting from the end (sales, subscriptions, awareness), then work down through the stages.
- Report platform metrics and business metrics separately.
- Check who is reacting: likes from teenagers do not sell cars.

## Example

In the Japan campaign, FMD posted at least one poll in stories every day and a link to the landing page under every video; a subscription was treated as the start of the relationship, like a person walking into a shop.

## Questions

- If likes have real monetary value in negotiations with influencers, how do you replace them in practice?
- What KPI would you choose for an image campaign where conversion is not immediate (the Red Bull case)?
