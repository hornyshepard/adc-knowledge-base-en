# Creators become brands: from endorsement to the creator economy

> **The idea in one sentence:** influencers are moving from promoting other people's brands to launching their own brands and media companies, and the brand–creator relationship is evolving from endorsement to a business partnership in what the industry calls the creator economy.

## Explanation

Glenister and his contributors describe an evolution in stages:

1. **Endorsement**: the brand buys a celebrity's image (the classic model, from Aunt Jemima and Coca-Cola's Santa Claus).
2. **The influencer as media**: the brand buys reach and engagement; Eric Dahan (Open Influence) speaks of the transition from endorsement deals to media budgets.
3. **Creators in advertising**: creators integrated into multichannel campaigns (TV, OOH, experiential), as Edward East (Billion Dollar Boy) puts it.
4. **The creator economy**: creators become stakeholders in brands or launch their own lines; brands integrate them into their wider business plans, not only into their advertising plans.

The book's examples: Michelle Phan (Icon), Shane Dawson and Jeffree Star's Conspiracy Collection cosmetics ($35 million in one day of sales), Rihanna with Savage X Fenty, Kayla Itsines (the Sweat app), the Prime drink. Some creators become media companies (Mythical Entertainment, which bought Smosh). The model was refined in **China**: through the incubator model, companies such as Ruhnn co-create brands with bloggers at the very start and split the profits; creators have access to the factories, and e-commerce platforms directly support influencer brands. There too, multi-channel networks (MCNs) typically take a 70 percent cut of fees, and earnings are very unequal (1 percent make a killing, 4 percent make a living, 95 percent are starving).

Related trends: professionalization (a class of professional creators separate from hobbyists, John Adams), **virtual influencers** (CGI; "Utility always wins," says Dudley Nevill-Spencer), generative AI, and social media as a search engine (40 percent of Gen Z users prefer TikTok and Instagram to Google for search, according to the studies cited).

## Application

- When you propose a partnership, think beyond posts: product co-creation, limited editions, licensing, equity stakes.
- Analyze big creators as brands: they have a positioning, a community, product lines, and they compete with your clients.
- Track influence in social search, not only in Google.

## Example

The comedy duo Rhett and Link became Mythical Entertainment; the Fine Brothers became FBE: creators turned into media companies.

## Questions

- What happens to the credibility of a creator who promotes their own brand? How do the disclosure rules apply?
- Will the creator economy replace the advertising agency as a source of ideas for brands?
