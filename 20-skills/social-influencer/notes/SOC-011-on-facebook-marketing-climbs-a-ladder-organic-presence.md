# On Facebook, marketing climbs a ladder (organic presence, engagement, leads, paid traffic, remarketing), because organic reach alone is no longer enough

> **The idea in one sentence:** each rung prepares the next: first you have an audience and a relationship, then you accelerate them with money.

## Explanation

Nishad (SRC-033) orders the lessons like a ladder:

1. **A complete page**: profile picture, cover, contact button (message, call, sign up), description, opening hours, privacy policy.
2. **Organic engagement**: posts with a headline that asks for an action, short text, keywords turned into hashtags, images, infographics and videos uploaded directly (those shared through an external link are said to be penalized); groups, Facebook Live, an editorial calendar.
3. **Promoting posts** (*boost*): a test with $5–10, an engagement objective, sometimes without a button, so people read the post first.
4. **Leads**: giveaways with a captcha and a minimum age, a sign-up form connected to Mailchimp.
5. **Paid traffic** to the website, with a daily budget and a cost-per-click cap.
6. **Remarketing** with a pixel, for visitors who left.

The recommended plan: realistic objectives (awareness at first, not sales), knowing the audience, an engagement strategy (page or group, what type of content the audience prefers, learned in the first 3–4 weeks), a calendar, an ads strategy, involving the team.

Schwarz-Musch and his colleagues (SRC-032) explain why the ladder does not stop at organic: the platforms have gradually restricted organic reach (the people on whose screens unpaid posts appear), so it is getting harder and harder to reach customers without ads. That is why small businesses too now invest in social ads, some reporting a ROAS of several hundred percent.

## Application

- Diagnose which rung the client is on and choose the matching campaign objective; do not ask for sales from a page with no audience.
- Budget the paid rungs from the start; organic is the foundation, not the whole strategy.

## Example

The "success stories" in SRC-033, presented without sources: Pandora repurposed a successful TV commercial into a 15-second Facebook video ad and is said to have achieved a 10-point lift in brand awareness in Germany; Buffer cut the number of posts by 50 percent and is said to have tripled its average weekly reach.

## Questions

- The interface and objectives described have changed since then. What holds true in the ladder if the buttons change?
- Does the ladder make sense for a big brand, which can start directly with paid media?
