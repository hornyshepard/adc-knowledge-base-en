# A livestream is planned in three phases: intro, execution, outro

> **The idea in one sentence:** live is "gold" in social media because it draws engagement and users who do not follow you yet, but it works only if it is planned, announced, moderated and documented like an event.

## Explanation

Mueller and Rajaram treat live (from Instagram Live to live social TV on Twitch, YouTube or Facebook) as a participatory conversation with no natural end. On most platforms, live also reaches non-followers; on Instagram only followers, so it has to be cross-promoted and, ideally, scheduled on a fixed day of the week and time.

The recommended structure:

1. **Intro**: jump right in and answer the W questions (who, what, where, why).
2. **Execution**: work on the topic of the day and engage users.
3. **Outro**: summarize, give a call to action, announce the next thing.

Three principles of moderation taken from journalism:

- **Third-party faces and voices** (interviews, guests) create an emotional experience and credibility.
- **Address people directly**: name them, respond in the chat and in the comments before, during and after the event.
- **A signal for the future** in every live: a future benefit or a next event, plus a CTA in the final piece to camera.

The authors add: monitor the interactions, block haters, define in advance what you want to achieve and measure it. They also warn about the trend of pushing followers, especially young ones, to donate to the creator.

## Application

- Write a run-of-show in three columns (intro / execution / outro) with the interaction moments marked (polls, questions).
- You need stable wi-fi, a technical test and a moderator for the chat.
- Reuse the recording: video on IGTV/YouTube, audio as a podcast, fragments as micro-stories.

## Example

During the pandemic, the actress Elena Uhlig created the live talk show "Stilles Örtchen" ("Quiet Loo") on Instagram, plus cooking and sales shows, reaching more than 100,000 followers by early 2021: a clear structure but a spontaneous tone, guests, contests, technical errors acknowledged openly. The pianist Igor Levit streamed concerts from his living room on Twitter Live to tens of thousands of fans.

## Questions

- What does a brand do when the live has 12 viewers? How do you measure a live's success beyond concurrent viewers?
- Where does live replace the physical event and where does it not (Maximilian Wolf is skeptical of online-only festivals and conferences)?
