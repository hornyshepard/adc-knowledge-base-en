# The creator is a one-person production house: gifting is no substitute for payment

> **The idea in one sentence:** a professional creator combines the roles of scriptwriter, director, camera operator, editor and community manager, so sending a free product in exchange for a content package is, in most cases, undervaluing the work, although gifting has a legitimate place in product seeding.

## Explanation

Glenister describes the tension between two practices:

**Legitimate gifting and product seeding.** Offering a product in exchange for a mention works when it is targeted (a personalized package, not a mass mailing), when the product resonates with the influencer and when there is early access or a discount for followers. Classic product seeding: the brand first works with larger influencers, then gifts the product to a number of micro-influencers, who love being part of a selected group (now they have one too). Packaging matters, because many creators present products in unboxing videos.

**Gifting as exploitation.** The creator Amber Doig-Thorne (3 million followers) says that half of the emails she gets are about gifting, often of this kind: a £10 product in exchange for £5,000 worth of content, with an imposed script, usage rights in perpetuity and 12 months of exclusivity. A 10-minute YouTube video paid with £10 means 10–20 hours of work, about 50 pence per hour. She compares the creator to a whole TV production crew (videographer, director, producer, sound engineer, actors, editors) brought together in one person.

The book's data support the second view: paying well is the main motivator for collaboration (5.3 out of 6 in a BCMA survey), followed by enough time and creative freedom; 56 percent of influencers rate monetary compensation as the number one motivator. The author's formula: "free dresses don't pay the mortgage".

## Application

- Use gifting without conditions (no imposed hashtag, no approval) for seeding; if you ask for deliverables, pay.
- Value the content as a production: time, equipment, rights, distribution.
- Respect the market's unwritten rule: a creator with high engagement and a low rate deserves a fair offer, not a negotiation downward.

## Example

Doig-Thorne tells how she discovered, through an Instagram account where influencers anonymously submitted their rates, that other creators with the same number of followers but a much lower engagement rate were charging three or four times what she charged; her management had told her to always charge less than the competitors.

## Questions

- Is "pure" gifting (with no obligations) a safe investment or a waste of product?
- How should a fair rate be set: by followers, by production time or by results?
