# Conversation is moving into digital campfires, small private communities, and the brand enters them as a useful host, not as a seller

> **The idea in one sentence:** people increasingly prefer small groups, where they feel less judged; the brand has a place there only if it brings value to the community.

## Explanation

Sachdev calls the trend *digital campfires*: consumers interact in smaller private communities, where they can be more personal and discuss what matters to them, with "less judgment". Marketers have three options: bring value to existing communities, partner with them (useful information, access to products) or create their own digital campfire community. The idea connects to community marketing: connection, engagement, building a community through blogs, forums, webcasts, social media.

Nishad (SRC-033) describes the classic implementation, the Facebook group:

- **The group's name** is not the brand's name (the brand already has a page), but the customer's interest or problem, even the search term itself (for example, "cheap" + the product category).
- **A private group**, but visible in search: approving members filters out bots, and the request to join works as a *double opt-in*.
- **Targeted content, not branded content**: discussions, recommendations, experiences, expertise regardless of source; a conversational tone, longer messages than on the page.
- **The goal**: relationships with people who may later become customers, and authority for the brand.

## Application

- Define the community's job before its name: what problem it solves for the members, not for the brand.
- Plan a content mix in which promotion is the exception.
- Set moderation rules and decide who on the team keeps the fire burning every day.

## Example

Harley-Davidson, cited by Sachdev in another chapter, shows the mature form: the communities around the brand keep customers close for years. Nishad's Facebook group is the accessible version for a small business.

## Questions

- Does a group created by a brand but given a generic name have to say who runs it? Where does the lack of transparency begin?
- What happens to members' trust if the group suddenly becomes a sales channel?
