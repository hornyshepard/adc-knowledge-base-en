# Universal best times to post are a weak shortcut: the calendar is built from your own audience's data and from tests

> **The idea in one sentence:** tables of "ideal" times are at most starting hypotheses; your audience, the platform and the type of business decide when and how much you post.

## Explanation

**The position of SRC-033.** Nishad gives fixed best times for Facebook: Monday, Wednesday and Thursday 8 a.m.–4 p.m., Tuesday and Friday 7 a.m.–4 p.m., Sunday 9 a.m.–1 p.m.; Saturday is said to be a low engagement day; the ideal frequency is said to be two posts per day. He claims they come from analyzing his own followers, without giving a method or data. The same book partly contradicts itself: it recommends finding out in the first 3–4 weeks what type of content the audience prefers, and among its success stories it says Buffer tripled its reach precisely by cutting the number of posts in half.

**The position of SRC-032.** Schwarz-Musch and his colleagues insist on continuous monitoring and on A/B testing of images, texts, audiences and publishing times. They also note that the schedule differs by type of business: in B2C, off-peak times and weekends work well; in B2B, Monday to Thursday between 8 a.m. and 4 p.m., and Friday until noon.

**SRC-031** adds that digital convenience differs from persona to persona: browsing times and the devices used have to be understood for each target.

## Application

- Start with a hypothesis calendar, then run four weeks of tests: different times and days, the same type of content.
- Compare reach and engagement per post, not the total; more posts can raise the total and lower the average.
- Review the calendar whenever the algorithm or the audience changes.

## Example

The Buffer case, as reported in SRC-033: fewer but better-crafted posts brought three times the weekly reach and double the daily engagement. The example contradicts the "two posts per day" rule in the same book.

## Questions

- Why do tables of "best" times spread so well, even though they have no method?
- What should a report contain that actually justifies an editorial calendar?
