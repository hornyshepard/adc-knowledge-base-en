# Social media algorithms rank the feed by relationship, engagement, content type and time

> **The idea in one sentence:** each platform has its own algorithm and changes it often, but almost all of them sort content by the same four signals: what the relationship is, how much engagement the post has, what type of content it is and how recent it is.

## Explanation

A social media algorithm is a set of mathematical rules that decides which organic and paid content each user sees and in what order. The stated purpose: to show the most relevant content, so people stay on the platform. The algorithms are controversial: users complain about content not being seen, and marketers about ever smaller organic reach, which pushes them to pay.

The four signals (fig. 9.14):

| Signal | What it includes |
|---|---|
| **Relationship** | direct relationship (friends, followers) or indirect (friends of friends); how often the users interact |
| **Engagement** | likes, reactions, comments, shares, the length and depth of engagement, including from similar users |
| **Content type** | videos, photos, links, text, news; most algorithms favor video; hashtags help discovery |
| **Time** | time since the post, time of day |

Some platforms (Pinterest, Facebook) also consider the credibility of links; since 2020 Facebook has also evaluated the quality of news articles, after the backlash over misinformation.

## Application

- Post engaging content consistently (see the 5Cs); a single viral post does not make up for silence.
- Use your own analytics to learn when your audience is active and which formats work; plan in a content calendar.
- Do hashtag research (e.g., Ritetag) and test short video formats.

## Example

The HubSpot content calendar (fig. 9.15) mixes over one month a holiday campaign, an ebook, a webinar, blog posts, a product launch and a Facebook "experiment": a constant stream that feeds the time signal and offers a variety of content types.

## Questions

- If the algorithm rewards engagement, don't brands risk producing only "reaction" content (rage-bait, trends)?
- How does creative planning change when organic reach tends toward zero?
