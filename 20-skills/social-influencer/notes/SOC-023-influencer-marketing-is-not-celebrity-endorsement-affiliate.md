# Influencer marketing is not celebrity endorsement, affiliate marketing or direct marketing

> **The idea in one sentence:** influencer marketing means involving in a campaign a person who can change the behavior of others through trusted opinion, knowledge and content, and it differs from four neighboring practices it is often confused with.

## Explanation

Gordon Glenister offers a minimal definition: influencer marketing happens when someone (an influencer) is involved in a campaign to promote a product or service. An influencer is an individual who can effect change in the behavior of others, often through trusted opinion, knowledge and content creation. The author then separates the influencer from the **creator**: all influencers produce content, but not all creators influence opinions or purchases; many influencers do not use the name "influencer", because they feel it has been maligned in some areas of the media.

What influencer marketing **isn't**:

| Practice | The difference |
|---|---|
| **Celebrity endorsement** | the celebrity is famous through traditional media and often lacks the relationship with followers that influencers have; expensive, useful for brand awareness |
| **Affiliate marketing** | a commission paid for clicks or sales tracked through a link; increasingly combined with influencers, but it is a payment mechanism, not a relationship |
| **Referral marketing** | a recommendation paid with a referral fee, an umbrella term that can include influencer and affiliate marketing |
| **Direct marketing** | a single message, controlled by the brand, aimed at a very specific person; influencer marketing has multiple messages around a theme, content largely driven by the influencer, and also reaches the influencer's friends and family and "like-minded tribes" |

The book's explanatory context: consumers no longer give blind trust to institutions, big brands or the media, but they still trust "people like us" (Scott Guthrie). Communication has to inform and entertain, not interrupt.

## Application

- When a client asks for "influencer marketing", clarify what they want: awareness through a celebrity, sales on commission or a content relationship with creators.
- Use the creator/influencer distinction in the brief: are you looking for the ability to produce content or the ability to move a community?
- The mechanisms can coexist (influencer plus affiliate code), but the objectives are measured differently.

## Example

In 2010, Old Spice used a character (Isaiah Mustafa) in a TV spot that went viral, a classic form of influence through a character; ASOS created the "ASOS Insiders" community, encouraging followers to "buy the look", which is influencer marketing proper.

## Questions

- Where do you place a famous actor who builds his own community on TikTok?
- Is a paid review on a blog influencer marketing or an advertorial? What do the disclosure rules say?
