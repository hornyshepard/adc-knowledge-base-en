# A paid collaboration is disclosed clearly, up front and in every format: where the audience sees the recommendation, not after a click

> **The idea in one sentence:** consumers have the right to know when someone is trying to influence them for a commercial purpose, so any material connection between a brand and an influencer is disclosed visibly and unambiguously, right at the start of the content.

## Explanation

Three sources converge almost word for word. Glenister sums up the general principles shared by the regulators (in the UK the ASA, CAP and CMA; in the US the FTC; in Europe the EASA): advertising must be easily recognizable as such on any channel, and disclosure must be obvious, up front, unambiguous and must not require a click or a scroll. Sachdev (SRC-031) gives the FTC's formula: the disclosure must be "clear and conspicuous", which means in the video if the endorsement is in the video. Grewal and Levy (SRC-001) ask for #ad at the start of the hashtags and repeated mentions in livestreams.

**What triggers the obligation:** any *material connection*: money, gift cards, free or discounted products, trips, employment, family relationships, the chance to win a prize. Sachdev notes that the rule also covers likes, pins and tags; the ASA's 2023 guidance requires #ad even when you promote a friend's business. The brand, the agency and the influencer are all liable.

**How to disclose:** acceptable: "#ad", "advertisement", "sponsored", "paid partnership", "#[Brand]Partner"; riskier: "#sp", "#spon", "#collab", "#ambassador" on its own, "thank you [brand]". The label goes at the start; in video, in both the picture and the audio; in stories, on every story; affiliate codes and competitions are advertising too.

**What each author adds.** Sachdev: influencers are not experts, and if they appear to give medical, legal or financial advice they can incur legal liability; saying they are not a doctor is not enough. Grewal and Levy: besides disclosure, *sincerity* matters: the influencer has to use the product (their counterexample: a supposedly spontaneous marriage proposal, planned in advance and funded by brands). Glenister: the ASA's 2021 report found a disappointing overall rate of compliance.

## Application

- Write the wording and placement of the disclosure into the brief and the contract; check before publication.
- Forbid health or financial claims the brand cannot substantiate.
- Check the rules of each market; in Romania and the EU, consumer protection and the guidelines of local authorities apply.

## Example

In 2020, the FTC sent warning letters to several influencers who had promoted Teami tea: the disclosure should have been in the video; $930,000 was ordered to be returned to buyers (Sachdev). The ASA ruled that a post by Molly-Mae Hague about PrettyLittleThing required #ad, even though the brand considered it outside the contract (Glenister).

## Questions

- Does the #ad label reduce effectiveness? (Rupa Shah, in Glenister, says no, provided the ad feels authentic.)
- Who is liable when the influencer does not disclose: the influencer or the brand?
