# Mission, Cast, Create: the mission comes before casting the creators

> **The idea in one sentence:** a partnership with a creator or a celebrity is built in three steps (you define the strategic mission, choose the talent by shared values and co-create with them from the start), and the final test is whether the same content would work with another partner.

## Explanation

The model comes from David Robson (Unsigned Group, the video game industry), quoted by Glenister:

| Step | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Mission** | What is the true strategic purpose? Not a sales number such as 1 million copies in the first 30 days but, for example, moving into a new market, acquiring a higher-value type of player, growing a title into a trans-media franchise. If you achieve your sales target but fail to acquire your target customer, the campaign has not been a long-term success. |
| **Cast** | Beyond matching audience demographics, you look for shared values, ideally already defined in the brand strategy. The exercise: which values would sit in a Venn diagram between the talent, the brand and the audience? How many reasons can an outsider list for why the partnership makes sense? |
| **Create** | Involve the talent as early as possible, even before the contract, so the conversations define the options. Do not hand them a finished creative framework to execute within; the process has to be symbiotic, and the deliverables follow from the idea, not from the budget. |

**The authenticity test**: "Could this content work with a different partner?" If other talent could pick up the campaign and deliver the same creative on the same channels, you need a more bespoke approach.

Robson insists that talent partnerships can be more than marketing: co-created products, bespoke collaborations, PR value in new markets.

## Application

- Write the mission in one sentence that describes the target customer behavior, not just a number.
- For each candidate, list the values in the Venn diagram and the reasons the audience would believe the partnership.
- Use the different-partner test when presenting the concept to the client.

## Example

Buffalo Trace Bourbon started small with Derek Wolf (@OverTheFireCooking), who used the bourbon in a recipe, then came back to him with an idea to help him improve his videos: a four-part web series featuring other influencers as guests. It would never have happened if the brand had started with a "how much" question.

## Questions

- When is it legitimate for the brand to keep creative control (regulated industries, crises)?
- How do you apply the model to a small budget, with nano-influencers?
