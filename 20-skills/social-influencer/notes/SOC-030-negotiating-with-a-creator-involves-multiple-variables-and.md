# Negotiating with a creator involves multiple variables, and the contract sets the rights

> **The idea in one sentence:** the price of a collaboration with a creator depends not only on the number of followers but on several negotiable variables, and a written contract (standard at every tier) protects both sides on payment, rights and exclusivity.

## Explanation

Glenister proposes a five-step negotiation: (1) research the influencer and their media kit; (2) decide on the compensation method; (3) offer additional deals and perks; (4) agree the campaign deliverables; (5) negotiate exclusivity and rights.

**The negotiable variables**: real reach (not the number of followers) and on which platforms; experience and production skills; the type of content (video, podcast, photo); the frequency of posts; the length of the partnership; how the brand works with the influencer's audience; when and how the creator is paid. An influencer may accept a lower fee if the brand covers the production costs. The market benchmark quoted: a cost per engagement of roughly £0.22–£0.25.

**Additional perks**: meals and experiences (always with a +1 option), access to VIP events (a very strong pull, because it makes them feel highly valued), commission on affiliate promotions, brand content the creator can use (a piece of joint research).

**The contract** (drafted as a template by a specialist firm) includes: the parties and the agent, the campaign name, the period and the total cost; payment and its timing (often 30 days after the campaign); the campaign requirements for both sides; responsibility for licenses (music); compliance with the law; content quality; the approval process; tax status; confidentiality (for launches); the consequences of a breach. Separately: **usage rights** (the brand often wants to reuse the content in other media, which is paid for) and **exclusivity** (more expensive, and for niche creators it can be lucrative or harmful).

## Application

- Ask for the media kit and check the average reach of the posts, not just the followers.
- Pay quickly: late payment is one of the most frequent complaints from creators, and they make their grievances public.
- Set out what happens if the creator's values later come into conflict with the brand.
- In outreach, contact more people than you need: the author allows for a 50 percent non-response rate and recommends getting at least 30 confirmations for 25 places.

## Example

Jim Tobin (Carusele) tells how another division of the same brand offered an influencer four times more than the agency had paid him for the same kind of work: an expensive mistake, easily repeated without a rates policy.

## Questions

- What usage rights should an agency ask for if it wants to turn the creator's content into a TV or OOH ad?
- How ethical is a 12-month exclusivity paid for with a free product?
