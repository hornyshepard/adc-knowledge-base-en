# Who owns influencer marketing: PR, media, the specialist agency or the brand

> **The idea in one sentence:** almost every type of agency claims to do influencer marketing, and the practitioners in Glenister's book disagree on whether the discipline belongs to PR, the media agency, specialist agencies or in-house teams, and even on whether it is anything new.

## Explanation

The positions in the book:

| Position | Argument |
|---|---|
| **PR** (Chris Lee) | wherever reputation is at stake (journalists or influencers), the responsibility lies with the PR team; internal silos have to be broken down, though, because the activity affects marketing, sales and SEO |
| **Just good PR** (Phil Szomszor) | influencer marketing often feels like the "Emperor's New Clothes"; with the shiny packaging thrown away, it is good old-fashioned public relations, meaning work with all the publics, not just the press |
| **The media agency** (the book's arguments, Tom Curtis, MediaCom) | integration with the overall plan, expertise and buying power for amplification, relationships with the platforms and first access to new opportunities; influencer marketing should not be treated as a silo |
| **The specialist agency** (Jim Tobin, Whalar) | experience with similar briefs, a vetted network, more commitment from creators who get regular work, contracts and tools; for beginners it is safer to work with an agency and bring the work in-house later |
| **The brand / self-service platform** | the book's table: small budget, limited geographic needs, a competent in-house team, need for real-time statistics → self-service; large budget, sensitive niches, few resources, lack of experience → full-service agency |
| **Everyone, together** (Scott Guthrie) | the future of the discipline should not be decided by one creative industry; marketing, advertising and PR must build good practice together |

Jenny Tsai (WeArisma) reframes the stakes: what matters is not influencers but **influence**: the press, celebrities, creators, influencers and consumer advocates taken together, across the whole funnel.

## Application

- In the pitch, clarify the roles: who selects the creators, who owns the creative brief, who buys the amplification, who measures.
- Hold a kick-off meeting with all the partners (brand, influencer agency or platform, PR, media, creative).
- For large clients, create the role of influencer marketing manager.

## Example

Glenister describes the talent managers who represent large creators (typically for a commission of about 20 percent) and notes, on the brand side, Britvic's preference for approaching small creators directly rather than through agents.

## Questions

- For a creative agency in Romania, does it make sense to build an influencer marketing department or to work with specialists?
- If Szomszor is right that it is just PR, why have dedicated associations and platforms formed?
