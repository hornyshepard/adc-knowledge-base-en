# FMD: influencers report live, the team produces the long formats

> **The idea in one sentence:** the tourism campaigns of the production company FMD (Filmmakers Germany) run at two speeds (influencers and the social team publish daily during the trip, while the core team produces the films and the landing page), so the reach and the community exist before the main product is ready.

## Explanation

Sascha Gottschalk and Tariq Khan (FMD) describe three campaigns that gave rise to the all-in-one strategy:

- **Uganda (2018), for the Uganda Tourism Board.** Instead of an influencer who already knows the region (the usual practice), FMD invited the British vlogger Thomas Alex Norman, who had never been to Africa, so he could authentically take users into unknown territory. Norman's series (four parts, over 90 minutes) went on to film festivals, cinemas and TV.
- **Japan (2019–2020), Bucket List.** Two sub-productions planned from the start: premium content with long post-production and a real-time social campaign with influencers. The sequence: live stories → planned Instagram and Facebook posts → YouTube videos → bucket list videos and ads → a landing page with routes and bookings. The material was shot in 9:16, 1:1, 4:5 and 16:9. FMD reports exceeding the reach targets by 150%.
- **Usedom Sunspot Award (2020).** A video competition: ten filmmakers selected from over 100 applicants in the FMD Facebook group had 100 hours to make a film exactly 100 seconds long about the island. The authors' formula: "content × reach × event". In seven days, more content about Usedom was created than in all the previous years put together, plus UGC, press coverage and films reusable for years; the reach target was exceeded by 250%.

## Application

- Separate from the start the fast speed (live, real time) from the slow speed (post-production), and plan what feeds each.
- An influencer who is a stranger to the subject can bring the fresh eye of discovery; the choice depends on the story, not only on the niche.
- A competition event can produce content, reach, UGC and press relations at the same time.

## Example

In Japan, every post led further: a photo to a detailed video, a video to a behind-the-scenes, that to a map on the landing page and then to the flight booking page.

## Questions

- The figures are reported by the agency that ran the campaigns; what independent data would you ask for before citing them in a pitch?
- Does the model work for a mass-market product, with no spectacular "destination"?
