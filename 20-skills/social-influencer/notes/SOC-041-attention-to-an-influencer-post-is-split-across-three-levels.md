# Attention to an influencer post is split across three levels

> **The idea in one sentence:** the likes and comments on an influencer post are owed largely to the platform and to the creator, and only a small share concern the product or place being promoted, so big numbers do not prove the campaign's success.

## Explanation

Sascha Gottschalk (FMD) calls the phenomenon "the disruptive tragedy". The hypothetical example: an artist-influencer, a photographer with over 500,000 subscribers and a well-defined audience (travel, nature, adventure), posts a picture of a hotel: 178,332 persons reached, 26,679 likes, 288 comments, 47 shares. It looks like a success. The analysis on three levels:

| Level | What generates the reaction | The author's estimate |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The platform** | habits, netiquette (liking as a sign of support), algorithms, organized groups that like each other's posts; likes are unlimited and free | 30–60% of reactions |
| **2. The profile (the artist-influencer)** | style, editing, lifestyle, the fans' loyalty to the person | 40–80% of reactions |
| **3. The story object** (the hotel, the destination) | the wish to travel there, confirmation from those who have been | usually far less than 5% of active users even seem aware of the destination |

The percentages are estimates from experience, not measurements; the key idea is that the environment of the post has to be evaluated. Gottschalk proposes further questions: how many destinations does the influencer promote per year, how many compete with yours, how does the post relate to the ones before and after it? The thought experiment: George Clooney in four ads for four coffee brands in the same month would be a comedy sketch; on social media it is ordinary.

## Application

- Read the comments, not just their number: how many talk about the product?
- Ask for the influencer's history of collaborations and check category exclusivity.
- Use the comments about the product as a separate qualitative indicator in reporting.

## Example

An influencer's post about a luxury hotel had very high views, likes and impressions, but not one of its more than 250 comments mentioned the hotel: they all commented on the influencer's clothing, appearance or status.

## Questions

- Is the attention given to the creator useless for the brand, or does it build associations indirectly?
- How would you design a brief so that level 3 gets more attention?
