# Person of Influence: look for whoever already has your customers in their network

> **The idea in one sentence:** instead of looking for leads one by one, you look for people or organizations that already have your target audience in their network, and you build referral relationships with them, ideally reciprocal ones.

## Explanation

The guide starts from the observation that referrals are free leads, easier to book and with a higher buying rate. So as not to depend on luck, they have to be turned into **systems**:

| Mechanism | How it works |
|---|---|
| The post-presentation referral system | a fixed moment in which you ask for referrals, with a form and a reward; the author modeled it on a top colleague and says it has brought him well over 8,000 referrals |
| Person of Influence (POI) | someone who has your target market inside their network; the key question: "who has my customer" |
| The reciprocal referral relationship | two professionals with the same customers but different offers send each other referrals (the insurance agent and the real estate agent) |

The author's POI example: the head of a network of more than 600 financial advisors made him the network's "recommended sales coach", which brings an ongoing stream of clients. The business analogy: Starbucks went into Barnes & Noble bookstores, then into grocery stores, airports and hotels, because all of them already had Starbucks customers. The guide also recommends "modeling" (*best practices*): you find the best person in the industry at referrals and copy their system, script included.

## Application

- For a small agency: who already has your clients? Research firms, production companies, business consultants, trade associations, startup accelerators. Propose collaborations and referrals in both directions.
- For a freelance designer: printers, web developers and photographers have clients who need a visual identity; a reciprocal partnership works better than ads.

## Example

A branding studio that often works with restaurants partners with an interior architect and a hospitality consultant: each recommends the others when a new venue opens.
