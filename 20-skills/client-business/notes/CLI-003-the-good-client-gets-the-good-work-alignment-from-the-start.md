# The good client gets the good work: alignment from the start, trust and strategic risk

> **The idea in one sentence:** exceptional work almost always appears where the client and the agency trust each other, and trust is built through clear briefs, aligned approvals and the courage to take strategic risks.

## Explanation

McTigue and Rucker link the quality of the creative work to the client's behavior:

- **Alignment from the start.** Everyone who will evaluate the work must see and approve the brief, including top management. Otherwise you risk an executive who is not used to evaluating creative work rejecting the execution after the resources have been spent. The approval points are also set at the start (does the CEO also need to approve casting?), because lagging approvals blow up the time line.
- **Trust and strategic risk.** Great work comes from the balance between the discipline of a strong brief and the creative freedom needed to break through the clutter. When you hire an agency, you are hiring people, not logos: the ones you share a vision with and who will disagree with you when disagreement is warranted, even if they cost 5 percent more or their pitch idea was your second favorite.
- **The relationship.** You talk with the agency about work you admire or dislike, inside and outside the category, you thank them for the effort, and you accept hearing "you are wrong here".

Bad clients have weak briefs and vague strategies, are combative and blame others. Agencies know who they are, and good people ask to be moved to other accounts. If your team keeps changing or agencies decline to pitch, look at yourself first. Do not rush to change agencies: there are frequently more differences within an agency than between them, and a bad strategy will follow you to the new agency.

The interviews in *Shareworthy* confirm this from the agency side: Kevin Koller gives the client's courage full credit for Reddit's "Superb Owl", Susan Young says radical ideas need a true client partner, and Greg Hahn brings the client into the process early, so that by the end the solution is "almost like math".

## Application

- Before the briefing, map the people who approve and get their sign-off on the brief.
- After each project, discuss with the agency what worked and what did not in the relationship, not only in the creative work.

## Questions

- How do you build trust as a junior on the client side, without the authority of a marketing director?
- When is it right, after all, to change agencies?
