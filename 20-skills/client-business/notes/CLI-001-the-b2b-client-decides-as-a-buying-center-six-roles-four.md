# The B2B client decides as a buying center: six roles, four decision cultures and three buying situations

> **The idea in one sentence:** when a company buys a campaign, the decision is not made by "the client" but by a group of people with different roles; if you do not know who initiates, who influences and who decides, you waste your time or offend the real decider.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy describe the **buying center** with six roles; one person can take on more than one role, and one role can be shared by several people:

| Role | Who it is | The typical equivalent at an advertising client |
|---|---|---|
| Initiator | first suggests the purchase | the brand manager who asks for the campaign |
| Influencer | influences the others | research, sales, a consultant |
| Decider | determines whether to buy, what, how and where | the marketing director or the CEO |
| Buyer | handles the paperwork of the purchase | the procurement department |
| User | uses the product | the brand team, the sales force that will use the campaign |
| Gatekeeper | controls information and access | the assistant, procurement, legal |

The book's example: in a surgical procedure, the doctor is the initiator, the medical device supplier the influencer, the hospital the decider, the purchasing manager the buyer, the patient the user, and the insurance company the gatekeeper.

**The decision culture** varies: *autocratic* (one person decides alone), *democratic* (the majority rules), *consultative* (one person decides after getting input from others) and *consensus* (all members must agree on the decision). It tells you whom you present to, and how.

**The buying situation** changes the game: in a *new buy* the group goes through all the steps and involves many people; in a *modified rebuy* the current vendor has an advantage, unless the change comes from dissatisfaction with that vendor; in a *straight rebuy* the order is repeated. An agency pitch is a new buy; renewing an account is a modified rebuy.

## Application

- Before a pitch or a creative presentation, map the roles to people's names and write down the decision culture; check the map in the pre-call.
- Give each role what it needs: the decider the big picture, the buyer the costs and terms, the user the way the campaign makes their work easier.

## Example

An agency that won over the brand team but never found out that the decision is consultative and belongs to the CEO sees the campaign stopped at the last meeting by someone it has never met. A role map made at the start would have shown that this person had to be brought into the discussion from the brief.

## Questions

- How do you find out a new client's decision culture without asking directly?
- What role does the agency itself actually play in the client's buying center?
