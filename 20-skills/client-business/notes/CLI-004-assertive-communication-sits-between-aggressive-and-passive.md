# Assertive communication sits between aggressive and passive: you say directly what you think and need, while respecting the other person's right to think differently

> **The idea in one sentence:** the aggressive person imposes their ideas, the passive person swallows them to avoid conflict; the assertive person states them clearly and without attacking, keeps their limits and looks for solutions that benefit both sides.

## Explanation

*La bible de la communication* describes three styles:

| Style | How it shows | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Aggressive** | dominates, imposes, criticizes or attacks verbally | conflicts, resentment |
| **Passive** | does not express needs and opinions, cannot say "no", conforms | frustration, misunderstandings, a feeling of being invalidated |
| **Assertive** (*communication assertive*) | direct and clear expression, respect for the other person, personal limits, active listening | mutual respect, understanding, conflicts resolved constructively |

The tools of assertiveness:

- **"I" messages** (*messages "je"*): "Je me sens frustré lorsque les tâches ne sont pas réparties équitablement…" [I feel frustrated when tasks are not shared out fairly…], followed by a wish to discuss solutions, instead of accusations in the second person.
- **Saying no respectfully**: clear and brief, with the reason and, where possible, an alternative (another deadline, another person).
- **Positive reinforcement**: you explicitly acknowledge good contributions.
- **Managing conflict**: you identify the source, state your concern through "I" messages, listen without interrupting and look for a compromise.

The book also presents the four modules of **nonviolent communication** (*communication non-violente*), without naming its author, Marshall Rosenberg: neutral observation of the facts, mastering and naming feelings, expressing needs, and making a clear request that leaves room for negotiation.

## Application

- In a discussion with the client about a rejected idea: "I understand that this direction seems risky to you. I believe it is precisely what answers the problem in the brief. May I show you why?" Not "you didn't understand the concept" (aggressive), nor "fine, we'll change it" (passive).
- When you get a brief with an impossible deadline, say "no" to the deadline, not to the project, and propose an alternative.
- In feedback between creatives: an observation about the work, not a judgment about the person.

## Example

The wording the book proposes for a conflict over deadlines: you say that you feel frustrated when the team does not meet the agreed deadlines, because the quality of the work suffers, and you propose more realistic deadlines and a system of regular follow-up.

## Questions

- Assertiveness reads differently in different cultures. What does "direct" mean for a client in Romania compared with one in the Netherlands?
- When is it strategic to be "passive" for a while with a new client?
