# The account manager sells in both directions: the agency's work to the client and the client's problem to the team

> **The idea in one sentence:** the account person is not a messenger between the client and the creative team but a translator and a negotiator: they carry the client's problem into the agency and the agency's ideas to the client, with the budget and the schedule under control.

## Explanation

*La boîte à outils de la publicité* describes two levels of the job:

| Role | What they do | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| **Account executive** (*chef de publicité*) | takes the brief, checks that the objectives fit the budget, gathers information, briefs the planner and the creatives, presents the proposal "avec force et conviction" [with force and conviction], negotiates the changes the client asks for, coordinates production, tracks the budget, writes a report after each stage | organization, responsiveness, diplomacy |
| **Account director** (*directeur de clientèle*) | the agency's ambassador, with three hats: salesperson (the client portfolio and prospecting), consultant (helps the client think through its strategy at the brief), coordinator of the teams | experience, knowledge of the market, of the competition and of the client's past communication |

The authors set out a double objective: to sell the teams' concept to the client and to sell the client's issues and stakes to the teams. When budgets are tight, the account person gets the teams to accept the client's financial constraints and, in turn, gets the client to accept how the budget is split. The key moment is taking the brief, which they must translate for the strategists and the creatives.

The other sources confirm this anchoring role. For McTigue and Rucker, in the creative meeting the account person reads through the brief to ground everyone in the strategy, and the client listens to check that the representation is true to the brief. Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott note that written objectives, known to everyone working on the account (account service, creative, media, planning), leave less room for misunderstandings between the agency and the client.

## Application

- After each meeting, send a report with the decisions, the people responsible and the deadlines; it becomes the shared memory of the account.
- Do not pass the client's feedback on as it is: translate it into a problem and an objective, so the creative team looks for the solution itself.
- In front of the client, defend the idea; in front of the team, defend the budget and the deadline; both belong to the same job.

## Example

The client cuts the budget by a fifth two weeks before production. A weak account person forwards the email; a good one comes to the team with two ways to redistribute it (fewer executions or a different media mix) and to the client with the consequences of each.

## Questions

- Is the account person "the client's voice in the agency" or "the agency's voice at the client"? What do you do when the two conflict?
- What is left of the role in agencies where the planner has taken over strategic thinking?
