# The Dream 100: a short list of target clients, approached repeatedly through different channels

> **The idea in one sentence:** instead of contacting everyone once, you choose your top 100 prospects and approach them systematically, several times and through different channels, until you book the first appointment.

## Explanation

The guide takes the *Dream 100* concept from the sales trainer Chet Holmes. The problem it starts from: most salespeople reach out to a prospect once and then give up. The Dream 100 reverses the logic:

1. **Identify** your top 100 opportunities by value (not the easiest ones).
2. **Use sequential marketing**: multiple contacts, using different techniques; for example, seven touches over 12 months: a phone call, an email, a postcard, a letter, a walk-in, a direct message on social media, a text.
3. **Do not stop** until you book the first appointment or until the person explicitly tells you not to contact them.

The author's analogy is the launch of a movie: "coming soon" posters, the trailer in the theater and on YouTube, a social media strategy, a joint venture with McDonald's, the stars' guest appearances on shows and podcasts. No single touch convinces you; the sixth one might. The prospect has to "feel" your commitment and your intention.

The section on intention adds: a name written on the whiteboard in your office is no longer just "a number in your activity" but a relationship pursued on purpose. This is how the author got a meeting, through an introduction made by a mutual friend, with the owner of an organization of about 3,000 people.

## Application

- For an agency: a Dream 25–50 is more realistic than 100. For each target client, write down the decider, an insight about their brand and a plan of 5–7 touches that bring value (a relevant study, a point of view on their category, an invitation to an event), not just "we can help you".
- For a student or freelancer: the list of the 20 agencies or studios where you want to work, with repeated and varied contacts.

## Example

Over a year, an agency that wants to work with a retailer sends: a short analysis of the category's communication, an invitation to a trend presentation, a message after a competitor's campaign and a proposal for a 20-minute conversation.
