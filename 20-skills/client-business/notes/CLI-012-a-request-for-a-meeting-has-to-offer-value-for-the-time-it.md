# A request for a meeting has to offer value for the time it asks for

> **The idea in one sentence:** in the prospect's mind there is a teeter-totter between the time you ask for and the value they get *just for agreeing to the meeting*; a good message is short, benefit-driven and ends with a clear call to action.

## Explanation

**The angle.** Before you write or call, think like the prospect: what do they gain from giving you 15 minutes, not from buying the product but from the meeting itself? The guide cites the GEICO car insurance slogan ("Give me 15 minutes and I can probably save you 15 percent or more…") as a model of a message that asks for time in exchange for a concrete benefit.

**The four-sentence structure** of the appointment-setting message:
1. This is who I am.
2. This is why I am contacting you.
3. This is what I would like to do.
4. Would you like to do it? A clear call to action (Tuesday at 4:00 or Wednesday?).

The author's example to a CEO: a big vision (he wants to build his company to a million clients), a link to the CEO's interest (he can refer those clients to him), a small ask (15 minutes): a big possibility for little time.

**A template, not copy-paste.** Repetitive messages (asking to speak at a company's weekly sales meeting) deserve a template, but a customized one: whoever receives a copy-pasted message can feel it, and a CEO will not answer it. The positive example: an email whose subject line names the mutual contact who made the referral (Michael Price, one of the sender's clients) and that mentions the recipient's specific goal.

**The "special" invitation.** When you invite someone to an event or a webinar, tell them why it is worth it. The story of the pastor who invited the author to his sermon with a self-mocking joke about bringing caffeine pills (even though the sermon was excellent) shows how the wording "shapes the listening". The *teaser email* (you are looking for seven people to mentor personally: will the reader be one of the seven?) turns the appointment into an exclusive selection.

## Application

- Rewrite your prospecting message (to an agency, a client, a possible mentor) in the four sentences and check: what value does the recipient get for the 15 minutes?
- When you invite a client to the presentation of an idea, do not play it down ("it's just a sketch"); say why it is worth their time.

## Example

"I'm Ioana, a junior art director; I'm writing because I followed the relaunch of your coffee brand and I have an activation idea for the cold season; I'd like to show it to you in 15 minutes; does Thursday at 11:00 work for you?"
