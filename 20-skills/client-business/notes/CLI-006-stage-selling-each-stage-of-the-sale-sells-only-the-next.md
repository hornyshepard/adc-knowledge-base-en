# Stage selling: each stage of the sale sells only the next step

> **The idea in one sentence:** the purpose of generating a lead is the lead, the purpose of setting an appointment is the appointment; whoever tries to sell the product at every step is trying to do "too much too soon" and loses.

## Explanation

The B2B guide attributed to Nishad calls the principle *stage selling* and presents it as one of its best ideas about sales. B2B selling is a sequence of stages, each with its own outcome:

| Stage | The only purpose |
|---|---|
| generating the lead | to get the contact and put it into your database |
| setting the appointment | to get the meeting |
| running the appointment | the outcome set for it: another appointment, a demo, a proposal or the sale |

Many salespeople say the idea was a game changer for them, because they had been trying to sell everything at once. The author also widens the definition of an "appointment": any chance to further the sales call with the prospect, including a group presentation on Zoom at the end of which you put your calendar link in the chat (six people out of 60 booked a call on their own).

**Convergence.** Colenso reaches the same idea from the point of view of the presentation: the ask in a pitch is not the final "yes" but the "logical next step". The two sources complement each other: Nishad thinks at the level of the sales funnel, Colenso at the level of a single presentation. Grewal and Levy's textbook, by contrast, keeps the full process, with the close and the follow-up ([[CLI-007-personal-selling-has-five-steps-and-the-presentation-starts|CLI-007]]), but says the same thing about the first meeting: its objective is rarely the signature.

## Application

- For every contact with a potential client of the agency, write down in advance the "next step" you want: a 20-minute meeting, access to the research data, an invitation to a chemistry session.
- A prospecting email does not need the full portfolio and the offer, only the reason why a short conversation is worth it.

## Example

A small agency invites 40 marketing directors to a webinar on retail trends. The purpose of the webinar is not to sign a contract but to have 5–6 participants ask for a one-to-one conversation; the contract is the purpose of the *next* meeting.

## Questions

- When does stage selling become a manipulative tactic ("just 15 minutes") that annoys the client?
- In large agency pitches (tenders), where are the stages and who controls them?
