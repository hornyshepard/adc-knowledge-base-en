# Prospecting is a game of consistency in which you count the activity, not the replies

> **The idea in one sentence:** the fear of rejection goes away when you measure success by the number of contacts you make (you win when you prospect, you win twice when you book the appointment) and when you prospect a little, but every day.

## Explanation

The guide names the fear of rejection as the main reason people do not prospect. The real rhythm of prospecting is no, no, no, no, no, no, yes; if every "no" is felt as a failure, procrastination is inevitable. The solution is a reframe: you win when you prospect and you double win when you book the appointment: **"I win or I double win. I win no matter what."** The activity itself is the victory.

Four mechanisms support the reframe:

| Mechanism | What it means |
|---|---|
| The 1–5+ system | a minimum of one, ideally five or more prospecting touches a day, five days a week; at about 2 minutes a touch, that is 2–10 minutes a day |
| The prospecting game | in the morning you declare the day's target and tick off each touch; everything counts (the wrong number and the voicemail too) |
| GSA: Goal, Strategy, Action | the written goal (how many touches, how many appointments), the written plan, then massive action every day |
| Consistency | as with a diet or the gym: results come from a system followed consistently, not from sporadic bursts |

The guide adds "prospect by any means necessary" (phone, email, text, LinkedIn, Facebook) and suggests turning your smartphone into an ATM: your message history and old contacts are leads you already have. The story that proves it: the author contacted Michael Gerber's wife (*The E-Myth*) only to hit his Friday target; the message led to a joint seminar and a public endorsement.

## Application

- For your first job search: five contacts a week (agencies, creative directors, graduates), logged in a table; the week's success is reaching five, not the number of replies.
- For new business at an agency: a written goal of contacts and meetings per month, reviewed every week.

## Example

An ADC student who sends 40 resumes at once and gets two replies gives up; one who sends five personalized messages a week for two months does the same work spread out and has a steady flow of conversations.

## Questions

- Does the "I win no matter what" reframe risk encouraging volume at the expense of the quality of the message?
- How does prospecting change when the client is a large company with procurement procedures?
