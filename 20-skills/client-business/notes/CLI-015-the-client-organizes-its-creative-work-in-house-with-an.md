# The client organizes its creative work in-house, with an agency of choice or with a roster of agencies, and the right criterion is the effectiveness of the work, not the cost

> **The idea in one sentence:** the question of who makes the advertising no longer has a single answer; an advertiser can have an in-house team, several agencies and independent collaborators at the same time, and the choice of model must be judged by the quality of the work, not by how much it saves.

## Explanation

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott point out that a great deal of advertising is not created by agencies. In 2016 PepsiCo created an in-house creative unit (Creators League Studio), then, in 2018, it turned to outside sources to help with the growing digital demand: the same advertiser worked with several agencies, with its own in-house agency and with independents. A 2017 study found that 98% of in-house creative teams expected to grow or at least stay the same size. Retailers employ their own creative people, and for small firms the ad is sometimes made by the very medium that runs it. The authors' conclusion: for most brands, the agency still remains the norm.

Keith Reinhard, in the final chapter of Richards's history, lists the options after the pandemic: more work brought in-house, bespoke agencies, a strong partnership with an agency of choice, or a roster of agencies. His criterion: not which structure is more "efficient" (that is, cheaper), but "which structure and arrangement results in the most effective work".

The arguments for each side appear in the other sources. For the agency: the outside view, which gives distance from the client's problem, and the specialized people few advertisers have (*La boîte à outils*). For the in-house team: Allan Peters says that when he moved in-house in 2009, at Target, he lost his fear of the client because they were on the same team, and he began to speak up. McTigue and Rucker note that the rules of a good brief are the same for a famous agency and for an in-house creative partner.

## Application

- For your career: an in-house studio is a legitimate first job, with access to data and to decision-makers, but with less variety of brands.
- For an agency: when you compete with the client's in-house team, sell what it does not have: the outside perspective, specialization, ideas that change the category.

## Example

The PepsiCo model combines the in-house studio for volume and speed with outside agencies and collaborators for new ideas and formats.

## Questions

- What does a brand lose by bringing all its creative work in-house? What does it gain?
- How do you measure "the effectiveness of the work" when you compare two very different partners?
