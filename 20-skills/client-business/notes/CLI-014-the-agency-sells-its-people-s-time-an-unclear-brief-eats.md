# The agency sells its people's time: an unclear brief eats its margin, and modern compensation ties the bonus to communication objectives

> **The idea in one sentence:** most agencies are paid according to the time of the people allotted to an account, so every needless round of revisions is a loss of money and energy; a clear brief and measurable objectives protect both the agency's margin and the quality of the work.

## Explanation

McTigue and Rucker start from a simple observation: good agencies have two primary goals, to make money and to do great work, and a strong brief serves both. Most agency compensation models are built on the time of specific people allotted to an account for a given project or time frame. A vague brief produces the churn or "swirl" of creative development: it costs money, burns out the team and leads to tepid creative work of the "I know they'll approve this" kind.

Under cost pressure, a number of agencies are moving to a **base retainer plus bonus** structure. The authors note that agencies are rightly hesitant to tie bonuses to sales, because sales depend on many factors besides advertising. Clear communication goals (awareness, changing a belief) instead give clear criteria for the bonus. In the chapter on measurement they add that the new models ask the agency to take on part of the risk and the reward, and a shared measurement strategy gives it evidence of its effectiveness.

*La boîte à outils de la publicité* asks the same question from the agency side (what exactly is strategic thinking and creativity worth?) and notes that tight budgets have reduced margins. Richards's history shows where the tension comes from: the media commission no longer had any connection with the amount of service provided, and the internet undermined its logic for good.

## Application

- As an account person or strategist: record every round of revisions and every change to the brief; they are real costs, not "small adjustments".
- When negotiating a contract, propose a bonus tied to measurable communication objectives written in the brief, not to sales.
- As a student: treat a vague brief as a business risk and ask for clarification before you start working.

## Example

A brief that says "we want to seem younger" produces four rounds of rejected routes; the same project, with the objective "a 10-point increase in the 'for people like me' association among 18–24-year-olds", closes in two rounds and also gives the criterion for the bonus.

## Questions

- Does payment for time reward efficiency or slowness?
- Who bears the risk when a good campaign does not sell because of distribution or price?
