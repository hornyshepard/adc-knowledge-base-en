# Personal selling has five steps, and the presentation starts by finding out where the customer is in the buying process

> **The idea in one sentence:** personal selling is two-way communication, not a monologue; the process has five steps, but each meeting has to match the stage the buyer is in.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy define personal selling as the two-way flow of communication between a buyer and a seller, designed to influence the buyer's purchase decision. Almost every profession uses it: the attorney in the book's example "sells" his ideas to clients, partners and juries. The process:

| Step | What it means |
|---|---|
| 1. Generate and qualify leads | you find prospects (existing customers, networks, events, social media) and check whether they are worth pursuing; in B2B a sales call costs a lot |
| 2. Preapproach | you research the customer, set the objective of the meeting (rarely a signature on the first visit) and rehearse through role playing |
| 3. Sales presentation and overcoming reservations | you start with questions to establish where the customer is in the buying process; you listen; you handle objections by asking questions, not by proving the person wrong |
| 4. Closing the sale | you obtain the commitment; in a relationship, a missed close prepares the next meeting |
| 5. Follow-up | reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy and tangibles; complaints are resolved quickly |

Step 3 connects to the six-stage B2B buying process: need recognition, product specification, the request for proposals (*RFP*), proposal analysis with vendor negotiation and selection, order specification, and vendor performance assessment. If the bank in the book's example is only just recognizing its need, comparing vendors is a mistake.

**Convergence and difference.** The guide attributed to Nishad reaches the same conclusion by another route: each stage sells only the next step. Grewal and Levy, however, keep the close as an explicit step and add the follow-up, that is, the relationship after the sale, which the B2B guide does not cover.

## Application

- For new business: if the prospect is at the need stage, bring a diagnosis of the category, not a campaign; if they are at the RFP stage, answer exactly the questions asked.
- Treat the follow-up as account management: the five dimensions of service quality also apply to an agency.

## Example

The book's example: the salesperson selling a bank a system that reduces checking account errors shows what the bank stands to gain (a 20 percent improvement, or $2 million in annual savings) against the cost ($150,000 per year); to the objection "it's expensive", she asks: "How much do you think the bank is losing through checking account errors?"
