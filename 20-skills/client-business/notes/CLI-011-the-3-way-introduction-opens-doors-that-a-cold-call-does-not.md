# The 3-way introduction opens doors that a cold call does not

> **The idea in one sentence:** to reach someone who is hard to reach, you do not contact them directly but through someone who knows them and who introduces you to each other in a single message addressed to both of you.

## Explanation

**Sphere of influence selling.** The guide starts from the story of Chris Gardner (*The Pursuit of Happyness*): the self-styled cold call king at Bear Stearns learns from his manager that the big players do not cold call but reach clients through people who know them. That is how Gardner reportedly got clients such as Quincy Jones. You do not land Michael Jordan through a cold call.

**The 3-way introduction** is the concrete tool. When someone tells you they can refer you to someone, you explicitly ask them to make a 3-way introduction. The message (email, text or LinkedIn message) has a simple structure:

1. recipients: both people; subject line: "Introduction: X and Y";
2. Y, I want to introduce you to X: who he is and why I am putting you in touch;
3. X, this is Y: who she is and why;
4. the last sentence: "I encourage the two of you to connect."

The author's story: one such introduction took him straight to the owner of a publishing company, who immediately sent him his calendar link; a referral from a former franchise owner got him hired at the company that had turned him down a few days earlier. The guide insists that it takes courage to ask for the favor, but the close ratio goes up massively.

## Application

- For a student: instead of sending the portfolio to an agency cold, look for a former teacher, a graduate or a collaborator who knows a creative director and ask them for a 3-way introduction.
- For an agency: after each successful project, ask the client who in their network might be interested in your work and ask for the introduction in the 3-way format.
- Make it easy for the person who recommends you: send them a two-line text about yourself that they can use.

## Example

"Ana, I want to introduce you to Matei, a junior copywriter who won the radio category at a student competition; I am putting you in touch because I know you are hiring. Matei, Ana is the creative director of agency X. I encourage the two of you to connect."

## Questions

- Does selling through relationships reproduce inequalities of access (those who have no network)? How do you compensate?
- What do you risk (the reputation of the person who recommends you) if you are not prepared for the meeting?
