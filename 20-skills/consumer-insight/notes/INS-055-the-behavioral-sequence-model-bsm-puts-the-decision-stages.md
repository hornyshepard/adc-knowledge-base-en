# The behavioral sequence model (BSM) puts the decision stages on a grid and, for each one, who, where, when and how

> **The idea in one sentence:** the BSM turns "understanding the consumer" into a working table that shows where, when and for whom communication can change the choice.

## Explanation

The behavioral sequence model (BSM) was introduced by Rossiter and Percy in 1987. Despite the word "behavioral" in its name, it is a cognitive model of the decision, more applied than the large models of the Engel or Howard–Sheth type. It is built in two steps:

1. **The decision stages**, adapted to the category. The generic model has four: need arousal, brand consideration, purchase, usage.
2. For each stage, four columns: **who** takes part and in which role, **where** the stage occurs, **when** it occurs (timing) and **how** it unfolds.

| Category | Stages (Table 7.2) |
|---|---|
| Laundry detergent | notice getting low → shop → select brand → purchase → use |
| Casual eating out | need arousal → decide what in mood for → decide on restaurant from that type → go to restaurant → eat |
| Holiday trip | need arousal → identify possible options → evaluate the options → decide on a destination → book the holiday → take the trip → post-holiday evaluation |
| Wholesale plumbing distributor | monitor manufacturer and wholesale competitors → identify items to evaluate → evaluate items → contact vendor → evaluate vendor → order and stock item → evaluate sales performance → reorder |

At low involvement, the consideration stage is short and the decision concentrates before the purchase, at the shelf and in use. At high involvement, consideration, purchase and usage get much more attention. The model can be built from what the team already knows. The gaps it reveals show what research needs to be done.

## Application

- Build the BSM before the media plan. The "where" column gives you the touchpoints directly, and the "when" column gives you the scheduling.
- Use the "how" column for the positioning insight. For holidays, proprietary research by one of the authors shows that other people are the main source of beliefs about destinations.
- Look for the stage where a message can change the outcome. It is not necessarily the first one.

## Example

For desserts, the BSM shows the critical stage: deciding to serve dessert happens *after* the meal, when Jell-O (gelatin crystals that must set in the refrigerator) is no longer an option. The Early Morning Reminder to Serve campaign placed messages in early morning in-home media: prepare Jell-O now, while the hot water is at hand for coffee. A cruise line, likewise, has to stimulate need arousal much earlier than the rest of holiday travel.
