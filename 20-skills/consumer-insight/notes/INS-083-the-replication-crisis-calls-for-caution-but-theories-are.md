# The replication crisis calls for caution, but theories are judged on research programs, not on isolated studies

> **The idea in one sentence:** many spectacular effects in psychology have not replicated, so a strategist should not build a campaign on a single study, but on theories supported by many studies.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe consider 2011 a watershed: the fraud of Diederik Stapel (more than 50 publications with fabricated data), the report of questionable research practices (selective reporting, hypothesizing after the results are known, or "harking", the arbitrary elimination of outliers) and improbable findings (Bem, "feeling the future"). Then came the failures to replicate: Doyen et al. (2012) did not reproduce the "elderly primes → slower walking" effect; the Open Science Collaboration (2015) replicated only 36% of 100 studies. Kahneman publicly warned researchers in social priming.

The authors' position (Stroebe is one of the participants in the debate) is nuanced:

- exact replication is impossible in social psychology, because manipulations derive their meaning from the cultural and historical context; what counts are **conceptual replications**;
- p-values have a sampling distribution; some original studies were "lucky", some replications "unlucky", especially with small samples;
- knowledge does not consist of single studies, but of **systematically tested theories** or meta-analyses;
- the meta-analysis of Weingarten et al. (2016) found real priming effects, but also severely underpowered studies (n ≈ 24 per cell, power ~.20).

The tension remains: other critics believe that part of the literature (including some studies cited in the book) should be treated with more suspicion than the authors do.

## Application

- When a business article cites "a study shows that…", ask: is it a single study? how many participants? has it been replicated?
- Prefer well-supported principles (dual process, the truth effect, spaced repetition) to spectacular, isolated ones.
- Test locally: an A/B test on your own target is worth more than a laboratory effect you read about.

## Example

The "elderly priming" effect: over 1,000 citations by 2012, then a failure to replicate and evidence of publication bias (Lakens, 2017).

## Questions

- Which advertising effects would you apply without a test, and which only after a test?
- Is the authors' critique of exact replications convincing or defensive?
