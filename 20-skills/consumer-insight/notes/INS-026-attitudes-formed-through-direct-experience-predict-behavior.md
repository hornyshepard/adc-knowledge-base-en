# Attitudes formed through direct experience predict behavior better than those formed from ads

> **The idea in one sentence:** trying the product creates attitudes that are more accessible, more stable and more predictive than a description of it does, provided the trial situation resembles the consumption situation.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe summarize the research program of Fazio and Zanna: attitudes based on **direct experience** contain more information, are held with more confidence, are more stable and more accessible and, above all, predict future behavior. In the study by Regan and Fazio (1977), declared interest in some puzzles predicted which puzzles were played much better among those who had tried them than among those who had only read their description.

Why? Fazio argues for accessibility, Doll and Ajzen (1992) for stability. The meta-analysis by Glasman and Albarracín (2006) shows that both contribute independently.

Two important limits:

- **The match of context.** A wine that was delicious on a lake terrace in sunny Italy does not taste the same on a cold winter's night in the Netherlands. Millar and Millar (1996): when participants were told the puzzles would assess their analytical ability (an instrumental orientation, not a hedonic one), the advantage of direct experience disappeared. Experience predicts behavior only if the motivational orientation during the trial is the same as at the moment of consumption.
- **The source can beat experience.** A good test drive can be cancelled by a negative report in a respected car journal; wine writers (Robert Parker) have a tremendous influence because they are seen as expert and trustworthy. Advertising starts at a disadvantage here: people distrust it.

## Application

- Combine advertising with direct-experience tools: free samples, test drives, free trial periods, demonstrations, "scratch-'n'-sniff" cards.
- Design the trial in the real consumption context (coffee in the morning, not at a mall stand at lunchtime).
- Keep the tone of the trial: for hedonic products, make the trial enjoyable, not a performance test.
- For credibility, let independent sources (reviews, experts) speak.

## Example

The test drive as a tool: the attitude formed behind the wheel predicts purchase better than the brochure, but a strong negative review can cancel it (p. 156).
