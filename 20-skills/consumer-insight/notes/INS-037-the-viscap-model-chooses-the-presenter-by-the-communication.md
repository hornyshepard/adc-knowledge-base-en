# The VisCAP model chooses the presenter by the communication objective (visibility, credibility, attractiveness, power), not by popularity

> **The idea in one sentence:** a celebrity brings awareness, a sincere expert brings acceptance, a likable person or one the target sees as similar brings emotion, and each characteristic serves a different objective and a different quadrant of the grid.

## Explanation

Psychologists have long studied how the person who presents a message influences how well it is received (the same person, in a white lab coat, is more convincing when talking about health problems). The first attempt to apply this work to advertising is the **VisCAP model** of Percy and Rossiter (1980). In the terms of Aristotle, cited by the authors through McGuire, VisCAP is the theory of *ethos*: persuasion through the source, not through the content.

| Characteristic | Components | The objective it serves |
|---|---|---|
| **Visibility** | how well known or recognizable the person or character is | brand awareness |
| **Credibility** | **expertise** (perceived knowledge) and **objectivity** (perceived sincerity or trustworthiness) | informational brand attitude: expertise at both levels of involvement, objectivity only at high involvement |
| **Attractiveness** | **likeability** and perceived **similarity** to the target | transformational brand attitude: likeability at low involvement, similarity (to whom the target *wants* to be) at high involvement |
| **Power** | perceived ability to instill compliance | brand purchase intention (rare: certain fear appeals, "nine out of ten doctors recommend") |

The logic is consistent with the grid. At low-involvement informational, the message does not need to be literally believed, so expertise is enough. At high involvement, it has to be accepted, so the presenter must also be sincere. With transformational strategies, the target has to feel, so the presenter must be liked or be a model to identify with.

Risks with celebrities: attention to the celebrity can overpower the brand (the visibility must be *transferred* to the brand), and the celebrity's popularity can change, especially with sports celebrities. Long-running cartoon characters can reach high visibility, always associated with the brand. The source can also be the medium: an ad for running shoes is received differently in Runner's World than in a general magazine.

## Application

- Before casting or choosing the influencer, write down the objective (awareness? acceptance? emotion?) and the matching VisCAP characteristic.
- For a high-involvement informational campaign, choose real, sincere experts, not celebrities.
- With a celebrity, check whether people remember the *brand*, not only the star.

## Example

Puma chose Rihanna (2015) as global ambassador for its women's products. She offered no athletic expertise, but attractiveness and aspirational similarity, exactly what a transformational strategy requires.

## Questions

- How does VisCAP apply to micro-influencers, who have low visibility but high similarity?
- Which VisCAP characteristic does an influencer lose when the audience discovers that a post was paid for and not disclosed?
