# Salience, vividness and novelty capture attention involuntarily, but salience disappears when everyone copies

> **The idea in one sentence:** focal attention is drawn by what stands out from its context, by what is concrete and emotionally interesting, and by what contradicts expectations, and the first of these is always relative.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe (following Kardes) describe three classes of stimulus properties that draw involuntary attention:

| Property | Definition | What the research shows |
|---|---|---|
| Salience | how noticeably different the stimulus is from its environment | depends entirely on context; a Harley Davidson is salient among cars, not amid other Harley Davidsons |
| Vividness | emotionally interesting, concrete and image-provoking, sensorially proximate (Nisbett & Ross) | inconsistent effects on persuasion; works for people with a strong tendency toward visual imagery |
| Novelty | unfamiliar information that disconfirms expectations | surprise mobilizes processing resources, the resolution of incongruity |

**Salience is a contrast effect.** The book's case: the Dutch insurer Centraal Beheer switched to humor ("even Apeldoorn bellen") in a serious category and got people talking. Competitors copied the style, insurance ads all became funny, and the salience effect disappeared (a bandwagon effect). Another device: an upward camera angle makes the product "bigger than life", but the effect appears mainly among people low in need for cognition.

**Vividness is a creative intuition stronger than the evidence.** Many creative directors swear by vivid ads, but research gives positive, null and even negative results. Fennis, Das and Fransen (2012) found an effect only among strong "visualizers" for functional products; for transformational products (champagne), vividness helped everyone. The medium matters: the anecdote works better face to face, the list of attributes in print.

## Application

- Before choosing the tone, audit the category: what does everyone do? Salience comes from departing from the norm.
- Watch for when competitors copy you: it is the signal that you need to change register.
- Use vividness deliberately: for experiential products it almost always works; for functional products, do not rely on it alone.

## Example

Centraal Beheer (the Netherlands): the humor created "rumour around the brand", although at first people did not correctly attribute the ads to the company.
