# Digital anthropology uncovers hidden anxieties through social listening, netnography and emphatic research

> **The idea in one sentence:** people do not tell you in a focus group what they really think, but they tell each other, in their communities; three research methods, from the most automated to the most immersive, capture these conversations.

## Explanation

Digital anthropology focuses on the nexus between humanity and digital technology: how people interact with digital interfaces, how they behave in the context of technologies and how they use technologies to interact with one another. For marketing, it uncovers the latent anxieties and desires that a human brand has to address. The book presents three methods:

| Method | What it involves | Advantage | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Social listening** | proactive monitoring of what is said about a brand on social media and in online communities, with software and big data | captures the real social dynamics that surveys and focus groups miss; also serves content-marketing evaluation, social selling, social CRM, competitive intelligence | unstructured data, read by an algorithm |
| **Netnography** (Robert Kozinets) | ethnography of online communities; the researcher becomes an active member, with permission and disclosure | empathy, understanding from the inside; done mostly in customer-run communities, not company-run ones | needs rare skills and personal reflection; cannot be automated |
| **Emphatic research** (popularized by IDEO and frog) | in-person participatory observation, multidisciplinary teams (psychologists, anthropologists, product designers, engineers, marketers), synthesis through brainstorming | the method closest to traditional ethnography; leads to new products, customer experiences or brand campaigns | expensive, slow |

Netnography often follows social listening, which identifies the right communities. Marketing 3.0 already described the hear, create and deliver process of IDEO's Human-Centered Design, with immersion in select communities to capture stories and metaphors.

**Convergence.** Batat ([[INS-088-experiential-ethnography-works-with-consumers-not-on-them|INS-088]]) describes the same Kozinets netnography, in four steps, and reaches the same conclusion: immersion in the community beats the direct question.

## Application

- Start an insight project with social listening (what is said, how much, with what sentiment), then choose one or two communities for netnography.
- Ask for permission and disclose your role in the community; covert research destroys trust.
- For a new product or service problem, organize a day of emphatic research in the field with a mixed team, and synthesize together.

## Example

Through emphatic research, MassMutual and IDEO discovered millennials' anxieties about becoming financially literate. The result was the Society of Grownups: classes and financial advice in a relaxed space that resembles a coffee shop, plus digital planning tools.

## Questions

- Social listening hears only what is written in public. Which groups stay invisible?
- Where is the ethical line between netnography and surveillance of a community?
