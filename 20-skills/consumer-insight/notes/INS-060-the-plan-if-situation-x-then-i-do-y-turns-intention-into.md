# The plan "if situation X, then I do Y" turns intention into action (implementation intentions)

> **The idea in one sentence:** people often fail to act because they forget to do it at the right moment; tying the action to a concrete context (when, where, how) makes the situation itself trigger the behavior.

## Explanation

A general intention ("I intend to do X") has the form of a goal; an **implementation intention** (Gollwitzer) has the form "I intend to do X in situation Y". Fennis and Stroebe show that the difference is large:

- Sheeran and Orbell (2000): women asked to indicate when, where and how they would make an appointment for a cervical smear test went to the test in 92% of cases, against 69%, although the strength of their stated intention did not differ.
- The meta-analysis of Gollwitzer and Sheeran (2006, 94 studies): an effect of medium-to-large magnitude (d = .65).
- The mechanism: the representation of the situation becomes highly accessible, and the link between situation and response gets stronger. Participants who had planned their route ("directly after the first swing door", "near the red fire hose") recognized these words faster and were more likely to collect the coupon.
- It also works for resisting temptation: "If I open the fridge I will think of dieting" produced weight loss in four weeks (Veling et al., 2014).

The problem for advertising: an ad that asks you to plan your purchase ("think about when you will go to the shop…") risks reactance. Tested solutions:

- Kardes et al. (2005): people who received a sample of household cleaning liquid and wrote down when and for what they would use it used it more often and in more situations.
- Fennis et al. (2011): a website about a pocket guide to fair trade products increased sustainable purchases only when the information about when, where and how to use the guide was told as a **vivid story** (a day in the life of Kirsten, a student, at the supermarket), not as a bulleted list.

## Application

- Show, do not ask: the story of a character who uses the product at a concrete moment creates the plan without reactance.
- In CRM and apps, help the user fix the moment ("Want a reminder on Saturday morning?").
- Tie the product to a cue that already exists in the target's routine (the morning coffee, the commute).

## Example

The fair trade guide from the Fennis et al. (2011) study: the same information, with an effect only in narrative form.
