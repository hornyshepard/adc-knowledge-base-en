# The focus group is good for language and for detecting disasters, not for the final decision

> **The idea in one sentence:** a few people in a room show you how consumers talk and what they do not understand, but they cannot tell you how many will buy.

## Explanation

In the era of big data, Struhl notes, many major decisions are still made on gut feeling or a few focus groups. The problem is **sampling error**. With 400 respondents, a result of 50% has a margin of about ±5 points. With 50 respondents, the margin is ±14 points: the real value could be anywhere between 36% and 64%. Below about 150 respondents, errors rise steeply. A focus group has 5–15 people; one or two groups gather between 5 and 30 or so people in total.

Focus groups still have real uses:

- you learn the **language** people use to talk about the product and the terms they understand;
- you find out whether your message is hopelessly confusing;
- you open up unexpected avenues for quantitative research;
- you detect an obvious **disaster**;
- you get a humbling lesson: people think about your product far less than you imagine.

Struhl's metaphor: small numbers give you a general view of the landscape you will cross; larger numbers give you the detailed directions. You need both.

**Convergence.** Four independent sources arrive here: Fennis and Stroebe (the focus group turns participants into "pseudo-experts", [[INS-081-the-effect-of-an-ad-is-proved-by-experiment-not-by-the|INS-081]]), Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott ([[ADV-079-the-pretest-is-built-on-the-communication-objectives-with|ADV-079]]), McTigue and Rucker (the meat packaging that was asked for in the group and rejected in the market, [[INS-093-an-insight-is-found-by-pulling-the-thread-of-a-surprising|INS-093]]) and Struhl.

## Application

- Use groups at the **start** of the process (language, hypotheses, creative territories), not as a final vote between concepts.
- In the brief, quote consumers' wording from the groups: it is their most valuable output.
- When a client wants to choose between campaigns on the basis of a group, propose a small quantitative test.

## Example

A manufacturer of men's jeans invested more than $800,000 (in current dollars) in a massive quantitative study of clothing needs, then decided to launch suits on the basis of a few focus groups asked "Would you buy these?". The suits were launched with national advertising and were an abject and expensive failure.

## Questions

- Why does group dynamics (the opinion leader in the room) make the small-sample problem worse?
- What questions do you ask in a focus group to get language, not "votes"?
