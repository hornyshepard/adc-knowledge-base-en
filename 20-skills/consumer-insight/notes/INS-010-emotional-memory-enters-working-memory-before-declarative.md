# Emotional memory enters working memory before declarative memory and frames processing, and emotion is passed on through authentic faces

> **The idea in one sentence:** before you "think" an ad, you have felt it; the emotion stored in the amygdala enters working memory first and colors everything that follows, which is why the faces, voices and smiles in an ad must be real.

## Explanation

**Memory.** What used to be called short-term memory is now called **working memory** (Baddeley & Hitch): the place where we hold information while we process it. Long-term memory has two forms:

- **declarative** (hippocampus): the facts (semantic memory) and events (episodic memory) we can consciously remember and put into words. Brand recall awareness and judgments about a brand extension rely on it.
- **non-declarative**: procedural, *priming*, emotional. It is not consciously accessible.

The general rule is that the two systems do not interact. **The exception is emotion**, the only non-declarative memory that interacts with declarative memory.

**The order.** Exposure to an ad activates both cognitive associations and the emotional memories linked to its elements. Emotional memories (amygdala-dependent) enter working memory **before** declarative ones (hippocampal-dependent) (Fig. 12.9). Eichenbaum's example: in a lift, a vaguely familiar perfume first gave him an innocent sense of happiness that took him back to high school. Only then did he remember his classmates and the name of the perfume, Shalimar. The limit: positive emotion cannot cancel a negative conscious evaluation. If something in the message is disliked, cognition has the last word.

**What we know about emotions:**

- There are six universal primary emotions (anger, fear, disgust, sadness, surprise, joy). Secondary emotions (guilt, pride, envy) are interpreted differently from one culture to another.
- Other people's emotions are perceived in the right hemisphere. The face that carries the key emotion should therefore preferably be placed on the **left** side of the page or screen.
- **Embodied emotion**: viewers take on the emotion from the posture, the tone of voice and above all the faces of the characters. The eyebrows signal negative emotions, the mouth positive ones.
- A real smile (a **Duchenne smile**) uses involuntary muscles. A forced one uses a different set of muscles and is not seen as real, even with experienced actors. This matters for the emotional authenticity that transformational strategies require.

## Application

- In casting and directing, look for real emotions (improvised situations, real people), not "advertising" smiles.
- Check the emotion of every key frame. Research on a washing powder campaign: the image of a child generated a 55% positive response and 50% buying interest, against 40% and 30% for a mother who was not even looking at the child.
- Use tests with open questions ("what came to mind?") to see which emotional memories the execution activates.

## Example

One of the authors' studies on Mexican food found chained associations: tacos, spicy, "yummy", then friends and family. Executions with friends eating together trigger this positive chain. Mexican cultural images are not part of the network and miss the emotion.

## Questions

- If emotion comes first, does it still make sense to test ads with rational questionnaires?
- What would a "left-side" ad for emotion look like in vertical mobile formats?
