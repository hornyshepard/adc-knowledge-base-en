# The insight checklist: eight checks before a "truth about the target" goes into the brief

> **The idea in one sentence:** an insight candidate that fails one of these checks is in fact a fact, an observation or a product benefit; better to leave the box blank than to fill it in to "check the box".

## Explanation

The books in the base define the insight in compatible ways, but each one stresses a different test. Put together, the tests give a checklist for the last stage of the work: after you have found the candidate ([[INS-093-an-insight-is-found-by-pulling-the-thread-of-a-surprising|INS-093]]) and before you write it into the brief.

| # | Check | Sign that it fails | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **It is about the target**, not about what the brand offers | the sentence starts with "we offer…"; the Grand Furniture ad lists low prices, high quality and extensive variety | McTigue & Rucker |
| 2 | **It is a "why"**, not only a "what" | it is a fact: "customers need to protect their data" (the first Stark version) | McTigue & Rucker |
| 3 | **It has a tension**: a desire or a frustration, and a brake | there is no "but"; the dilemma "I would like to eat organic, but it is expensive" has one | *La boîte à outils*; revised Stark |
| 4 | **It is true in behavior**, not only declared | it comes from a single focus group: customers asked for transparent packaging for meat, then did not buy it | McTigue & Rucker |
| 5 | **It is meaningful** and unused by the competition | it is a well-worn trope: "moms are busy" | McTigue & Rucker |
| 6 | **The target recognizes itself in it** | the light does not "come on", as in Bullmore's refrigerator | Braun & Landa |
| 7 | **The brand can play a credible role** in resolving it | the tension is real, but the product does not resolve it (Angear: the idea is born from the insight and resolves it) | Braun & Landa; *La boîte à outils* |
| 8 | **It helps the creative work and binds the target to the positioning** | it does not suggest what you would tell the target in ten seconds; Singleton O&M asks for a "helpful" insight | McTigue & Rucker; SRC-023 |

Two extra checks: is it still valid now (a fixed or a dynamic insight, in Landa's terms)? Does it meet a truth about the product and one about the category (the Euro RSCG triangulation)?

## Application

- Put every candidate through the table. If it fails 1–3, go back to research; if it fails 4, validate it in behavior ([[INS-078-asked-directly-what-matters-people-say-everything|INS-078]]); if it fails 7, change the brand's role or the insight.
- Write the final version in the target's voice, in one or two sentences, with the frustration and the brake in plain view.
- The brake in the insight becomes the barrier that the communication objective moves ([[BRF-007-the-objective-in-the-brief-is-a-communication-objective|BRF-007]]).

## Example

Luvs (P&G): with the second child, moms feel like experts, are less stressed about germs and more often choose the value-priced diapers. The insight passes the tests: it is about the target, it explains an observed behavior, nobody was using it, and Luvs could play the role without cannibalizing Pampers.

## Questions

- Does every strong insight have an explicit tension? Where is the "but" in "you are not you when you are hungry"?
- Who decides whether an insight passes test 6: the planner, the creative or the target?
