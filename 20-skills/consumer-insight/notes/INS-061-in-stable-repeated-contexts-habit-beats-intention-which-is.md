# In stable, repeated contexts habit beats intention, which is why habitual consumers can hardly be "stolen"

> **The idea in one sentence:** behavior performed often, regularly and in the same context becomes automatic, triggered by the situation and not by intention, and that makes it very hard to change even when the person wants to.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe define habits (Verplanken & Aarts) as learned sequences of acts that have become automatic responses to specific cues and are functional for obtaining certain goals. They form when behavior is performed **frequently, regularly and under stable conditions** (brushing your teeth, shopping at the same supermarket, the same brand of washing powder).

Evidence:

- Verplanken et al. (1998): among people with a strong habit of using the car, the stated intention barely predicted the choice of travel mode, recorded in a diary over three weeks.
- The meta-analysis of Ouellette and Wood (1998): for infrequent behaviors in unstable contexts, intention is the better predictor; for regular ones in stable contexts, **past behavior** is better.
- Aarts and Dijksterhuis (2000): in habitual bicycle users, activating a travel goal ("I am going to a lecture") automatically activated "bicycle".
- People with habits acquire less information and use less elaborate choice strategies (Verplanken et al., 1997).

Business consequences:

- The authors state that for habitually acquired products **no successful marketing strategies for brand switching have yet been documented**.
- **Cognitive lock-in**: once you have learned a store, a service or an interface, switching costs effort.
- That is why advertisers try to instill habits through **frequency marketing**, for example frequent-flyer programs.

## Application

- If you lead a habitual category, protect the context of the habit: packaging, shelf position, availability. Any change to these breaks the automatism.
- If you are a challenger, target the **moments of discontinuity** (moving house, a new job, a child, a product out of stock), when the habit is weakened and intention matters again.
- Design the product to create positive lock-in (familiarity, acquired skill).

## Example

Your usual supermarket: you know where everything is; moving to another one means searching, so you stay, even if the other one is cheaper (p. 288).

## Questions

- Which consumption habits did the pandemic break suddenly, and which brands profited?
- Is it ethical to design a product to create a habit?
