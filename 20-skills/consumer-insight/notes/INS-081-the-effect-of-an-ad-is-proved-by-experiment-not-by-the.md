# The effect of an ad is proved by experiment, not by the "before and after" survey

> **The idea in one sentence:** only the controlled manipulation of the ad, with people randomly assigned, lets you say that the ad, and not something else, produced the effect.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe are so convinced of this that their book contains no case studies at all: a case is a "one-shot" study that does not allow causal conclusions (Campbell & Stanley). Sales can rise for other reasons.

To infer causality, three conditions must be met: (1) the cause precedes the effect, (2) they vary together, (3) there is no other explanation. Correlation covers only the second condition. The real problem is the **third variable**: the advertising budget went up and so did sales, but it was also a hot summer.

The recommended toolkit:

| Concept | What it means | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| Experiment | you manipulate the independent variable and assign people at random | an ad with 2 vs. 5 arguments |
| Mediation | the process through which the effect occurs | the number of thoughts generated explains the effect of the arguments |
| Moderation | the condition under which the effect is stronger or reverses | arguments matter more under high involvement |

The authors explicitly criticize industry practice: concept tests with focus groups yield a false sense of confidence, because they turn participants into "pseudo experts"; the post-campaign survey cannot isolate the role of the campaign, and awards such as the Effies are based on this flawed model. The experimental pretest, as a "disaster check", is far more useful.

The underlying conclusion: advertising effects are **interactions** between situational variables (source, message, medium) and person variables (involvement, knowledge, need for cognition).

## Application

- When you want to find out what works, test two versions on randomly assigned groups (an online A/B test, a panel test).
- Formulate the hypothesis with a moderator: "for whom and when does it work?"
- Read festival case studies critically: what else happened in that period?

## Example

The soft drink manufacturer that raises its budget and sees higher sales cannot conclude that advertising was the cause, because of the weather (p. 18).

## Questions

- How could a real experiment be run for a national TV campaign (for example, test regions)?
- What does an advertising school lose if it drops case studies altogether, as the authors propose?
