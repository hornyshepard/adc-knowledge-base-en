# Brand attitude is the sum of a few salient beliefs multiplied by their value (the expectancy-value model)

> **The idea in one sentence:** for each attribute, what counts is how sure a person is that the brand has it and how much the person values the attribute, and only a few attributes are active in the mind at any given moment.

## Explanation

According to Fennis and Stroebe, expectancy-value models are the most widely accepted models of the relationship between beliefs and attitudes. Fishbein's formula (1963):

**A = Σ bᵢ × eᵢ**

where *b* is the belief (the subjective probability that the brand has attribute *i*) and *e* is the evaluation of the attribute. The book's example: we may be quite certain that the new Mini has great road holding, trendy looks and little baggage space, but much less certain about its acceleration. Little baggage space matters little to a bachelor and a lot to a family man.

Two important findings:

- Each attitude object is associated with **a very limited number of salient beliefs**, accessible at the time of judgment, which anticipates the attitude-as-construction view.
- Rosenberg (1960) proposed a consistency version: if you change the evaluation, the beliefs change too (not only the other way around). Studies on evaluative-cognitive consistency partly support him.

The authors add that not all attitudes have cognitive components: an attitude based on evaluative conditioning may be only a feeling, without any reason. The persuasion strategy has to target the predominant component of the attitude.

## Application

The model offers four clear levers for a message:

1. **Increase the certainty** of an existing belief (evidence, demonstrations).
2. **Increase the value** of an attribute you already have ("why baggage space matters").
3. **Add a new**, salient, positive **belief**.
4. **Lower the value** of an attribute on which you are weak, or remove it from the salient set.

In research: ask the target audience to list freely what comes to mind about the brand (the salient beliefs), then to rate the certainty and importance of each one.

## Example

Avis, "we're number two … so we try harder" (ch. 1), lowers the value of the "market leader" attribute and adds the belief "more effort for the customer".

## Questions

- When is the model too rational (impulse products, purely affective attitudes)?
- Which salient belief should be added to your favorite brand?
