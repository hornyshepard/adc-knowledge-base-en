# McDonald's and the worm rumor: denial strengthens the association, and the solution is to dilute it with other associations

> **The idea in one sentence:** when a rumor has linked the brand to something repulsive, the rational argument that it is not true reactivates the link; it is more effective to fill the brand's network with other associations.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe use the case from the study by Tybout, Calder and Sternthal (1981). A false rumor said that McDonald's used worms in its hamburgers. Consumers said they did not believe the rumor, yet sales plummeted by more than 30 per cent.

The company's first response was an appeal to reason: ads emphasizing "100 per cent pure beef", a letter from the secretary of agriculture posted in the restaurants, the argument that worm meat would be more expensive to process than ground beef. It did not work and, if anything, strengthened the rumor.

The explanation lies in the structure of memory: hearing the rumor, people formed an associative link between McDonald's and worms. Every time the brand name was activated, the concept of worms was activated too, whatever their belief that the rumor was false. The disconfirming belief did not serve as a basis for judgment; the association did. The denial repeats exactly that association.

The solution the researchers tested: customers filled in a survey about many features of the restaurants: the French fries, the milkshakes, the cleanliness, the friendliness of the personnel, the location. This forged new links in memory and weakened, in relative terms, the association with worms. Those who completed the survey were less likely to think of worms and evaluated McDonald's more favorably.

## Application

- In an image crisis, do not repeat the accusation in your communication ("We don't use X!"). Build competing associations that are positive and concrete.
- Move the conversation to attributes you control and that are easy to imagine.
- Test crisis messages on implicit measures too (what comes to people's minds at the brand name), not only on stated beliefs.

## Example

The case itself; in the same spirit, the Mercedes A-class has kept an association of instability since the moose test (ch. 1, p. 31), although the technical problem was solved.

## Questions

- When is an explicit denial still necessary (safety, legal issues)?
- How does the idea apply to fake news and to social media crises?
