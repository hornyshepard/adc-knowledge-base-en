# Every product satisfies functional and psychological needs, in different proportions, and a want is not satisfied by just any solution

> **The idea in one sentence:** functional needs pertain to the product's performance, psychological needs to the personal gratification it brings, and successful marketing finds the right balance for its target.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy separate two pairs of concepts:

**Need vs. want.** A need is the gap between the actual state and the desired state (you are hungry). A want is a good or service that is not necessarily needed but is desired (you want ice cream). The authors' example: "your desire for ice cream will never be satisfied by any type of salad", whatever the level of your hunger. A need accepts several solutions; a want demands its own.

**Functional vs. psychological needs.**

| Type | What it means | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| Functional | the performance of a product or service | BMW K1600: inline six-cylinder motor, aluminum frame; it outperforms both luxury touring bikes and sporty motorcycles |
| Psychological | the personal gratification associated with the product | Jimmy Choo shoes costing up to $4,695, seen on the red carpet: they do not protect the feet any better than a $50 pair |

The central idea: most products satisfy both, to different degrees. The BMW motorcycle sells on performance, but it comes in several colors for aesthetics. Jimmy Choo shoes sell on status, but they still protect the feet. The task of marketing is to find the balance that appeals to the target.

## Application

- In the brief, write the functional benefit and the psychological benefit separately. Then decide which one leads the message and which one is supporting proof.
- In categories where function is at parity (water, detergent), the win usually comes from the psychological need (identity, status, belonging).
- For luxury products, do not justify the price only through function. The customer is not buying the function.

## Example

The reusable bottles in chapter 1: the same function (having cold water with you), but Hydro Flask became an identity accessory for Generation Z, personalized with colors and stickers.

## Questions

- For a bank or an insurance company, what are the psychological needs? How would you put them into images?
- Is the need-want distinction useful for agencies, or only a difference of degree?
