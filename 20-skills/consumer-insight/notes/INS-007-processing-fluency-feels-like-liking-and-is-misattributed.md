# Processing fluency feels like liking and is misattributed to the brand

> **The idea in one sentence:** what is easy to process we like a little more, because we attribute the pleasant feeling of ease to the brand, not to the earlier exposure.

## Explanation

*Hedonic fluency* is the subjective ease with which a stimulus can be perceived and processed. It is experienced as a mildly positive emotion and is used as information: "I like it." Fennis and Stroebe distinguish:

- **perceptual fluency**: the ease of processing physical features such as modality, shape or brightness;
- **conceptual fluency**: the ease with which the meaning comes to mind;
- **goal fluency**: Labroo and Lee (2006) found that an ad is evaluated more favorably when it follows another ad that activates the same goal;
- **response fluency**: an explanation for the question-behaviour effect.

The main source of fluency is familiarity: a stimulus encountered before, even incidentally, is processed more easily the second time. The resulting affect is "misattributed" to the ad and the brand. An example from the book: songs with more repetitive lyrics had a higher chance to move up the Billboard Hot 100 (1958–2012).

An important limit (ch. 3): the mere exposure effect increases liking mainly for **unfamiliar** stimuli. For a brand that is already known, one more exposure of the logo adds no fluency; persuasive information is needed.

Related: the **question-behaviour effect**. Asking people whether they intend to do something increases the likelihood that they do it (voting, health behaviors, even risky behaviors among adolescents). Market research can therefore increase sales of the products it asks about.

## Application

- Build repeated distinctive codes (jingle, color, shape) for new brands: fluency works for you.
- Make the message easy to process: clear typography, a name that is easy to pronounce, rhythm.
- In surveys, be aware that a question about intention influences behavior.

## Example

A simple, repetitive jingle that "gets stuck in your head" also works through fluency, not only through memorization.

## Questions

- When does fluency turn into boredom (ad wear-out)?
- If people realize the source of the familiarity, the effect disappears. What does that mean for ads that are too insistent?
