# When the audience sees the intent to persuade, it may do exactly the opposite (persuasion knowledge and reactance)

> **The idea in one sentence:** perceiving the intent to persuade activates resistance, and reactance (the need to reassert one's freedom) can push consumers automatically in the direction opposite to the message.

## Explanation

**Persuasion knowledge** (Friestad & Wright) refers to the theories consumers have about the motives, strategies and tactics of marketers, plus their belief in their ability to resist them. Adults have it; children have very little. Fennis and Stroebe show that perceiving the intention to persuade increases resistance in two ways:

- **cognitive**: warned about the content, people prepare counterarguments; this works only if the subject is relevant and there is time;
- **motivational**: merely warning that a persuasion attempt is coming, without the content, produces **reactance** (Brehm): a motivational state that drives people to re-establish the threatened freedom. It is most likely when the source uses pressure tactics or has a vested interest (sales personnel).

Two striking studies:

- **Laran, Dalton and Andrade (2011):** after exposure to slogans praising saving ("Saving keeps you going"), participants decided to spend **more**; after spending slogans ("Luxury, you deserve it"), **less**. The effect disappeared and reversed in the intended direction when participants judged the slogans for creativity, not for persuasive intent.
- **Zemack-Rugar et al. (2017):** assertive ads ("Visit us", "Like us on Facebook") produce reactance only among consumers in a committed relationship with the brand, because they make them feel guilty if they do not comply.

Sources with an obvious interest (the BMW dealer on BMW vs. Mercedes) are believed less readily than those perceived as neutral.

## Application

- Avoid pressing imperatives with loyal customers; for them, an invitation works better than an order.
- A slogan that sounds like a moral lesson can produce the opposite effect. Test the reaction, not just the liking.
- Formats that divert attention from the persuasive intent (creativity, entertainment) reduce reactance, but they raise ethical questions.

## Example

The Laran et al. study: the same slogans had an opposite or a direct effect, depending on whether people were thinking about their intent or their creativity.

## Questions

- What does reactance mean for public health campaigns phrased as prohibitions?
- How should a call to action be phrased for a brand's loyal customers?
