# The shopping environment activates goals, norms and "mindsets" that guide choice without people realizing it

> **The idea in one sentence:** a smell, a piece of music, an image or an earlier task can activate a goal or a rule of behavior, and people act on it believing they choose freely.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe show that attitudes, norms and goals can be activated automatically by the environment (Bargh). Examples:

- **Goals.** The smell of an all-purpose cleaner, without participants being consciously aware of it, made more people list cleaning among their plans for the day (Holland et al., 2005). Exposure to a bottle of Gatorade (associated with endurance) extended holding a leg raised from 87 to 187 seconds (Friedman & Elliot, 2008). The logo of an insurance company, presented subliminally, increased the preference for domestic products (terror management).
- **Music.** North, Hargreaves and McKendrick (1999): on days with French music more French wine was sold, on days with German music more German wine.
- **Norms.** Pictures of a library made participants speak less loudly, but only those who expected to visit the library (Aarts & Dijksterhuis, 2003). Christmas music in shops reminds people of their "duty" to buy presents; the glass of prosecco offered in shops that sell fine clothes triggers the norm of reciprocity and impairs self-control.
- **Goal-related messages.** An ad for a drink described as the best thirst-quencher was more persuasive for thirsty people whose thirst had been primed (Strahan et al., 2002).
- **Mindsets.** After a series of choices between pairs (even of animals), people were more willing to buy something than to buy nothing (Xu & Wyer, 2008). After a political speech they agreed with, a neutral Toyota ad was rated better than after one they disagreed with (a bolstering mindset vs. a counterarguing one).
- **Impulse.** The chocolate at the checkout profits from moments of low control; impulse buying mostly involves cheap hedonic products.

The authors warn that many studies do not distinguish between activating a concept and activating a goal; unsatisfied goals grow stronger over time, concepts fade. Some results (the Apple logo that increases creativity) would probably not be replicated today.

## Application

- Think of the store (physical or online) as a system of cues: music, smell, the order of choices, the earlier context.
- Schedule ads in contexts that activate the goal the product serves (evenings for food delivery, after sport for isotonic drinks).
- Ethics: tactics that exploit low control (the checkout, alcohol) are manipulation; use them with judgment.

## Example

The French and German wine study (p. 282): customers did not mention the music as the reason for their choice.
