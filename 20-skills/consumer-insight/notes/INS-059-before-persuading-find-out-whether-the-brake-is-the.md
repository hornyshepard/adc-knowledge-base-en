# Before persuading, find out whether the brake is the attitude, the subjective norm or perceived control (theory of planned behavior)

> **The idea in one sentence:** specific behavior is predicted by intention, and intention by three things: what people believe about the consequences, what the people who matter to them think and how capable they feel; the message has to hit exactly the component that blocks.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe start from the crisis of the 1960s: general attitudes (towards a group, towards religion) hardly predict single behaviors at all (Wicker: correlations "rarely above .30"). The solution of Ajzen and Fishbein (1977) is the **principle of compatibility**: an attitude predicts behavior only at the same level of specificity (action, target, context, time). Kraus's meta-analysis (1995): r = .13 at the lowest level of compatibility, .54 when compatibility was high. For advertising: measure the attitude towards *buying product X this month*, not towards the brand in general.

The **theory of planned behavior** (Ajzen):

- **attitude towards the behavior**: expected consequences × their evaluation;
- **subjective norm**: what reference persons expect × motivation to comply;
- **perceived behavioral control**: resources, skills, obstacles.

The meta-analyses: the three account for about 40% of the variance in intentions; intention accounts for 20–28% of the variance in behavior. Adding **identity similarity** (how much the person resembles the typical user of the brand) raises the prediction of intention by 5–12% (mobile phones, Invicta backpacks, Swatch watches, beer). The **theory of reasoned goal pursuit** (Ajzen & Kruglanski, 2019) adds that the goal active at that moment changes attitudes: the diet "wins" at the office, the enjoyment of eating in front of a bakery window displaying cakes.

The practical implication the authors stress: identify the **beliefs that differ** between those who buy and those who do not. It is pointless to praise the qualities of the product if people do not buy it because their family does not want them to or because they cannot afford it.

## Application

- Run the research in three columns: beliefs about consequences, about what others say, about obstacles. Compare buyers with non-buyers.
- Build the message on the belief that makes the difference, not on the one that is easiest to communicate.
- For identity products, show a typical user the target recognizes themselves in.

## Example

Seafood: despite its health advantage, it is often not bought because the family dislikes it (norm) and the person who cooks does not know how to prepare it (control), not because of the attitude. And the customers of an expensive phone company do not switch because they fear the effort and a disruption of service; a better deal does not solve that (pp. 273–274).

## Questions

- Which component blocks, in your case, the purchase of an electric bike?
- Where does the model not apply (habitual, impulsive behaviors)?
