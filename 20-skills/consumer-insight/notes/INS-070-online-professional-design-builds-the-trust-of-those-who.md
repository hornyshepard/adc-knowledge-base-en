# Online, professional design builds the trust of those who seek gains, while safety cues build only the trust of those who avoid losses

> **The idea in one sentence:** trust is the precondition of buying online, and what builds it depends on a person's regulatory focus: visible competence for the promotion-focused, guarantees for the prevention-focused.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe define trust, after Kim et al. (2004), as "a willingness to make oneself vulnerable to another in the presence of risk". Online it is harder to earn: you pay before delivery, sellers are often unknown, social presence is low, and delays in responding are read negatively.

Trust has three dimensions: beliefs about **ability** (the company can deliver), **benevolence** (it cares about the customer) and **integrity** (it follows rules). The findings:

- **Schlosser, Barnett White and Lloyd (2006):** a furniture website with a professional design (an advanced zoom option, elegant fonts, a white background) increased ability beliefs, and these drove purchase intentions. Security and privacy cues increased perceived benevolence and integrity, but not intention. The effect of design appeared mostly for risky purchases and for people searching for something specific, not for those who were only browsing.
- **Van Noort, Kerkhof and Fennis (2008):** safety cues (warranties, terms and conditions, customer reviews, a money-back guarantee) improved attitudes and reduced perceived risk **only** for people in a **prevention focus** (Higgins), not for those in a **promotion focus**.
- **Influencers:** although the audience knows they are paid, trust in them depends on the informational value of what they communicate, their general trustworthiness, their similarity to the follower and their attractiveness (Lou & Yuan, 2019), and it spills over to brand awareness and purchase intentions.

## Application

- For most online stores (purchases "for gain"), invest in the quality of the visuals and the experience: design is an argument for competence.
- For prevention categories (insurance, medicines, financial services), put the guarantees, the return policy and the reviews in plain view.
- If you do not know the audience's focus, play it safe: show both competence and safety.

## Example

The Manhattan furniture website (Schlosser et al., 2006): the same content, more trust through the quality of the design alone.

## Questions

- What regulatory focus does the customer of a holiday booking site have? And that of a banking app?
- Why does trust in influencers not drop, although everyone knows they are paid?
