# Brand, country of origin and price work as heuristics that change the consumption experience itself

> **The idea in one sentence:** when quality is hard to judge, consumers infer it from simple cues (brand, country, price), and these expectations change the taste and the perceived efficacy of the product.

## Explanation

Heuristics are learned "if-then" relationships. Fennis and Stroebe describe three, used mainly when quality is ambiguous:

**The brand heuristic.** Brand images are the consumer equivalent of stereotypes; their effect on judgment is what we call *brand equity*. Allison and Uhl (1964): beer drinkers could not distinguish five brands in blind tasting, but gave different ratings when the bottles were labeled, and all the beers tasted better with a label. The same newspaper article was rated better when it was attributed to the *Neue Zürcher Zeitung* than to the tabloid *Blick*.

**The country-of-origin heuristic.** The meta-analysis by Verlegh and Steenkamp (1999, 41 studies): a moderate to strong effect (r = .39), strongest on perceived quality, smallest on purchase intention. An ironic piece of history: the "Made in Germany" label was introduced in Britain in 1897 to mark foreign products, but it soon became associated with quality. Cheese "from France" tasted better than the same cheese "from Kansas".

**The price-quality heuristic.** Plassmann et al. (2008): the same wine was enjoyed more at $45 than at $5, and the brain centers associated with pleasure were more strongly activated: a self-fulfilling prophecy. Shiv, Carmon and Ariely (2005): an energy drink bought at a discount led to fewer puzzles solved (a placebo effect of price). Cronley et al. (2005): with a lot of information and time pressure, the perceived price-quality correlation (.83) far exceeded the real one (.27). For services, duration plays a similar role.

## Application

- Price cuts also communicate low quality; use them carefully for products where perceived efficacy matters.
- Country of origin is an asset: use it when the category stereotype helps you (Italian pasta, French cheese, German engineering).
- The packaging and the label are part of the product: they literally change the experience.

## Example

Bertolli uses "Italian mamas" who prepare the sauce in the Italian countryside (p. 158), even though the brand has become the property of international companies.

## Questions

- When does the heuristic become a trap (the blocking effect: consumers ignore more diagnostic cues)?
- What "country of origin" is associated with Romanian products, and how could it be used?
