# An insight is a meaningful human truth and has to pass three tests: meaningful, human, true

> **The idea in one sentence:** an insight is not a fact about the product but a truth about the target that can make it believe or do something different; if you do not have one, leave the box blank.

## Explanation

McTigue and Rucker define the insight as a meaningful human truth and test each word:

| Test | What it requires | Counterexample from the book |
|---|---|---|
| Meaningful | it has value that can change a belief or a behavior, and it is not the same knowledge that all competitors already use | the embossed floor mats in a sports car: pleasant, but with no power to change anything |
| Human | it is about the target, not about what the brand offers | the sales associate who describes the lightweight aluminum frame, while the customer only asks whether they have red bicycles |
| True | it is an accurate observation, checked in the market | transparent packaging for meat: the focus group asked for it, buyers rejected it |

The insight is **the glue that binds the target and the position**. It does not have to be an unheard-of discovery: Axe used one of the oldest human truths (attracting a mate), but it was the first to leverage it in the category. Nor is it an exercise in style: the creative brief never reaches the consumer, so clarity matters more than wording. And it is not universal: it is true for the chosen target.

Braun and Landa see the insight as a revelation or realization about the audience's need or belief, or the true nature of how they think, feel or behave, a truth no one has yet noticed, which becomes the catalyst for the idea. They cite Jeremy Bullmore's image: a good insight is like a refrigerator: as soon as you look into it, the light comes on. Bridget Angear (AMV BBDO) adds that the idea is born from the insight and resolves it, but does not have to show it. The two books meet in the middle: the novelty lies in the use, not necessarily in the discovery.

## Application

- Put every candidate through the three tests; if it fails one, it is not an insight.
- Rephrase the insight from the target's point of view ("I…"), not the brand's ("we offer…").
- Distinguish fixed insights from dynamic ones (Landa): some stay valid for years, others change suddenly, as happened in the pandemic.

## Example

Snickers, "you are not you when you are hungry": a truth everyone lives, a good fit with the brand's calories and history. IKEA's "Lamp" (Spike Jonze) brings to light the sentimental attachment that makes us keep old furniture.

## Questions

- "Moms are busy" is true and human. Why is it no longer an insight?
- Do insights apply in B2B too? (Yes: retailers stressed by Black Friday, ch. 4.)
