# Youth, women and netizens (YWN) are the most influential subcultures of the digital era

> **The idea in one sentence:** if you want advocacy, place your bets on youth (who win the market's mind share), women (who win market share) and netizens (who win its heart share), because these former "peripheries" now influence the mainstream.

## Explanation

When it comes to recommendations, not all customers are created equal. In the past, authority belonged to seniors, men and offline "citizens". Today the subcultures of youth, women and netizens (YWN) set the tone, because they have larger networks.

| Segment | Roles | What the brand wins |
|---|---|---|
| **Youth** | early adopters, trendsetters (fragmented into "tribes"), game changers (youth empowerment movements such as RockCorps, WE.org, Indonesia Mengajar) | ***mind share***: if you convince them, the mind of the mainstream follows |
| **Women** | information collectors (their path to purchase is a spiral, not a line, according to Martha Barletta), holistic shoppers, household managers (the "chief financial officer" of the family) | ***market share***: they go through the most comprehensive process, so they are more loyal and recommend |
| **Netizens** | social connectors, expressive evangelists, content contributors | ***heart share***: the best amplifiers of word of mouth |

Netizens are not all internet users. According to Forrester's Social Technographics segmentation, the hierarchy runs from inactives, spectators and joiners to collectors, critics and creators; only the last three contribute actively. The figures cited: in 41% of U.S. households women call the shots (Pew, 2008); in Indonesia, 74% of women manage all the family finances, although only 51% of them work (MarkPlus, 2015).

*Marketing 3.0* had already identified women and youth (along with the elderly) as key audiences for social transformation. *Marketing 4.0* keeps the first two, adds netizens and makes them the engine of advocacy.

## Application

- New launch: a youth-first strategy for adoption and trend (the iPod, Netflix streaming), but choose the right tribe.
- Family categories: design for women's spiral path, with complete information, comparisons and points to come back to.
- Community: identify the critics and creators in your niche. They are your earned media.

## Example

Justin Bieber started as a trending YouTube artist followed by millions of youth before reaching the mainstream. Spotify and Apple Music were also brought to the mainstream market by young customers.

## Questions

- The description of women (better suited to multitasking, while "men just want to get things done") risks stereotypes. What data would you use instead?
- Is "netizen" still a useful category when everyone is online? *Marketing 5.0* moves to segmentation by generation.
