# Anchoring and recency: under time pressure, the fast brain answers with the last thing it saw, and price is read through references

> **The idea in one sentence:** when there is no time to think, people use the last number or brand they came across as a reference point, even if they did not consciously notice it; advertising and pricing exploit this shortcut.

## Explanation

**Milavsky's experiment.** A group of mature students claimed they could not be influenced by advertising. Without comment, the teacher wrote a "19" in a corner of the blackboard. Half an hour into the class, Milavsky asked each of them to write down immediately, with no time to think, how many countries there are in Africa. The answers clustered from 18 to 21, with a single outlier. The correct answer is 54. Almost nobody had "seen" the number on the blackboard. His conclusion: "It isn't subliminal; it is because the fast brain is lazy." Under time pressure, the response is what you most recently saw or heard (**recency bias**); hence the role of repetition for the brand chosen at the shelf.

**Price as the fast brain sees it** (ch. 6):

- **99**: the price is associated with the number in front of it, not with the closer value;
- **price per month or per week**: the fast brain manages its cash flow based on paychecks and defers the total cost to the "future self";
- **anchor pricing**: a reference price (the old price crossed out, the previous model) makes the discount visible, without anyone checking whether the anchor is accurate;
- **immediate gratification**: Walter Mischel's marshmallow test shows how hard it is to give up a reward now for a bigger one later. That is why sweets sit at the checkout.

Milavsky also warns about price as a promise: it is the weakest one, because it can be matched quickly, and discounts work only if there is a plan for returning to the normal price.

## Application

- When you communicate a price, think about which reference people will use. If you do not give it to them, they will take it from the competitor or from memory.
- For discounts, show the anchor; for expensive products, show the monthly cost, but the total price must be stated clearly later, in the paperwork.
- Repetition close to the moment of purchase (radio in the morning, digital on shopping day) uses recency bias.

## Example

The students who denied any influence discovered, as they counted countries on their fingers, that they had not thought about Africa at all: they had simply written down the last number they had in front of them.

## Questions

- Where is the ethical line between using an anchor and misleading people ("crossed-out" prices that never existed)?
- Should legislation ban showing only the monthly payment in car ads?
