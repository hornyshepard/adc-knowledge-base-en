# You can persuade by increasing the attractiveness of the offer (alpha strategies) or by reducing resistance to it (omega strategies)

> **The idea in one sentence:** all persuasion works on one of two levers (it either pushes toward the advocated position or weakens what pulls back), and the second is often more effective and more neglected.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe take up the distinction of Knowles and Linn (2004):

| Type | What it does | Examples of tactics |
|---|---|---|
| **Alpha** | increases approach motivation, the tendency to move toward the advocated position | strong, compelling arguments, incentives and inducements, scarcity (available only for a limited period) |
| **Omega** | reduces avoidance motivation, the tendency to move away from the position | directly counterarguing concerns, distraction, reframing the message so that it does not appear to be a persuasive attack, negative emotions (fear, guilt) |

Chapter 5 details the strategies advertisers use to reduce the resistance that persuasion knowledge triggers: **two-sided messages** (admitting a minor flaw), **product placement**, **native advertising** and **sponsorship**. All of them try to blur the perception of persuasive intent. Chapter 7 adds influence techniques that work without attitude change (for example, the disrupt-then-reframe technique).

The limit the authors note: omega does not guarantee success. The McDonald's case shows that directly counterarguing an objection (the worm rumor) can strengthen the problem, and product placement that is too visible reactivates resistance.

**Convergence.** The same axis appears in Joannis ([[ADV-040-joannis-s-psychological-axis-the-campaign-chooses-either-to|ADV-040]]) and in *La boîte à outils* ([[INS-094-an-insight-is-a-consumer-dilemma-desire-versus-obstacle|INS-094]]): motivations versus brakes. Fennis and Stroebe add the evidence that the brake is often the neglected lever.

## Application

- In the brief, separate explicitly: what is the reason to buy (alpha) and what is the main brake (omega)? Often the brake is the real insight.
- When the target already knows the benefits and still does not buy, stop adding arguments: work on the brake (risk, effort, shame, distrust).
- Combine: an attractive offer plus the removal of an objection (guarantee, free trial).

## Example

Avis, "we're number two, so we try harder": admitting the weakness (omega: it reduces suspicion) turned into a benefit (alpha).

## Questions

- What is the main brake for the target of a gym membership? What would a purely omega campaign look like?
- When do omega tactics (distraction, confusion) become manipulation?
