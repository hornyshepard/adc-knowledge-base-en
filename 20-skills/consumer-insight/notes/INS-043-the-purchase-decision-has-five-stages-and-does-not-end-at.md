# The purchase decision has five stages and does not end at the checkout: satisfaction, dissonance and loyalty come after

> **The idea in one sentence:** consumers move from need recognition to information search, alternative evaluation, purchase and postpurchase, and each stage is a different opportunity for communication.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy present the consumer decision process (Exhibit 6.1) through the case of Lauren, who is looking for an outfit for a job interview:

| Stage | What happens | The marketing lever |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Need recognition | a gap appears between the actual state and the desired state; the greater it is, the stronger the need | advertising can make the gap visible |
| 2. Information search | internal (memory, experiences) and external (friends, reviews, Google, Instagram) | presence where people search; the intensity of the search grows with perceived risk |
| 3. Alternative evaluation | the consumer narrows the options (the evoked set) and compares attributes | getting into the evoked set, the determinant attribute |
| 4. Purchase and consumption | intention does not automatically become a purchase (out of stock, abandoned cart) | the conversion rate; Best Buy: prices matched with Amazon, in-home advisers |
| 5. Postpurchase | satisfaction, cognitive dissonance, loyalty or disloyalty | reassurance, feedback, relationship |

**After the purchase** there are three possible outcomes (Exhibit 6.3):

- **Customer satisfaction** depends on expectations. Advertising that promises too much increases initial sales but produces dissatisfaction. Advertising that promises too little does not bring the customer in. The authors' recommendations: realistic expectations, demonstrating correct product use, guarantees, encouraging feedback, periodic thanks.
- **Postpurchase cognitive dissonance** (buyer's remorse) appears with products that are expensive, infrequently purchased and risky. General Electric sends letters that reaffirm the quality of the appliance bought. Consumers reduce dissonance by returning the product, seeking positive information or favorable opinions.
- **Customer loyalty** or, in the opposite direction, negative word of mouth, amplified by social media. Whirlpool keeps negative comments on its pages, to show how it solves problems.

Policarpio (SRC-002) describes the same five stages and adds that need recognition can be triggered by internal stimuli (hunger) or external ones (an advertisement). Hence the role of advertising: the brand must come to mind at the moment of need.

## Application

- Build the decision map for your target: at which stage do people get stuck? The brief must name the stage the campaign addresses.
- Do not neglect stage 5. Postpurchase communication (onboarding, thanks, reassurance) is cheap and increases loyalty.
- For large purchases, prepare content that reduces dissonance: reviews, explanations, community.

## Example

Lauren buys the outfit at Macy's. Her boyfriend loves it, her girlfriends seem less impressed, so dissonance appears. To reduce it, she looks up articles about the designer and positive opinions. An attentive brand would have offered her exactly this content.

## Questions

- In impulse or habitual purchases, which stages disappear?
- The model is linear. What does the decision look like in a TikTok feed, where discovery, evaluation and purchase happen within 30 seconds?
