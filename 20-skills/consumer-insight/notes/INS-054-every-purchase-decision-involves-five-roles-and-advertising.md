# Every purchase decision involves five roles, and advertising speaks to an individual in a role, not to a group

> **The idea in one sentence:** even a toothpaste can involve several people; the strategist has to know who initiates, who influences, who decides, who pays and who uses, and write for that role.

## Explanation

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott take five **decision roles** from consumer behavior:

| Role | What it does |
|---|---|
| Initiator | suggests the purchase or use |
| Influencer | recommends or discourages |
| Decider | makes the actual choice |
| Purchaser | makes the purchase |
| User | consumes the product or service |

One person can play all the roles or only some of them, and the roles change from one stage of the decision to the next. The authors' thesis is firm: there is much talk about group decisions (in the family, in the company), but "Advertising and other forms of marketing communication do not influence groups." They influence individuals, in the role each plays in the group. That is why the message addresses the role: to the initiator you talk about the need, to the decider about the decisive benefit, to the purchaser about where and how to find the product.

The roles often include people outside the consumer: the travel agent, the salesperson, the retailer or the distributor. In B2B or with high-ticket goods, the trade is often the initiator or influencer. That is why communication to the trade must keep the same creative umbrella as communication to the consumer.

## Application

- For each stage of the decision, note who is involved and in which role, then decide whether you need different messages.
- In advertising to children (toys, cereal), the child is the initiator and influencer, while the parent is the decider and purchaser. The purchase intention generated in the child is in fact a recommendation.
- In B2B, do not write a single message for the company. Write for the specialist who evaluates, for the manager who approves and for the buyer who negotiates.

## Example

For a holiday trip, the initiators can be the traveler, the family, friends just back from a trip or the travel agent. The same people become influencers when destinations are evaluated. The traveler and their companion decide, they or the agent book, and everyone who goes is a user and, on their return, an influencer for others.
