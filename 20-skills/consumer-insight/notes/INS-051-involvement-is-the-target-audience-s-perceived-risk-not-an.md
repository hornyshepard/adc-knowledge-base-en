# Involvement is the target audience's perceived risk, not an attribute of the product: at low involvement curiosity is enough, at high involvement the message must be believed

> **The idea in one sentence:** the question is not "what product are we selling?" but "how much does our target risk, financially or psychologically, by choosing wrongly?"; the answer changes what the message has to achieve.

## Explanation

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott define **involvement** through the **perceived risk** of the decision, which can be **financial** or **psychological**. The risk belongs to the target audience. A new car is a risky decision for most people, but not for a rock star who buys a Ferrari and trades it in after a month if they do not like it. Psychological risk varies even more: the absolutely required dress for one group of teenagers is something another group would not be seen wearing.

Involvement changes the processing requirement:

| | Low involvement | High involvement |
|---|---|---|
| What the message has to achieve | attention and something positive learned. Curious disbelief (Maloney) is enough. | the message must be **accepted as true** before the brand is considered |
| Decision model | Awareness–Trial–Reinforcement (Ehrenberg): awareness → tentatively favorable attitude → trial → attitude reinforced by experience | hierarchy of effects: awareness → positive attitude formed → trial |
| Example | a new chocolate bar: you try it on a mere impression | a new personal computer: you want to know a lot first |

The authors also mention the ELM (Petty & Cacioppo). The central route requires comprehension and acceptance of the content. The peripheral route links cues from the execution (the presenter, the actors, humor, limbic reactions) to the brand without thinking about benefits. The practical conclusion is the same: at low risk, the execution can be enough to prompt trial, while at high risk the argument has to convince.

**Convergence, and a partly unfair criticism.** Fennis and Stroebe (outcome-relevant involvement, [[INS-002-processing-an-ad-goes-through-four-stages-from-preattentive|INS-002]]) and Grewal and Levy (involvement differs from person to person, [[INS-053-involvement-and-perceived-brand-differences-set-the-type-of|INS-053]]) say the same thing: involvement belongs to the person and the moment. Their criticism of the grids ([[ADV-039-the-fcb-grid-vaughn-orders-think-feel-do-by-involvement-and|ADV-039]]) mostly hits their use by category; Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott explicitly define involvement through the specific target audience.

## Application

- Assess the risk for the specific target through research, not by category. The same product can be low involvement for one segment and high for another.
- At low involvement, do not overload the ad with proof: the objective is to arouse curiosity for a trial.
- At high involvement, check acceptance and credibility in the pretest, not only liking.

## Example

Psychological risk makes a pair of 100-euro sneakers high involvement for a status-conscious teenager. The same pair is low involvement for an adult who only wants something comfortable to wear around the house.

## Questions

- Why is advertising credibility generally higher for high-involvement strategies, as the authors note in ch. 1?
- How does perceived risk change when there is a free 30-day return?
