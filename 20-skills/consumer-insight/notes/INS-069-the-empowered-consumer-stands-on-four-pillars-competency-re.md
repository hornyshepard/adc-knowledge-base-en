# The empowered consumer stands on four pillars: competency, re(creation), consciousness of rights, resistance

> **The idea in one sentence:** digital technology has shifted power from companies to consumers, and Batat describes this power through four capabilities that brands have to recognize and respect.

## Explanation

Hunter and Garnefeld (2008) define consumer empowerment as the consumer's subjective experience of having a greater ability than before to produce desired outcomes and prevent undesired ones. Denegri-Knott, Zwick and Schroeder (2006) distinguish three approaches: consumer sovereignty, cultural power (the market as a struggle between buyer and seller) and discursive power (the consumer helps determine what "can be known" about a brand).

The **four pillars** proposed by Batat:
1. **Consumer competency**: cognitive, functional, social; it is a social construction. Companies gain an advantage when they transform consumer skills into innovation (the "operant assets" of Vargo and Lusch).
2. **Consumer re(creation)**: through **re-appropriation** (consumers combine resources and divert objects from their original use, by bricolage, after Lévi-Strauss) and through **transgression** (breaking rules as part of building an identity; some consumers see illegal downloading as a skill).
3. **Consciousness of consumer rights**: understanding prices and the marketplace, knowing one's rights and the organizations that defend them, the ability to complain.
4. **Consumer resistance**: consumers decode advertising messages and gather information beforehand to resist pressure from salespeople; even vulnerable situations can generate coping mechanisms.

The conclusion for brands: empowered consumers need to be stimulated, nurtured, informed, respected and valued. Brand success does not depend only on a good idea of products or a good marketing strategy, but also on a good connection with the customer.

## Application

- In insight work, look for how people "divert" their products: re-appropriation reveals unmet needs.
- For a young audience, transgression is a value: communication can play on it without losing its sense of responsibility.
- Treat skepticism towards advertising as a given, not as an obstacle: give verifiable information.

## Example

Virgin Radio, "Louder is Better" (France, 2011): a teenager turns up the music in the car to drown out his mother yelling at him; it looks as if she is lip-synching to the Black Eyed Peas. Transgression, a key value for the 15–35-year-old audience, becomes humor. Bank of America and Khan Academy ("Better Money Habits") illustrate the other pole: the brand that educates the customer.

## Questions

- Is the "empowered consumer" a reality for everyone, or only for those who are digital, educated and self-confident?
- When does valuing transgression become an ethical problem for a brand?
