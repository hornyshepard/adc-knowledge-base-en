# Semiotics decodes the signs of a category to find the cultural meaning that consumers cannot put into words

> **The idea in one sentence:** experience acquires meaning through symbols, and semiotics, the study of signs within their cultural context, reveals the tacit motivations that a questionnaire cannot obtain.

## Explanation

For Batat, the meaning the customer gives to an experience is an idiocentric driver: an experience becomes memorable (and repeatable) only if it has a meaning. Symbolic consumption has been studied since the 1980s from two perspectives, identity and meaning construction (Levy, McCracken). A wedding ring is not only gold of a certain carat but love, commitment and faithfulness.

The basic mechanism is the relation between **signifier** and **signified**: the color green in an ad for an eco-friendly product (the signifier) evokes nature, renewal, freshness, harmony (the signified). Meaning is not fixed at exposure alone; it keeps being built in conversations among consumers, who create a collective meaning.

The fundamentals of semiotics, as Batat sums them up:
- the relationship between symbol, object and individual;
- symbols become meaningful in discourse;
- every symbol has one or more meanings;
- meanings are socially and culturally embedded;
- signs have a **denotative** meaning (literal) and a **connotative** one (positive or negative associations);
- signs can be discursive (verbal) or non-discursive (emotion, art, music, dance).

The difference from conventional research: conventional research asks people what they think; semiotics studies consumption culture to find the tacit motivations behind the answers.

## Application

- Make an inventory of the category's codes (colors, gestures, settings, words) and separate the dominant codes from the emerging ones.
- In the brief to the art director, name explicitly the signifiers to avoid and the ones to take on.
- Check the connotations in each target culture: a sign that is positive in one market can be negative in another.

## Example

Nintendo Wii: video games were associated with isolation, "geek" culture and inactivity. The semiotic analysis showed the importance of interaction and socialization, and the Wii redefined gaming as a family activity. It succeeded even though its graphics were weaker than the competition's. KFC in Japan (since 1974) gave fried chicken the meaning of a Christmas meal in a country where about 1% of the population is Christian; the meal is ordered months in advance.

## Questions

- How do you keep semiotic analysis from becoming the subjective interpretation of a single researcher?
- Can a brand change the meaning of a category on purpose, as Nintendo did, or only ride a change that is already under way?
