# Consumer emotions are primary or secondary and fluctuate throughout the experience

> **The idea in one sentence:** emotion is a curve with positive and negative peaks rather than a single reaction at the end, built from universal elementary emotions and from more complex mixtures shaped by each person's culture and history.

## Explanation

Batat starts from Kotler, Kartajaya and Setiawan (2010): "emotion leads to actions while reason leads to conclusions"; the decision to buy and to stay loyal is strongly influenced by emotions. Paul and Anne Kleinginna (1981) found more than 90 definitions of emotion. Their synthesis: emotions result from the interaction between subjective and objective factors, attained by neural systems, and they induce experiences (feelings of awakening, pleasure, displeasure) that can lead to adaptive behaviors. Hence three characteristics: physiological, behavioral, and the emotion/rationality dyad.

**Elementary (primary) emotions** are universal and can be recognized on the face in any culture: joy (the wish for closeness, expectations met), sadness (loss), disgust (rejection, protection), anger (a defense against an obstacle), fear (alert), surprise (positive or negative). **Elaborated (secondary) emotions** are mixtures (contempt combines fear and anger), formed from childhood and influenced by each person's experiences and environment.

Batat groups feelings into families: related to joy (serenity, enchantment, enthusiasm), to anticipation (curiosity, interest, expectation), to fear, to surprise (amazement, fun), to sadness, to disgust (including boredom), to anger, and to trust (admiration, tolerance, acceptance). In a restaurant experience, the customer goes through different emotions at the welcome, with the employees, with the other customers, with the decoration and with the food. In luxury, the emotions used most often are love, happiness and admiration.

Against this background, Batat mentions **emotional branding** (Morrison & Crane, 2007): a profound, long-term emotional connection with the brand that goes beyond satisfaction.

## Application

- Draw the emotional curve of the journey: where are the negative peaks the brand can repair and the positive peaks it can amplify?
- Choose the brand's target emotions, secondary ones included (pride, admiration, belonging), and check whether the execution produces them.

## Example

Ariel India, #ShareTheLoad (BBDO): a father watches his daughter, a working mother, do everything alone while her husband watches TV, and he feels remorse for not having shown her something different when she was a child. His remorse, a strong secondary emotion, carries the final question: is laundry only a woman's job?

## Questions

- Are primary emotions universal and secondary ones cultural? What does that mean for a global campaign?
- Can a brand use negative emotions on purpose without damaging its image?
