# Associating the brand with a celebrity transfers meaning as well as liking, including unwanted meanings

> **The idea in one sentence:** evaluative conditioning works through three different pathways, and the choice between a single constant celebrity and several pleasant sources changes the risk for the brand.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe show that evaluative conditioning is not Pavlov-style signal learning (nobody expects to be transported into the tropics by using a mouthwash). It can work through at least three mechanisms:

1. **Direct affect transfer**: the liking produced by varied, pleasant stimuli "rubs off" on the brand through misattribution. Sweldens et al. (2010) suggest this happens mainly when the brand is paired with **a variety of** positive stimuli.
2. **Indirect affective response**: when the brand always appears with **the same** source (Nespresso and George Clooney), a strong brand-celebrity link forms; the brand activates the celebrity, and the celebrity activates the affect. The risk: if the celebrity falls into disgrace (the authors' examples: O.J. Simpson, Lance Armstrong), the brand evaluation takes a similar nosedive.
3. **Meaning transfer** (Fishbein & Ajzen): the pairing creates beliefs. Kim, Allen and Kardes (1996): after Brand L Pizza boxes were repeatedly paired with images of a race car, people believed the pizza would be delivered hot and fast. Miller and Allen (2012): pairing the Gap brand with celebrities seen as "trashy" and "irreverent" (for example, Paris Hilton) made the brand seem more trashy and lowered the attitude toward it.

Other findings: awareness of the link between brand and stimulus makes the effect stronger (the meta-analysis by Hofmann et al., 2010); presenting the brand repeatedly without the stimulus extinguishes it; conditioning can change implicit attitudes even for Coca-Cola and Pepsi, but only among people with no strong preference (Gibson, 2008). Pairing with humor increases the implicit attitude toward an energy drink (Strick et al., 2009).

## Application

- Choose the celebrity for the meanings they transfer, not only for popularity: which traits do you want the brand to "borrow"?
- A single constant celebrity = a strong association, but concentrated risk; several pleasant ambassadors = diffuse affect, lower risk.
- When an ambassador is caught in a scandal, the effect on the brand is directly proportional to how exclusive the association was.

## Example

Nespresso and George Clooney (the authors' example): an exclusive, long-term association, with the benefits and risks described above.

## Questions

- Which meanings would a well-known Romanian influencer transfer to a banking brand?
- How would you test before launch whether the association produces the transfer you want?
