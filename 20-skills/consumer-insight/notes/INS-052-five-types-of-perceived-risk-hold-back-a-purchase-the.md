# Five types of perceived risk hold back a purchase: the higher the risk, the more the consumer searches

> **The idea in one sentence:** performance, financial, social, physiological and psychological risk can delay or discourage a purchase, and the intensity of the information search grows with perceived risk.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy say that the length and intensity of the information search depend on three factors: the perceived benefits versus the perceived costs of search (you research a home a lot, an inexpensive dollhouse very little), the **locus of control** (people who believe the outcome depends on them search more; those who put it down to fate search less) and the **actual or perceived risk**.

The five types of risk:

| Risk | The fear | Lauren's example (the interview outfit) |
|---|---|---|
| Performance | the product does not perform as expected | the outfit shrinks when dry cleaned |
| Financial | the initial cost and the cost of use | dry cleaning is expensive |
| Social | others will not regard the choice positively | what her friends think, what her future boss thinks |
| Physiological (safety) | the product does you actual harm if it fails | rare with apparel, essential with cars (the brakes, protection if the car flips) |
| Psychological | the product does not convey the right image | she wants to look great and get the job |

Research cited by the authors links psychological risk to the preference for "bigger is better": when they feel powerless or more vulnerable, consumers equate larger sizes (televisions, houses, menu items) with improved status.

Marketers reduce risk through guarantees (professional apparel retailers guarantee that their products will perform as expected), easy-to-care-for fabrics and independent safety ratings.

## Application

- Identify the target's dominant risk and build the message as its antidote: proof and demonstrations for performance, guarantees and clear costs for financial risk, social proof for social risk.
- In high-risk categories, remember that the audience searches actively: useful content (reviews, comparisons, tutorials) is worth more than a short commercial.
- With low-risk products, the search is short, so presence and awareness at the shelf win.

## Example

Lauren reduces social and psychological risk by reading fashion magazines and asking her friends' opinions. Retailers reduce financial risk through guarantees, and suppliers through washable fabrics.

## Questions

- Do influencers reduce social risk or only move it (the risk of looking like you follow fashion)?
- Which risk dominates in a first online purchase, where you cannot touch the product?
