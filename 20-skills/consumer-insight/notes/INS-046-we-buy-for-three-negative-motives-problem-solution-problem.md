# We buy for three negative motives (problem solution, problem avoidance, incomplete satisfaction) or for two positive ones (sensory gratification, social approval)

> **The idea in one sentence:** if you do not know *why* the target buys, you cannot choose the benefit or the creative tactics; and "negative" does not mean "bad", it means negatively originated, starting from a problem.

## Explanation

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott reduce the motivation relevant to communication to five motives (Table 9.3):

| Type | Motive | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| Negative | Problem solution | you have a headache and want relief |
| Negative | Problem avoidance | you buy the best tires you can so as not to worry about a flat |
| Negative | Incomplete satisfaction | several products do an adequate job of getting out stains, but you want one that does better |
| Positive | Sensory gratification | new clothes, wine, ice cream: you want to enjoy it |
| Positive | Social approval | you buy something to impress others |

Occasionally **intellectual stimulation** matters too (the inner drive to explore and learn). Negative motives call for **information** that solves or prevents a problem, hence **informational** strategies. Positive motives call for an **emotional experience** that transforms the person's state, hence **transformational** strategies.

Motivation depends on the situation, not only on the category. A beer with friends after a ball game is sensory gratification. The same beer at lunch with a prospective employer is social approval, and the brand chosen will probably be a different one. People do not put their motives into words (removing hunger). They think about benefits (something in the house that is not too filling).

**Other typologies in the base.** Grewal and Levy ([[INS-044-every-product-satisfies-functional-and-psychological-needs|INS-044]]) use the functional/psychological pair, Fennis and Stroebe ([[INS-047-products-serve-four-goals-utilitarian-self-expression|INS-047]]) four goals, and Joannis (*La boîte à outils*) hedonist (*hédoniste*), oblative (*oblative*) and self-expression (*auto-expression*) motivations. All of them separate the problem to solve from the pleasure or image sought; only Percy ties the motive directly to the creative tactic.

## Application

- For each segment, ask: what problem does the product solve, *or* what pleasure or recognition does it bring? If the answer differs between segments, you need different executions.
- Do not combine motives in the same execution without a reason. A price offer (negative motive) can spoil an ad built on pleasure.
- Use qualitative research on consumption situations, not only general questions such as "why is brand X the best?".

## Example

The research the authors cite on watching one's weight: women are positively motivated ("I would like to look like that"), men negatively (they see someone overweight and out of breath from climbing stairs and do not want to end up like that). The same creative execution cannot serve both motives.

## Questions

- Some psychologists say that all motivation is negative. What would be lost, in practice, if we accepted that?
- In which quadrant would you put a gym membership: problem avoided, pleasure or social approval?
