# An ad is processed through four responses (attention, learning, acceptance, emotion), element by element, and reflexive attention lasts less than a second

> **The idea in one sentence:** exposure is only an opportunity; processing starts with attention, continues with learning and, at high involvement, with acceptance, all colored by emotion, and it happens separately for each picture, word or sound.

## Explanation

For Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott, **processing** is everything that goes on in a person's mind in response to the ad, during exposure and after. It requires consciousness: the unconscious mind "can only propose. It is the conscious mind that selects." The four responses (Table 12.1):

| Response | What it means | The Whirlpool example |
|---|---|---|
| **Attention** | you pay attention to the ad, even if only enough to identify it as an ad | you notice the page with the "floating" washing machine |
| **Learning** | you take in the content | you learn it is a washing machine that is easy to unload ("It's not magic, it's Whirlpool") |
| **Acceptance** | you believe what the ad says | it does seem easy, a conviction that comes mainly from the picture |
| **Emotion** | the response of the autonomic nervous system, which energizes learning and acceptance | the pleasant sense of floating |

Each element is processed separately. For recognition, the package must be processed. For recall, the category need and the brand name must be processed, in that order. Attitude, especially on TV, comes largely from the visuals. A useful test: watch the commercial with the sound off.

**Attention** takes two forms:

- **selective** (top-down, voluntary): a laptop ad when you actually want to buy one. It appears only if the message connects with your current motivation. A poster for BP's MAC brand in Sweden was recognized by 25% of people who buy from full-service garages, 35% of those who buy from self-service garages, 60% of those who buy low-priced petrol and 72% of current customers.
- **reflexive** (bottom-up, involuntary): any change (a pop-up, the commercial break, a page turn) draws attention for about one-tenth of a second for a picture and three-tenths of a second for a word. Only if something holds attention in that interval does the person stay with the ad.

The limits of attention matter directly for the creative work. In the "gorilla" study, over 70% of those counting the passes did not see the gorilla. And people can never consciously process two unrelated things at the same time. What draws attention to one element can take it away from another. Executions and packaging similar to a competitor's risk being attributed to the competitor.

## Application

- Make an inventory of the elements of the execution and ask, for each one, what it has to produce: attention, learning, acceptance or emotion.
- Put something in the first fraction of a second that holds reflexive attention, and tie it to the brand.
- For selective attention, show the target's need or motive at first glance. Only then do people who are in the market stop.

## Example

A commercial that opens with a long joke and shows the product only at the end can win reflexive attention but lose it before the brand. The right version brings the brand in during the first seconds, as part of the joke.

## Questions

- At low involvement, acceptance only means "it might be true". What does that imply for how much you argue?
- How do the reflexive attention figures apply to scrolling on TikTok?
