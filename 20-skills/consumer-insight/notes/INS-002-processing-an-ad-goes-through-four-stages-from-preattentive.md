# Processing an ad goes through four stages, from preattentive analysis to elaborative reasoning

> **The idea in one sentence:** an ad can work at any of four levels of processing, and the level it reaches depends mainly on how relevant the product is to the person at that moment (involvement).

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe organize the chapter on information acquisition around the model of Greenwald and Leavitt (1984). A consumer exposed to around 1,000 ads a day cannot process them all; working memory is limited. That is why processing has levels:

| Stage | What happens | Type of process |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Preattentive analysis | a general, non-goal-directed "surveillance" of the environment | automatic, non-conscious |
| 2. Focal attention | the stimulus is brought into conscious awareness, identified and categorized | partly automatic |
| 3. Comprehension | inferences are formed about the meaning of the message | conscious |
| 4. Elaborative reasoning | the message is related to previously stored knowledge, and new conclusions arise | conscious, effortful |

The variable that decides how far up processing goes is **outcome-relevant involvement**: the extent to which choosing the product has significant personal consequences. It is not only a matter of price: car ads are irrelevant to people who are not in the market for a car, and hungry people pay more attention to information about food. When involvement is low, information is processed with very little effort; when it is moderately low, focal attention appears; when it is relatively high, consumers elaborate and reach their own conclusions.

**Convergence.** Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott ([[INS-003-an-ad-is-processed-through-four-responses-attention|INS-003]]) and *La boîte à outils* (exposure → attention → decoding → response, [[INS-004-perception-is-selective-so-the-ad-needs-attention-devices|INS-004]]) propose similar sequences. All three make involvement, or the motivation of the moment, the variable that decides how far processing goes.

## Application

- Before writing, the strategist decides which stage the message can realistically reach for the given target: a washing powder ad also has to work preattentively (pack, color, association), not only through arguments.
- Involvement is a variable of the person and the moment, not of the category: look for the moments when the target comes "into the market" (a move, a pregnancy, winter).
- Use the model as a diagnostic in pretesting: where is the message lost? Is it not noticed, misunderstood or counterargued?

## Example

The same car ad is ignored by a student without a driving license (stage 1), but read carefully, with fuel consumption comparisons, by someone who has just sold their old car (stage 4).

## Questions

- Which elements of an ad can work even if the person does not get past stage 1?
- Is the model too linear? Can emotions skip stages (see preattentive fear reactions)?
