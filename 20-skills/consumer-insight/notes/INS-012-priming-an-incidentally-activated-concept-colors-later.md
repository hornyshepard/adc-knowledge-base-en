# Priming: an incidentally activated concept colors later judgments, if we do not recognize its source

> **The idea in one sentence:** what has recently been activated in the mind becomes the lens through which we interpret ambiguous information, provided we do not realize where that activation comes from.

## Explanation

Priming is the increase in the accessibility of a mental representation after an incidental activation, with an unintended influence on later, apparently unrelated tasks (Bargh & Chartrand). Fennis and Stroebe distinguish:

- **semantic priming**: the word "nurse" comes to mind faster after "doctor";
- **trait priming**: Higgins, Rholes and Jones (1977) found that after words such as "reckless" or "adventurous", the same ambiguous character ("Donald") was judged negatively or positively; the effect appeared only for traits applicable to the behavior;
- **price category priming**: Herr (1989) found that after a word puzzle with expensive car brands, hypothetical cars were rated more expensive (assimilation), while known models were rated cheaper (contrast).

The "situated inference theory of priming" (Loersch & Payne) adds a second requirement: the activated thoughts must be **misattributed** to the object being judged. Schwarz and Clore (1983): people asked on a sunny day are more satisfied with their lives, but the effect disappears if they are first asked about the weather.

Subliminal priming (a masked stimulus below the threshold of awareness) produces effects comparable to supraliminal priming in the meta-analysis by Weingarten et al. (2016). But **behavioral** priming (for example, "elderly" → walking more slowly) failed to replicate and remains disputed.

## Application

- The media context is a prime: a luxury spot in a program about saving money is read differently from one in a lifestyle program.
- For new (ambiguous) products, the category activated by the context "pulls" them toward it; for known products, the context can produce contrast.
- If the audience notices the attempt to influence it, the effect cancels out: visible priming is no longer priming.

## Example

A Mini looks cheap in a BMW dealership and expensive next to a VW Golf or Polo (p. 105).

- Which priming effects in the book are solid and which are disputed?
- How would you choose the TV programs or websites where a spot appears, based on priming?
