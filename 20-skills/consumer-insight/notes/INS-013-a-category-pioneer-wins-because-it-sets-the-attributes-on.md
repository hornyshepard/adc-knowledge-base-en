# A category pioneer wins because it sets the attributes on which all the others are judged

> **The idea in one sentence:** the first brand in a new category becomes its prototype, and competitors are compared to it rather than the other way around.

## Explanation

Categorization is a central process of focal attention: consumers place the product in a category and then use what they know about the category. Fennis and Stroebe show that **prototypical** products (the ones most similar to the other category members) are usually better liked, but at a cost: they stand out less easily in ads.

The pioneer of a new category becomes, by definition, its prototype. Research (Carpenter & Nakamoto, Kardes & Gurumurthy) identifies three advantages of this pioneering advantage:

1. **The inherent advantage**: being novel and interesting, the pioneer is processed more elaborately; consumers learn more attributes and form more extreme judgments.
2. **Setting the attribute agenda**: the pioneer defines the dimensions on which the category is evaluated, usually the ones on which it scores best.
3. **The direction-of-comparison effect**: followers are compared to the pioneer; since it set the criteria, the pioneer "wins".

The authors' example: the iPhone created the category of designer smartphones and made design the key attribute, the one on which it was the best.

The same research program (Carpenter & Nakamoto) appears in *Kellogg on Branding* ([[BRD-016-timing-of-entry-pioneer-fast-follower-or-late-mover|BRD-016]]), from a strategic angle and with a limit: the typical pioneer stays the leader for only a few years, and early followers lead in most markets (Golder & Tellis).

## Application

- When you launch something new, do not settle for entering an existing category: name the category and set the evaluation criteria.
- If you are a follower, do not fight on the pioneer's attributes; change the dimension of comparison or create a subcategory.
- In the brief, ask: "On which criterion do we want to be judged?"

## Example

The iPhone (ch. 2, p. 71): first in the "designer smartphone" category and the best on its defining attribute, design.

## Questions

- When does the pioneer lose (for example, when a follower redefines the category)?
- How does this idea connect to positioning and to "being first in the mind"?
