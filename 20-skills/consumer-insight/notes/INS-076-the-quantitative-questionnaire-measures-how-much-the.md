# The quantitative questionnaire measures "how much", the interview and the focus group explain "why", each with its own rules

> **The idea in one sentence:** quantitative studies describe behavior on representative samples, qualitative ones explore motivations on small samples; the best results come from combining them, provided each is done properly.

## Explanation

The practical rules from *La boîte à outils de la publicité*:

**The quantitative study (survey)**

1. Define the parent population (*population mère*): who would have to be asked if you could ask them all?
2. Build the sample. The most widely used method is the quota method (*méthode des quotas*): a miniature of the population by sex, age, socio-professional category. A sample of about 1,000 people gives an average uncertainty of ±3%.
3. Choose the collection mode: face to face, telephone, self-administered (post, online).
4. Build the questionnaire: a filter question at the start; the **funnel** technique (*technique de l'entonnoir*), from general and simple to precise; logical blocks by theme; sensitive questions and identification data at the end. Almost only closed questions, with attitude scales (very satisfied … very dissatisfied); as few open questions as possible.
5. Pretest the questionnaire on 8–10 people from the target.
6. Administer it, then process it (simple and cross tables).

The repeated forms: the **panel** (a constant sample, more precise) and the **barometer** (*baromètre*: successive samples of different people, for example for brand awareness).

**The qualitative study**

- **The semi-structured individual interview** (*entretien individuel semi-directif*): a guide of themes, not a questionnaire; open questions, broad at first; active listening; the interviewer stays neutral, asks for clarification and periodically summarizes to check their interpretation.
- **The focus group** (*réunion de groupe*): 8–10 people, about 3 hours, in a room with video recording. Homogeneous groups (age, gender, status), with no experts or focus group "professionals". The invitation is made on a fictitious, related theme, so that answers are spontaneous. The moderator gets everyone to take part, keeps the dominating "leaders" in check, brings the discussion back to the subject and covers all the themes. The client watches from outside the room through the video system and can suggest questions.

## Application

- Before any study, write three lines: what you want to measure or understand, who the population is, whom you will ask.
- In a questionnaire, avoid technical terms and vague words ("often", "rarely"); ask for concrete frequencies.
- Use qualitative research for hypotheses and quantitative research to test them, not the other way round.

## Example

For a satisfaction questionnaire handed out at the exit of a leisure park, the authors accept a convenience sample (*échantillon de convenance*): it is not representative, but it is enough for its purpose.
