# Experiential ethnography works with consumers, not on them: socialization, collaboration, auto-confrontation

> **The idea in one sentence:** to understand an experience from the point of view of the person living it, you immerse yourself in their culture, build the results together with them and let them comment on them.

## Explanation

Batat considers ethnography the most appropriate tool for understanding the customer experience, because it goes beyond the cognitive and rational vision of consumer behavior and captures the symbolic and emotional dimensions of consumption. She proposes **three stages**:
1. **Socialization** (three to six months): observation and informal conversations until the group accepts the researcher as a member;
2. **Collaboration**: interviews and participatory workshops, on the principles of Participatory Action Research;
3. **Auto-confrontation**: participants comment on the primary results about their own experiences and those of others.

Companies can choose between **multi-site** ethnography (long) and **"quick & dirty"** ethnography (short). Tools: observation, photographs taken by participants, life narratives. For observation, Batat recommends the **PERCEIVE** framework: Proximity, Expressions, Relative orientation, Contact, Eyes, Individual gestures, Voice, Existence of adapters (small mood accommodating behaviors).

Other experiential methods in the same chapter:
- **qualitative diary research** (QDR): the customer records the little experiences of everyday life in their natural environment, with the risk of recall errors;
- **subjective personal introspection** (SPI, Holbrook): the researcher is also the informant (variants: researcher self-introspection, interactive, guided, syncretic).

**Online (e-experiential) variants:**
- **netnography** (Kozinets, 1997), in four steps: entry into the community, data collection, analysis (first emic, then etic), validation by participants;
- **mobile self-ethnography**: the smartphone replaces the observer, and participants send video, photos, audio and text from the real situation.

## Application

- For an insight project with a small budget: one week of "quick & dirty" ethnography plus a mobile diary with 10–12 participants.
- Budget for auto-confrontation: a session in which participants react to the agency's hypotheses prevents forced interpretations.
- Use PERCEIVE as an observation sheet for store visits.

## Example

L'Oréal followed women's beauty routines during the day, which are hard to observe with classic techniques: 12 participants sent 295 recordings (112 videos, 117 images, 52 audio comments, 100 texts). EDF had the anthropologists in its R&D department study how customers manage their energy, and found choices that are not the most efficient from a technical or economic point of view.
