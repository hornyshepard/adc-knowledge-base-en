# Strong attitudes resist and guide behavior; weak ones are built from context and from one's own behavior

> **The idea in one sentence:** what an ad can do depends on how strong the existing attitude is: with weak attitudes, context and recent behavior matter; strong ones are almost impossible to move.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe settle the dispute "are attitudes stable or constructed on the spot?" through a continuum of **attitude strength**. Strong attitudes are more stable, have a greater impact on behavior and on information processing, and resist persuasion (Krosnick & Petty).

| Determinant | What it means | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Accessibility | how quickly the evaluation comes to mind | predicts product choice (Fazio et al., 1989) and voting |
| Importance | the link with values, interests, reference groups | stability, resistance |
| Knowledge | how much the person knows about the object | experts respond to arguments, novices to cues (price, country) |
| Certainty | the confidence that the attitude is correct | it persists, resists and is shared with others |
| Ambivalence | equally strong positive and negative evaluations | an unstable attitude, easy to influence, but processed more carefully |
| Evaluative-cognitive consistency | the evaluation fits the beliefs | stability, better prediction |

With weak attitudes, **self-perception** comes in (Bem): people infer their attitude from their behavior ("I drink it often, so I must like it"). Holland et al. (2002): donating to Greenpeace changed the attitude of people with weak attitudes; among those with strong attitudes, the attitude predicted the donation. Haddock (2002): asking for five negative attributes of Tony Blair improved the attitude toward him only among people uninterested in politics.

Advertising can increase attitude strength: through heavy advertising, an unfamiliar brand becomes a household name, highly accessible.

## Application

- Diagnose the target audience's attitude: is it weak (a new brand, a low-involvement category) or strong (a fan of the competition)? The strategy differs radically.
- For weak attitudes, generate behavior (samples, trial, participation); the attitude will follow.
- For ambivalent people, good arguments work: they read carefully.
- Build certainty (social proof, personal experience) to protect current customers from the competition.

## Example

Heineken beer or Absolut Vodka (the authors' examples, p. 142): repeated advertising that turns a foreign name into an accessible, stable attitude.

## Questions

- What would a campaign built on self-perception look like for a new product?
- Which signals in research tell you that the target audience has an ambivalent attitude?
