# The experiential view: consumption also means fantasies, feelings and fun, not only information processing

> **The idea in one sentence:** Holbrook & Hirschman (1982) showed that the model of the rational consumer, who processes information to maximize utility, does not explain the sensory pleasures, daydreams, play and emotions of consumption.

## Explanation

Batat places the article by Morris Holbrook and Elizabeth Hirschman, "The experiential aspects of consumption: consumer fantasies, feelings, and fun" (*Journal of Consumer Research*, 1982), at the origin of the whole field. The authors criticized the hegemony of information processing theory, which portrayed consumers as rational thinkers. It left out playful leisure activities, sensory pleasures, daydreams, aesthetic enjoyment and emotional responses.

They define **hedonic consumption** as the facets of behavior that relate to the "multi-sensory, fantasy, and emotive aspects" of one's experience with products (1982: 92, cited by Batat). The view is phenomenological: consumption is a subjective state of consciousness, with symbolic meanings, hedonic responses and aesthetic criteria. For hedonically consumed products, the choice rests mainly on the symbolic elements, not on the tangible features.

Two important nuances:
- the experiential view **does not reject** information processing; it complements it;
- the idea was so unusual that it took almost two decades to be taken up by management (Pine & Gilmore, Schmitt, in the late 1990s).

Batat takes the idea further through the contrasting marker: a product is never only hedonic or only utilitarian. Buyers weigh a sneaker's comfort and its style at the same time. Firat & Dholakia even argue that the domain of experience has extended to all consumption activities.

## Application

- In insight interviews, ask explicitly about the three Fs: what *fantasies* link people to the category, what they *feel*, what they find *fun*.
- For functional categories (insurance, detergents, utilities), look for the hidden hedonic layer: the pleasure of control, play, aesthetics.
- Write the communication benefit on two levels: what it solves and what pleasure it brings.

## Example

In-flight safety videos (Air New Zealand, Virgin America, Delta) turn a purely utilitarian message into a moment of entertainment. Passengers pay attention, remember the instructions and share them.

## Questions

- Where does the rational model remain more useful than the experiential one (B2B, large purchases)?
- If all consumption has a hedonic side, is the hedonic/utilitarian distinction still useful in segmentation?
