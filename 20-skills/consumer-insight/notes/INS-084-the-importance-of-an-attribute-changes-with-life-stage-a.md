# The importance of an attribute changes with life stage: "a brand I trust" matters hugely at ages 4–8 and hardly at all for pre-teens

> **The idea in one sentence:** one market hides several markets; the reason people buy can flip from one segment to the next, and the message has to follow the reason, not the category.

## Explanation

A major manufacturer of children's apparel ran large semi-annual surveys: awareness, shopping habits, ratings on many attributes (value for money, appropriateness for the child) and the percentage of spending that went to each brand. Regression-based models and classification trees gave no clear results; Bayes Nets did.

The key was not to treat children's apparel as a single market. The data (about 9,000 parents, 46 variables) were analyzed separately for four ages: infants (up to two), toddlers (2–4), early school age (4–8) and pre-teens (8–13). The attribute **"a brand I trust"** followed a striking path:

| Age of the child | Importance of "a brand I trust" |
|---|---|
| infants | exceptionally low (all brands are new to the parents) |
| 2–4 | about average |
| 4–8 | one of the three most important attributes out of 46 |
| 8–13 | plummets again |

For pre-teens, trust was replaced by three related attributes: a style appealing to me (the parent), a brand or style the child likes, and clothing appropriate for a child this age. Struhl sums it up as a family argument over one question: "You are thinking of going out wearing WHAT?"

## Application

- For early-school children: communicate **trust** (safety, durability, a family brand).
- For pre-teens: communicate **the successful compromise**, clothes the parent accepts and the child does not refuse.
- In any category with "stages" (babies, students, retirees), analyze the drivers separately for each stage before you write the brief's proposition.

## Example

The analysis followed a segmentation study in which the company's target was a segment called "Savvy Sylvia". Management believed in the segment so strongly that the whole company had started asking "What would Sylvia do?", an example of a persona that actually guides decisions.

## Questions

- Which other categories show a "flip" in the reason to buy over time?
- What would two ads for the same brand look like, one for parents of 6-year-olds and one for parents of 11-year-olds?
