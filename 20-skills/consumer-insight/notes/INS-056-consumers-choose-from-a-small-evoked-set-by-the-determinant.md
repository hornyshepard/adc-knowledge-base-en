# Consumers choose from a small evoked set, by the determinant attribute: the one that is important and differs between brands

> **The idea in one sentence:** of all the existing options, consumers remember only a few and consider even fewer, then decide by the attributes that matter to them and differ between brands, using compensatory or noncompensatory rules.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy describe the evaluation of alternatives through three mechanisms.

**1. Attribute sets.** The mind organizes the options in layers:

- the **universal set**: all possible choices in the category (all apparel stores);
- the **retrieval set**: the ones that can be readily brought forth from memory (Macy's, The Gap, Banana Republic);
- the **evoked set**: the ones the consumer states they would consider (Lauren: only Banana Republic and Macy's).

If the brand gets into the evoked set, the likelihood of purchase increases and search time decreases. Loyal customers have an evoked set made up of a single brand.

**2. Evaluative criteria and determinant attributes.** The criteria are the salient attributes (price, fit, materials and construction quality, the reputation of the brand, service). But many of them are equal among competitors. **Determinant attributes** are those that are important to the buyer and on which brands are perceived to differ. They can be rational (health claims) or subtle and psychological (a particular stitching).

**3. Decision rules.**

- **Compensatory**: a good attribute compensates for a weak one. The multi-attribute model (Exhibit 6.2) multiplies the weights by the ratings: Cheerios scores 8.2 (taste 0.4 × 10 + calories 0.1 × 8 + natural 0.3 × 6 + price 0.2 × 8).
- **Noncompensatory**: the decision is made on a single attribute, regardless of the rest. Hanna still picks Kashi, the only one rated 10 on natural/organic claims.

## Application

- The real objective of many awareness campaigns is getting into the evoked set, not just recognition. Top-of-mind awareness is a good indicator of this.
- Look for the determinant attribute, not just the important one. If the whole category has "good taste", taste does not decide.
- If the target uses a noncompensatory rule, a single strong message on the filtering attribute beats a list of benefits.

## Example

LaCroix (Adding Value 6.1) entered Millennials' evoked set through micro-influencers and recipe posts. It put the determinant attributes in the foreground: no calories, no sugar, no artificial sweeteners, plus a hard-to-define ethereal attribute.

- How do you win a place in the evoked set of a consumer who is very loyal to another brand?
- Is the multi-attribute model a description of the real mind or only an analyst's tool?
