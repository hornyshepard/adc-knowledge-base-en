# Brand attitude is a relative evaluation, made of beliefs and emotions, of the brand's ability to meet the current motivation

> **The idea in one sentence:** attitude is how well I believe and feel the brand helps me with what I am looking for now, compared with the alternatives; it is not "how much I like the brand".

## Explanation

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott define brand attitude as the evaluation of the brand in relation to its perceived ability to meet what a person is looking for in the product, that is, the relevant motivation. It has four characteristics (Box 9.1):

1. **It depends on what the target audience wants now.** If the motivation changes, brands are re-evaluated. A basic calculator was fine for avoiding arithmetic by hand. The same calculator no longer measures up for an economics student.
2. **It has two components: what you know and what you feel.** The cognitive component (beliefs) is the perceived link between the brand and the motivation. It **directs** toward a brand. The affective component (feelings) is generated by the motivation itself, experienced as a departure from the present state. It **energizes** the choice. The authors leave behavioral intention (the conative component) outside the attitude, as a separate effect, and cite neuroscience: the brain processes cognitive and affective inputs in parallel.
3. **It is made up of several beliefs.** Benefit beliefs, weighted by their importance, give the overall attitude (the expectancy-value model).
4. **It is relative.** A brand rarely offers exactly what everyone wants. One will be seen as relatively better. Even a monopoly must think in relative terms, because a competitor can appear at any time.

A consequence for research: general questions ("why is X the best?") miss situational motivations and produce information that is practically meaningless.

## Application

- Research attitude *by consumption situation*, not in the abstract: "when you choose a beer at a business lunch…".
- Always measure attitude comparatively, with your brand and the direct competitors on the same benefits.
- When a brand loses ground for no apparent reason, check whether the target audience's motivation has changed, not only the perception of the brand.

## Example

People do not tell themselves that they want to remove their feeling of hunger. That is a category decision (a snack). For the brand, they think in benefits: "what do I have around that's not too filling?", or what tastes good. The ad must link attributes, characteristics or emotions to the brand as the unique solution for that motive.

## Questions

- If emotion "energizes" and belief "directs", what happens to a very moving ad that does not link the emotion to a specific brand?
- How does the relativity of attitude fit with the idea of "brand love"?
