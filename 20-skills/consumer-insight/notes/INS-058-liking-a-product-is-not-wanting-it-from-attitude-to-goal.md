# Liking a product is not wanting it: from attitude to goal you need attainability, discrepancy and a cue to action

> **The idea in one sentence:** many brands are liked, but few become goals: for that, the desired state has to be attainable, clearly better than the present one and activated by a cue at the right moment.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe define goals as cognitive representations of desired future states that one intends to attain through action (Kruglanski). Attitudes reflect **liking**, which does not necessarily translate into **wanting**; liking is neither necessary (many people dislike going to funerals and yet they go) nor sufficient.

The steps from liking to action:

1. **Desirability**: the state has to be desirable.
2. **Attainability**: many car lovers would like to own a Ferrari, but the price tag takes it off their list of goals.
3. **Positive discrepancy**: someone who already owns a Porsche they like very much does not adopt a Ferrari as a goal, even if they can afford one.
4. **Activation**: once formed, the goal has to be activated and must not be beaten by competing goals.
5. **Behavioral intention**: choosing a particular means (buying it in a shop, not by mail order).
6. **Cue to action**: one of the authors gives his own example. His Swiss automatic watch tended to stop, he read about a Seiko GPS solar watch and decided he wanted to own it, but he bought it only several months later, when he saw it in a shop window. The shop window was the cue to action. For mundane products (running out of milk), the discovery is both the formation of a goal and the cue to action.

## Application

- Do not stop at making the brand liked: show that it is attainable (price, installments, access) and clearly better than what people have now.
- Identify the trigger moments in the target's life and be present there (shop window, search, shelf, notification).
- For aspirational products, build an attainable intermediate step (an accessory, an entry-level range).

## Example

The Seiko GPS watch (p. 176): a goal formed after reading about it, an action triggered by a shop window, several months later.
