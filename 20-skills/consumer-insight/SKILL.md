# Consumer psychology and insight (INS)

The skill goes from mechanics to strategy: first how an ad gets (or fails to get) through people's attention and memory, then how what they believe and feel changes, then why they buy or do not, in what cultural context, how you find all this out through research and, at the end, how you turn what you found into an insight fit for the brief ([[20-skills/creative-brief/SKILL|BRF]]).

## What mastering this skill means

Observable competencies ("you can…"):

- **C1** You can explain, on a concrete ad, which element gets past the attention filter in the first second, what stays in memory and why (preattentive processing, salience, emotion, depth of processing).
- **C2** You can diagnose the target's involvement and motivation and choose the right persuasion route: arguments or cues, an informational or a transformational strategy.
- **C3** You can identify the brake that blocks the purchase (risk, attitude, social norm, perceived control, habit) and propose the right lever: alpha (increase the attractiveness) or omega (weaken the resistance).
- **C4** You can choose the message source and the type of appeal (celebrity, expert, fear, social proof) on the basis of evidence, not fashion, and anticipate reactance.
- **C5** You can design mixed research (qualitative for "why", quantitative for "how much") and read a result critically: sample, significance, importance ratings, recall.
- **C6** You can find an insight by pulling a surprising "what" until you reach the "why", through observation in context, ethnography, projective techniques or semiotics.
- **C7** You can word the insight as a tension ("I want… but…") in the target's voice and check it with the checklist before it goes into the brief.
- **C8** You can show where the authors disagree (ELM vs. the unimodel, the subliminal, the dose of fear, the definition of attitude) and what the tension means for a practical decision.

## How an AI agent uses this skill

1. **When you are asked to find or write the insight of a brief:** read [[INS-092-an-insight-is-a-meaningful-human-truth-and-has-to-pass|INS-092]] (definition and tests) and [[INS-093-an-insight-is-found-by-pulling-the-thread-of-a-surprising|INS-093]] (the "what" → "why" method); look for the hidden "why" with [[INS-088-experiential-ethnography-works-with-consumers-not-on-them|INS-088]], [[INS-089-projective-techniques-bring-to-the-surface-what-consumers|INS-089]] or [[INS-087-digital-anthropology-uncovers-hidden-anxieties-through|INS-087]]; word it as a dilemma following [[INS-094-an-insight-is-a-consumer-dilemma-desire-versus-obstacle|INS-094]]; check it with [[INS-095-the-insight-checklist-eight-checks-before-a-truth-about-the|INS-095]]; then link it to [[BRF-005-the-mctigue-and-rucker-brief-has-six-components-and-fits-on|BRF-005]] and to the communication objective in [[BRF-007-the-objective-in-the-brief-is-a-communication-objective|BRF-007]]. If you have no real data on the target, say so and leave the section empty, as McTigue and Rucker ask.
