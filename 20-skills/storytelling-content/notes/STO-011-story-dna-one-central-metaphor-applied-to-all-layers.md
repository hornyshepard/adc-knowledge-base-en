# Story DNA: one central metaphor applied to all layers

> **The idea in one sentence:** the DNA of a story is the imagery through which the benefit becomes emotion; a good story has one strong metaphor, carried consistently through narrative, design, layout and UX.

## Explanation

Mueller and Rajaram start from the idea that feelings make us have experiences, and that the relevance of a story comes from the benefits for people. The story DNA is the way this benefit is turned into imagery. The main tool is the **metaphor**, which they define as "a comparison without a 'how'", or a visual anchor that translates a fact into a picture:

- lipstick becomes an irresistible smile;
- the light of digital headlights is the life-saving sun at night ("light is life", Mercedes-Benz, 2018);
- an aircraft turbine is a flock of birds ("Nature inspires innovation", General Electric, since 2016);
- an energy drink is a rocket engine.

The metaphor connects the story to the collective memory and docks onto an associative space that resonates with many different groups. The authors insist on two rules: **only one** (several metaphors cancel each other out) and **on all layers**. In the turbine example, feathers and aerodynamic arrows appear in the graphics and layout, while the narrative uses linguistic images from the same association space. The metaphor is defined at the beginning of story development and can then be integrated into branding and brand claims.

## Application

- In a content brief, write the central metaphor in one line before any execution.
- Check every piece (visual, copy, transitions, UX): does it express the same metaphor, or does it bring in a competing one?
- Use the metaphor sparingly; repeating it literally tires people, while variations on it enrich it.

## Example

The Mercedes-Benz film about its digital lighting technology uses the "light is life" metaphor and applies it "pars pro toto": the headlights stand for the entire car.

## Questions

- How does story DNA differ from a brand platform or a big idea?
- What do you do when the central metaphor works visually but does not translate into another language or culture?
