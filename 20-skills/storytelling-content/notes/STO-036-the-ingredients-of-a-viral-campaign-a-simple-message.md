# The ingredients of a viral campaign: a simple message, emotion, participation, urgency

> **The idea in one sentence:** there are no magic ingredients for virality, but the benchmark campaigns share a simple message with a clear CTA, an emotional connection, direct address ("you"), audience participation across several platforms, continuous dialogue and, often, a time limit.

## Explanation

Glenister analyzes three campaigns:

- **Old Spice, "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" (2010)**: the ad with Isaiah Mustafa, launched before the Super Bowl, has amassed about 61 million views on YouTube; the agency Wieden + Kennedy then answered the questions it had received on Facebook and Twitter (a 1,000 per cent increase in engagement). Lessons: an idea that starts from what the audience wants (women want their men to smell nice), with no discounts, plus the integration of TV and social: you can't respond to a TV ad, but you can react to it online.
- **Dove, "Real Beauty Sketches" (Ogilvy & Mather Brazil, 2013)**: an FBI-trained sketch artist draws the same woman from her own description and from a stranger's. An emotional connection (women are more critical of themselves), conversations managed in real time, direct address to the viewer ("YOU are more Beautiful than YOU think"). Over 114 million views, uploaded in 25 languages, the Titanium Grand Prix at Cannes Lions.
- **Ice Bucket Challenge (2014)**: a simple rule (pour iced water over yourself, challenge three people, donate), a 24-hour deadline, massive UGC, celebrities (Bill Gates, Oprah Winfrey). Over 17 million participants.

The author's checklist: (1) a simple message and a clear CTA; (2) an emotional connection and persuasive language that addresses the audience as "you"; (3) several platforms, so that UGC appears; (4) continuous communication with the audience during the campaign; (5) celebrities, where you can.

## Application

- Test the idea: can you explain it in one sentence, and can anyone repeat it without instructions?
- Design the act of participation (what the user does, whom they pass it on to, by when).
- Plan the real-time response team from the concept stage.

## Example

The two books give different figures for the Ice Bucket Challenge: Glenister speaks of around $115 million raised (in five years, in the introduction), Mueller and Rajaram of over $220 million by 2019. A good exercise in checking sources.

## Questions

- Is virality a legitimate objective? (Sascha Gottschalk, in *Social Media Storytelling*, compares viral attention to a paper-fed fire that quickly fades away.)
- How much of Dove's success comes from the idea, and how much from the media budget that amplified it?
