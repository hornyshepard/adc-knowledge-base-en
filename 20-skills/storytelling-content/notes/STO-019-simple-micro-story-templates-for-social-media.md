# Simple micro-story templates for social media

> **The idea in one sentence:** for a beginner, a successful micro-story starts from a few fixed schemes of 3 or 4 steps, which can then be varied as experience grows.

## Explanation

Mueller and Rajaram advise beginners to follow "a clear, simple scheme" on a storyboard. The book offers several complementary templates:

| Template | The steps | When you use it |
|---|---|---|
| **People-action-result** | show real people → show them doing something → show what happens then or what results from it | abstract topics (a technology, a service) explained through a concrete use case |
| **Theme-twist-future** | establish the theme (who is telling, where, what) → bring a twist (an unexpected perspective) → end with a cliffhanger and/or call-to-action | micro-stories of 3 clips |
| **Behind the Scenes** | about me (journey of the founder) → about us (team, communities) → what matters now (what problem is being solved) → what's next | brand, start-up and team stories |
| **Intro-execution-outro** | jump right in, answer the W questions → work on the topic of the day and involve users → summarize, CTA, announce what comes next | lives, podcasts, app templates |

The authors insist that "intro" does not mean a preamble: the first seconds have to capture attention, and every story is told toward the future. For a general topic (Greta Thunberg's visit to Germany), you ask what is specifically relevant for your users and find the angle that can be told in a headline or a short pitch.

## Application

- Choose the template before shooting and write one line for each step.
- If you cannot sum up the story in a headline, you do not understand it well enough to tell it in 15 seconds.
- For "boring" brands (logistics, B2B), start from people: founders, employees, customers.

## Example

The Streem app (augmented reality for craftsmen) explains its business model through funny clips built on a real case: the craftsman takes a live 3D picture of the damage in the customer's home, orders the spare part and arrives prepared. People, action, result, summed up in the claim "Better than being there".

## Questions

- Are these templates anything other than variants of the beginning, middle and end structure? What does each one add?
- What is lost if all of a brand's posts follow the same template?
