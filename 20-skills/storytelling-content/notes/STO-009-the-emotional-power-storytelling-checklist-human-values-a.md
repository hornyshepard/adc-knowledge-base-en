# The emotional power storytelling checklist: human values, a narrative arc, one primary emotion and the "would I share it?" test

> **The idea in one sentence:** before you launch a story, check whether it relates to human values, has an arc, is bold and honest, and whether you would send it to your friends yourself.

## Explanation

Braun and Landa open the chapter with Hyundai's "Operation Better" (Super Bowl LII, 2018, Innocean/USA). U.S. soldiers stationed at a base in Zagan, Poland, entered a sensory immersion room where, through a live link, they met their families. The spot reverses the cliché of the soldier who surprises the family, and it shows no car at all. From cases like this the authors draw their Emotional Power Storytelling Checklist:

1. Does the story relate to **human values** (family, friendship, courage, independence) over merely business ones?
2. Is it **relatable**: can the audience see themselves reflected in it?
3. Is there an **arc**: a protagonist who must triumph over a challenge or conflict?
4. Is it **fresh**, not a cliché?
5. Is it **bold**: does it have an opinion, a perspective?
6. Is it **honest**? A story that is artificial has no emotional power.
7. **Would you share it** yourself, if you did not work on it?
8. Is it told in its own best **voice and on the optimum channel**?
9. Does it have a **primary emotion**? Several emotions may be present, but never of equal weight.

Their arguments: Jonah Berger's research shows that high-arousal emotions (amusement, anger, fear) increase sharing, and the same facts persuade better when told as a story than when presented as data (their examples: vaccination for hepatitis B, sympathy for people experiencing depression). Source credibility, according to James McCroskey, has three attributes: **expertise, trustworthiness and goodwill**. In "Second Chances" (Donate Life California), police officers who pull over drivers registered as organ donors and give them a "second-chance ticket" instead of a citation bring all three together.

## Application

- Run every concept through the nine questions. A "no" to 6 (honesty) or to 9 (primary emotion) means rework.
- For test 7, ask people outside the project: would they send it to someone?
- Choose the source (the spokesperson, the voice) by the three attributes of credibility.

## Example

According to the book, "Second Chances" led to a 19 percent increase in organ donor registrations over the previous year, and other police programs adopted it.

## Questions

- The "would I share it?" test is subjective. How do you turn it into a pretest with the target audience?
- Negative emotions (anger) increase sharing. What risk do they bring the brand (see [[IDE-025-debate-is-shock-the-most-powerful-weapon-for-attention-or|IDE-025]])?
