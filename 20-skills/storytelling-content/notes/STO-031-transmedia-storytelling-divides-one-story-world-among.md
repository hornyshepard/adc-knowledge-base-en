# Transmedia storytelling divides one story world among channels with different roles

> **The idea in one sentence:** cross-media means the same story on several channels; transmedia means a single story world from which each channel tells a different part, which draws people deeper and deeper into it.

## Explanation

Mohanbir Sawhney takes up Henry Jenkins's definition: transmedia storytelling disperses the integral elements of a story across multiple channels to create a coordinated experience. The classic example is *The Matrix*: live-action films, animated short features, comic books and video games; to grasp the entire Matrix universe, you need to experience them all. Unlike the *cross-media* approach, where the same content is only repurposed, in transmedia each channel does what it does best. The technique emerged in entertainment (*The Blair Witch Project*, 1999, with televised "documentaries" and missing-person flyers) and was adopted by brands.

The case analyzed: *The Hunger Games: Catching Fire* (2013). The campaign began with teaser billboards in April, for a film that opened in November. On Tumblr, the studio created *Capitol Couture*, an online fashion magazine of the fictional capital, with links to Facebook, Twitter, YouTube and Instagram. A section called "Citizen Activity" encouraged fans to post their own fashion creations, turning them into brand ambassadors. Results: the Capitol's Facebook page had over 10 million likes, over 850,000 followers on Twitter, $158.1 million on the opening weekend and $864.9 million globally.

The **four Ps of transmedia**:

| P | What it requires | In *The Hunger Games* |
|---|---|---|
| **Pervasive** | the story stays coherent as it expands | excitement built online and offline as the release approached |
| **Persistent** | continuous presence across social, digital and traditional channels | the distinction between reality and fiction blurred |
| **Participatory** | challenges, fan-created content, active sharing | fans helped shape the story world |
| **Personalized** | fans co-create their experience | identity cards, victors' contests, access to limited information |

## Application

- When you plan an integrated campaign, write for each channel which part of the story it tells and what action it asks for, instead of adapting the same spot.
- Design a space for participation from the start (what can fans create?).
- Start from a core fan base that will spread the story, then expand.

## Example

*Capitol Couture* did not promote the film directly; it treated the fictional world as a real one. That is exactly what transmedia asks: the channel does something the trailer cannot do.

## Questions

- Can consumer goods brands without a fictional world do transmedia? What would the "world" of a dairy brand be?
- What does narrative coherence across ten channels cost, and who guards it?
