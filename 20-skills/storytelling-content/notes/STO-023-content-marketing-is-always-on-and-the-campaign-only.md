# Content marketing is "always on" and the campaign only highlights it: content contains what customers want to use, not what the brand wants to sell

> **The idea in one sentence:** content marketing constantly creates and distributes valuable, relevant content for a clearly defined audience, with the objective of profitable customer action; it is a permanent activity that campaigns only highlight.

## Explanation

**The definition.** Zahay and her coauthors take the Content Marketing Institute's definition: a strategic marketing approach of creating and distributing valuable, relevant and consistent content to attract and acquire a clearly defined audience, with the objective of driving profitable customer action. Jennifer Rowley's table explains why: in traditional media, space and time are expensive commodities and communication is *push*; online, space is cheap, **information is everything**, and communication is *pull* and interactive.

**Three sources converge.** Kotler and his coauthors (SRC-004) give the shortest formula: an advertisement contains what the brand wants to convey to sell, content contains what customers want to use. "Content Is the New Ad, #Hashtag Is the New Tagline." Barre and Gayrard-Carrera call the same mechanism a *pull* strategy: people come to the brand for information, utility or entertainment ([[STO-024-brand-content-reverses-the-direction-of-advertising|STO-024]]). Wuebben goes further and argues that content marketing has replaced advertising as the way to build relationships, but his own data show that content also needs paid distribution ([[ADV-086-content-instead-of-advertising-the-thesis-that-ads-do-not|ADV-086]]).

**Content strategy.** The authors separate it from content marketing: it is the planning of content throughout its lifecycle (alignment with business goals, production, evaluation, sunsetting and governance, meaning who decides what). A small team is a jazz ensemble that improvises; a large one is a symphony orchestra that needs sheet music. The basic difference: campaigns have a beginning and an end, while content has an indefinite life span. Hence "always on".

## Application

- The simplest test: if you remove the logo, does the content still have value for the audience? If not, it is an ad.
- In the annual proposal for a client, separate the permanent flow of content from the campaign peaks.
- Write governance rules: who approves, who updates, who takes down outdated content.

## Example

Content marketing is old: John Deere has published *The Furrow* since 1895, a magazine that solves farmers' problems without directly selling tractors. Today, the *AARP Bulletin* and *Costco Connection* reach tens of millions of readers on the same principle.

## Questions

- Is content marketing "marketing without advertising", or only advertising in another form?
- Less than 40 percent of marketers have a documented content strategy (CMI). What do the rest risk?
