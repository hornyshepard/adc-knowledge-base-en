# The point of mutuality is the topic that both the brand and the audience want to talk about

> **The idea in one sentence:** brand content engages only when it is about a topic the audience finds interesting and on which it accepts that the brand has the right to speak; it is not about the product, but it is not about just anything either.

## Explanation

Aly Richards and Scott McLean start from a simple observation: a good conversation needs something to talk about that both people want to engage about. Most brands have not moved past the stage of talking about themselves. Even the storytelling fashion often means only moving from "buy our product" to "let me tell you about us", which is still broadcasting, not dialogue.

The authors call the **point of mutuality** (POM) the subject matter that the brand and the audience can engage about together. It can come from four sources:

| Source | Example from the book |
|---|---|
| The product | a bank selling mortgages talks about **property** (planning law, how to choose a good builder, what insulation is best for a 1970s property) |
| The audience's goal | a greetings card company talks about making **occasions** special |
| The brand's values | the book gives no direct example; the closest is the recruiter of engineers that chose as its topic the overlooked voice of UK engineers |
| Sponsorship | a sponsor of the World Cup offers travel advice and guidance on the stadiums |

The book's warning: sponsorship with no real link to what the brand does struggles to engage the audience. And changing the POM is the hardest thing to do after launch, because the platforms and the content are built around it.

## Application

- Test a POM with two questions: *would the audience look for this topic without us?* and *would it accept us as a voice in it?* If one answer is "no", look for another.
- Look for the POM by **listening**, not only by brainstorming: what people ask in forums, which articles they read most.
- In the brief, write the POM as a territory, not as a message: "property", not "our mortgage is a good deal".

## Example

On the Coca-Cola forum, a common question was the recipe for a Coca-Cola cake. The company created an area devoted to food and recipes, a topic that might have seemed obvious but that nobody had chosen before they listened.

## Questions

- What would the POM be for a car insurance brand? And for a beer?
- When does content "about the shared topic" become so far from the brand that it no longer helps sales?
