# The Channel 4 case, "Superhumans" → "Super. Human": when your own successful idea creates a new stereotype

> **The idea in one sentence:** a platform that changed perceptions of disability ended up producing its own cliché, and Channel 4 had the courage to break its successful idea.

## Explanation

Eoin McLaughlin (deputy executive creative director at Channel 4) tells the story of the campaign's evolution in three stages in the book:

| Year | The move | Details |
|---|---|---|
| 2012 | Flipping the perception: Paralympians are not inferior amateurs who tried their best, but "superhumans" | the business problem: the Paralympics were seen as an Olympic sideshow, with terrible viewing figures; the execution: elite athletes with swagger, not a campaign "about disability" |
| 2016 | Broadening the definition: "superhuman" for all disabled people | the largest disabled cast ever assembled, more presenters and film crew living with disability |
| 2021 | Breaking the word: "Super. Human" | after talking with disabled journalists, artists, athletes, academics and comedians: the campaign had spawned a new stereotype, "the invincible disabled sports terminator" |

In 2021 the team showed what made the athletes *human*: screaming toddlers, popped blisters, frustrations. The background is the term *inspiration porn*, coined by the journalist Stella Young: images of disabled people used to motivate everyone else (the thought that things could be worse), which objectify one group of people for the benefit of another.

McLaughlin says the secret was the tone of voice: Channel 4 does work with a purpose, but with a sense of humor. Humor humanizes, and the pity-me piano music of earnest advertising is not inclusion, it is othering. His lessons: listen widely before any purpose-driven project; to solve a problem, you change perceptions; a serious subject does not mean you have to take yourself seriously; when you've done something well, don't be afraid to break it next time.

The stated results: three Cannes Lions Film Grand Prix, three D&AD Black Pencils, the campaign taught on the UK school syllabus and used by the United Nations.

## Application

- Re-evaluate a successful platform regularly with the people it represents, not only with the campaign metrics.
- Put a "whom did we listen to?" field in every purpose brief.
- Look for the tone that takes the subject out of the register of pity (see [[IDE-019-humor-irony-and-satire-overturn-expectation-and-create|IDE-019]]).
