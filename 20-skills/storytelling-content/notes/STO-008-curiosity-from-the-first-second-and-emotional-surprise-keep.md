# Curiosity from the first second and emotional surprise keep the audience to the end

> **The idea in one sentence:** an ad does not have the time of a movie, so it has to raise a question immediately and turn the emotion sharply just when the audience thinks it knows where the story is going.

## Explanation

**Curiosity.** Braun and Landa bet that we have all watched a weak movie to the end just to find out how it ends. Advertising has to create this effect right off: Will she or won't she? How did that car back up without a driver? What does it mean to "Dream Crazy"? The book cites a study at the University of California Davis: participants who were highly curious about a fact were 30 percent more likely to recall it, and curiosity activated the release of dopamine, which contributes to a stronger memory.

Their example of curiosity turned into action is "The Whopper Detour". Burger King (FCB New York) wanted downloads for its app with order-ahead functionality. The offer: a Whopper for one cent, but only if you ordered it on the app within 600 feet of a McDonald's. The reported result: number one on both app stores and 1.5 million downloads in nine days.

**Emotional surprise.** Yousuke Ozawa (UltraSuperNew Tokyo) says it is all about emotional surprises: just when you think the story is going down a certain path, it makes a quick turn toward an unexpected destination. His recipe: first ask how you want people to feel, then see if you can get them to feel the opposite emotion. If you want to make people laugh, see if you can also make them cry. Andrew Stanton (Pixar) makes the same point about a sense of wonder. The book's examples: KFC in Australia turned into an eleven-course fine-dining experience; a dress filled with snacks at the MTV VMAs for Instacart.

The logic is the same as in Ingledew: the viewer expects a pattern, and breaking it produces attention. "Predictability is the death of any good stories" (Guan Hin Tay, BBDO Singapore).

## Application

- Write down the question the first shot asks. If the first shot asks no question, the audience scrolls on.
- Work on an emotional curve with two points: the starting emotion and its opposite at the turn.
- Tie the surprise to the product: a twist with no link to the brand is remembered without the brand (see *linkage* in [[BRF-026-adplan-six-questions-for-an-execution-that-is-on-brief-but|BRF-026]]).
