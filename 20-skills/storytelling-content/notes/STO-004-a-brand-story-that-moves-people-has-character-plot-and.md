# A brand story that moves people has character, plot and metaphor

> **The idea in one sentence:** a brand story persuades when the brand is a character that symbolizes a social movement, follows a recognizable plot and rests on a deep metaphor in the audience's unconscious mind.

## Explanation

Marketing 3.0 takes up Robert McKee's distinction: you can convince people with facts and numbers or with stories that engage their emotions, and the second way is more effective. Steve Jobs always began with the story ("1984", the iPod as a way to carry your entire music library in your pocket) and only then moved on to the features. The complete story of a brand, though, is continuously rewritten by employees, channel partners and consumers, so it has to be authentic from the beginning.

Following Holt, the authors describe a model with three components, each filled from a different source:

| Component | Source | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Character** | Douglas Holt | the brand becomes the symbol of a movement that addresses a problem in society: The Body Shop = social activism, Disney = family ideals, Wikipedia = collaboration, eBay = user governance |
| **Plot** | Chip and Dan Heath, *Made to Stick* | **challenge** (David and Goliath: the fair-trade farmers at The Body Shop), **connection** (bridging gaps of race, age, gender: Facebook), **creativity** (the brilliant MacGyver-type hero: Branson at Virgin) |
| **Metaphor** | Gerald and Lindsay Zaltman, ZMET | deep metaphors, encoded at a very young age; the "Seven Giants" (about 70% of all metaphors): balance, transformation, journey, container, connection, resource, control |

In ZMET, participants make collages from pictures, and the interviewer probes them systematically to bring the metaphor to light. Examples from the book: the container can be protection (the pension fund) or a trap (poverty); the iPhone was told as a *resource*; in an age of pandemics, people talk about *control* over their own immunity.

## Application

- Before the concept, answer three questions in the brief: what movement does the brand symbolize? what type of plot is it? what metaphor does the target audience use when talking about the problem?
- Do not make up stories: most visionary leaders, the authors say, spot the ones already floating around in everyday life.
- Check whether the campaign's metaphor is compatible with the audience's. Stories with compatible metaphors are perceived as true.

## Example

For a company that helps people lose weight, the *balance* metaphor ("overweight") gives the story its language; for a bank in a recession, the *journey* metaphor ("an uphill task") fits better.

## Questions

- Can several types of plot be combined in a single campaign without diluting the character?
- ZMET is a costly qualitative method. What "pocket" version can an agency team use?
