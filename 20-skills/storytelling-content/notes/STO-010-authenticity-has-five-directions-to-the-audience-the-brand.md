# Authenticity has five directions: to the audience, the brand, the purpose, the zeitgeist and the team that produces the story

> **The idea in one sentence:** "authentic" is not a tone but a fit on five axes, and the audience recognizes it by voice, tone, demonstration, consistency and transparency.

## Explanation

Braun and Landa start from the audience's distrust. The book cites research according to which only 13 percent of Americans have a great deal of trust in advertising, and 67 percent agree that once a brand loses their trust, it cannot be restored. For them, an ad is authentic on five axes:

| Direction | The question | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| To the audience | does it validate the audience's desires, aspirations and needs? | the LEGO playground in West Harlem, built from the ideas of the neighborhood's children |
| To the brand | is it consistent with the brand's values? | "Moldy Whopper": Burger King shows its burger rotting as proof that it has no preservatives |
| To the purpose | does it serve a real cause of the company? | Patagonia, "Don't Buy This Jacket" |
| To the zeitgeist | is it relevant now, in the culture of the moment? | Burberry and the Sylvanian drama series of animal figurines on TikTok |
| In creation and production | is it made by people from the community it talks about? | Pure Leaf's "No Grants", produced by a production company owned by a Black woman |

Harsh Kapadia (MRM) sums it up: authentic means knowing who you are. If your brand is quirky, it stays quirky.

**How the audience perceives the truth.** The Heineken "Shutter Ads" case study (Publicis Italy, during the pandemic) gives five tools:

- **voice**: plain language, no hyperbole ("See this ad today, enjoy this bar tomorrow");
- **tone**: reverence for the future of the bars, not a celebration of the brand;
- **demonstration**: the ads appeared on the very shutters of the closed bars, which received the money;
- **consistency**: a program, not a one-off;
- **transparency**: the amount stated publicly, 7.5 million euros.

The reported result: 40 percent more media value than traditional OOH, and every bar in the initiative reopened.

## Application

- Run the idea through the five directions; for each "no", write down which fact is missing.
- In the copy, cut the hyperbole and add a verifiable fact (an amount, a number, a place).
- Check who is at the table: does the team reflect the audience the story talks about?

## Questions

- In the same book, 59 percent of respondents say they prefer brands to remain neutral on social issues. How does that square with "authenticity to the purpose"?
- Can a new brand be "consistent"?
