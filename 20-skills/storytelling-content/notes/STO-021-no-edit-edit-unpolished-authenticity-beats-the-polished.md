# No-edit edit: unpolished authenticity beats the polished image film

> **The idea in one sentence:** on social platforms, content that is produced professionally but has a snapshot aesthetic ("no-edit edit") performs better than expensive, perfectly polished image films.

## Explanation

Mueller and Rajaram give a key example: The Guardian found as early as 2018 that polished video was performing less well on Instagram, and said goodbye to it. The term "no-edit edit" means professionally edited content that deliberately keeps an unpolished look, close to the quick stories users post. The authors' arguments:

- You produce **with the same tools as your followers** (smartphone, the platform's editors), and so you show that you understand their language.
- You produce **more content, faster and closer to trends**.
- You do not lock your annual budget into a single product (a corporate video that may stay on screen for years, on a single platform).
- The expert Sascha Gottschalk confirms the trend: algorithms prioritize authentic testimonials about everyday experiences, even shaky ones.

The authors do not say that anything goes, though. The difference between amateur and professional remains: original content, executed with planning and precision, with visible multimedia competence. Users forgive slightly shaky footage if it fits the style, but they leave at once when the audio is noisy or too quiet. The authors also cite the view that companies will rely on a mix of polished content (on the website, YouTube, the online store) and unpolished content (in stories and toks).

## Application

- Keep big productions for long-form content and for the channels that require them; for stories and toks, shoot fast, but with clean sound.
- Invest in audio first (a microphone, a quiet room), then in the image.
- The making-of and the behind-the-scenes of a big shoot often perform better than the final material.

## Example

On TikTok, the making-of stories of Younes Zarou and of @HerrAnwalt have often achieved more reach than the original clips they referred to.

## Questions

- Hasn't "authentic" become a manufactured aesthetic too? How do you stay credible when the unpolished look is calculated?
- Which brands (luxury, automotive) lose if they give up polished production? Where does the principle stop?
