# Each multimedia layer must add new meaning, not repeat

> **The idea in one sentence:** in a micro-story of a few seconds, text, image, sound, GIF or poll are stacked as layers, and each has to say something different from the others, or the story becomes redundant and loses attention.

## Explanation

Mueller and Rajaram describe storytelling as "connecting the dots": you link people, facts and things that seemed disconnected into a meaningful pattern. On social media this happens simultaneously, through multimedia layers in a single card or clip: a photo with a quote and a commenting GIF, a split screen with two videos and subtitles, a song under a still image. The authors' rules:

- **Each element adds new narrative, information or context**; duplication bores and "throws" the user out of the story.
- **Less is more**: only as many sequences and effects as needed; effects that do not support the story and the brand are gimmicks.
- **You build the story by anticipating the user's W questions** (who, how, where, when), and the most important is "why?" (why is this happening, why am I telling you this, why should you watch on). Motives are invisible, so the "why" builds tension and conversation.
- **Faces and voices in close-up** are the layers with the strongest empathetic and immersive effect, but a close-up without context looks like an abstract work; the context has to come before or after.
- In explainers, the voice says something other than what the image shows, or the image shows concretely what the voice explains in the abstract.

## Application

- For each card, note on the storyboard what each layer adds; if two layers say the same thing, cut one.
- Visualize everything: audio becomes video with a waveform or a photo gallery, text becomes a poster.
- Contrast and legibility: white or black text remains the most accessible.

## Example

"13 Minutes to the Moon" (BBC) tells the story of the Apollo flight through the original voices of the astronauts and their relatives, sound effects and Hans Zimmer's music. Each audio layer adds a dimension the others do not have.

## Questions

- When is redundancy useful (for example, subtitling the voice for people who watch without sound)?
- How do you apply the principle in a print ad, where you have only two layers: image and headline?
