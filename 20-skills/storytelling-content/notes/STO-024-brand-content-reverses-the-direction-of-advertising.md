# Brand content reverses the direction of advertising: consumers come to the brand for information, utility or entertainment, if the theme is legitimate for the brand and can be renewed

> **The idea in one sentence:** instead of interrupting people, the brand produces content worth looking for and sharing; the content has to be credibly linked to what the brand is and has to be renewable over time.

## Explanation

*La boîte à outils de la publicité* describes brand content as a **pull** strategy (*stratégie pull*): people go to the brand to find information, practical tips, humor or dreams. The authors also give a six-step process (objectives, the link with the brand's storytelling, copy strategy and tone, formats, consumer participation, measurement), compared with the Kotler and Zahay versions in [[STO-025-content-marketing-strategy-is-a-cycle-objectives-and|STO-025]].

The distinctive contribution of the French school is its **rules of legitimacy**:

- the theme has to be close to the brand's *savoir-faire*, universe and history;
- the universe has to be rich enough to last over time: 3 or 4 founding, recurring elements (*éléments fondateurs et récurrents*), taken up again and varied;
- the content cannot have only a commercial aim: it has to bring something extra to the person who shares it;
- a concept that runs out of steam is stopped.

Extensions from the same family: **brand culture** (brands become cultural objects that compete with other forms of entertainment), **UGC** (when fans showcase the brand, the impact is stronger than that of brand content) and **native advertising** (*publicité contextuelle*: sponsored content built into the editorial flow but clearly flagged as advertising, so that the reader's trust is not lost).

## Application

- The utility test: would anyone look for this content if the brand did not pay for it? Would they send the link to a friend?
- Tie the format to the brand's expertise: Leroy Merlin gives DIY tips, Red Bull strong sensations, Dior and Chanel dreams.
- Plan the renewal paths from the start, without too tight a calendar.

## Example

**Oasis** (agency Marcel, Grand Prix Stratégies du Brand Content 2012): the fruit characters, the parodies ("Captain Ananamerica") and the wordplay on current events gathered more than 3.4 million fans on Facebook. **CIC** with Stéphane de Groodt got 700,000 views in a week with the episode about the "virement" (bank transfer), talking about banking products through play.

## Questions

- How do you tell good brand content from a long ad posted on YouTube?
- Is native advertising that is not clearly flagged an ethical problem or only a problem of trust?
