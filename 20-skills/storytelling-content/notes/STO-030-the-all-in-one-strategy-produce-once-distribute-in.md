# The all-in-one strategy: produce once, distribute in fragments over time

> **The idea in one sentence:** an effective content campaign starts from platform-independent material, produces many native micro-stories for each channel at once and distributes them over a long timeline, mirroring the user's journey across platforms.

## Explanation

The term "all-in-one" comes from Sascha Gottschalk (FMD); Mueller and Rajaram also call it the "integrated communication strategy". The premise: users travel from platform to platform, like a flock of birds that stops at different stations. The storyteller therefore builds a **story journey** that mirrors the customer journey. The steps the authors propose:

1. Find the **agnostic material**, the substance that works independently of format (for example, the fates of the people at the arrivals gate of an airport).
2. Define the **anatomical story moment**.
3. Develop **platform-specific** micro-stories and serial formats (tone, aspect ratio, hashtags, length).
4. Do the cross-media editorial planning over a long timeline; if useful, plan collaborations with influencers.
5. In parallel with production, publish short live stories.
6. Make a recording schedule, a production plan and a distribution plan for all the micro-stories.
7. Measure success on each platform, recycle the elements that work and plan new material starting from them.
8. At the end, bring the micro-stories together in a **long-form** piece (a multimedia report, a landing page, a film), which becomes the campaign's "showroom".

The authors also offer three factors for choosing platforms: **time** (how long the user stays with a story), **immersion** (how much they can interact) and **depth** (how much context and research fit).

## Application

- At a shoot, film the same subject in 9:16, 1:1/4:5 and 16:9; you save on post-production.
- Plan the conversion (the sale) only after the relationship has been built on the social platforms.
- Being present on several platforms reduces dependence on changes to a single algorithm.

## Example

The Boston Globe developed more than 600 stories, over the course of years, from a single investigation into abuse in the local archdiocese. The New York Times Magazine's #1619Project website gathers the micro-stories first published on other platforms (podcasts, photo essays, video) into a long-form report. Honda reports an increase of more than 85% in the time users spend with the stories in its "Engine Room", built in Shorthand.

## Questions

- What is the difference between "all-in-one" and the classic concepts of IMC or content repurposing? What does it add?
- When is it better to be excellent on a single platform than present on all of them?
