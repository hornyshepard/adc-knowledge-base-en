# In brand storytelling, the brand is oxygen: everywhere, but never the protagonist

> **The idea in one sentence:** a good brand story is born at the intersection of the brand promise and a customer's pain point or passion, and the brand enters it only as the solution to the conflict, without taking the hero's place.

## Explanation

Mohanbir Sawhney starts from the idea that stories are universal (Christopher Booker's seven basic plots: Overcoming the Monster, Rags to Riches, The Quest, Voyage and Return, Comedy, Tragedy, Rebirth), that they inspire, stick and simplify. Nike relies on Overcoming the Monster (the "monster inside"), Johnnie Walker's "Keep Walking" on The Quest, and Cisco's "Circle Story" explains the Internet of Everything like a children's rhyme.

The central rule: the brand should be like oxygen, "present everywhere, but invisible." It enters the story by resolving a conflict or removing an obstacle. In "Reunion" (Google), a granddaughter uses Google to search for her grandfather's childhood friend, the two separated by the India-Pakistan partition of 1947; Google appears seamlessly, with no overt commerciality. In the Humira story (AbbVie), the protagonist is John, virtually bedridden with rheumatoid arthritis, who is able to walk his daughter down the aisle after six months of therapy; the disease is the "villain", the drug is the "knight in shining armor".

Stories can be found anywhere in the organization: the origins (HP's garage), the people (Microsoft Story Labs), the customers (Toyota "Auto-Biography"), a cause (TOMS), behind the scenes ("A day in the life of a fish" at Jerónimo Martins). They require an editorial process (a team, a "content czar", a database, distribution): brands have become publishers.

Five ways to tell a powerful story:
1. **Be yourself**: authenticity starts from a purpose that is lived (Microsoft #MakeWhatsNext).
2. **Be human**: not B2B or B2C, but H2H (FedEx "I Am FedEx").
3. **Be pervasive**: "surround-sound storytelling" across several channels (Dove Self-Esteem Project).
4. **Be persistent**: one theme with variations, as in jazz (P&G "Thank You, Mom", from one Olympics to the next).
5. **Be visual**: show, don't tell ("This is your brain on drugs").

## Application

- Build the story starting from the protagonist (the customer), their obstacle and the precise role of the brand in resolving it.
- Run the "oxygen" test: if you take out the logo, is the story still interesting? And if you take the brand out of the action, is the conflict still resolved?
- Look in the client's organization for real stories of employees and customers before inventing one.

## Example

"#LikeAGirl" (Always) never mentions the product: it attacks the stereotype of what it means to do something "like a girl", starting from research that showed girls' self-confidence dropping at puberty.
