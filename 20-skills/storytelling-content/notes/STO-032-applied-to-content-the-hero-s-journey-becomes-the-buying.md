# Applied to content, the hero's journey becomes the buying cycle seen by the customer: engagement, purchase, then loyalty and advocacy

> **The idea in one sentence:** Richards and McLean show that the three acts of the hero's journey ([[STO-002-the-hero-s-journey-the-protagonist-leaves-the-ordinary|STO-002]]) map onto the buyer's real path, so a content strategy can be planned act by act, with the customer as the hero and the brand as the mentor.

## Explanation

The authors "translate" their scheme into business language:

| Act | Stages in story language | Stages in business language |
|---|---|---|
| I. Engage the Audience | Ordinary World → Call to Adventure → Refusal of the Call (fear, uncertainty and doubt: **FUD**) → Mentor/Sage (the proof) → Cross the Threshold | attract the audience, engage it while taking into account FUD and the need for proof, help it reach and cross the threshold |
| II. The Buying Cycle | Tests & Challenges → Approach to the Centre of Unknown → The Ordeal | compare → review → buy |
| III. Loyalty, Retention & Advocacy | The Reward → The Road Back → Resurrection → Return with the Elixir | receive → experience → acknowledge success → advocate |

Their conclusion: the hero's journey "is nothing more than the complete buying cycle", seen from the audience's point of view, not the brand's. Two corrections follow at once. The real path is rarely linear: people doubt, compare, go back, drop out or wander off to take part in a different story; that is why storytelling has to be paired with audience journey maps by persona. And in **B2B** the emphasis shifts: in Act I what matters is **resistance** (engagement is hard to turn into a two-way conversation) and **influence** (credibility from third parties), and the threshold is very concrete: securing a meeting.

The authors apply the scheme to the reader as well: the chapter on internal obstacles is written as an answer to the marketing director's "Refusal of the Call".

## Application

- Place each item of the content plan in an act and a stage; the gaps show at once (usually Act III is empty).
- Plan content explicitly for FUD: what fears the audience has before the threshold and what proof you give it.
- In Act III, do not switch to invoices and plain customer service; continue the content pillar that brought the customer in.

## Example

Chordiant, a B2B technology company, set its sights on winning Lloyds TSB (2008). It identified the key buyers and influencers at the bank, produced thought leadership for their exact pains and activated it through the channels they consumed: press, direct marketing, events. The deal was won; the authors worked directly on the project.

## Questions

- What role is left for the brand if the customer is the hero: mentor, tool, ally?
- Where is the "Refusal of the Call" for someone who might sign up for a gym?
