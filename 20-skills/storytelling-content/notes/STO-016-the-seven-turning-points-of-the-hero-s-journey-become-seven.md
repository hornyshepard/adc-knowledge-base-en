# The seven turning points of the hero's journey become seven brand questions

> **The idea in one sentence:** the dramatic turning points of a long narrative can be translated into seven working questions that clarify the "why" of a social story about a product, a service or a person.

## Explanation

Mueller and Rajaram observe that dramatic moments worth telling occur because something is missing: a flaw, a dilemma, a challenge that can't be solved with existing resources. In the classic narrative, the hero goes through seven turning points. The authors transfer them to social storytelling as planning questions:

| The classic turning point | The question for the social story |
|---|---|
| 1. Weakness and need | What problem for users am I solving with my story / my product? |
| 2. Desire | What benefit do others get? |
| 3. Opponent | What disadvantages could users see? How are my direct competitors positioning themselves? |
| 4. Plan | What call-to-action do I include? What do users need to know, what's next? |
| 5. Battle | How can I best use a hashtag challenge? |
| 6. Self-revelation | What are my strengths and the focus topics I can build my brand on? |
| 7. New equilibrium (arrival) | How do I celebrate my followers, and with which influencers and communities do I network? |

If the answers (the "why") are clear from the beginning, it is easier to anticipate and plan the dramatic moments of the entire story.

## Application

- Use the grid as a debriefing exercise before a social campaign: each row becomes a section of the plan.
- Questions 3 and 6 tie the story to positioning (competition, strengths); 4, 5 and 7 tie it to the platform's mechanisms (CTA, challenge, community).
- You don't have to answer all of them in the same post; spread them across episodes.

## Example

For a travel vlog about Wengen (Switzerland), the weakness can be a bet that triggers the trip; the plan, the online booking shown step by step; the battle, the ascent on the cogwheel railroad; and the arrival, the invitation to the community to tell their own routes.

## Questions

- Is this translation still dramaturgy, or already a marketing plan? What is lost from the narrative tension?
- Which turning point is missing from most brand posts (hint: the opponent)?
