# Witness or participant: three questions for empowering the audience, from respect to super fans

> **The idea in one sentence:** an empowered audience becomes an engaged audience, and engagement makes the story worth sharing; it all starts with respect for the audience's intelligence.

## Explanation

**Respect.** Braun and Landa start from the assumption that the audience is intelligent and has values and opinions that matter. The *New York Times* "Truth" campaign (2017) defined its target as the "curious reader", who puts truth before sensationalism and sees a subscription as support for journalists. The reported results: twenty-four hours after launch, "a 100 percent increase in its subscriber base" versus the previous six weeks (the book's wording), and the best quarter for subscriber growth in the paper's history.

**Empowerment.** Traditional media assign the audience the passive role of a viewer. Beyond basic interactivity, a brand can grant the audience the ability to build, create, celebrate. Three questions:

| Question | What you check |
|---|---|
| Is the audience a witness or a participant? | a lean-back medium (TV) or a lean-in medium (mobile, experiential) |
| Is the experience personalized? | each person can affect their own experience instead of getting one-size-fits-all |
| Is the audience's voice amplified? | the brand showcases, celebrates and shares what the community does, at a scale the individual cannot reach |

Examples: Cox's "Drawn Closer" turned a middle school's canceled play into an animated film acted out by the students from home; Heinz and Activision ("Hidden Spots") mapped the in-game sanctuaries in *Call of Duty* where gamers could snack in safety without getting "killed", and the gamer community expanded the maps.

**Super fans.** Some audiences (soccer fans, Comic-Con fans, *Game of Thrones* fans) are not satisfied with just being witnesses, and they will tear apart a brand that is not authentic to their universe. Hyundai's app for *The Walking Dead*, where you built a zombie survival machine, was rejected by fans for unrealistic details. The agency gave them the tools to design the weapons themselves, and the app reached 150,000 builds.

## Application

- For every idea, rewrite the audience's role: from "watches" to "does".
- Leave a space the audience fills in (a map, a build, a remix) and plan how you will amplify the results.
- With super fans, research the universe in detail before launch; they will check.

## Questions

- When does participation become free labor asked of the audience?
- What is the difference between a brand that "listens" and one that only asks for user-generated content?
