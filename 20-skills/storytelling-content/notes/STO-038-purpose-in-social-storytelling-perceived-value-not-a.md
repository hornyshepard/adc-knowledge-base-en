# Purpose in social storytelling: perceived value, not a declared mission

> **The idea in one sentence:** on social media, "purpose" means that users feel, at that moment, that the story adds value for them (information, inspiration, entertainment), and the book's experts disagree on whether a deeper meaning is required.

## Explanation

Mueller and Rajaram define purpose as a sense of meaning that we share with others; stories are ideal for it because they connect us to individual and collective memories. The two practitioner voices in the book, however, set out a useful tension:

| Tim Hendrik Walter (@HerrAnwalt) | Adil Sbai (weCreate) |
|---|---|
| Purpose = perceived or factual value: information, inspiration, entertainment, ideally a mixture of at least two of these three. | Purpose is only *one* path to success. |
| It can't be sketched out on the drawing board: it emerges over time, iteratively, with the community. An estimated 90% of his ideas come from the comments; he keeps a list of 600 real-world questions. | Dancers and comedians on TikTok succeed without deeper messages; primarily monetary goals are not objectionable either. |
| Personal messages from followers (a young person who grew up without a father) show him what the content means to them. | For creators like "Mr. Lawyer", however, only meaningfulness creates valuable messages; asking yourself the question of purpose is half the battle. |

The authors' conclusion: algorithms are designed to identify shared value so that users keep coming back; purpose is "the definitive nutrient for a loyal community", but every brand or creator has to find it for themselves.

## Application

- Before writing "the brand's purpose", ask: what value does the user get in the next 30 seconds?
- Read the comments as a brief: people's real questions are raw material for content.
- Accept that pure entertainment is a legitimate value; not every brand has to save the world.

## Example

Walter reached more than three million followers on TikTok in 2021 with one-minute legal advice videos, answering his community's concrete questions (such as at what point you can get married).

## Questions

- Are "brand purpose" in the strategic sense (a social mission) and "purpose" in the sense of value for the user the same thing? What confusions arise in agencies?
- What does a brand risk when it declares a social purpose without offering concrete value in its content?
