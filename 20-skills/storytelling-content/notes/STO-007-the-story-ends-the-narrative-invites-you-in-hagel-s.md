# The story ends, the narrative invites you in: Hagel's distinction between story and narrative

> **The idea in one sentence:** a story is closed: it has a beginning, a middle and a resolution, and the hero is someone else. A narrative is open-ended: it describes an opportunity that has not yet materialized, and the resolution depends on what the listener chooses.

## Explanation

Wuebben takes the distinction from John Hagel (*The Power of Pull*):

| | Story | Narrative |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | self-contained, with a beginning, a middle and a resolution | open-ended, no resolution yet |
| About whom | the storyteller or other people ("over there") | the listener, that is "you" |
| Content | what happened | an opportunity that has not yet materialized |
| Effect | attention and emotional engagement | a call to action: "How it ends is up to you." |

Hagel observes that over the centuries millions of people have given their lives for narratives (religious, revolutionary or social), which shows how powerful they are. Very few companies have used them. Wuebben's example is Apple with "Think different": the narrative was not about Apple but about the people Apple was trying to speak to. Wozniak and Jobs *lived* it, as examples of people thinking differently.

In chapter 2, Wuebben raises the idea to the level of strategy. Trends alone are not enough: values, implications, systems, design and aspirations must combine into **guiding narratives**, which explain the world in which the trends exist and where we are going.

## Application

- When you write a brand platform, test its manifesto: is the audience the protagonist? Does it end with a question or an invitation, and not with "and that is why we are the best"?
- For copy: turn statements about the brand ("we believe…") into statements about a possibility for the audience ("you can…").
- A narrative demands coherence: the brand has to live it in its behavior, not only declare it.

## Example

In "Don't Buy This Jacket", Patagonia does not tell what the company did. It opens a choice for the reader: buy or repair? The resolution stays with the reader.

## Questions

- Can an open-ended narrative be too vague to sell something concrete?
- What happens to a narrative when the brand's behavior contradicts it?
