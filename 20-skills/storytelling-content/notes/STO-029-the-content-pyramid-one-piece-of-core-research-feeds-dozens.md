# The content pyramid: one piece of core research feeds dozens of derivative and promotional pieces

> **The idea in one sentence:** content is organized as a pyramid: at the top, a few high-effort pieces (research, e-book, white paper); in the middle, their derivatives; at the base, many quick posts; and repurposing links the levels.

## Explanation

The Curata pyramid, taken up by the authors, has five levels organized into three main parts:

| Level | Content | Effort / frequency |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (top) | primary or secondary research and thought leadership: books, e-books, white papers | high effort, rare |
| 2–4 (derivatives) | long-form blog posts and presentations; infographics and slideshares; short-form blog posts and contributed content | medium |
| 5 (base) | social media posts and promotional content | low effort, often |

The **core content** at the top is made up of the insights and research the brand has developed and, above all, it must embody the mission and values of the business. The **derivative assets** allow more focused, more frequent, more targeted and more visual pieces; they improve reach and frequency because the same material can be repurposed for different channels (a blog with an embedded video that then goes to YouTube, an infographic built from the data in a set of slides). The base of the pyramid is the best place for "humanizing" the brand: timely, emotional, humorous, as long as it stays true to the themes at the top. "Content marketing has a voracious appetite," the authors say, and repurposing is essential. Connors (SRC-030) gives the same recipe: a blog post becomes an infographic, a video, a podcast episode and a series of social media posts.

## Application

- Plan backward: first decide the core piece of the quarter (a study, a report), then the list of derivatives and posts.
- Put "tweet this" boxes in reports and articles, so that quotes travel easily.
- Check whether the quick posts at the base still point to the values at the top or have turned into noise.

## Example

A research report provides almost limitless content opportunities: videos, slide shows, infographics, blog posts and dozens of posts; in B2B, the same corporate material can become a webinar, and a video can sit both on the website and on YouTube.

## Questions

- What does an agency do when the client has nothing to put at the top of the pyramid (no research, no expertise)?
- Is there a risk that repurposing bores the audience that follows the brand on several channels?
