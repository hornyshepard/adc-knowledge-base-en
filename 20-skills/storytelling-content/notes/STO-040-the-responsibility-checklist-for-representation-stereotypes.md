# The responsibility checklist for representation: stereotypes, power, appropriation, setting and a diverse team at every stage

> **The idea in one sentence:** advertising sets out to persuade a mass audience, so any stereotype in the image or the copy is multiplied; that is why every idea goes through a list of questions before production.

## Explanation

Braun and Landa propose moving from *storytelling* to what they call *storiestelling*: many voices, many perspectives, diverse experiences, told best by the people who live them. Their arguments are both ethical and commercial: the book cites a Microsoft study (70 percent of Gen Z are more trusting of brands that represent diversity in ads) and a Nielsen analysis (just 1 percent of the primetime TV ads analyzed included disability-related themes, visuals or topics).

The checklist has four areas:

| Area | Sample questions |
|---|---|
| The idea, the images, the copy | Does anything perpetuate a stereotype or trope? Have you tried swapping the image or audience for another race, gender, age, sexual orientation or disability, and did any stereotypes emerge? Is an immutable human characteristic used as a punchline? Have you sought feedback from a diverse group? Have you considered intersecting identities? |
| Power | Who holds the power in your representation? Does the solution build on a stereotype of who holds power and who is subordinate? Would any group be marginalized by the message? |
| Appropriation | Are you employing another's narrative or culture? Is it yours to appropriate? Are you respecting traditions held sacred? Is anything represented out of context? Is credit properly given? |
| Storyline and settings | What is the objective of choosing a metropolitan, suburban or rural location? Is any discriminatory language employed? Gender stereotypes (women gathering in a cafe, men on a basketball court)? Can real families be used rather than families "built" in casting? |

Inclusion has to be built into the **whole process**: ideation (teams, characters, research), preproduction (casting, location), production (directors, photography, voiceover, makeup, costume), postproduction (editing, retouching, music, accessibility, testing), events and distribution. One associate creative director of color at the table does not represent all voices.

## Application

- Make the list a formal step before the client presentation, not an optional discussion.
- Apply the swap to every character in the script.
- Plan for backlash: taking a stand draws criticism (the book gives the examples of Ulta Beauty and Bud Light).

## Questions

- The list is written from an American perspective. What questions would you add for Romania (ethnicity, region, religion, social class)?
- How do you apply the list to humor without killing the joke (see [[IDE-019-humor-irony-and-satire-overturn-expectation-and-create|IDE-019]])?
