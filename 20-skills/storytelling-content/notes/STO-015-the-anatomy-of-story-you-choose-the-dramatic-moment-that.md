# The anatomy of story: you choose the dramatic moment that tells the whole

> **The idea in one sentence:** an epic story does not fit into a 10–15-second format, so the storyteller works like an anatomist: dissects the entire story, finds the moments when something culminates and tells a part for the whole (*pars pro toto*).

## Explanation

Mueller and Rajaram adapt *The Anatomy of Story* (John Truby, 2007) to social media. The method has two steps:

1. **Imagine the entire story**, from idea to end (for example, a trip: idea, planning, booking, execution, arrival, return).
2. **Identify the key dramatic moments**, the points where something happens, culminates or turns, and choose the ones that can suggest the whole, with images and sounds that activate associations.

A well-chosen detail works because the audience is connected to the "collective pool of stories" and fills in the rest on its own. The authors call these tiny fragments (a ten-second clip, a facial expression, a hashtag) **nanoparticles**. As with the levels of a computer game, stages can be skipped: what you omit now can be told later, in longer formats, on other platforms. The "unboxing" genre, invented by users, is a complete journey compressed into one moment.

## Application

- Before shooting, write the list of dramatic moments; you shoot with a target, not "everything that happens".
- Examples of pars pro toto: a shoe for a new self-cleaning material, the color of a sliced potato for sustainable agriculture, a rack railroad ride for local transport in a mountain village.
- A good close-up works as a nanoparticle, but it needs context before or after.

## Example

"The Arrivals" (SAS, 2018): from the anatomy of a flight, the makers pick out the arrival gate and film only the faces of the people waiting, at the moment the door opens. For an airline, the choice is a strong narrative surprise. The authors criticize the execution, though: a film over three minutes long, slow pacing, no one speaks except the narrator, no hashtag or challenge. A perfect moment for social, used only on YouTube and the website.

## Questions

- How do you check that the detail you chose is "understood by everyone else" and not only by the creative team?
- Does the method resemble the single-minded proposition of the brief? Where does it differ (a visual moment vs. a verbal idea)?
