# A good brand story has identifiable characters, authentic emotion, a significant moment and specific details

> **The idea in one sentence:** according to Kindra Hall (*Stories That Stick*), a story that sticks has four components (characters, emotion, moment, details); an "about us" text that misses them is a statement, not a story.

## Explanation

Zahay and her coauthors note that advice about "engaging content" usually comes down to "develop audience personas, and create relevant content for the audience", which is useless at the level of a single piece of content. They offer two tools.

The first comes from Neil Patel and Ritika Puri: the brand story is **not about the company** but about the customers and the value they get; the customers are the stars, the company is a supporting character. Consultant Heidi Cohen adds where the stories are found: in the company's heritage, in its products, in the brand (logo, mascot, causes) and in its employees.

The second is Kindra Hall's grid: (1) **identifiable characters**; (2) **authentic emotion**; (3) **a significant moment**; (4) **specific details**. Hall also proposes four types of stories for four audiences: the value story (for customers), the founder story (for investors and partners), the purpose story (for the team) and the customer story (told by the customers themselves).

**Other lists, same direction.** Batat (SRC-036) gives four ingredients: strong and unique emotional content; a theatrical and artistic staging; visual and textual content that is easy to understand and memorize; culturally appropriate content that keeps the brand's DNA. Hall and Batat both put emotion first; Hall asks for precision (the moment, the detail), Batat asks for spectacle and cultural fit. Kotler's Marketing 3.0 reads the story at brand level, through character, plot and metaphor ([[STO-004-a-brand-story-that-moves-people-has-character-plot-and|STO-004]]).

## Application

- Rewrite a client's "About us" page by running it through the four components; mark what is missing.
- In interviews, look for the "moment" (a particular day, a number, a scene), not the abstract mission.
- Choose the type of story by audience: investors want the founder story, customers the value story.

## Example

The **Square** text ("Our story starts with you.") has no characters, emotion, moment or details. **La Brea Bakery** has them all: the pastry chefs at Campanile Restaurant could not find artisan bread good enough, spent a year crafting the recipe, opened the bakery on La Brea Avenue and knew they had a hit when the bread sold out by 11 a.m.

## Questions

- Is "the customer as hero" compatible with the founder story? When do they compete?
- Does Hall's grid work in a 6-second video?
