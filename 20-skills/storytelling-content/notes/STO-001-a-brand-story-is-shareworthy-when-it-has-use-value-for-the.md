# A brand story is "shareworthy" when it has use-value for the audience, not only for the brand

> **The idea in one sentence:** people share a brand story when it satisfies a need of theirs (it entertains, informs, inspires or helps them) and when it says something good about themselves.

## Explanation

Braun and Landa start from an observation: tens of thousands of hours of video uploaded every hour and thousands of advertising messages a day. Being noticed is not the same as being shared, and the leap between the two is large: a story becomes *shareworthy* when it resonates with a person on a personal level. The key is **use-value**: how the story satisfies a social need or an audience's want through its attributes.

Stories that get shared "land in culture" and have at least one of the functions below (the examples are the authors'):

| Function | Example from the book |
|---|---|
| the media finds them newsworthy | Good Humor: a new jingle composed by RZA to replace a song with racist origins |
| they start, amplify or change a conversation | Nike "Dream Crazy"; Dove "Real Beauty" |
| they are authentic to the brand's values | Ben & Jerry's; Patagonia |
| people participate with them | Wendy's in Fortnite and on X |
| they entertain | Midea: a 90-minute movie with an air conditioner |
| they inspire people or inform them about important issues | "Love Has No Labels"; #SignalforHelp |
| they do something worthwhile for people or the planet | Coors "Chillboards"; the Beer Cap Project |

Greg Hahn (Mischief) adds two psychological mechanisms. People share what says something about themselves: the story becomes a badge (it shows I'm the kind of person who gets this). And emotion comes from **tension**: when the product has no tension of its own, you build one (a cultural "enemy" or one from the category). A great idea seems completely obvious after someone else does it.

## Application

- Before the concept, fill in: "Someone will send this to a friend because…". If the answer is "because it is brand X's ad", you have no use-value.
- Choose the dominant function (entertainment, information, inspiration, utility) and build around it.
- Respect the audience's intelligence: a story that treats people as smart gives them a reason to show it to others.

## Example

The Beer Cap Project (AB InBev, MullenLowe SSP3, Colombia): Aguila beer removed its logo from the caps and gave the space to food and transportation brands (Uber, Cabify, KFC), with discounts that encouraged responsible drinking.
