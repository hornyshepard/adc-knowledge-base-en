# The brand manifesto is an aspirational declaration of intent, not the corporate mission rewritten

> **The idea in one sentence:** the manifesto says "why" and "what we believe" in an emotional tone, to turn people into believers; it is the primary story from which the others follow.

## Explanation

Braun and Landa define the brand manifesto as a creative, aspirational declaration of intent. It transcends the corporate mission but aligns with its values, and it positions the brand on an emotional plane. Most manifestos started as internal documents meant to motivate employees; they gained so much momentum that clients pushed for them to become consumer-facing. The authors call it the **primary story** in the larger brand narrative.

What a manifesto aims for:

- contributing to individuals, society and the planet;
- the brand's social values, declared and demonstrated;
- the benefit the brand offers to people;
- the "why" behind the brand;
- the brand essence, personality, spirit;
- the higher purpose;
- turning people into believers who take up the cause.

The form is free: film, typographic design, spoken or written text, even an object. Lululemon prints its manifesto on its reusable shopping bags.

Examples: Avis, "No.2ism", the manifesto of the number two that tries harder; Benetton, "Clothes for Humans" (180 Amsterdam); ProColombia, "The most welcoming place on Earth", built on research about the essence of Colombians; Mozilla, "Pledge for a Healthy Internet", with ten principles.

Melissa Grady Dias (Cadillac's global chief marketing officer) makes a useful distinction. The **brand manifesto** is longitudinal, the brand's voice and North Star; it informs briefs, content, partnerships and even design and sales. The **campaign manifesto** is anchored in a moment in time and has a tactical objective (a launch, the opening of orders). Cadillac's "Make Your Way" rewrite started from an analysis of its owner base: many were self-made people, like the founder, Henry Leland.

## Application

- Write the manifesto after you have the brand construct and the audience insight, not before (see [[BRD-029-the-brand-construct-leads-to-archetype-personality-and|BRD-029]]).
- Use it as a test for briefs: an idea that contradicts the manifesto is off-brand.
- In the copy: short sentences, rhythm, beliefs in the first person plural; no corporate jargon.
