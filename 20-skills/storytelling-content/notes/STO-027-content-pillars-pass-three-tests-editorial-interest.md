# Content pillars pass three tests (editorial interest, actionable, sustainable) and combine four functions

> **The idea in one sentence:** a few big themes (pillars) stretch from the point of mutuality, and each theme has to be able to feed years of content, not a single campaign.

## Explanation

Richards and McLean see **content pillars** as the "glue" between the audience, the business objectives and production. For the bank whose point of mutuality is property, the pillars become: *Property Investment*, *Extending & Renovating*, *Moving Home*, *Becoming a Landlord*.

Each pillar passes **three tests**:

1. **Editorial interest**: topics that are too narrow cannot sustain a sequence of articles. The test is whether you can create, curate and co-create on the topic indefinitely.
2. **Actionable**: the maxim "create it and they will come" does not apply; the topic has to allow campaigns that draw the audience in, one after another, not a single brilliant stunt.
3. **Sustainable**: the topic has to last, or you build an audience that you will lose.

The pillars sit on a foundation of **four storytelling functions**: **entertain**, **educate**, **convince**, **inspire**. A good pillar contains at least one.

The **editorial calendar** comes out of the pillars, and it is a strategic tool, not a list of posts. It has three types of content, borrowed from the press: **news** (including *newsjacking*), **features** (in B2B, *thought leadership*) and **campaigning** (a sustained topic). Content comes from three sources: created, curated, co-created. The authors add three words to keep in mind: **velocity** (too much overwhelms, too little bores), **creativity**, **mutuality**.

## Application

- Propose 3 to 5 pillars to the client and run them through the three tests before you write the first article.
- Label each item in the calendar with its function (entertain / educate / convince / inspire) and check the balance.
- Write the brief for each piece with: persona, pillar, stage of the journey, goal, call to action, format. The book gives a model exactly like this.

## Example

The book's model brief: the "Dinkys" persona (a couple with no children and good incomes), the *Moving Home* pillar, Act 1 ("Ordinary World"), a microsite, a 1,000-word article on why moving home is one of the most stressful things that happen in life. Call to action: the next article in the series.

## Questions

- Which pillar would pass the "sustainable" test for a juice brand? Which one would fail?
- What does the balance of the four functions look like for a "dry" B2B brand?
