# UGC is earned content: the most credible content for a brand precisely because the brand cannot control it

> **The idea in one sentence:** user-generated content (UGC) is free, authentic and more persuasive than the brand's own content, but it does not meet the brand's standards, and the authors disagree on how explicitly it should be asked for.

## Explanation

**What it is and why it is valuable.** Mueller and Rajaram define UGC as stories about a brand or product produced by users on their own initiative, around a predefined topic, product or hashtag: earned content, for which the brand pays nothing. Barre and Gayrard-Carrera (SRC-019) say the same: when fans extol the brand, the impact is stronger, and earned media (*retombées gagnées par la marque*) is worth more because it makes others want to take part. Labrecque (SRC-029) explains the motivation: "create" is one of the 5Cs of using social media ([[SOC-002-the-5cs-people-use-social-media-to-consume-connect-control|SOC-002]]), and the brand feeds it with tools and incentives.

**What the brand cannot control.** Maximilian Wolf (in SRC-034) adds three observations: UGC often does not meet your communication standards and sometimes not even compliance guidelines, which is precisely what makes it authentic; its quality rises the better informed the community is and the more it identifies with your messages; gratitude matters (share, comment on and like people's posts).

**Where the authors differ: an implicit call or an explicit mechanic.** Wolf argues that the more implicit a UGC call, the greater the participation, because people do not feel they are carrying out an order. Other sources describe explicit calls: Nishad (SRC-033), UGC contests that demand a lot of effort and draw few but highly engaged people ([[SOC-012-social-media-contests-are-chosen-by-the-effort-they-ask-for|SOC-012]]); Glenister, the Robinsons "Guess My Gargle" AR filter, designed to incentivize UGC after the TV spot, with over 1,300 pieces. Sascha Gottschalk (SRC-034) sits in the middle: a simple question with a hashtag ("Have you ever been to Japan? … upload your favourite spot with the hashtag #bucketlistjapan") can start a wave.

## Application

- Give the community a pretext, not a brief: a question, a hashtag, a challenge with something at stake.
- Plan how you will reply, repost and say thank you; ignored UGC dies out.
- Decide in advance what you do with UGC that contradicts the brand guidelines (moderation, not censorship) and ask for the author's consent before reusing it in ads ([[STO-035-on-social-media-other-people-s-content-is-taken-over|STO-035]]).

## Example

Lego benefits greatly from UGC, which is often hashtag-based; during the May 2020 lockdown, Porsche launched #GetCreativeWithPorsche: its newsroom shared stories with replica Porsche models in Lego landscapes and encouraged users to contribute UGC. Ice Bucket-style challenges show that UGC can build global communities ([[STO-036-the-ingredients-of-a-viral-campaign-a-simple-message|STO-036]]).

## Questions

- Where does "initiating" UGC turn into disguised commissioned content?
- Is UGC empowerment or unpaid labor for the brand?
