# On social media, the guide replaces the hero of the classic journey

> **The idea in one sentence:** for stories on social platforms, the linear matrix of the hero's journey is too long and too closed; the effective storyteller becomes a guide who talks with users and helps them learn, have fun and create.

## Explanation

Mueller and Rajaram start from Campbell and Vogler (12 stages, three acts) and acknowledge the power of archetypes: the mentor, the shadow and the messenger live on in memes and hashtags. But they argue that the matrix comes from the age of linear analog technologies (film, frame by frame), while the social story has other properties:

| The classic story | The social story |
|---|---|
| a long, complete arc | often extremely short, with no room for an entire hero cycle |
| transmitted to an audience | conversational, negotiated through dialogue and play |
| a single reality | 3D filters, avatars, many digital identities |
| one thread, from A to Z | non-linear, multi-strand, hybrid |
| a dominant hero, often patriarchal | inclusive, diverse |
| closed, with an ending | live, always in motion |

Users "are already heroes in their own lives"; they do not need another hero but guides, mentors and credible friends. The creator in an endless exchange with the community becomes a much more interesting character than the classic movie hero. The storyteller speaks in the first person, in selfie mode, and vouches for the credibility of the content with their own profile.

## Application

- When you write for social, ask "what guide role does the brand play?" instead of "who is the hero?".
- Use archetypes as nanoparticles (a gesture, a line), not as a complete structure.
- Build the story so the user can step in and out at any time (episodes, cards, parallel threads).

## Example

The lawyer Tim Hendrik Walter (@HerrAnwalt) is not a hero: he is a guide who answers teenagers' legal questions in one-minute videos, reads hundreds of comments and names the author of each question. The SAS ad "The Arrivals" presents the airline as a mentor for travelers' personal growth.

## Questions

- Is the guide anything other than the mentor of the hero's journey, moved to the center of the narrative? (Many brand storytelling authors make the customer the hero and the brand the guide: the same idea, a different vocabulary.)
- Which social formats (long YouTube series, documentaries) still use the hero's complete arc successfully?
