# Symbols, icons and gestures communicate instantly because their meaning is already installed in the viewer's mind

> **The idea in one sentence:** a known symbol is "read" in an instant, but its meaning depends on culture and generation; a new symbol gives the viewer the pleasure of discovering the connection.

## Explanation

For Ingledew, a symbol is something that visually represents something else: a heart for love, a dove for peace, scales for justice, a skull for death. The object often relates directly to what it symbolizes: our hearts pound when we are in love, scales weigh right against wrong. The mechanism ("Brainjack") has two variants: established symbols communicate quickly because their meaning is preset, and freshly created symbols reward the viewer for making the connection on their own.

The family of these visual shortcuts:

| Type | What it brings | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Symbols | compact meanings, from the carrot (reward) to the emptying hourglass (time is running out) | meanings change (the frog is no longer recognized as a symbol of Satan) and are local (in China the fish stands for abundance, and a green hat for a man with a cheating partner) |
| Flags | "the world's first logos", recognized even when cut up or distorted, charged with emotion | the same flag says opposite things to different audiences (the Union Jack for the Sex Pistols) |
| Icons | Che, Einstein, Marilyn, "read" instantly as values (rebellion, genius, glamour) | image rights are protected; a celebrity is not an icon and can become a risk (Tiger Woods and Gillette) |
| Signs | the voice of authority; subverted, they become memorable | none |
| Eyes, hands, faces | "You can solve any brief with an image of an eye," or with hands; the finger pointed at the viewer (Alfred Leete's recruiting poster) draws us in with irresistible force | gestures mean different things in different cultures (the V-sign) |

Famous photographs work the same way: their composition is imprinted on the collective memory, and recreating them transfers some of the original's power.

## Application

- Make an inventory of the symbols related to the brief and of their meanings *for your target*, not for you.
- Prefer a fresh twist on a known symbol to the symbol as it is (see [[IDE-017-cliche-convention-and-the-familiar-format-become-ideas-when|IDE-017]]).
- For international campaigns, check gestures, colors and symbolic animals in each market.

## Example

Ogilvy & Mather turned Marilyn Monroe with a skirt that does not move into an ad for an anti-flatulence product: the icon and what everyone knows about her scene make the joke on their own.
