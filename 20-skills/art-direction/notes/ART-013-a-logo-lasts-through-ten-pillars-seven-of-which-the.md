# A logo lasts through ten pillars, seven of which the designer controls

> **The idea in one sentence:** a logo becomes iconic through time, the marketing budget and a good product, which the designer cannot control, but also through passion, beauty, originality, functionality, color, story and simplicity, which the designer can.

## Explanation

Allan Peters (Peters Design Company, formerly a designer at Target) starts from marks such as the CBS Eye, the Shell, the Nike Swoosh: why do they still feel modern after decades? His formula has ten pillars.

**Three pillars you cannot control:**
- **Time**: a well-crafted mark grows stronger with every viewing, until it no longer needs words. The Swoosh was not as impactful in 1971 as it is today; a useful argument when the client says the logo "does not feel iconic".
- **The marketing budget**: becoming iconic takes repeat viewings (the McDonald's arches are everywhere).
- **Product quality**: a good identity does not save a bad product.

**Seven pillars you can control:**
1. **Personal passion**: long-lasting marks are clearly and undeniably a labor of love.
2. **Universal beauty**: balance between positive and negative space, rhythm between the linework, balance between simplicity and storytelling.
3. **Originality**: too simple means generic; a circle or a square is not memorable, and a trite logo makes the whole brand seem cliché.
4. **Functionality**: it must work as a 16x16-pixel favicon, embroidered at small sizes or stamped into concrete.
5. **Color**: it tells the story (a tree is green) and sets you apart from competitors; long-lasting marks tend toward saturated colors; pure black and white are warmed up or cooled down for harmony.
6. **Story / memorability**: the Swoosh is a story about speed; a name like "Hawk" is easier to remember than "Bob". "There is nothing more forgettable than a letter in a circle."
7. **Simplicity**: remove elements until the mark stops communicating; that tipping point is the goal.

Carl Damgaard (SRC-015) arrives at a converging, shorter list: simplicity, relevance, versatility, timelessness.

## Application

- **The size test:** shrink the mark until it becomes unrecognizable; overly thin lines and tight negative space break down first.
- **The hat test:** put the logo on a hat and ask the client whether they would be proud to wear it.
- **The originality test:** search for similar images on Google, Behance and Dribbble, and ask for a trademark search.
- Analyze the competitors' colors before you choose the palette.

## Example

Raymond Loewy's Shell logo was simple enough to work, unchanged, as a social media avatar, a medium that did not exist when it was designed.
