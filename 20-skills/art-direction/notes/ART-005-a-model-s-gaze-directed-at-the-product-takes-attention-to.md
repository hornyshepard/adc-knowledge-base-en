# A model's gaze directed at the product takes attention to the product; a gaze at the reader stops it on the face

> **The idea in one sentence:** faces capture attention, but where they look decides where the viewer's attention goes, and the placement of elements on the page matters even for ads seen only peripherally.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe bring together several eye-tracking and placement findings relevant to art direction:

- **Gaze direction.** Sajjacholapunt and Ball (2014): in a banner ad, a model's gaze **averted toward the product** improved memorability for the ad and the product, compared with a mutual gaze at the viewer. A mutual gaze keeps attention on the face; an averted gaze "funnels" it toward the product and brand.
- **Yet a direct gaze can increase liking.** Strick, Holland and van Knippenberg (2008): attractive faces improved the ratings of unknown brands of peppermints, particularly when they looked at the participant (ch. 7). So the choice depends on the goal: memory of the product or transfer of liking.
- **Banner position.** Simola et al. (2011): vertical banners to the right of the text attracted more attention, especially when animated, among Western readers used to reading from left to right.
- **Matching activation** (Janiszewski, ch. 2): the central text occupies the left hemisphere, and images in the left visual field reach the "idle" right hemisphere. Ryu et al. (2007): pictorial banners were evaluated more positively on the left of the page, textual ones on the right. On a printed page, the brand name was evaluated better to the right of the face and to the left of the slogan.

The authors note that a banner, small and peripheral, cannot carry dense messages; its persuasion comes from subtle cues, and the human face is one of them, making up for the reduced social presence online.

## Application

- If the goal is memory of the product, have the model look at the product or the brand name.
- If the goal is liking and the transfer of affect, a direct gaze can be justified.
- Test the position (left/right) and the type of banner (picture/text) against the content of the host page.
