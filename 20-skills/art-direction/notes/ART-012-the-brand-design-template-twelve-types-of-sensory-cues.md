# The brand design template: twelve types of sensory cues

> **The idea in one sentence:** a brand's identity is more than its logo; Calder lists twelve types of cues (verbal, auditory, visual and olfactory) that must all trigger the same associations.

## Explanation

Bobby Calder proposes a template (*brand design template*) used as a checklist. Each element must be explored and checked against the map of target associations:

| Modality | Cue | Examples from the book |
|---|---|---|
| Verbal | **Brand name** | UberCab → Uber; BlackBerry vs "Priv" |
| Verbal | **Corporate identity** | FedEx (the fast brand) under Federal Express (the company with its own fleet of planes); Reach from Johnson & Johnson |
| Verbal | **Signature line** | "I'm loving it"; "A diamond is forever"; the failure of "Cooler and Warmer" (Rhode Island), which takes effort to decode |
| Verbal | **Category name** | "uncola", "Greek yogurt", vitaminwater |
| Verbal | **Brand lexicon** | Big Mac, Happy Meal, "flame grilled" (Burger King), "lick, twist, dunk" (Oreo) |
| Auditory | **Sound** | the MGM lion's roar, the Intel Inside audio logo |
| Visual | **Illustration** | the loaded spoon on Chunky cans; the Gerber baby |
| Visual | **Brand symbol** | the Walmart sunburst ("living better"); the UPS shield |
| Visual | **Corporate symbol** | the Ford badge, VW; the RJMetrics failure (the dodecahedron seen as a pair of Y-fronts) |
| Visual | **Color palette** | UPS brown (basic, routine); Pepsi red and blue |
| Visual | **Shape** | the McDonald's fries container that overflows; the Bordeaux vs the Burgundy bottle |
| Olfactory | **Scent** | hotel fragrances (W, Westin, Hyatt Place); Cinnabon ovens placed to reach passersby |

Two rules. **Signature lines should not require System 2 thinking**: a good line often leads to just one strong association. **The design must be holistic**: all the cues link to the same associations and reinforce each other, and the whole is more than the constituent elements. Sound and scent are less commonly used, but they become important as brands become experiences.

## Application

- Use the table as an audit: fill in each row for the client's brand and mark the gaps or contradictions.
- Build a brand lexicon (the brand's own repeated words) as deliberately as the color palette.
- Test any new logo with people from the target markets before launch.

## Example

Gerber Singles (one-dish meals for young adults eating alone) inherited the image of the Gerber baby: an illustration that had made the brand, but that broke the extension.
