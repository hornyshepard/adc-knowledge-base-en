# The brand noun list sets the subject of the logo before the sketches

> **The idea in one sentence:** before drawing, the designer and the client agree on a list of about 15 concrete nouns (things, places, beings) that could be the subject of the mark; that way the later discussion is about the quality of the design, not about what it should represent.

## Explanation

Allan Peters starts every logo project with a *brand noun process*. The logic: a symbol has to show *something*, and abstract values cannot be drawn directly.

The steps:

1. **The designer makes a list of 15 to 25 nouns** (person, place or thing) that could be the subject matter of the symbol. If an important idea is not a noun, you find a noun that serves as a metaphor for it. For a bank that wants to represent "trust", a Google image search for "trust icons" brings up a shield, a key, a keyhole, shaking hands, a lock.
2. **The client gets homework.** Each person who will approve the logo makes their own list. Peters explains in detail what a noun is, because many of the lists he receives are less than half nouns.
3. **The lists are merged**, organized by category, and the doubles removed.
4. **In a joint meeting, the list is narrowed down to about 15 nouns** that will inspire the sketches.

Narrowing down matters more than making the list: it eliminates everything the client does *not* want to see, so at the presentation the decision is made on aesthetics, not on subject matter. The client is also involved strategically early on: no one knows a brand better than someone working on it day in and day out.

Peters warns against letting the client remove the nouns that feel "overused": an apple on its own would feel like a knock-off of Apple, a heart on its own is boring, but combined they can become fresh.

## Application

- Use the noun list as a visual brief for any identity project, including at school.
- For each abstract value in the brand platform (innovation, care, speed), look for three or four metaphor nouns.
- Keep the approved list in the presentation: next to each proposal, note which nouns it combines.

## Example

For New South Outdoor (billboards), the competitors had unremarkable typographic logos. The approved list included ram, star, sunrise, bell, pedestal and falcon; the final mark completes the edge of the rising sun in the feathers of an eagle: motion, new beginnings, "rising above the clutter".
