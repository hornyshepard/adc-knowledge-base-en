# Five techniques for combining two nouns into one original mark

> **The idea in one sentence:** a shield or a lightning bolt on its own is a cliché, but a shield combined with a lightning bolt is original; Peters uses five techniques to merge two or more nouns into a single simple, memorable mark.

## Explanation

For Allan Peters, the best route to originality without complexity is to combine the approved brand nouns. The five techniques:

| Technique | How it works | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| **Overlapping geometry** | you draw many simple versions of each object, draw the geometric skeleton of each and look for shared curves (can the curve of a droplet double as the vein of a leaf?) | exploration of leaf + sun + water droplet |
| **Negative space** | the shape of one object is defined by the empty space in the other | Firecraft Pizza: the outer edge of the pizza slice is defined by the negative space of the flame |
| **Visual rhythm** | evenly spaced lines transition the geometry from one shape to the other and create visual energy | Armor of God: cross + shield tied together by lines running around the cross |
| **An unexpected twist** | two expected subjects become fresh together | Love of Learning: apple + heart |
| **Visual flow** | the linework directs the eye to the center of the mark, as in Renaissance compositions | City of Shoreview: the waves lead toward the trees, and the negative space of the trees leads the eye back down to the water |

The trick for negative space: fill a page with icon sketches for the first object, then one for the second, and look for similar shapes (the triangle of a pizza slice in the geometry of a flame). Peters admits that combinations in full visual harmony are rare: "The best combinations feel like they belong together."

Warnings: visual rhythm can be overdone (if you can shave off a few lines while keeping the same idea, shave them off: you are making a logo, not an illustration), and "Frankensteining" two different sketches is usually a horrible idea, unless you blend the essence of one with the outer shape of the other (Kwikbit).

## Application

- In the sketch phase, go through the five techniques systematically for each pair of nouns; that gets you to 50 sketches quickly.
- Draw the separate icons first, simply, then look for overlaps; do not try for the final combination straight away.
- Check each combination: does it stay legible at small sizes?

## Example

Freadom (clothing and books, net profits donated to literacy) combines an eagle (freedom), a star (the individual) and an open book (literacy for all) in a mark that can be embroidered, engraved and pressed into ceramic.
