# Brand design works in System 1 and starts from 3–4 target associations

> **The idea in one sentence:** unlike advertising, which persuades consciously, brand design triggers automatic associations; so its brief is not a long text but a perceptual mind map with three or four associations, checked against every element.

## Explanation

Bobby Calder prefers the term *brand design* to the older "brand identity" and separates it from advertising. Using Daniel Kahneman's distinction, he shows that design works in **System 1** (fast, automatic, nonverbal thinking), while an advertisement watched with attention engages **System 2**, slow and deliberative. A word such as "pure" on a package is processed automatically and forms a perception without the person realizing it; these associations can then shape conscious thought as well.

The method in three steps:
1. **Define the target associations**: three or four, no more, drawn in a **perceptual mind map** around the central idea of the positioning.
2. **Prototype the cues** that trigger these associations, with the help of visual representations of design intent (*mood boards*).
3. **Check every element** against the map. Functionality, competitive differentiation or design aesthetics are secondary criteria; a design that does not trigger the intended associations is a bad brand design.

Calder says that many managers literally freeze when given a Sharpie and asked to think in "making mode", so they end up in an "I like that one" role. The right roles: the marketer specifies the associations and evaluates whether each cue triggers them; the designer prototypes and polishes.

## Application

- For any identity project, ask for (or write) a map with 3–4 associations, not a ten-page brief.
- When presenting proposals, justify every choice (letter, color, shape) by the association it triggers.
- When the client says "I don't like it", bring the discussion back to the map: which association is missing or contradicted?

## Example

The Coca-Cola map: "Coke brings joy" at the center, with refreshing, timeless, celebratory and "fast-moving lifestyle" around it. The Spenserian script suggests timelessness and movement, the dynamic contour ribbon mimics the shape of the bottle laid on its side (movement), and the red is celebratory. Cadbury had applied the same logic as early as 1866: the name "Cocoa Essence", the signature line "Absolutely Pure Therefore Best" and the picture of a young girl, all for purity.

## Questions

- How do you test whether a cue works in System 1, and not just whether it is pleasing when you analyze it?
- What happens when a target association changes (Uber's repositioning from "affordable luxury" to "everyday routine convenience")?
