# "Garage genius": minimum means, maximum impact; no budget forces an idea, and hand-made work communicates intimately

> **The idea in one sentence:** having no money, no budget or no computer is no barrier to strong communication; and in a screen-saturated world, something hand-made reaches the viewer at a visceral level.

## Explanation

The "Garage genius" entry collects examples of big ideas made with almost nothing. Kurt Schwitters, interned in a camp during the Second World War, made sculptures from porridge and prints from stolen pieces of lino tile. The linocut posters of the 1968 student protests and the punk fanzine *Sniffin' Glue*, with its felt-penned lettering and photocopied production, look like urgent messages from the front line precisely because they were made fast and cheaply. Abram Games drew all his posters by hand and summarized his approach as "minimum means, maximum impact". Ingledew quotes the physicist Ernest Rutherford on the idea that having no money forces you to think.

Two related entries complete the principle. **Materials**: every material has its own inherent qualities; Ron Arad made a chair from a car seat found in a scrapyard, and Carl Andre's bricks changed the palette of possible creative materials forever. **Happy accidents**: go to the printer and the factory, because what gets thrown away (misprints, miscuts, rejects, tests) can be inspiration; Man Ray and Warhol turned their darkroom and silk-screen mistakes into technique.

Braun and Landa give the contemporary version. "Icelandverse", the spoof of the metaverse announcement, cost less than $50,000 to produce and, according to the book, reached 6 billion impressions. At the Super Bowl, Reddit bought only a five-second "bumper". Luiz Sanches says that Brazilian advertising people, living in constant crisis, have learned to do so much with so little.

## Application

- When the budget is small, reverse the problem: the things you already have are exactly right for the job.
- Make the prototype by hand (pencil, stencil, scissors, photocopier) before the computer: it forces you to stay with the idea.
- Visit the supplier (printer, workshop, factory) at the start of the project, not only at delivery.

## Example

The London design group johnson banks made its Christmas cards by hand-punching magazines and brochures found around the office. The cost was zero, and the message to clients was twofold: happy Christmas, and we are smart and we recycle.
