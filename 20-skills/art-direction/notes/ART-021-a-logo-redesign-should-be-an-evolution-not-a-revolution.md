# A logo redesign should be an evolution, not a revolution

> **The idea in one sentence:** every viewing of a logo adds visual equity, and a redesign from scratch erases it; it is usually wiser to keep what works and fix only what does not.

## Explanation

Allan Peters calls *brand equity* the memory built up over years of viewing and millions of dollars. When you start from scratch, you erase it: "Even ugly logos can become memorable over time." Even a poorly designed logo usually has something worth keeping: for Heartland Technology, Peters kept the world shape, the colors and the lines that create motion.

**What to focus on**, in Peters's order:
1. **Functional issues**: ask the client what does not work: gradients that are hard to reproduce, typography that is hard to read at small sizes, colors that clash.
2. **The concept**: what is the root of the visual idea? Can it be expressed more simply? Can you shave off an element or two without making it worse?
3. **Color**: can the story be told better with more saturated or more neutral colors? Why depend on four colors when two will do?

The method is a two-column audit: **what was not working** and **what was working**. Examples:

- **Urbane Coffee**: not working: the icon worked into the logotype (legibility), awkward and inconsistent kerning, an outdated type choice, descriptive type too light, the "U/Cup" too thick. Working: the "U" + coffee cup idea. The solution: the U/Cup became an icon that could live on its own, with multiple lines for motion.
- **Eagan Police**: a hand-drawn badge, eight colors, very thin linework, stretched and squished typography; working: the traditional shape of a police badge. The solution: mono-weight lines, the leaf from the new city brand mark in place of the wreath, three colors instead of eight.
- **Rush Creek Church**: unnecessary gradients, words set too close together, an overly illustrative mark; working: the creek integrated into the "R" and the blue. The solution: overlapping geometry between the leg of the "R" and the creek, the mark separated in a shape container.

## Application

- Start any redesign with the "working / not working" table, filled in together with the client.
- Keep at least one recognizable asset (shape, color, idea) and explain to the client why.
- Simplify first of all for reproduction: one color, engraving, embroidery.

## Example

The City of Shoreview's old logo used four colors and a gradient that could not be embroidered or engraved; the new mark keeps the same subject matter but transforms the illustration into a functional logo, with the water reduced to three thick lines.

## Questions

- When is a revolution justified after all (mergers, a change of name, toxic associations)?
- How do you show the client the value of the existing visual equity?
