# A modular identity system has a brand mark, a horizontal logo, a vertical logo and a badge

> **The idea in one sentence:** a brand does not have a single logo but a family of versions for different spaces: the brand mark for avatars, the horizontal version for compact spaces, the vertical one for large displays and the badge for large surfaces that allow a richer story.

## Explanation

Allan Peters contradicts the firms that steer clients away from badges (emblems) as "too complicated and old-fashioned". A great brand system is **modular**:

| Version | Where it is used |
|---|---|
| **Brand mark** | avatars, favicon, small spaces |
| **Horizontal logo** | compact spaces, mobile site |
| **Vertical logo** | signage, T-shirts |
| **Badge** | murals, packaging, posters |

The badge is more complex, but that lets it tell a more elaborate story. Peters describes the process in three parts:

1. **Content organization.** The obvious candidates: the name, the brand mark, the tagline (if there is none, the designer can write a two- or three-word tagline, sometimes then adopted by the client). **Balance words** are added: pairs of the same visual length placed on each side: "ESTD" and "2023", "MPLS" and "MINN", "QLTY" and "CRFT". The hierarchy: the name, then the brand mark, the tagline, the balance words.
2. **The external shape.** The circle and the oval are neutral solutions; Peters prefers shapes inspired by the geometry of the brand mark (for Wits Realty, by the roof shape of the "W").
3. **Shape containers and visual balance.** Typography does not have to be wrapped around the edge; it can sit in symmetrical inner shapes. Every element on the left is balanced by one on the right, negative space included.

The badge should not be forced: for many tech companies it would feel out of place. Two arguments for the client: it tells a story through both words and visuals, and it can explain the function of the brand.

## Application

- Always deliver a set of versions, not a single logo file, and show where each one is used.
- When a brand has two good marks (a monogram and a symbol), define clearly which is primary and which is secondary, as sports teams do.
- Use the badge for merch, packaging and community materials.

## Example

The City of Shoreview has a short version, "Shoreview" (preferred), and a "City of Shoreview" badge, used on city vehicles and employees' gear, where the official function has to be made clear.

- When is a responsive logo (one that simplifies automatically) better than a system with a badge?
