# The brand mark process: fifty sketches before any polish

> **The idea in one sentence:** a good logo comes from many rough ideas, not from polishing the first one; that is why Peters's process asks for at least 50 sketches, after which only the best 15 are vectorized and 3 become full systems.

## Explanation

Allan Peters argues that a clear process helps the client understand why a logo costs thousands of dollars and takes six weeks, and gives a frame for being paid for extra rounds. The process has six phases:

1. **Brand nouns**: the client and the designer approve the possible subjects.
2. **Sketches**: quantity is more important than quality. At least **50 rough sketches**, just detailed enough to capture the idea. The fishing analogy: the more times you cast your lure, the better your chance of a trophy-sized fish. Inexperienced designers take their first idea straight into the final phase and spend a week "polishing" a mediocre logo.
3. **Combining the nouns**: the main source of original ideas.
4. **Vectorizing and gridding**: the best 15 sketches go into Illustrator, with geometric rules and simple shapes. Peters prefers thick lines (they work at small sizes), balanced against the negative space. He tries different angles, weights and numbers of elements, looking for how to communicate the idea in the fewest "moves". He then picks out the five weakest marks and goes back to the sketchbook to replace them.
5. **Exploration and refinement**: only the top ideas.
6. **The brand system**: the three marks the client chooses are developed into full systems (horizontal logo, vertical logo, badge), with typography and geometry taken from the mark.

**The first presentation** shows all 15 marks **in black and white only**, with no color and no typography, each in black on white and white on black, with the brand nouns it references listed next to it; at the end, all of them on one slide. The goal of the meeting: the client picks three.

## Application

- Set yourself a sketch quota (50) and do not open the computer until you reach it.
- Present the marks in black and white: color and type pull the discussion away from the idea and the form.
- Test each version at small sizes and in reverse before the presentation.

## Example

For the City of Eagan, the revisions the client asked for (type legible on a water tower seen from the highway, fewer lines on the hill for stamped concrete) led to a custom font inspired by the geometry of the mark and to a tighter crop; the logo came out of the revisions stronger.

## Questions

- Can the 50-sketch rule also be applied to headlines or campaign concepts?
- How much should the client be involved in the intermediate phases?
