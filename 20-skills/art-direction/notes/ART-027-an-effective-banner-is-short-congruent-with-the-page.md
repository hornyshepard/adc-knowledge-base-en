# An effective banner is short, congruent with the page, emotional, with a concrete incentive and measured animation

> **The idea in one sentence:** the studies the authors summarize give a short list of content, graphic design and placement rules that raise a banner's CTR.

## Explanation

Schwarz-Musch and colleagues summarize the research cited by Arholdt et al. (2023):

**Content**

- targeted to the interests and motivations of the target audience;
- congruent with the content of the website on which it is placed;
- emotional (humor, for example) and combined with a purchase incentive, more effective than a purely rational banner;
- with a concrete call to action: a discount, a free offer, free shipping;
- short: long or complex messages lower the CTR;
- the brand name: well-known brands achieve a higher CTR, but a non-branded banner can create curiosity.

**Graphic design**: animation increases attention and memorability, especially when brand awareness is low, but animations that are too fast or excessive overwhelm the user's cognitive performance and make the message unclear.

**Formats**: *medium rectangle* 300×250 (common on mobile), square 250×250, rectangle 336×280, *skyscraper* 120×600 or 160×600, *leaderboard* 728×90, mobile 320×480, *billboard* up to 1024×768, buttons 125×125. On mobile, ads are embedded or scrollable and touch-based, so the CTA is designed differently than for a mouse. Static banners are cheap and quick but have low engagement; interactive or animated ones have higher engagement but cost more and load more slowly.

## Application

- Before hand-off, check every banner against the list: one message, a visible incentive, a CTA legible on mobile, animation that ends on the brand and the offer.
- Design the smallest format first: if the idea works in 300×250, it can be expanded.
- Ask the media team for the list of sites, so you can check congruence with the content.

## Example

The first banner ad ran on October 27, 1994: AT&T paid $30,000 for three months on the website of *Wired* magazine and got a 44% CTR. Today the average CTR of banner ads is below 1%, which explains why each of the rules above matters.

## Questions

- The rules raise the CTR, but what do they do for the brand in the long term?
- Where is there room for a surprising visual idea in such a strict list of rules?
