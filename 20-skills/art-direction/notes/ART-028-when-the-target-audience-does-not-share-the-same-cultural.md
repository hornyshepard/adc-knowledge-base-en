# When the target audience does not share the same cultural schemas, the image must tap into universal biological schemas, such as prospect and refuge

> **The idea in one sentence:** people read images through learned, cultural or biological schemas; only the last are shared by everyone, which is why a safe multinational campaign works with infants, primary emotions and landscapes of "prospect" or "refuge".

## Explanation

The target audience must understand at once that the message is meant for them, and the words and images must be read exactly as the advertiser intended. That depends on the **schemas** used in processing. Kroeber-Riel suggests three levels:

| Schema | What it contains | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Learned** | associations restricted to a particular group to which you belong | the images linked to your football team |
| **Cultural** | the myths, arts and norms of a culture | the English queue to enter a theater, other cultures push in a mass, and neither is "correct" |
| **Biological** | psychobiological responses shared by all humans, often unconscious, linked to survival | the warm, nurturing feeling toward an infant, the alarm at the high-pitched cry of a baby |

Even hearing is cultural: the phonemes of the native language are fixed before the age of one, and after that we become "functionally deaf" to sounds not found in it (English has 43 phonemes, Hawaiian only 15).

The authors' rule: whenever you are not certain that the target audience shares a common learned or cultural schema, or when a campaign runs across many cultural groupings, use **biological schemas**, with primary emotions and images associated in memory at a limbic level.

A concrete tool is Jay Appleton's **prospect and refuge theory**. Humans evolved needing to see out to the horizon (**prospect**: opportunity and ample warning of danger) and a place to hide (**refuge**: security). We still respond unconsciously to the visual environment in these ways. If you want the target audience to feel secure, the image should be consistent with refuge. If you want them to feel a sense of opportunity, the image should be consistent with prospect.

## Application

- For a multinational brief, list the visual elements that require cultural knowledge (gestures, colors, humor) and replace them, where you can, with biological triggers.
- Choose the composition by the emotion you want: an open horizon, water, height for opportunity; enclosed spaces, shelter, warm light for security.
- Check the soundtrack: key words may be hard to hear for native speakers of other languages.

## Example

In chapter 9, the ad for a holiday in the Indian Ocean (high-involvement transformational strategy) shows the view out over the water to the horizon. Following Appleton, the image reinforces the sense of opportunity and the positive emotional response. A home insurance ad would rather choose the image of a lit, sheltered house on a rainy evening: refuge.

## Questions

- Are primary emotions universal at all? What would the anthropologists in chapter 2 say?
- What risks arise if you constantly use infants and animals as a biological "shortcut"?
