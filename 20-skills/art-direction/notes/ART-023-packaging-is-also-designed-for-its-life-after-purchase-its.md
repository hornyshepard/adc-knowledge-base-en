# Packaging is also designed for its life after purchase: its shape is a brand cue, and every use repeats the benefit

> **The idea in one sentence:** the package's role does not end at the shelf; many products are used directly from the package, so it is an ad people take home, and its shape, label and benefit are designed for every use.

## Explanation

The package's role at the shelf (attention, recognition, differentiation from competitors) is covered under advertising strategy. For art direction, Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott (SRC-049, ch. 15) add the second half of its life. Breakfast cereals, cold remedies, toothpaste or household cleaners are used directly from the package, so every use is a new exposure to the primary benefit. The authors call the package **post-purchase advertising**, and Rossiter and Bellman describe packages as "take-away" communication vehicles. Hence the design condition: the message on the package must be the same as the campaign's. Bold repeats on the box the "2-in-1" benefit from the ad, Fairy reminds you that it is pure for a baby's skin, and the Glenfiddich bottle later added "the original" to support its nostalgic strategy.

Bobby Calder (SRC-014) adds **shape** as a symbolic cue. The McDonald's French fries container is designed to showcase an overflowing number of fries, and the tool used to fill it puts in so many that some fall into the bag. The result is associations of caring and friendliness. The shape of the Bordeaux bottle, with its squared-off high shoulders, and that of the more rounded Burgundy bottle probably had a functional origin, but today each has its own associations. The two sources complement each other: the package works through what it says (the benefit) and through what it is (the shape).

## Application

- Put the campaign's primary benefit on the package, not just the logo and the ingredient list.
- Look for a shape or a gesture of use that can become the brand's own (how it opens, how it pours, how it sits on the table).
- Ask what people see on the package on the tenth day of use, not only in the first second at the shelf.
- Change the package by evolution, keeping the recognition cues, and think about the end of its life from the start.

## Example

For a more premium expression launched in 2004, The Macallan went back to elements from an 1861 bottle. The label gave the whisky the aura of a tradition and made it stand out on the back of a bar.

## Questions

- What is left of "post-purchase advertising" when the product is decanted or the package is thrown away at once?
- Can a package shape be protected as a brand asset, just like a logo?
