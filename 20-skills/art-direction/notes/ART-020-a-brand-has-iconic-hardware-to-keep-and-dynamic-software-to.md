# A brand has iconic hardware to keep and dynamic software to change

> **The idea in one sentence:** some brand elements (symbol, colors, name, voice) must be chosen early and repeated for years so they become unmistakable; others (campaigns, limited editions, timely content) must change often so the brand stays alive.

## Explanation

*Goddess Guide to Branding* splits a brand's style in two:

- **Iconic elements**: created and committed to as early as possible, then carried far into the future: the symbols, colors, name, voice, signature experience. They make the brand recognizable.
- **Dynamic storytelling**: created in real time to move with culture: a limited edition flavor, influencer content around a timely event such as Coachella, reactions to cultural moments.

Sarah Moffat, chief creative officer at Turner Duckworth (Coca-Cola, McDonald's, Samsung, Levi's), calls the two layers **hardware** and **software**. The hardware is sustaining: you have to decide at the start what the brand will be famous for. The software changes regularly to keep the brand ticking. Her advice to anyone building a business is consistency: brand equity isn't built overnight. The studio's saying is "Love the unmistakable": always elevate what you are already known for, and when the familiar elements start to feel stale, put them into a new context instead of abandoning them.

Ada Mayer adds the *worldbuilding* perspective: brands now build living "worlds" in motion (social, retail, events), not only static identities; that is exactly why they need a clear core character (the archetype), which allows variation without losing coherence. She also notes that many clients ask for a rebrand when what they need is to *expand* the brand.

The idea converges with Allan Peters (SRC-017): visual equity accumulates through repeated viewings, and redesigns should be evolutions that keep what works.

## Application

- In a brand guide, separate explicitly "what does not change" (hardware) from "what can be played with" (software), and give examples of both.
- When a client wants "something new", first propose a new execution of the existing iconic elements, not new elements.
- Plan a calendar of dynamic storytelling (editions, collaborations, cultural moments) anchored in the same assets.

## Example

At Yonder Cider (an interview in the book), every new product carries the same tall "YONDER" running along the side of the can and the two-tone jewel colors, while the seasonal flavors change: stable hardware, variable software.
