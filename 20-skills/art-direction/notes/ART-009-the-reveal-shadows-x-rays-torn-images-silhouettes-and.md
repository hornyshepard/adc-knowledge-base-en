# The reveal: shadows, X-rays, torn images, silhouettes and hidden messages let viewers discover for themselves

> **The idea in one sentence:** when an image has a surface layer and a hidden truth, the viewer who discovers the second layer feels a small jolt of revelation and remembers the message.

## Explanation

Several entries in Ingledew share the same mechanism: the image hides something, and the viewer brings it to light.

| Technique | What it reveals | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| Torn images | an underlying layer, a secret truth | the anti-Vietnam poster (1972): Uncle Sam torn apart, with a symbol of death underneath; John Smith's beer: the wind seems to have "torn away" the billboard, revealing an old one beneath |
| X-rays | the inside, the hidden thought | John Heartfield (1932): Hitler "X-rayed", a hollow, spineless automaton supported by piles of gold |
| Shadows | a second reality, often sinister | a Chinese anti-smoking campaign in which the father's smoking shadow holds a gun to his child's head |
| Silhouettes | an incomplete image the viewer fills in | the iPod dancers: no features, age or ethnicity, so viewers project themselves into them |
| Hidden messages | a code for those in the know | Paul Rand took a full page in *The New York Times* with R, C and A in Morse code, for a single reader: the head of RCA, a former wireless operator; Peter Saville and the album sleeves coded in colors, a "mass-produced secret" |
| Illusion and the mirror | the double take | illusions make viewers look twice, so they "buy" twice the time; the mirrored illy cup that reassembles the logo |

The mechanism ("Brainjack") has two effects. The discovery produces a jolt of revelation. The code creates complicity: whoever cracks it feels part of a club on the same wavelength.

## Application

- Start from the insight: the "hidden layer" is often exactly the truth the target avoids (see [[INS-092-an-insight-is-a-meaningful-human-truth-and-has-to-pass|INS-092]]).
- Choose the device by the type of truth: the inside → X-ray; a consequence → shadow; hypocrisy → torn image; identification → silhouette.
- Match the decoding effort to the audience: a code too hard for the general public becomes silence.

## Example

In *Sea Cannot Be Cut Apart* (1988), Shigeo Fukuda welded 2,084 pairs of scissors together. The heap of metal looks like nothing, but its shadow on the wall is a galleon. The viewer sees the object first, then discovers the image, and the revelation makes the work memorable.

## Questions

- Do hidden messages work in a feed, where the viewer stays for two seconds? Which format gives them time (poster, packaging, OOH)?
- Where is the line between a hidden message and manipulation (see the myth of subliminal advertising)?
