# Visual wordplay: the pun, the visualized expression, the rebus and the question

> **The idea in one sentence:** words have two, three or more meanings, and an idea is born when the picture or the context activates two of them at once.

## Explanation

Ingledew groups several verbal and visual techniques.

| Technique | Mechanism | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| The pun | two meanings of the same word, or two words that sound alike | "Optical Nerve": the optic nerve and visual bravado, on a picture of the inside of an eye |
| Wordplay | the same word read in two ways | "Labour Isn't Working" (Saatchi & Saatchi): "labour" is both the party and the workers who were unemployed; "Hallo Boys" (Wonderbra) greets both the admirers and the cleavage |
| Expressions visualized | an idiom translated literally into a picture | posters wrapped in red tape (bureaucracy), a protest against the closure of the Greater London Council |
| The rebus | words replaced by things | "I ❤ NY" (Milton Glaser), "the most famous rebus of all time" |
| Cryptic communication | the clue leads you down a false trail, the answer needs a different reading | "delayed-effect" communication: the reward comes when you decode it |
| Questions | a question presumes a dialogue and asks for a reply | Volkswagen: how does the man who drives the snowplow drive to the snowplow? |

Three rules run through all the entries. **Economy**: Ingledew suggests the exercise of paying for every word, as with telegrams; "Labour Isn't Working" has three words, "Hallo Boys" two. **Collecting**: Leo Burnett gathered phrases and analogies he heard in conversations or read in newspapers. **Wavelength**: a cryptic message has to be calibrated for its audience; if the audience doesn't "get it", there is no reward.

## Application

- Write the list of the brief's key words and look up all their meanings in the dictionary and the thesaurus.
- List the idioms linked to the product or the problem (in Romanian, for instance, "to put your shoulder to it" or "to take your heart in your teeth") and draw them literally.
- Test the decoding with five people from the target: how many get the second meaning within three seconds?

## Example

The "Labour Isn't Working" poster, made by Saatchi & Saatchi for the British Conservatives, rests on the double meaning of *labour*: the party under attack and the people left without work. The poster has only three words, and the writer Anthony Howard called it "the most powerful poster in post-war politics".

## Questions

- Puns rarely translate. How do you adapt a pun-based campaign for another language?
- When does a pun become "cheap" (the kind of hairdresser's name Ingledew cites)?
