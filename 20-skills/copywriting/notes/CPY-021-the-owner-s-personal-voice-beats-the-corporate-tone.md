# The owner's personal voice beats the corporate tone

> **The idea in one sentence:** in Watkins's collection, several highly successful ads are written as a letter or a conversation between the owner and the customer: familiar, honest, sometimes self-deprecating, the exact opposite of "company" language.

## Explanation

The cases that support the principle:

- **Harry & David (G. Lynn Sumner, 1934–1936).** The agency wrote, in the name of the two farmer brothers, a simple letter to 15 top New York executives, each delivered with a box of pears: 11 of the 15 ordered 489 boxes within 24 hours. The *Fortune* ad kept the same tone of a man from the ranch who wonders whether he is foolish to spend the price of a tractor on a page. It opened a new industry: selling fruit by mail.
- **Zareh (Boston).** A haberdasher writes a weekly column in a "loose" style, on his cousin's advice: stories from the shop, jokes about the sartorial conservatism of Bostonians, warnings to bargain hunters. Sales grew from $60,000 to $250,000 in five years, on an advertising budget of $3,500 a year.
- **Bruggner's (South Bend).** The owner wrote the ad for a "White Elephant Sale" himself, with unsellable goods and self-mockery. His explanation: people want to laugh and like honesty, and the best tactic is to make yourself look stupid and let the customer feel superior.
- **Fels Naptha (Sid Ward, Y&R, 1928).** Ward had read the letters women customers had written in, fresh and intimate, and wrote an ad in the same form. It drew letters "like a radio program".
- **Henry Ford (1927).** The Model A announcement began with a 1,500-word letter he signed, in his own style.

Watkins notes a shared condition: the client did not "correct" the voice. Wallachs published 1,560 editorials by Les Pearl, with the promise to make no changes apart from matters of fact or legality.

## Application

- For small businesses or well-known founders, write in the first person; the person's real story is an advantage the big companies do not have.
- Read the customers' letters, reviews and messages and borrow their register.
- Self-deprecation works when the product is good; otherwise it confirms the weakness.

## Example

Rogers Peet asked its copywriters for the tone of a friendly chat across the luncheon table, with the facts told "with a smile, and a bit of whimsy".

## Questions

- How do you keep the founder's voice when the company grows and dozens of people write?
- Is the "authentic" voice of influencers today's version of Zareh?
