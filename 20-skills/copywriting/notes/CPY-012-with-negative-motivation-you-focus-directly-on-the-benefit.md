# With negative motivation you focus directly on the benefit, and with positive motivation on its emotional consequence

> **The idea in one sentence:** a benefit can be something the product has (attribute), something you experience with it (characteristic) or something you feel (emotion); how you bring it to the fore depends on the purchase motive.

## Explanation

Once you have chosen the benefit, one decision remains: how to **focus** it in the message. The authors break the benefit down into three aspects (Table 8.5):

- **Attribute (A)**: an objective component ("antibacterial", "no calories");
- **Characteristic (C)**: a subjective claim ("easy to use", "tastes great");
- **Emotion (E)**: a feeling associated with the product ("excitement", "relief").

The focus options (Tables 8.6–8.7, with the book's examples):

| Motivation | Focus | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Negative | **A**, only for an expert target audience, which infers the benefit itself | Kira (St. John's Wort): "900 micrograms of hypericin" |
| Negative | **C**, the subjective characteristic directly | Dove Summer Glow: "Basking in the Glow" |
| Negative | **A → C**, the attribute supports the subjective claim | Nivea Visage: the best facial skincare product, in a poll of over 12,000 consumers |
| Negative | **E⁻ → C**, a negative emotion dispelled by the characteristic | Max Factor Lipfinity: "Is your lipstick still on?" and "Sexy lips 100% guaranteed all night" |
| Positive | **C → E⁺**, the characteristic leads to the emotion | Mercedes-Benz: memories, the most valuable commodity |
| Positive | **E⁺**, pure emotion | San Pellegrino: "Live in Italian", a couple on a scooter in a cobbled street |

The rule: with negative motives, the information goes directly to the benefit. With positive motives, the emphasis falls on the emotional consequences. The feeling experienced in the ad *is* the benefit, and it is linked to the brand in memory so that it can be felt again at the shelf.

## Application

- Before the headline, note the motive (negative/positive) and the aspect of the benefit (A/C/E). Then choose the formula from the table.
- Don't write only attributes for a broad audience. The attribute convinces only experts; the others need the characteristic it supports.
- With positive motives, don't "explain" the emotion with arguments. Show it.

## Example

For an anti-dandruff shampoo (a problem to solve), the E⁻ → C version: "Dark shoulders make you avoid your favorite shirt? No visible dandruff from the first week." For a premium ice cream (pleasure), the E⁺ version: the picture of a spoon sinking slowly, with no explanatory text.

## Questions

- In which situations could a luxury brand still use an attribute focus (mechanical watches, for example)?
- How does the table translate into the format of a six-second post?
