# Humor attracts attention and liking but steals brand memory: it works mainly for low-cost products bought without much thought

> **The idea in one sentence:** a funny ad is watched, liked and remembered, but people remember the joke, not the product, and the effect on sales runs through implicit attitudes.

## Explanation

Fennis and Stroebe sum up three effects of humor:

1. **It attracts attention.** Eisend's meta-analysis (2009): r = 0.42 on attention; humor counters the avoidance of advertising.
2. **It distracts.** The eye-tracking study by Strick et al. (2010): humorous texts received more attention, but brands were recognized less often, and the longer people looked at the joke, the less they recognized the brand. Hansen et al. (2009): humorous ads were remembered better, but the advertised product worse. Humor impairs explicit brand memory, not implicit brand memory.
3. **It transfers affect.** The pleasure becomes associated with the brand through evaluative conditioning: r = 0.37 on attitude toward the ad, 0.19 toward the brand, 0.19 on purchase intention, but about 0 on actual purchasing (only 4 studies). Strick et al. (2009): the brand paired with humorous cartoons was chosen more often (an energy drink), and the effect was mediated by the **implicit** attitude, even among people who did not recognize the product.

The more recent meta-analysis (Walter et al., 2018, 89 studies) confirms the pattern and shows stronger effects for **related humor**, mainly with highly involved audiences.

Limits: most studies use humor unrelated to the product, unfamiliar brands and low-cost items. Humor is most often used, and most effective, for low-cost hedonic goods: snacks, desserts, alcohol. And, like any source of salience, it loses its power when the whole category becomes funny.

## Application

- Tie the joke to the benefit or the brand: related humor is remembered together with the message.
- Put the brand inside the mechanism of the joke, not after it (the brand should be the punchline, not the signature).
- For high-involvement decisions, humor alone is not enough; combine it with arguments.

## Example

The BMW ad in which the Jaguar logo seems so frightened by the BMW that it tries to escape (p. 226): relevant humor that carries the "more powerful car" message itself.

## Questions

- Why are the ads that win awards for humor not always the ones that sell?
- How do you test whether people remember the brand and not only the joke?
