# Execution formats have predictable strengths and weaknesses: slice-of-life, announcer, spokesperson, testimonial, vignettes, music, humor

> **The idea in one sentence:** the same strategy can be dressed in very different formats; the choice of format decides how credible, memorable and expensive the message will be.

## Explanation

Milavsky's synthesis, drawn from his experience with TV commercials (and valid for other media too):

| Format | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Slice-of-life** (a little playlet) | the viewer "overhears", so the message seems unmotivated; empathy; often problem/solution | needs a good script, good actors and good production |
| **The announcer** ("the voice of God", the barker who shouts) | cheap, easy to dub into other languages | tells, does not convince; the shouting triggers defenses; a lazy option |
| **The celebrity** | comes in halfway up the learning curve; brings trust and aura | cost, ego, risk of scandal; the *video vampire*: the celebrity sucks the attention away from the product |
| **The invented spokesperson** (the GEICO gecko, Flo) | a character that can be shaped, with no star fee | needs a long-term vision and a well-thought-out biography |
| **The owner as presenter** | cheap, easy approval | his motivation is obvious; it rarely works (Dave Thomas at Wendy's is the exception) |
| **The spontaneous testimonial** | ticks expertise, trust, lack of motive | needs many interviews for a few gems; reading from a prompter kills it |
| **Vignettes** | show several uses, users, settings; suggest normality | the link with the product can be lost if it comes too late |
| **Music and song** | the strongest memory aid, positive emotion | the message gets lost if it is not simple and repeated |

**Humor**: Milavsky loves humor, but not jokes. A joke wears out after the second airing; situational humor (as in Seinfeld or Cosby) produces a warm smile that lowers defenses and bonds the viewer to the story. The condition is relevance: humor with no link to the product is **borrowed interest** and distracts.

## Application

- Choose the format by strategy: problem/solution for a benefit that can be demonstrated, testimonial for credibility, vignettes for a range, a character for a campaign lasting years.
- With celebrities: a fit with the product, a script in their voice, a strong close on the brand. Molson ended its commercials with rock stars on a huge outdoor deck in the shape of a guitar, bearing the Molson logo.
- With testimonials: get a signed release and pay the participants.

## Example

To save a radio testimonial that the lawyers would not let them air in the real voice, the team hired an actress. After a few reads that sounded like reading a script, they took the script away from her and asked her to tell the story as if it had happened to her. She became believable at once.

## Questions

- Which formats in the table are specific to TV, and how do they translate to TikTok or podcasts?
- Are influencers celebrities, testimonials or a new format?
