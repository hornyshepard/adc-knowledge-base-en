# The brand's voice stays constant, while the tone adapts to channel, audience and situation

> **The idea in one sentence:** the voice is the brand's personality put into words and does not change from one channel to another; the tone is the way this voice is modulated by context, from a joke on social media to seriousness in a complaint.

## Explanation

Three sources, written from different angles, arrive at the same distinction:

| | **Voice** | **Tone** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | the brand's personality expressed through language | the emotional expression of that personality at a given moment |
| How much it changes | stays relatively constant, like the brand | varies with channel, audience, situation, mood |
| Examples | authoritative, conversational, educational, offbeat | funny, reassuring, empathetic, formal |

Sachdev (SRC-031) gives the example of Mailchimp: the same conversational voice, with offbeat, dry humor, on the website, on Twitter and on Facebook, matched to each platform. His warning: consumers notice, and are even confused by, inconsistencies of voice and tone across channels. Myra El-Bayoumi, in *Goddess Guide to Branding* (SRC-016), gives the simple test: a brand with a serious, formal voice still becomes a little more relaxed on social media, and one with a playful voice becomes formal in a regulatory environment. That is tone. Najafov (SRC-039) adds the audience: casual for younger people, professional for B2B, empathetic in complaints, but always in line with the brand's values.

The exercise in SRC-016 turns the idea into a tool: you choose 3–5 personality traits that guide the voice, then a vocabulary, that is, the words the brand uses often and the ones it will never use.

## Application

- Write the voice guide on one page: 3–5 traits, each with a "like this / not like this" and a few allowed and banned words.
- Add a tone matrix: the same voice in a post, a newsletter, an error message, a reply to a complaint.
- Check a new text with the signature test: if you cover the logo, can the brand be recognized by the way it speaks?

## Example

Mailchimp keeps a conversational voice with dry humor on all channels. On Twitter it jokes about daylight saving time, and on Facebook it asks why only the big advertisers should have all the fun during the Super Bowl.

## Questions

- Can a brand have a strong voice without taking a risk in moments of crisis, when the tone has to change abruptly?
- Who keeps the voice when dozens of people, agencies and creators write on the brand's behalf?
