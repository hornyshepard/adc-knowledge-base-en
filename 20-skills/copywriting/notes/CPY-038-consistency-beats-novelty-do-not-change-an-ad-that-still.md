# Consistency beats novelty: do not change an ad that still works

> **The idea in one sentence:** many of the lasting successes in Watkins's collection come from the same idea, the same format or even the same text repeated year after year, while the "improvements" made because the team was bored cost readers.

## Explanation

Watkins gathers evidence for a virtue that creatives underrate:

| Brand | What it repeated | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Salada Tea | about sixty years of small ads, "Salada Is Delicious Tea" | the largest selling brand of tea in North America in 1948; it seems to "break all the rules but one" (consistency) |
| B. F. Goodrich | fifteen consecutive years with the same layout, the same type faces, the same kind of narrative copy | the director of advertising says the basic reason for the success is the running time itself |
| Sherwin Cody | the same text almost unchanged for decades | inquiries running into millions |
| Smith Brothers | small space, plenty of insertions, simple copy, for a century | "Trade" and "Mark", better known than the reasons to buy |

The counterexample is just as instructive. The *Wally's Wagon* column, which grew out of the Shell ads, ran unchanged for nine years. The only time the team tried to "improve" the formula, it cost about 20 percent of the readership, and they hastily went back. At Hathaway, the client promised never to change a word of the copy, and the campaign ran for eight years with steady growth.

Watkins does not preach standing still. The format stays, the interpretation is renewed: Ivory and Woodbury kept their idea and changed their look with each era, and Four Roses never noticeably changed its format but had new ideas in every ad.

Modern sources agree. Fernández Gómez and Milavsky state the principle of perseverance: the people who get bored with a campaign are the client and the agency, not the public. Fennis and Stroebe (SRC-012) add the mechanism: repetition raises attitudes up to a peak, then wears them out, and substantive variation within the same idea delays wear-out, exactly what Ivory and Four Roses did.

## Application

- Before you propose a new campaign, ask whether the old one still works or whether only the team is bored with it; the public sees it far less often than you do.
- Separate what stays fixed (idea, format, character, slogan) from what is renewed (situations, examples, offers).
- Measure wear-out with real readership or sales data, not internal taste.

## Example

Consistency requires a brave client and a patient agency. Watkins takes his hat off to an advertiser who knows he has something good and sticks to it.

## Questions

- How does consistency square with social platforms' demand for constantly new content?
- When does consistency become rigidity, for example an outdated image that has to be repositioned (Marlboro)?
