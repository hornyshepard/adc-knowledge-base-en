# The mail-order school: the coupon turned copywriting into a measurable experiment

> **The idea in one sentence:** because every clipped coupon could be counted, mail-order copywriters were the first to learn exactly which headline, which offer and which position in the magazine worked, long before A/B testing.

## Explanation

Watkins brings together a whole school, much of it from Ruthrauff & Ryan, an agency that specialized in selling by mail:

- **John Caples**, "They Laughed When I Sat Down at the Piano" (1925): the story of humiliation turned into triumph. At BBDO, Caples became an advocate of testing the effectiveness of creative work, and the Caples Awards (1978) bear his name.
- **Max Sackheim**, "Do You Make These Mistakes in English?": it ran almost unchanged for decades; only the testimonials changed.
- **Lillian Eichler**, *Book of Etiquette*: 2,000,000 copies sold with a $1,500,000 campaign.
- **Bruce Barton**, Marie Antoinette: a quarter page asking whether you had ever read her tragic story pulled eight times as many coupons as the double spreads.
- **Phoenix Mutual** (Barton and Caples): the agencies said life insurance could not be sold by coupon advertising and that coupons were undignified; pretesting found the Fisherman ad, with nearly 3 times as many inquiries and 4 times the sales.
- **I.C.S.**: even the position was tested; the ad nearest the second cover usually did best, and third-party testimonials sometimes upset the rule.

Watkins notes that more well-rounded copywriters have come from the ranks of mail order than from any other field, because the discipline forces clarity: the ad has to make the whole sale. Richards completes the history: Homer J. Buckley (1905) created the first real direct mail advertising agency, and Lester Wunderman coined the term "direct marketing" (1967).

## Application

- Build every response ad as an experiment: a single variable changed (the headline, the offer, the image), a tracking code, a decision made from the numbers.
- When you find a formula that works, do not change it out of boredom; change only what the test shows can be better.
- Use the lessons in structure: problem, story, proof (testimonial), risk-free offer, urgent call to action.

## Example

Every Sherwin Cody, Book of Etiquette or I.C.S. coupon carried a department code ("Dept. 3911"), so that it was known which magazine and which version the response came from.

## Questions

- What is lost when you optimize only what can be counted (the immediate response) and not the brand?
- Are today's landing pages and performance ads simply new editions of the coupon?
