# Advertising discourse comes in four types (narrative, descriptive, explanatory, argumentative) and is checked after it is written

> **The idea in one sentence:** with little time and space, the ad tells the essence of the brand through one of four types of discourse; the structure is rarely chosen in advance, but it has to be checked afterwards, so that the message is coherent.

## Explanation

*La boîte à outils de la publicité* classifies advertising discourses, in picture or text:

| Discourse | What it does | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Narrative** (*discours narratif*) | tells an event, with a beginning and an end, like a tale or a fable; the structure of storytelling | emotion, empathy |
| **Descriptive** (*discours descriptif*) | shows, creates an atmosphere, time seems suspended, the brand is sublimated; uses metaphor and sensations | imagination, desire |
| **Explanatory** (*discours explicatif*) | conveys objective information, instructions for use, a technical description | an impression of reality, authenticity |
| **Argumentative** (*discours argumentatif*) | supports or opposes a thesis, expresses an opinion; subjective vocabulary, an injunctive tone | pushes to action (a conative objective) |

The **narrative schema** recommended for narrative discourse: initial situation (*situation initiale*) → disruptive element (*élément perturbateur*, the problem) → twists (*péripéties*) → resolution (the solution the brand brings) → final situation, satisfying thanks to the brand.

In the register of persuasion, the book takes up the rhetorical triad: **logos** (rational discourse, figures, graphs), **ethos** (the brand and its reputation, credible and desirable) and **pathos** (centered on the consumer, emotion, empathy). It also distinguishes four approaches to persuasion: rational, repetitive (the slogan repeated mechanically until it is memorized), affective (unconscious motivations) and social (belonging to a reference group).

Writing rules: simple, short, punchy; repeat the key words; double meaning and wordplay help memory, but **the brand must be remembered, not the wordplay**.

## Application

- Once you have written the script or the body copy, label each paragraph with its type of discourse. If you mix four types in 30 seconds, simplify.
- In a narrative script, check whether the brand is in fact the resolution of the disruptive element or only shows up at the end.
- Choose the type by objective: explanatory for new and technical products, narrative for image, argumentative for action.

## Example

The Bouygues Télécom web series "Les Dumas" (DDB, 2012) is a classic narrative discourse: a family gets tangled up in new technologies (the disruptive element), and with Bouygues Télécom everything goes smoothly, in a light tone that creates attachment.
