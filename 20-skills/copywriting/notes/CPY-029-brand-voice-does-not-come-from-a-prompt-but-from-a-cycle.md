# Brand voice does not come from a prompt but from a cycle: examples → draft → human editing → feedback → final check

> **The idea in one sentence:** left on its own, a generative model produces correct but generic texts; a brand's voice appears only when people give it examples, edit and correct it systematically.

## Explanation

Najafov openly admits a limit of AI: without human oversight, the model may struggle to capture the unique voice and nuanced tone of a brand. The result is generic or misaligned messages. The two anonymous cases in the book tell the same story:

- **Company A** (e-commerce blog posts) automated the first drafts. At first, the content lacked the brand's unique tone and required substantial edits. The fix was to give the model examples of past content and to establish a review framework.
- **Company B** (product descriptions for a large inventory) got a solid base, but some descriptions were too generic. The fix was a hybrid approach: AI efficiency plus the team's creativity.

From the cases, the author draws a **framework for integrating human oversight** in five steps:

| Step | What is done |
|---|---|
| 1. Training the AI | a dataset of past content that exemplifies the voice + clear guidelines on tone, style and messaging |
| 2. Initial draft creation | the AI quickly generates the first version |
| 3. Human review and refinement | editors check alignment with the guidelines and add the nuances the model misses |
| 4. Feedback loop | the team's corrections go back into the examples and guidelines; performance is monitored |
| 5. Quality assurance | a final check before publication |

The chapter on tone adds: tone adapts to the audience (casual for young people, formal for B2B) and to the context (empathetic in complaints), but has to stay consistent with the brand's values.

## Application

- Build a "voice pack": 5–10 approved texts, a list of allowed and banned words, *do / don't* examples.
- Measure how much you edit: if you rewrite more than half of each draft, the problem is in step 1, not with the editor.
- Keep the final decision with a person for texts where the brand is at stake (campaigns, manifestos, crises).

## Example

Company A's case shows the typical pattern: the time saved comes only after the team invests in examples and review rules, not from day one.

## Questions

- Can a brand with a very distinctive voice delegate its drafts? Or would it risk diluting its voice?
- Who in an agency is responsible for the "voice guide for AI": the copywriter, the strategist or the client?
