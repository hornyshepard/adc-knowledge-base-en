# The journalistic intro does three jobs in a single sentence, and the text is written as an inverted triangle

> **The idea in one sentence:** a brand that publishes has to write like a journalist: the headline grabs, the first sentence informs and lets readers select themselves, and the rest of the text can be cut from the bottom up without losing anything essential.

## Explanation

When they explain why brands have to become "publishers", Richards and McLean go into the technique of the British press (Fleet Street). A few rules useful to any content copywriter:

- **The headline does not tell the story; it sells it.** In newsrooms, the headline is written by the sub-editor, not the reporter. It is the advert for the article.
- **The intro**, the first sentence, has three jobs:
  1. convey the key elements of the story, so that someone who reads only that sentence is informed;
  2. make the story interesting enough to entice the reader to read on;
  3. allow the reader to self-select whether the story is for them.
  The book's example: a story about how pre-school childcare is funded. A non-parent gets the key facts and stops; a parent reads on.
- **The inverted triangle**: each paragraph loses readers, and that is intended. A well-written story can be edited from the bottom up without losing anything important. The hardest texts are often the shortest: the tabloid writer who tells a political story in 150 words has a harder job than the broadsheet journalist with 1,500.
- **The dropped intro**: some papers open with an entertaining detour and deliver the intro only three or more paragraphs in, because engaging the reader is sometimes more important than the story itself.
- **The mix of article types**: news stories, features, opinion pieces, leaders and sketches serve different functions (humor, authority, inspiration, deeper reading). The same mix keeps a brand's audience.

The authors also note that length is not the enemy: news sites with only short-form articles lack authority, so long articles and supporting stories have come back.

## Application

- Test your first sentence: if someone reads only that, do they know what happened and who it matters to?
- Write the headline last and separately from the text. It is a different copywriting exercise.
- In a newsletter or a brand article, put the benefit for the reader in the intro, not the company's story.

## Example

The parallel with a newspaper edition: editors choose articles according to what their readers expect, not according to the paper's stance. The result is a loyal readership that defines itself by the paper it reads. The same reasoning justifies personas in content marketing.

## Questions

- When is a dropped intro appropriate in a brand text? When does it become manipulation?
- Does the inverted triangle also work in a 30-second video script?
