# Humor in advertising: from forbidden "cleverness" to the reward that buys attention

> **The idea in one sentence:** for decades, the great practitioners saw humor as a distraction from selling; a more recent formula treats it as the reward that makes the reader accept the strategic message.

## Explanation

Richards, drawing on Beard's history of advertising humor (2005), shows a step-by-step evolution. The Powers style and Kennedy's reason-why largely shunned humor. In the 1920s, Lord & Thomas was running ads warning advertisers not to rely on "cleverness", or even novelty. Ogilvy, in the 1950s and 1960s, shunned humor; Burnett and Bernbach used it. Toward the end of the century, television, tabloids and video games made the public more accepting of entertainment, although events such as the attacks of 11 September 2001 put limits on humor for a time.

To get out of the "sell or entertain?" dilemma, Richards cites the formula of Professor Deborah Morrison (University of Oregon): an ad must both **"work and reward."** The "work" part is the strategy (reason-why, USP, long-term image). The "reward" part is the reason the consumer pays attention to it, and humor is its oldest form. Later came other rewards, such as activations that get consumers directly involved.

The research supports the formula. The meta-analyses summarized by Fennis and Stroebe (SRC-012) show that humor related to the message is remembered together with the brand, while the gratuitous joke steals its memory. Milavsky (SRC-021) makes the same distinction between the humor of a situation and the joke that stops being funny after a couple of hearings.

Watkins offers evidence from practice, from before the Creative Revolution. The Shell series with likeable service station men, cartooned and full of dialogue, was better read than the magazines' editorial content. The owner of Bruggner's found on radio that listeners responded only once the commercials became humorous.

## Application

- Make the humor carry the benefit, not cover it up: the Arrow horse joke is itself about Arrow shirts not shrinking.
- Check whether the jokes belong to the product (inherent drama) or are "borrowed" from anywhere.
- Adjust to the context: sensitive categories and moments of crisis call for caution.
