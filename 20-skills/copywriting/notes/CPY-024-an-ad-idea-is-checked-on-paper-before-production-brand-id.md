# An ad idea is checked on paper before production: brand ID, provocative, clear, memorable, unique, affordable

> **The idea in one sentence:** persuasion has sequential steps (exposure, attention, comprehension, acceptance, retention, action), and an idea has to get through all of them; Milavsky's checklist shows where it gets stuck.

## Explanation

Milavsky starts from William McGuire's model: **exposure → attention → comprehension → acceptance → retention → action**. Each step does not naturally follow the one before; the audience needs to be motivated to take the next step. Buying media takes care of exposure; the rest is the idea's job.

**The six criteria for the idea on paper:**

| Criterion | What you check |
|---|---|
| **Brand ID** | is it clear who the message is from? Not five mechanical mentions, but a clear presence. Weak branding makes viewers credit the commercial to the category leader |
| **Provocative** | does it stop attention? The attention device is related to the product, not gratuitous (the scantily clad woman in the auto parts ad: you remember her, not the brand) |
| **Clarity** | is it understood in an instant, without internal jargon, acronyms or double negatives? |
| **Memorable** | rhyme, rhythm, repetition, a twist on an older saying? |
| **Uniqueness** | does it avoid looking like someone else's ad? Me-too advertising implies me-too products |
| **Cost of producing ideas** | can it be produced well within the budget? As a guide, 10 percent of the total budget for production |

**When the ideas are presented** (ch. 7): reread the strategy out loud; listen to all the options before commenting; ask for the team's recommendation and the reason for it; toss out any idea that does not express the strategy, however interesting; check audio–video synchronization; ask whether the target would identify with the ad and whether the idea can be explained in less than 30 seconds and stands on its own without the agency's preamble. The client's comments are phrased as questions, not rewrites.

## Application

- Put the table on the wall of the presentation room and tick it for each idea.
- The preamble test: show the idea to a colleague who has not heard the brief. The public will not get the agency's explanation.
- Ask what the "net impression" is: what did the person understand, in one simple sentence?

## Example

With almost no production budget for a regional natural gas utility, Milavsky's agency shot a single static shot: someone sits in front of a natural gas fireplace, and off screen a man and a woman argue that the fireplace is so comfortable their visitor will never want to leave. The visitor is Santa Claus: "He could at least get his reindeer off the roof." The commercial ran for several years.

## Questions

- Can the "provocative" and "clear" criteria conflict? How do you balance them?
- Is it good for a client to judge ideas with a grid, or does the grid kill intuition?
