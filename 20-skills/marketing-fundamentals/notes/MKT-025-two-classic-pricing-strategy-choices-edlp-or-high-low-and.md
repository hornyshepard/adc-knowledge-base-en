# Two classic pricing strategy choices: EDLP or high/low and, at launch, skimming or penetration

> **The idea in one sentence:** a pricing strategy is a long-term approach for the whole offering: either consistently low prices (EDLP) or regular prices with temporary reductions (high/low); for a new product, either you start high and come down gradually (skimming) or you come in low to take the market quickly (penetration).

## Explanation

**EDLP vs. high/low** (Grewal and Levy, ch. 15):

| | EDLP (everyday low pricing) | High/low |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | stable prices, between the regular price and the competitors' deep discounts | regular prices, with temporary sales |
| Value for the customer | lower search costs | attracts both the customers who are not price sensitive and the bargain hunters |
| Typical communication | on an average purchase, your basket is cheaper here (Walmart) | "get them while they last"; a reference price (originally $250, now $169) |

The **reference price** is the price against which buyers compare the sale price. Advertised reference prices must be genuine.

**Skimming vs. penetration** (new products):

| | Skimming | Penetration |
|---|---|---|
| The logic | innovators pay a premium to be first; the price comes down segment by segment | a low initial price, for sales, market share and to discourage competitors |
| Conditions | a product that breaks new ground, barriers to entry | production capacity, costs that fall with volume |
| Risks | early customers are upset when the price falls | a low price does not signal quality |

One argument for skimming: you can always lower a price that is too high, but you can almost never raise one that is too low without resistance.

**What the other sources add.** Policarpio (SRC-002) adds useful tactics: psychological pricing (9.99), bundles, captive products (the cheap printer, the expensive ink), dynamic pricing and geographic pricing. His definitions of high/low and penetration are confused, however; use the Grewal-Levy version. Marketing 4.0 turns dynamic pricing into a principle (price as "currency", MKT-035), and the digital textbooks add subscriptions and freemium (MKT-030).

## Application

- The pricing strategy changes the role of advertising: with EDLP, communication builds trust in "always cheap"; with high/low, it announces events.
- A product launched through skimming first needs status communication for innovators, then value arguments for the majority.
- Promotions that are too frequent erode the reference price and the perception of quality.

## Example

Video games (Far Cry 6) are launched through skimming: fans pay the top price to be first, then the price falls for price-sensitive players.

## Questions

- Can a brand move from high/low to EDLP without scaring off customers used to the sales?
- Do dynamic prices online make the EDLP vs. high/low distinction obsolete?
