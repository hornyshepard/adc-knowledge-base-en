# Services differ from goods in four ways: intangible, inseparable, heterogeneous, perishable

> **The idea in one sentence:** a service cannot be touched before purchase, is produced while it is consumed, varies from one delivery to the next and cannot be stored, and each of these traits calls for its own marketing response.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy (Exhibit 13.2) explain the four differences:

| Trait | What it means | Marketing response | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intangible | cannot be touched, tasted or seen | cues, symbols and images | Six Flags shows happy families and friends on a roller coaster |
| Inseparable | production and consumption happen at the same time; the customer participates | satisfaction guarantees, attention to the interaction | the hairstylist who appears to be having fun while cutting hair |
| Heterogeneous | quality varies with the person and the day | standards, training, machines, or deliberate customization | Planet Fitness, "Judgment Free Zone" |
| Perishable | cannot be stored | matching demand and supply through price and schedule | less expensive ski tickets at night |

The authors stress that firms cannot be split cleanly into service sellers and product sellers: most offerings combine goods and services (at an optical center you get your eyes examined and buy contact lenses).

Policarpio (SRC-002) adds a fifth trait, **no ownership** (a service is experienced, not owned), and three levels at which services marketing must excel: **external marketing** (to consumers), **internal marketing** (training and motivating employees) and **interactive marketing** (professionalism in contact with the client).

**Convergence.** The point that goods and services cannot be separated is taken to its end by Vargo and Lusch's service-dominant logic: entire economies are service-based, and goods only deliver the service (MKT-046). Kotler states it as a credo in Marketing 3.0: "Every business is a service business" (MKT-065).

## Application

- For a service, the creative work has to make the invisible visible: people, results, quality cues, proof.
- Inseparability turns employees into media: every interaction communicates the brand. The campaign has to be credible to the people who deliver the service.
- Perishability is an opening for promotions that fill the gaps (off-peak).

## Example

Casper, known for mattresses sold in impossibly small boxes, opened The Dreamery in New York City: curtained nooks with a bed for a paid 45-minute nap. The service is perishable and inseparable, and it is also a marketing tool for the product.

## Questions

- Does heterogeneity still hold for digital services (streaming, apps)? Or does the algorithm remove it?
- Is the experience a fifth type of offering, separate from goods and services?
