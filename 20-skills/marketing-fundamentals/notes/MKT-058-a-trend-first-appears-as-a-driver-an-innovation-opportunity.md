# A trend first appears as a driver (an innovation opportunity) and then becomes a force (pressure to adapt)

> **The idea in one sentence:** the same trend has two faces over time: at first it is a driver you can exploit proactively; as it matures, it becomes a force that makes you adapt.

## Explanation

Von Scheel separates two notions that are often mixed up (p. 254):

- A **force** is an external or internal circumstance that shapes an industry in a specific direction. If acted upon, it is an opportunity; if ignored, it becomes a threat and a risk to current operations.
- A **business driver** is the internal way you structure your value stream (resources, operations, services) to exploit the opportunity created by an external force. If acted upon, you grow and capture new markets.

The author ties them to a trend's maturity cycle. At first, the trend manifests itself as a driver, a "window of innovation opportunities" that requires proactivity. As adoption grows, it becomes a force that disrupts the usual way of working (pp. 88, 211). This gives the division of labor (p. 167):

| | Driver | Force |
|---|---|---|
| Time | creates the future | manages the present |
| Response | innovation | transformation |
| Business models concerned | service, value, revenue | operating, cost, performance |

**The same pattern in another source.** In the Kotler series, purpose moves from differentiator (Marketing 3.0) to "hygiene factor" (Marketing 5.0): exactly the move from driver to force (MKT-040).

## Application

- When you put a trend in a client deck, say explicitly whether it is still a driver (an advantage for whoever moves first) or has become a force (hygiene: without it you lose).
- For each force, ask: what breaks if we ignore it for 12 months? For each driver: what do we gain if we are among the first 5%?

## Example

Illustration from 2026: generative AI in content production was a driver in 2023, when few agencies used it for fast prototyping. It then became a force: clients expect costs and timelines set around these tools, and agencies that do not adapt lose pitches.

## Questions

- Can a trend jump straight to the force stage, for example a new regulation?
- Who in the agency should track the drivers, and who the forces?
