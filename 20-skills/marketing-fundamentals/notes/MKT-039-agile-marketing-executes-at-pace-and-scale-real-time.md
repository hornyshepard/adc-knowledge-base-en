# Agile marketing executes at pace and scale: real-time analytics, decentralized teams, a flexible platform, MVPs and rapid experimentation

> **The idea in one sentence:** when the lifecycles of products, and even of experiences, get shorter, the long-term marketing plan is outdated before it is carried out, and its place is taken by small teams that keep testing and adjusting.

## Explanation

The opening case is Zara: more than 10,000 different designs a year, from the catwalk to the storefront in a couple of weeks, based on the sales of each SKU (tracked with RFID) and on trends, with decentralized teams and production in small batches. The context: products have short lifecycles, even customer experience has an expiry date because social media broadcasts it, and customers want everything on demand (the new WWW: "whatever, whenever, wherever"), in a VUCA world.

Large companies do not become agile everywhere: they keep stable operations and set up separate agile teams for new growth projects. The components (Fig. 12.1):

1. **Real-time analytics**: social listening, traffic, transactions, RFID.
2. **Decentralized teams**: small, cross-functional (product development, marketing, technology), autonomous, on the scrum model; senior management monitors and coaches.
3. **A flexible product platform**: a core plus modules (features, touchpoints, creative designs), so you do not start from scratch.
4. **A concurrent process**: stages run in parallel, not in a waterfall or stage-gate sequence, with short daily meetings.
5. **Rapid experimentation**: a **minimum viable product** (MVP; it can also be a campaign idea), launched in small batches, with iterations and, if needed, **pivoting**.
6. **Open innovation** (Chesbrough): open innovation challenges, marketplaces, networks of partners (P&G's Connect+Develop).

The working tool is a **one-page worksheet** per iteration: market requirements, the solution and the MVP, tasks, timelines, market testing results.

**Convergences.** Von Scheel also recommends small experiments, which can fail safely, in complex contexts (MKT-054), and Wuebben describes temporary think–do–feel teams led by an orchestrator (MKT-061). The Grewal-Levy textbook stays with the iterative annual plan (MKT-012).

## Application

- For digital campaigns, propose short cycles: one core idea, several MVP variants tested in small markets, then a rollout of the winner.
- Build the campaign as a modular platform (a central idea plus interchangeable elements).
- Use the one-page worksheet as the status format between agency and client.

## Example

Zara responded quickly to criticism of fast fashion's waste and unfair labor practices: it announced its support for the circular economy and pledged that all its clothing would be made from sustainable materials by 2025.

## Questions

- Can strong brands be built through continuous iteration, or does long-term consistency matter more?
- How does rapid MVP testing fit with big creative ideas, which need time and trust?
