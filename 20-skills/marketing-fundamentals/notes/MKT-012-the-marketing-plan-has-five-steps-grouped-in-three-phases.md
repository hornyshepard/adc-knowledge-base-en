# The marketing plan has five steps grouped in three phases: planning, implementation, control

> **The idea in one sentence:** a marketing plan is the written document that links the firm's mission to the situation analysis, to the choice of target and positioning, to the mix and to the metrics, and the steps are repeated whenever the data call for it.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy define the marketing plan as a written document composed of an analysis of the current marketing situation, opportunities and threats, marketing objectives, the strategy in terms of the four Ps, action programs and projected financial statements. The process has five steps (Exhibit 2.2):

| Phase | Step | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Planning | 1. Define the business mission and objectives | what kind of business we are (Audi: "mobility", not "cars") |
| Planning | 2. Conduct a situation analysis | SWOT plus the environmental factors (CDSTEP) |
| Implementation | 3. Identify and evaluate opportunities | segmentation, targeting, positioning (STP) |
| Implementation | 4. Implement the marketing mix and allocate resources | the four Ps for each product |
| Control | 5. Evaluate performance using marketing metrics | sales, profit, shares; portfolio analysis |

Ideas missing from the shorter textbooks:

- **Strategic planning is not sequential.** The situation analysis can change the mission; implementation can show that the resources are not enough.
- **Responsibility is measured only on what you control.** A store manager is not to blame for low profit if headquarters cut prices.
- **No single metric tells the whole story.** Sales rise easily through discounts, but the margin falls.
- **The BCG matrix** (stars, cash cows, question marks, dogs) helps allocate resources, but placing a product in a quadrant can become a self-fulfilling prophecy: a product labeled a dog loses support and may actually become one.

Appendix 2A gives the structure of the document: executive summary, the company, objectives, situation analysis, STP, strategy, financial projections, implementation, control metrics.

**Tension.** Marketing 5.0 (SRC-005) argues that when life cycles get shorter, the long-term plan is out of date before it is completed and must be supplemented by agile teams that test continuously (MKT-039). Grewal and Levy answer this in part through the iterative nature of the steps.

## Application

- Ask the client for the marketing plan before the brief. The communication brief is a slice of steps 3 and 4.
- When you write a campaign strategy, keep the logic: situation → target and positioning → communication mix → metrics.
- An exercise from the book: write your personal marketing plan for getting a job.

## Example

Harley-Davidson (ch. 2): the metrics showed falling sales in the U.S., because of aging Baby Boomers. The firm moved production to Thailand for Asian markets and launched an electric motorcycle for younger riders.

- How realistic is an annual plan in a market that changes from one month to the next?
