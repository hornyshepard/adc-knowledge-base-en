# Trends are read on three levels: megatrend (10–15 years), macrotrend (5–10 years), microtrend (3–5 years)

> **The idea in one sentence:** every trend observation has to be placed on a level: megatrends are global forces lasting 10–15 years, macrotrends make them up over 5–10 years, and microtrends are the first local signs, lasting 3–5 years.

## Explanation

Von Scheel proposes a simple hierarchy (p. 255):

| Level | What it is | Typical duration | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Megatrend** | a global force that affects society, culture, finance, technology | 10–15 years | technology, globalization, consumer demands, environment |
| **Macrotrend** | a large-scale force, global and local, that always belongs to a megatrend | 5–10 years | smart cloud, digital marketing, 3D printing |
| **Microtrend** | a local, smaller-scale force affecting small groups; the first concrete sign of a movement | 3–5 years | blockchain → decentralized finance; 3D printing → bioprinting |

In chapter 4 the author gathers 77 megatrends into an "Industry 4.0 Periodic Table" of the fourth industrial revolution (technology, regulatory, workforce, environment, globalization, demographic, economic, consumer). For advertising, the **consumer megatrends** matter most: Radical Personalization, Reality Merge (digital, virtual and physical), Privacy & Security, Wellness, Ethical Living, Sensory Stimulation, Convenience, Authenticity, Value Price Reality and the Empowered Individual.

**Links to other models.** The Grewal-Levy textbook puts the same forces in the CDSTEP macroenvironment, without time levels (MKT-005). Kotler (Marketing 6.0) also uses the term "micro-trends" for the signals that push toward immersion (SOC-048). The book, however, counts its groups and trends inconsistently (6 or 8 groups, 10 or 11 consumer trends), a sign that trend lists have to be read critically.

## Application

- In a trend report for a brand, label each observation with its level and horizon. Do not build a five-year brand platform on a TikTok microtrend.
- Use microtrends as early signals: if several point to the same macrotrend, you have a solid hypothesis.
- Tie consumer megatrends to insights: "authenticity" becomes useful only when you say what it means for your audience.

## Example

Illustration: "sobriety" (people drinking less alcohol) can be a local microtrend in a university town. It becomes credible as a macrotrend when the wellness megatrend supports it and it shows up in several markets; only then does it justify an alcohol-free product line.

## Questions

- Are the 3/5/10/15-year durations rules of thumb or conventions?
- How would you check whether a microtrend actually belongs to a megatrend?
