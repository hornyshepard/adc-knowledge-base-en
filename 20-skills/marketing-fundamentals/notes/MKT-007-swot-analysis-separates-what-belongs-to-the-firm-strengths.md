# SWOT analysis separates what belongs to the firm (strengths and weaknesses) from what belongs to the market (opportunities and threats): the same double view that von Scheel calls inside-out and outside-in

> **The idea in one sentence:** the situation analysis looks in two directions, toward the market (opportunities and threats) and toward the firm (strengths and weaknesses), and a good strategy chooses the opportunities the firm can exploit with its own strengths.

## Explanation

In Grewal and Levy's marketing plan, SWOT is the tool of step 2 (Exhibit 2.3):

| | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** (the firm) | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| **External** (the market) | Opportunities | Threats |

The book's example: PepsiCo has a diverse portfolio and music campaigns (S), but lower awareness than Coca-Cola and packaging that is not environmentally friendly (W); the market for healthy foods is growing (O), while taxes on soft drinks and reusable bottles threaten it (T). The authors recommend doing the SWOT for the main competitor too, because its weaknesses are often your opportunities. The decision rule: you pursue the opportunities that rest on your strengths, relative to the competition. The external part is fed by CDSTEP.

**The same idea in von Scheel (SRC-007).** Strategic positioning starts from two views. The *outside-in view*: you scan new entrants, the competition, regulation, customers, suppliers, substitutes and trends, with a team from which you exclude anyone who appears wedded to the status quo. The *inside-out view*: resources, capabilities and the reward system; a strategy that requires entrepreneurial behavior dies if the firm rewards years of service over performance. The meeting point is Porter's value chain, mapped into core differentiating, core competitive and non-core competencies.

**Where they agree and where they differ.** Both sources ask you to start from the outside and check on the inside. Grewal and Levy give a static matrix; von Scheel gives a process, with checking assumptions, looking for the missing information and the test of explaining the roadmap to the junior-most person in the organization.

## Application

- Keep 3 or 4 items per box, only the ones that change the communication decision.
- Check the direction: "the market for healthy products is growing" is an opportunity, not a strength. Confusing internal and external is the most frequent student mistake.
- Audit the capabilities before the idea: an "always-on" social media platform fails if nobody at the client approves content every day.

## Example

For PepsiCo, plastic packaging is an internal weakness that meets an external threat (consumers switching to reusable bottles). The answer: bubly only in cans, LIFEWTR in recycled plastic.

## Questions

- SWOT is a snapshot. How do you make it useful in a market that changes every month?
- Who decides what counts as a "strength": the customer's perception or management's opinion?
