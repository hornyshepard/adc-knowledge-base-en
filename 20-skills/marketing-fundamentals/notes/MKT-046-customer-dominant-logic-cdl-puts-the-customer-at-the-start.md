# Customer-dominant logic (CDL) puts the customer at the start of the value chain, not at its end

> **The idea in one sentence:** marketing has moved from good-dominant logic (GDL), in which the customer receives a good product at the end, to service-dominant logic (SDL) and then to customer-dominant logic (CDL), in which value arises in the customer's life and experience.

## Explanation

**Good-dominant logic (GDL).** Porter's value chain: the company transforms raw materials into products, delivers them to the end customer and provides after-sales service. The departments focus on tangible attributes, and the customer sits at the end of the process.

**Service-dominant logic (SDL).** Vargo and Lusch (2004) state that entire economies are service-based. They distinguish *operand* assets (physical: decor, design) from *operant* assets (knowledge and skills, like the waiter who knows how to explain wine). Competitive advantage lies in operant assets. Resources have no value as such: value is co-created with the customer when they are used, which makes the customer a "partial employee".

**Critiques and extensions.** Heinonen and colleagues (2010) propose **customer-dominant logic**: SDL still views the consumer as essentially passive, so the firm has to start from the customer's goals, intentions and activities. Value is experienced in the customer's life context (Helkkula), and the firm becomes a "value facilitator". Sometimes customers create value for themselves (Grönroos).

**The organizational consequence** (ch. 2): in CDL, all departments (R&D, marketing, finance, sales, HR) work together from the start, including on budgets, to understand the customer's functional and experiential expectations.

**Links with the rest of the skill.** The textbooks reach the SDL premise by a practical route: goods and services cannot be cleanly separated (MKT-027). The critique of marketing, however, reads the "partial employee" customer as unpaid work (MKT-047).

## Application

- In the brief, replace the question "what does our product offer?" with "what is the customer trying to do in their life, and where do we come in?".
- Check who takes part in defining the offer. If only marketing and R&D, you are still in GDL.

## Example

The Amazon bookstore (mini-case 4.1) is cited as an example of customer centricity: the physical store is organized like a website, with the top-rated books, a "You may also like" section and labels showing readers' reviews instead of just the price.

- Is CDL a new theory or a rewording of the textbooks' market orientation (MKT-003)?
- What does a firm risk if it lets itself be led entirely by what the customer does, with no vision of its own?
