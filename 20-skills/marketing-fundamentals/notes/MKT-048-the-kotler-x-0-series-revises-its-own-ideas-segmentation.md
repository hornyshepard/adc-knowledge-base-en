# The Kotler X.0 series revises its own ideas: segmentation, the role of purpose and the online–offline balance change from one book to the next

> **The idea in one sentence:** the four books do not form a cumulative theory but a series of dated positions in which the authors tacitly rewrite their earlier theses; each model has to be cited with its year and context.

## Explanation

Compared side by side, the books show at least five revisions, rarely acknowledged explicitly:

| Theme | The earlier position | The revision |
|---|---|---|
| **What 1.0 and 2.0 were** | 3.0 (2010): 1.0 aims to *sell*, 2.0 to *satisfy and retain* | 5.0 (2021): in 1.0 *satisfaction* is already the goal, in 2.0 *retention* |
| **The status of 3.0** | 3.0: a new era; 4.0 presents itself as its continuation | 5.0: 3.0 was "the ultimate stage of traditional marketing" |
| **Segmentation** | 3.0: communitization replaces STP; 4.0: communities are the new segments, the brand asks for permission | 5.0: "segments of one", targeting from big data; 6.0: AI segments down to the individual |
| **Purpose** | 3.0: values are the source of differentiation | 5.0: purpose is a hygiene factor; 6.0: Gen Z weighs the functional benefit more than the emotional appeal |
| **Online vs. offline** | 4.0: traditional marketing opens the relationship, digital closes it; 5.0: the ideal is the *digital-first* brand | 6.0: the physical remains central (e-commerce under 15 percent in the US), digital fatigue and the return to IRL appear |

The cases are reinterpreted too: New Coke is in 3.0 a lesson about a mission owned by consumers, and in 6.0 a lesson about taste tied to the brand.

**Two possible readings.** A charitable one: marketing has to follow the market, so revision is a virtue. A critical one: the models are formulated retrospectively, without data that would allow them to be tested, and each book rewrites history to justify the new stage. The same caution applies to the textbooks: Grewal-Levy and Kotler-Armstrong describe even the classic evolution differently (MKT-003).

## Application

- When you cite a model from the series, give the book and the year ("the five A's, Marketing 4.0, 2017"), not "according to Kotler".
- Do not combine contradictory recommendations mechanically (dropping segments, from 3.0, and segmenting down to the individual, from 5.0). Choose the one that fits the client and argue for the choice.
- Use the table in a seminar as an exercise on how "stages" are built in business literature.

## Example

The same Generation Z appears in 5.0 as concerned with social change and sustainability, and in 6.0 as pragmatic, focused on function and price, inclined toward large, safe companies. Both portraits are plausible, but they have opposite consequences for a purpose-based campaign.

## Questions

- Can a series that redefines its stages in every book offer a stable "map"?
- Which revision is a correction backed by data, and which is a change of fashion?
