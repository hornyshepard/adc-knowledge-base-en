# The 4P marketing mix: product creates, price captures, place delivers, promotion communicates value

> **The idea in one sentence:** the four Ps (formulated by E. Jerome McCarthy in 1960) are the controllable decisions through which the firm responds to the wants of its target market, and each has a distinct role in the value chain.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy link each P to a value verb (Exhibit 1.3):

| P | Role in value | What decisions it contains | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product | creating value | goods, services, ideas | Dyson: bladeless fans |
| Price | capturing value | everything the buyer gives up: money, time, energy | Frontier: a low fare, if you accept the middle seat |
| Place | delivering value | distribution, retail, supply chain | Starbucks: kiosks in airports |
| Promotion | communicating value | advertising, PR, sales promotions, personal selling, online | Oatly: baristas first, then packaging with bold messages |

Two observations matter for agencies. **Price is the only P that brings in money**; the other three generate costs (Policarpio confirms this and adds that price also signals the positioning). **Promotion is IMC**: in Chapter 2, the fourth P becomes integrated marketing communications, through which the value proposition is communicated. Product also includes ideas: a campaign for wearing a helmet "sells" an idea.

**Who wants to replace the 4Ps.** All the alternatives in the library keep the decisions but shift the perspective from the firm to the customer. Kotler (Marketing 4.0) proposes the four Cs: co-creation, currency, communal activation, conversation (MKT-035). Wuebben (SRC-008) also recalls the classic 4 Cs (consumer wants and needs, convenience, cost, communication), then rejects them all in favor of EP² (MKT-043). Batat replaces each P with an experiential "E" (EXP-007), and Sachdev adds 7 digital As *next to* the 4Ps, not in their place (MKT-030). For services, the mix extends to 7Ps (MKT-029).

## Application

- Before you propose a campaign, check the other three Ps: advertising cannot repair a wrong price or a product missing from the stores.
- Use the 4Ps as an audit grid: for each P, what value does it add and what contradicts the promise?
- A classic exercise: apply the 4Ps to yourself when you are looking for a job.

## Example

The teenagers who resell clothes on Poshmark (Adding Value 1.2) apply the mix intuitively: they choose distinctive pieces (product), describe them vividly (promotion), price them to sell quickly (price) and post them in the evening, after school, when buyers are online (place).

## Questions

- Is the 4P a seller's perspective? What concrete decisions are lost if you replace it with EP² or the 4 Cs?
- For services, are four Ps enough?
