# Carrotmob: organized consumers reward a firm only if it makes a responsible change

> **The idea in one sentence:** Carrotmob turns the boycott upside down. Instead of punishing a company (the stick), an organized group promises to spend money with it (the carrot), on condition that it first makes a verifiable social or environmental improvement.

## Explanation

Wuebben describes Carrotmob as a quasi-cause marketing agency. It uses social media to help groups of people, or "mobs", organize how to spend money with a brand, but only if the company commits to a responsible action. Here the marketer does not choose the cause: the community dreams up the campaign, signs up the company and organizes its members (ch. 16).

Two examples from the book:
- A cheese shop in San Francisco got $3,000 in sales after it agreed to install bike parking and water-saving technology.
- Unilever partnered with Carrotmob at Fresh & Easy stores in Pasadena. About 250 participants agreed to shop on a certain day if the stores installed non-ozone-depleting freezers. For every shopper who bought at least three Unilever products, the company gave a donation to an environmental group.

Paul Polman, Unilever's CEO, described a business world "moving from value-based transactions to values-based partnerships", in which the everyday consumer helps shape the social contract. In the same chapter, the Repair Cafe run by Hassett Ace Hardware (free repairs done by volunteers from the community) shows the peer-to-peer version of the same logic: the community does the brand's marketing.

## Application

- As a campaign mechanic: a conditional commitment ("if X people sign up, we do Y") turns the audience from spectator into co-author of the change.
- The change must be concrete and verifiable (an installation, a policy), not a vague donation.
- Afterwards, communicate the measured result, not only the promise.

## Example

In the Unilever case, a single event linked three things: a technical improvement in the store, a sales peak and a donation. Each actor (retailer, brand, community) gained something visible.

## Questions

- When does a "carrotmob" become just a promotion with an activist look? How many stores kept the freezers after the campaign?
- Can a brand organize a carrotmob itself without losing credibility?
