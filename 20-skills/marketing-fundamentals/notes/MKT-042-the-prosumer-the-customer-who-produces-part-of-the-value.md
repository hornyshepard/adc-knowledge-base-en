# The prosumer: the customer who produces part of the value, and whom Kotler already saw in 1986 as a market segment

> **The idea in one sentence:** the prosumer (*producer* + *consumer*) does not only buy but produces part of the service, the content or the marketing. For brands it is a distinct segment, to be equipped, not controlled.

## Explanation

The term comes from Alvin Toffler (*The Third Wave*, 1980): in the post-industrial society, the roles of producer and consumer merge. Jon Wuebben gives everyday examples of prosuming: self-checkouts at the supermarket, the DIY furniture of IKEA, Amazon reviews (unpaid work that lowers the company's costs), bug reporting, helping other users in forums, content on YouTube and Wikipedia (ch. 6).

The most useful text for marketing is Philip Kotler's 1986 paper "The Prosumer Movement: A New Challenge for Marketers": "prosumers should be looked at as another market segment." You identify the people with a strong need to produce their own goods and help them, instead of protecting the exchange system. Kotler takes the idea up again in Marketing 3.0, where "the age of participation" is the first of the three forces that produce values-driven marketing (MKT-033).

What motivates prosumers: enjoyment and learning, recognition from peers, the goals of the community. Self-determination theory (Deci and Ryan) adds three needs: competence, autonomy and relatedness. Identity moves from *what you consume* to *what you contribute to* (ch. 9).

**Where the authors part ways.** Wuebben and Kotler celebrate the prosumer; Parsons, Maclaran and Chatzidakis see the same co-creation as unpaid work, which the customer sometimes even pays for (MKT-047).

## Application

- Map the brand's existing prosumers: who already reviews, modifies, films tutorials or answers others?
- Give them tools (kits, templates), recognition (name, credit) and a real decision (a vote, a selection).
- Do not treat them as a "free marketing team". The exchange has to be fair.

## Example

LEGO created the Ambassador Program (40 fans in near-daily dialogue with the marketing team) and the LEGO Click community, where fans shared their own creations (ch. 16).

## Questions

- When does "prosuming" become exploitation of unpaid work? Wuebben even calls Uber drivers prosumers, and they are paid.
- What happens to a brand when its prosumers decide they no longer need it?
