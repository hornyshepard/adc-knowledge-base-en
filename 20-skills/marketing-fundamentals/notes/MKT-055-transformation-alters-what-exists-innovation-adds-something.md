# Transformation alters what exists, innovation adds something new: diagnose the problem chain or the goal chain before you choose

> **The idea in one sentence:** a cost or performance pain calls for transformation (you change what exists), while a value or revenue opportunity calls for innovation (you add something new); many programs fail because they confuse the two.

## Explanation

Von Scheel cites an aphorism attributed (perhaps apocryphally) to Einstein: given 20 days to solve a problem, he would spend 19 defining it. Innovation and transformation are "sticky" problems because they remain undefined and are treated as monoliths (p. 218). His distinction:

| | Problem chain | Goal chain |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | a challenge, a performance driver | an opportunity, a value driver |
| How it appears | performance problem, cost issue, operational pain point | service opportunity, revenue potential, desired client value |
| Response | **transformation**: you alter something that already exists | **innovation**: you add something new |
| Business models | operating, cost, performance | value, service, revenue |
| Order of the questions | where, when, whence, how, what, why, who, whether | why, whether, what, where, how, whence, when, who |

The book illustrates the six business models (pp. 174–184): Toyota and Kaizen (performance); McDonald's and its work with suppliers (cost); Apple, with the customer experience at the center (value); iPod/iTunes, and Virgin leveraging its brand across industries (service); Gillette, giving away razors and making money on the blades, and Netflix with the subscription (revenue).

**Convergence.** Zahay and her coauthors make a related distinction in digital: disruption changes the value proposition, and digital transformation is a strategy response, not a technology one (DIG-005). The same pair appears in von Scheel's other tools: drivers call for innovation, forces call for transformation (MKT-058).

## Application

- When the client asks for "an innovative idea", first ask what hurts. If the real problem is acquisition cost or weak conversion, you need transformation (optimization, integration), not a spectacular campaign.
- For value opportunities (a new service, a new audience), start with "why" and "whether", not with "how".

## Example

Netflix shifted its revenue model from rental to subscription (p. 184): an innovation in the goal chain, that is, new value for the client, not cost cutting on an old model.

## Questions

- Can the same facts be read as a problem and as an opportunity, depending on who makes the diagnosis?
- Why does the order of the questions differ between the two chains?
