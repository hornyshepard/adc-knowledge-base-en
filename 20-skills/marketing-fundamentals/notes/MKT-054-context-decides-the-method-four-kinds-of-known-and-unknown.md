# Context decides the method: four kinds of known and unknown call for four ways of deciding (an uncredited version of the Cynefin framework)

> **The idea in one sentence:** before you decide, you establish what kind of situation you face: a predictable problem is solved by categorizing, a complex one by small experiments, and a crisis by immediate action.

## Explanation

Von Scheel notes that many decisions that worked in the past fail in new contexts, because the problem was classified wrongly, usually oversimplified (p. 189). He proposes four contexts:

| Context | The situation | How you decide |
|---|---|---|
| **Known knowns** (predictable, simple) | cause and effect are clear to everyone | sense → categorize → respond |
| **Known unknowns** (blind spot, complicated) | the cause-and-effect relationship exists, but not everyone can see it; there are multiple right answers | sense → analyze → respond |
| **Unknown knowns** (uncertainty, complex) | the right answer cannot be worked out; patterns emerge only from experiments | probe (experiments that can safely fail) → sense → respond |
| **Unknown unknowns** (unexpected, chaotic) | cause and effect shift constantly | act to establish order → sense → move the situation from chaos to complexity |

The author's matrix adds what each quadrant requires: fact, analogy and assumption; hypothesis and iteration; bias and risk mitigation; exploration of forces and drivers (p. 190). As a tool that cuts across all four, he recommends the premortem.

**Where the model comes from.** The structure and the verbs (*sense–categorize–respond*, *probe–sense–respond*, *act–sense–respond*) are those of the Cynefin framework (Snowden and Boone, 2007). The book does not cite it and attributes the idea to Epiphanius of Salamis. Use the model, but credit it correctly. For complex contexts, Kotler reaches the same practical answer: agile teams and MVPs tested in small batches (MKT-039).

## Application

- Launching on a new platform or to an unknown audience is a complex context: small test budgets, several variants, decisions made from data, not from opinions.
- A reputation crisis is a chaotic context: you act first (stop the campaign, communicate) and analyze afterwards.
- Optimizing a performance campaign with a track record is a complicated context: here expert analysis works.

## Example

The author places events such as September 11, 2001 in the "unknown unknowns" quadrant and states that much of contemporary business operates there (p. 190).
