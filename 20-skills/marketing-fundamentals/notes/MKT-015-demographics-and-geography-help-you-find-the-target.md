# Demographics and geography help you find the target, benefits and lifestyle help you persuade it

> **The idea in one sentence:** no segmentation base is enough on its own; the criteria that are easy to measure say where the target is and how you reach it, while the psychological ones say what it wants and what to tell it.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy present five methods (Exhibit 9.2) and insist on combining them:

| Method | Typical segments | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Geographic | region, city, neighborhood | Kroger's different assortments by neighborhood |
| Demographic | age, gender, income, education | Froot Loops for kids, Special K for adults |
| Psychographic | values, self-concept, lifestyle | Dove's "Real Beauty Pledge"; Harley-Davidson with four targets |
| Benefits sought | convenience, economy, prestige | a comedy if you want to laugh, a drama if you want to cry |
| Behavioral | the occasion, loyalty | everyday suits vs. wedding suits |

Psychographics has three layers: **values** (the big goals of life), **self-concept** (how you see yourself) and **lifestyle** (how you live to reach your goals). Demographics are easy to find and to reach, but they predict poorly: athletic apparel is worn by people of all ages, including sedentary ones.

The rule for combining them: behavior shows how groups use the product, demographics and geography say where you find them, and benefits and lifestyle give substance to the product and the message. **Geodemographics** (Tapestry, 65 neighborhood segments) combines place, demographics and lifestyle. Policarpio (SRC-002) adds **firmographic** segmentation for B2B (size, industry, location) and **buyer personas**, hypothetical consumers with names, who sum up the research.

**Where the authors disagree.** Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott (MKT-016) accept any variable for defining the *market*, but ask that a campaign's *audience* be chosen only by attitude and loyalty; psychographics and demographics only describe it. Grewal and Levy use psychographics for selection as well. The digital sources add a third position: the persona is not the target but the way the target decides (DIG-012).

## Application

- In the brief, separate the "media targeting profile" (demographic, geographic) from the "portrait for the creative team" (values, self-concept, tensions).
- For an insight, look for the gap between the ideal and the real self-image. Dove built a platform on it.
- Test whether the segment described demographically actually behaves the same way.

## Example

Dove tested billboards with "real" women and two tick boxes, "flawed?" or "flawless?"; 1.5 million people texted their vote. The campaign became the basis of Dove's communication and of the "Real Beauty Pledge" commitment.
