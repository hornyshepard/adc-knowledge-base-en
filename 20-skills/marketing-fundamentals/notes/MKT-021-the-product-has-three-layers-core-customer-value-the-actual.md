# The product has three layers: core customer value, the actual product and associated services (the augmented product)

> **The idea in one sentence:** every product starts from the problem it solves for the customer, becomes a concrete object through its name, design, quality and packaging, and is completed by services (warranty, financing, support) that add value.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy describe the product as three concentric circles (Exhibit 11.1):

| Layer | What it contains | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Core customer value | the basic benefit, the problem solved | Trek: green transportation or speed? |
| Actual product | brand, features, design, quality, packaging | the Trek Madone: carbon frame, advanced shifters |
| Associated services (the augmented product) | warranty, financing, support, after-sale service | frame guaranteed for life, sold only in stores with service |

You work from the inside out. The importance of the layers varies: for M&M's, the associated services come down to a customer complaint line; for a bicycle that costs thousands of dollars, they are decisive.

The authors also classify **consumer products** by shopping effort, which changes promotion, price and distribution: **specialty** (great effort for the preferred supplier: luxury cars, doctors), **shopping** (furniture, clothes), **convenience** (bread, soap) and **unsought** (insurance, entirely new products), which require a lot of marketing.

**Two variants.** Policarpio (SRC-002) presents the Kotler scheme in five levels: the core benefit, the tangible product, the *expected* product, the augmented product and the potential product. The "expected" level is useful for communication: what is merely expected does not differentiate. Pine and Gilmore read the same rise in value as a progression from commodity to good, service and experience (EXP-001).

## Application

- When you look for a campaign idea, go down to the core layer: what problem does the product actually solve? The bicycle ad can sell freedom, not carbon.
- In mature categories, differentiation often comes from the outer layer (services, warranties).
- The product type dictates the communication task: for convenience products, awareness and presence; for unsought products, education and triggering the need.

## Example

JS Watch Company (Adding Value 11.1), a small Icelandic watchmaker, sells specialty products at luxury prices, but with self-mocking ads. The core value (status, story) is served by a premium actual product and an unexpected tone.

## Questions

- For a digital service (Spotify), where does the actual product end and where do the associated services begin?
- Can a product jump from one category to another (from convenience to specialty) through branding?
