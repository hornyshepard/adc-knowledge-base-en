# Marketing 5.0 applies human-mimicking technologies across the whole customer journey, through two disciplines and three applications

> **The idea in one sentence:** Marketing 5.0 joins the human-centricity of 3.0 with the technology of 4.0 and defines marketing as the application of human-mimicking technologies to create, communicate, deliver and enhance value across the customer journey.

## Explanation

The authors explain why a new book was needed: the use of technologies in Marketing 4.0 was "fairly basic" (social media, omnichannel), and AI, natural language processing (NLP), sensors and the IoT were not yet mainstream. The pandemic accelerated digitalization; the inspiration also comes from Japan's Society 5.0 initiative. The context holds three challenges: the **generation gap**, **prosperity polarization** and the **digital divide**.

Technology helps marketing in five ways: decisions based on big data, predicting outcomes, bringing the contextual digital experience into the physical world, augmenting frontline marketers, and faster execution. Hence the five components (Fig. 1.3):

| Type | Component | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Discipline | **Data-driven marketing** | collecting and analyzing big data, building a data ecosystem |
| Discipline | **Agile marketing** | small, decentralized teams that conceptualize and validate products and campaigns rapidly |
| Application | **Predictive marketing** | predicting results before launch, with predictive analytics and machine learning |
| Application | **Contextual marketing** | personalized real-time interactions in the physical space, with sensors and interfaces |
| Application | **Augmented marketing** | chatbots and virtual assistants raise the productivity of customer-facing marketers |

The book's example ties the three applications together: a predictive model feeds a kiosk with a camera that displays a contextual ad, and a frontline staff member, augmented with the same model, steps in when the self-service option is not satisfactory.

The principles: **technology should follow strategy**, and **humanity remains the central focus**: AI discovers patterns, but only humans can interpret the motives behind actions. The definition keeps the verbs of the AMA definition from the textbooks (MKT-001); what is new is the means, not the goal. The book was written before the wave of generative AI, which only Marketing 6.0 takes up.

## Application

- Start from the map of the customer journey (the five A's) and mark where technology can add value, not from the list of tools.
- Build the data discipline first; without it, the predictive and contextual applications do not work.
- Use the table as a diagnostic grid: which component is the client missing?

## Example

Lexus analyzed award-winning campaigns from the past 15 years, especially in the luxury market, to have AI write the script of a TV ad for the new ES sedan, then hired an Oscar-winning director to shoot it. Chase opted for an AI engine instead of a human copywriter to write the copy for its digital banners.

## Questions

- If strategy comes before technology, why does the book structure everything around technologies?
- Is "technology for humanity" an operational concept or a slogan?
