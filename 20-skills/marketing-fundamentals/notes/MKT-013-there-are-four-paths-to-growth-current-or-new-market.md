# There are four paths to growth: current or new market × current or new product (the Ansoff matrix)

> **The idea in one sentence:** a firm can grow by selling more of what it has to the customers it has, by taking what it has to new customers, by bringing new products to current customers or by entering new markets with new products, and the risk rises in that order.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy present the markets/products matrix (Exhibit 2.7, known as the Ansoff matrix) with the example of Marvel:

| | Current products | New products |
|---|---|---|
| **Current markets** | **Market penetration**: the same customers buy more often or more, through advertising, promotions, distribution. Marvel: more films, on more channels | **Product development**: a new offering for the current target. Marvel: series on Netflix, then on Disney+ |
| **New markets** | **Market development**: the same offering, new segments or countries. Avengers: Endgame made over half its gross outside the U.S. | **Diversification**: a new product in a segment not served today; related (Marvel home décor) or unrelated (a Marvel child day care service, very risky) |

Related diversification shares something with the current business: vendors, distribution, systems or media. Unrelated diversification does not capitalize on any existing strength, which is why it is the riskiest. Penetration is the easiest to implement, but it usually requires the greatest communication effort.

The matrix is one of the "basic" tools that Sachdev (SRC-031) asks for in the overall strategy section of the digital plan, next to Porter's generic strategies, the five forces and the product life cycle.

## Application

- Each cell calls for a different kind of communication: penetration calls for frequency and new consumption occasions, market development for cultural adaptation, product development for launches, and diversification often for a new brand or a carefully justified extension.
- When the client asks "to grow", ask in which cell. Otherwise the brief stays vague.
- Product development under the same name is a line or brand extension, with its risks.

## Example

Nike sells on Amazon, a new channel for the same customers (penetration through distribution). LEGO, with sets for stressed adults (ch. 9), does market development with an almost unchanged product.

## Questions

- Are related diversification and brand extension the same thing? Where is the difference?
- Why would a mature brand choose penetration, even though it seems the least ambitious?
