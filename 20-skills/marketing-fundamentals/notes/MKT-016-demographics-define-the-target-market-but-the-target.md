# Demographics define the target market, but the target audience of communication is segmented only by attitude

> **The idea in one sentence:** "women with families" describes the market for a detergent, not the audience of a campaign; the audience is chosen by loyalty and attitude, while demographics, geography and lifestyle only describe it.

## Explanation

Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott make a distinction that, they say, few people respect: the **target market** versus the **target audience**. Market segmentation can use any variable (behavior, demographics, geography, channel) and defines the marketing strategy. Within the market, the audience is chosen through **attitudinal segmentation**: the importance of benefits, affect toward the brand, loyalty. Only then is the descriptive profile added.

The authors' arguments against descriptive variables as a selection criterion:

- **Demographics** hide individual variation. In a Nestlé study, wealthy shoppers refused higher-priced frozen foods: the difference was a euro or two a month, but the luxury image made them feel they could not indulge themselves.
- **Geodemographics** (ACORN, Mosaic) assumes that people in the same neighborhood buy the same way. The assumption is false, but the systems are popular because they make direct mail by postal code easier.
- **Psychographics** (AIO) and **values** (VALS) help the creative work and the choice of media vehicles. But lifestyle is not stable from one category to another, VALS does not transfer between countries, and many people with the same lifestyle have no interest in the category.
- **Social class** works as a lifestyle variable and also belongs to the market, not to the audience.

**The contrast with the textbooks.** Grewal and Levy (MKT-015) treat psychographics and benefits as bases for selection, to be combined with demographics. Percy accepts them only for description, after you have chosen the buyer group.

## Application

- Build the brief in two layers: "who the target is" (buyer group + attitude) and "what they look like" (demographics, lifestyle, media).
- Use psychographics for tone, images and media vehicles, not to decide whom you talk to.
- When the client comes with a demographic segment, ask: among these people, whom do we want to try the brand, and whom do we want to stay?

## Example

PG Tips Decaf made an ad only for expectant mothers, with a pregnant-looking box of tea. The market is broader (anyone who wants caffeine-free tea), but the campaign chose a subgroup with a precise attitude and need.

## Questions

- Targeting on digital platforms is mostly demographic and behavioral. How do you apply the principle there?
- Are there categories where demographics do in fact define the audience (diapers, products for seniors)?
