# Sustainable marketing, green marketing, eco-efficiency, CSR and cause marketing are not synonyms

> **The idea in one sentence:** the five notions cover different levels: a philosophy for all of marketing, products with a reduced impact, more efficient processes, the firm's overall commitment and a partnership with a cause. Confusing them produces promises bigger than the facts.

## Explanation

| Notion | Definition in the sources | Level |
|---|---|---|
| **Sustainable marketing** | creating, communicating and delivering value to the customer in a way that recognizes and respects environmental and social concerns (Belz & Peattie, 2012). It covers the whole value chain, from raw materials to the disposal of the product. | the philosophy of all marketing |
| **Green marketing** | creating, promoting and distributing products and services with a reduced environmental impact compared to conventional alternatives: sustainable products, ecological production, transparent communication (SRC-011, ch. 3) | offer and communication |
| **Eco-efficiency** | more economic value using fewer resources and generating less waste and emissions: optimizing processes (SRC-011, ch. 3) | operations |
| **CSR** (corporate social responsibility) | the firm's commitment to contributing to the well-being of the community and to sustainable development, beyond legal obligations (Kotler & Lee, 2005). Pillars: transparency, ethics, sustainable innovation, stakeholder engagement. | company |
| **Cause marketing** | a partnership between a company and a non-profit organization or a cause, with mutual benefits: fundraising campaigns, volunteering, products with part of the price donated (SRC-011, ch. 4) | campaign / partnership |

Viturino sees the relationship between sustainable marketing and CSR as intrinsic and mutually reinforcing. Her examples are Interface (recycled carpets, Mission Zero) and Unilever (Sustainable Living Plan) (ch. 1).

An important nuance comes from Ejeke (SRC-010, trend 15): green marketing promotes goods based on their "actual or perceived environmental sustainability". The gap between actual and perceived is exactly where greenwashing appears.

**How it connects to the other sources.** Three authors build similar hierarchies. Grewal and Levy separate CSR, which can remain a PR activity, from conscious marketing, where the purpose sits at the core of the business (MKT-063). Kotler climbs from philanthropy to cause marketing and to socio-cultural transformation (MKT-064). Viturino gives the clearest vocabulary, but without a hierarchy, and defines CSR more broadly than Grewal and Levy, as the commitment of the whole company.

## Application

- In the brief, say explicitly which of the five you mean. A cause marketing campaign does not entitle you to call the brand "sustainable".
- Keep the promise in proportion: a recyclable package (green marketing) does not make a company responsible (CSR).

## Example

Dove (Unilever) combines two levels: the self-esteem campaigns are CSR / cause, while the certified palm oil and the recyclable packaging belong to green marketing (SRC-011, ch. 1).

## Questions

- Can there be green marketing without CSR? And CSR without green marketing?
- Which notion best describes Patagonia's "Don't Buy This Jacket" campaign?
