# The five A's customer path (aware, appeal, ask, act, advocate) replaces the AIDA funnel in the connected world

> **The idea in one sentence:** in the era of connectivity, the customer path is no longer a personal funnel that ends in repurchase but a social route, sometimes a spiral, that ends in advocacy.

## Explanation

The model's genealogy: **AIDA** (attention, interest, desire, action; E. St. Elmo Lewis), then Derek Rucker's **four A's** (aware, attitude, act, act again), a personal funnel driven by the company's touchpoints. Connectivity calls for three corrections: the initial appeal is confirmed by the community, loyalty is defined as the willingness to advocate (not as repurchase), and customers connect with one another to ask and to advocate. The result is the **five A's** model:

| Stage | What the customer does | Typical touchpoints |
|---|---|---|
| **Aware** | is passively exposed to a long list of brands | hears from others, sees an ad |
| **Appeal** | processes the messages and is attracted to a short list | feels attracted; the "wow" factor counts |
| **Ask** | actively researches with friends and family, in the media, with the brand; here the path becomes **social** | searches online reviews, compares prices, tries products in store |
| **Act** | purchases, but also uses, consumes and calls on post-purchase services | buys, uses, makes a complaint |
| **Advocate** | stays loyal: retention, repurchase, advocacy | recommends, defends the brand, writes reviews |

Stages can be skipped (Tesla is well advocated by non-buyers), the path can be a spiral, and its length varies by category: in consumer goods, aware and appeal occur almost simultaneously; for real estate and cars, ask takes a long time. The brand has to trigger the **right** amount of curiosity: too little means weak appeal, too much means a confusing message.

**The surrounding debate.** Fennis and Stroebe show that even the AIDA sequence has no empirical support (ADV-005), so the five A's should also be read as a working map, not a law. The digital sources reach the same conclusion by another road: the real journey has loops and closes in a loyalty loop (DIG-016).

## Application

- Map the typical path in the industry and the path of your own customers. A deviation reveals either an authentic differentiation or a hidden customer experience problem.
- Do not multiply touchpoints. Focus on the few critical ones: one moment of unexpected delight can make a customer an advocate.
- Use the five A's as a brief structure: what does the idea have to do at each stage?

## Example

First-time buyers in a category go through all five A's and rely a lot on outer influence, so they often end up buying the brand with the highest share of voice. Experienced customers skip stages and stay with their favorite brand until it disappoints them.

## Questions

- Is the five A's a description or a norm? What data would confirm the shape of the path in a category?
- What role is left for advertising if the decisive stage (ask) is controlled by others?
