# The product life cycle has four stages, but used mechanically it becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy

> **The idea in one sentence:** product categories go through introduction, growth, maturity and decline, and each stage calls for a different mix; but nobody knows for sure which stage they are in, and a wrong diagnosis can kill a healthy product.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy describe the product life cycle (PLC) through the category's sales and profits over time (Exhibits 12.5, 12.6):

| | Introduction | Growth | Maturity | Decline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | low | rising | at their peak | falling |
| Profits | negative or low | rising rapidly | from the peak toward decline | falling |
| Typical consumers | innovators | early adopters, early majority | late majority | laggards |
| Competitors | one or few | more and more | many | fewer and fewer |

In growth, the market is segmented, and firms without a solid base drop out in an industry shakeout. In maturity, competition shifts to price; firms enter new markets or launch variants. In decline, the firm stays in a niche of loyal customers or exits. The curve is not always a bell: the Xbox Kinect reached decline after two years, while household appliances stay in maturity for decades.

**The trap.** If a manager sees several seasons of declining sales, declares the product "in decline" and stops promoting it, sales fall further, and the manager believes he or she was right. The decline might have come from a poor strategy or from a new competitor.

**How the other sources add to this.** Policarpio (SRC-002) lists the levers for extending the cycle (revitalization, line extensions, repositioning, segmentation) and points out the debate: some ask for investment in new products, not in extending old ones. Marketing 5.0 (SRC-005) starts from the observation that cycles are getting so short that stage-by-stage planning gives way to rapid experimentation (MKT-039).

## Application

- The stage changes the advertising objective: informing in introduction, persuading in growth and maturity, reminding in late maturity.
- Before you accept the diagnosis "brand in decline", ask for the data: is it the category that is declining or only the brand?
- In maturity, ideas of "new consumption occasions" and "new segments" are classic ways of rejuvenation.

## Example

Hallmark, a mature leader in greeting cards, is trying customizable greeting cards, interactive storybooks and a 3D sign featuring the card character Maxine, to defend its share.

## Questions

- Does the life cycle describe categories, brands or models? What changes in each case?
- Does the comeback of vinyl contradict the model, or does it only show a profitable niche in decline?
