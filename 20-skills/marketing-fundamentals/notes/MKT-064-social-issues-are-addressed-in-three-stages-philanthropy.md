# Social issues are addressed in three stages: philanthropy, cause marketing, socio-cultural transformation

> **The idea in one sentence:** donations and cause campaigns have a limited effect if people stay the same; the highest stage is transforming their behavior, built into the business model.

## Explanation

Chapter 7 proposes a ladder with three stages (Fig. 7.1):

1. **Philanthropy**: the company donates money; it is vertical, company-empowered and addresses basic needs. The authors warn that philanthropy does not stimulate transformation in society, but transformation in society drives philanthropy, so the impact is short-term.
2. **Cause marketing**: the company directs its energy, not just its money, to the cause and links the cause to its products. The first famous case: American Express donated 1 percent of the charges to its card to the fund for repairing the Statue of Liberty, and many Americans charged their purchases to the American Express Card. Other examples: Häagen-Dazs's "Help the Honey Bee", Avon for breast cancer research.
3. **Socio-cultural transformation**: horizontal, with consumers empowered to move up the Maslow pyramid toward self-actualization. The social issue goes into the business model, not into the PR department.

For the third stage, the book gives three steps (Fig. 7.2): **identify socio-cultural challenges** (by relevance to the vision-mission-values, business impact and social impact; common themes: wellness, education, social justice, privacy), **select target constituents** (women, the middle class and the elderly for immediate impact; children and youth for future impact; minorities) and **offer transformational solutions** (employment, innovation, products). For innovation, the authors cite IDEO's Human-Centered Design (*hear, create, deliver*).

In mature markets, two forces oblige companies to support a transformation: the need for future growth and the call for strong differentiation.

**Three authors, the same ladder.** Grewal and Levy separate CSR, which can remain a PR activity, from conscious marketing, with the purpose at the core of the business (MKT-063). Viturino gives a vocabulary without a hierarchy (MKT-068). Kotler himself repeats the ladder in the final chapter, as three stages in the relationship between marketing and values: polarization, balancing, integration (MKT-065). All converge: lasting impact comes from the business model, not from communication.

## Application

- Place the client's brand on the ladder: does it give money, run campaigns or change behaviors?
- For a purpose campaign, state the behavior change you want, not only the awareness message.
- Look for partners, even competitors: transformation is not done alone.

## Example

Disney Consumer Products created the "better for you" nutrition guidelines for its food franchisees and Disney-branded private label products with Kroger, in response to child obesity (pp. 140–141). Wegmans set itself apart from Wal-Mart with the "eat well, live well" principle. Together with Whole Foods, it even pushed Wal-Mart to address health.

## Questions

- How do you show a client that transformation brings more than a cause marketing campaign, which is easier to measure?
- When does a company become the consumers' "educator" in a paternalistic way?
