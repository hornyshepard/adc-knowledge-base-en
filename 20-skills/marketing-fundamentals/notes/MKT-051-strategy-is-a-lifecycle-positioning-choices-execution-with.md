# Strategy is a lifecycle (positioning, choices, execution) with the 40-20-40 time rule

> **The idea in one sentence:** strategy is not a document but a lifecycle that has to be managed: of the total time, about 40% goes to understanding the situation, 20% to the choices and 40% to execution.

## Explanation

Von Scheel treats strategy as an end-to-end lifecycle with five blocks (p. 59):

1. **Strategic positioning**: understanding the market and the brand, the purpose (mission, vision), the forecast, the culture, the capabilities.
2. **Strategic choices**: what not to do, where to differentiate, what to focus on.
3. **Execution**: reporting and measurement, the project portfolio, change management.
4. **Governance**: oversight and audit.
5. **Continuous improvement**.

The author's "golden rule", presented as a visual interpretation of Sun Tzu ("Know thyself, know thy enemy."), divides the time (p. 60):

| Phase | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning | 40% | forces and drivers, the outside-in and inside-out view, scenarios |
| Choices | 20% | scenario thinking and prototyping |
| Execution | 40% | diligent execution of the plan |

Strategy is both art (intuition, the ability to visualize the future) and science (analysis, data). The lifecycle starts again periodically, because every strategy becomes outdated at some point, and many teams do not notice when that happens (p. 228).

**Comparison and caveat.** The lifecycle resembles the textbook marketing plan (planning, implementation, control, MKT-012); what is new is the share of time. The 40-20-40 rule and the argument about the lifetime of S&P 500 companies (from 93 to 15 years, p. 43) are given without a method, so use them as a planning heuristic, not as evidence.

## Application

- Plan a strategy project (for example a six-week pitch) on 40-20-40, not "one week of research, five of executions".
- Put review points in the calendar: what has changed in the market forces since we made the choice?
- For execution, the book proposes six delivery phases: direction, responsibilities and indicators, realistic budgets, follow-up, performance dialogues, consequences (pp. 230–231).

## Example

Agency teams often spend most of their time producing materials, that is, in execution. The rule asks that understanding the situation (insight, category, culture) get as much time as production, and that the decision be a separate, visible step.

## Questions

- What is lost when the choices get only 20% of the time? Can this phase be short precisely because it is hard?
- How would you check the 93 → 15 years figure?
