# Marketing research has five steps, from objectives to an action plan, and turns data into information

> **The idea in one sentence:** marketing research systematically collects, records, analyzes and interprets data to reduce the uncertainty of decisions, and its value comes from the step in which data become information and then action.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy give research three roles: it reduces uncertainty, it links the firm to its environment (continual feedback from customers) and it monitors competitors. Before a project, managers ask three questions: will the research provide insights beyond what they already know? Is top management willing to abide by the results? Should the project be small or large? Expensive research that nobody uses is wasted money.

The five-step process (Exhibit 10.1), on the hypothetical example of McDonald's vs. Wendy's:

| Step | What you do | In the example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Defining the objectives and research needs | what problem you are solving; you compare the benefit of the answer with its cost | the customer experience; the price of the new menu |
| 2. Designing the research | what data you need and how you get them | people do not tell everything in surveys, so you also observe behavior |
| 3. Collecting the data | secondary or primary data; the sample | syndicated data plus a questionnaire |
| 4. Analyzing the data and developing insights | the raw data become information | tied on cleanliness, McDonald's cheaper, Wendy's tastier |
| 5. Developing and implementing an action plan | a report: summary, objectives, method, results, limitations | improve the taste without raising the price too much |

The key distinction: **data** are raw numbers or facts; **information** comes from organizing, analyzing and interpreting them into a form useful for a decision. The steps are not strictly linear: a discovery during collection can redefine the objective.

**Tension.** The textbook treats research as reducing uncertainty. Von Scheel (SRC-007) observes that market research is used mainly to reinforce existing conclusions, which is why he places it in the "Want" circle (what management wants), not in "Should" (MKT-053, MKT-052).

## Application

- When you receive "the client's research", look for step 4: what insight follows from it? A table of percentages is not yet an insight.
- Write the research objective as a decision to be made ("we choose between two positionings"), not as a curiosity.
- Check whether the study tested the hypothesis or only confirmed it.

## Example

Netflix (ch. 10) uses viewing data to recommend content and to decide on productions, but it faces the "creepy" feeling of customers who know how much is known about them. That is why it promises control over the profile and transparency.

## Questions

- When is it wiser to decide by intuition than to do research?
- Who should write step 5: the researcher or the agency strategist?
