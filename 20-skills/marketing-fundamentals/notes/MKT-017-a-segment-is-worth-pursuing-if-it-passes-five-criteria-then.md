# A segment is worth pursuing if it passes five criteria; then you choose one of four targeting strategies

> **The idea in one sentence:** before choosing the target, you check that the segment is identifiable, substantial, reachable, responsive and profitable; then you decide whether you serve everyone the same way, several segments differently, a single segment or each individual.

## Explanation

**Step 3 of STP: attractiveness** (Grewal and Levy, Exhibit 9.5):

| Criterion | The question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Identifiable | Do we know who is in the segment, and is it distinct from the others? | segments that overlap too much do not call for different strategies |
| Substantial | Is it large enough, and does it have buying power? | carmakers waited for China's middle class to grow |
| Reachable | Can we reach it through communication and distribution? | affordable offers that are not advertised do not bring customers into the store |
| Responsive | Does it respond to our offering, and can we deliver it? | Cadillac in the luxury segment, dominated by BMW and Lexus |
| Profitable | Does it bring profit now and over the customer's lifetime? | growth, competition, access to channels |

The authors also give a formula: segment profitability = (segment size × segment adoption percentage × purchase behavior × profit margin percentage) − fixed costs. In the example of a lawn service, 1,000 businesses bring more profit than 75,000 homeowners, because adoption, price and frequency are higher.

Policarpio (SRC-002) adds a useful criterion, **stability**: avoid segments built on passing fads. His definition of micromarketing (an age group), however, describes concentrated targeting; use the Grewal-Levy version.

**Step 4: the targeting strategy** (Exhibit 9.7):

- **Undifferentiated (mass marketing)**: the same offering for everyone; rare today, even salt has been differentiated.
- **Differentiated**: different offerings for several segments (Condé Nast, 26 magazines); lower risk, higher cost.
- **Concentrated**: a single segment (Newton Running, shoes for runners who land on the forefoot).
- **Micromarketing (one-to-one)**: an offering tailored to each individual. Marketing 5.0 calls it the "segment of one", made possible by big data (DIG-061).

## Application

- Use the five criteria as a checklist when the client proposes a "fashionable" target. "Reachable" and "responsive" are the ones checked least often.
- The targeting strategy dictates the architecture of the campaign: one for everyone, executions by segment or personalized messages.
- A start-up with a small budget almost always wins with concentrated targeting.

## Example

Patagonia (Adding Value 9.2) protects its concentrated target, people who believe in environmental causes: it refuses bulk orders of logo vests from firms that do not "prioritize the planet", even though the vests were in fashion on Wall Street.

## Questions

- Does micromarketing based on personal data cross the ethical line? Where is the line?
- Can a brand with a concentrated target also be loved by the non-target?
