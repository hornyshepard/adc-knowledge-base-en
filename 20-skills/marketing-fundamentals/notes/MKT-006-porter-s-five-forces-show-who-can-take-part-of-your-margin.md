# Porter's five forces show who can take part of your margin: rivals, new entrants, suppliers, buyers and substitutes

> **The idea in one sentence:** beyond the macroenvironment (CDSTEP, PESTEL), a market is judged by five competitive pressures: the competition in the industry, the threat of new entrants, the power of suppliers, the power of buyers and the threat of substitutes.

## Explanation

The library does not hold Michael Porter's book (*Competitive Strategy*, cited only in the bibliography of SRC-007), but three sources use the model. Von Scheel (SRC-007) places it among the choices in the "value chain 4.0" (p. 78), and his list of threats for the outside-in view contains the same forces: new entrants, competitors, suppliers who might cut you off, substitute products (p. 64). Sachdev (SRC-031) asks for it explicitly in the overall strategy section of the digital plan, next to the generic strategies and the Ansoff matrix.

| Force | The working question |
|---|---|
| Competition in the industry | How many competitors fight on the same criteria, and how aggressively? |
| New entrants | What barriers keep them out: brand, distribution, relationships, costs? |
| Power of suppliers | Do we depend on a few suppliers who can impose the price or cut us off? |
| Power of buyers | Can customers or retailers impose the price and terms on us? |
| Substitutes | What else meets the same need, from another category? |

The logic of the model (this note's synthesis): each force bargains over how much of the value created stays with the firm. That is why the forces feed the O and T boxes of the SWOT, and the generic strategies (cost, differentiation, focus) are the answer to them.

## Application

- When the client complains about "price pressure", ask where it comes from: rivals, powerful retailers or a new substitute? Each calls for a different communication answer.
- The brand is a barrier and a defense: awareness and preference reduce the power of buyers and the pull of substitutes.
- Define substitutes the way the consumer sees them, not the way the industry does.

## Example

Policarpio (SRC-002) applies the forces to the rural market in India: consumers become more powerful through literacy and television, entry is blocked through good relationships with small retailers, and the main substitute is counterfeit goods. The recommended answer is educating consumers through packaging and brand recognition. The application is schematic, but it shows how a force turns into a communication task.

## Questions

- Does the model apply to a platform (MKT-045), where the suppliers and the buyers are the users themselves?
- Who are the "powerful suppliers" of an advertising agency: the talent, the media platforms?
