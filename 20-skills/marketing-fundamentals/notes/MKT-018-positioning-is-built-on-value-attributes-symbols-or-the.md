# Positioning is built on value, attributes, symbols or the competition, and is checked on the perceptual map

> **The idea in one sentence:** to position means to adjust the mix so that the target has a clear, distinctive and desirable understanding of what the product does relative to competitors, and the perceptual map shows where you are, where the others are and where the unserved demand is.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy define market positioning as the process of defining the marketing mix variables so that target customers have a **clear, distinctive and desirable** understanding of the product relative to the competition. There are four bases for positioning:

| Base | The idea | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Value | a price-quality relationship that suits the target | KIND (natural ingredients) vs. PowerBar (protein for athletes) |
| Salient attributes | the feature most important to the target | all-wheel drive: Audi's Quattro on performance, Subaru on safety |
| Symbols | a sign so strong that it creates the position | Tony the Tiger, the Nike swoosh |
| Competition | relative to a rival or a category | Saddleback Leather: rugged durability, not light weight |

A warning: if you position yourself too close to a competitor (similar packaging or logo), you risk a trademark infringement lawsuit.

The **perceptual map** shows, on two or more dimensions, the position of brands in the consumer's mind. The steps (the Gatorade example, on the sweet–light and less natural–healthy axes): determine the perceptions of yourself and your competitors, identify the market's ideal points and their size, place the competitors, find the unserved segment (a less sweet sports drink), choose the position (a new product or repositioning) and monitor, because tastes and competitors move.

**Where the authors agree and where they differ.** Barre and Gayrard-Carrera give the same map in four steps and add six tests for the chosen position (BRD-014). Trout and Ries shift the emphasis from the mix to the mind: positioning is what you do to the mind of the prospect, not to the product (ADV-021). Kotler (Marketing 4.0) is the most critical: positioning without a community-driven consensus is "corporate posturing", and what must stay consistent is the brand's character (MKT-035).

## Application

- Draw the map before the brief. If you cannot name the two axes that matter to the target, you do not yet know what you differentiate on.
- Choose a single dominant base. Communication that tries value, attribute and symbol at once fixes nothing.
- For repositioning, the map shows the distance perception has to travel; the move is always hard.

## Example

Hertz (ch. 2) positions itself as the first choice for each target segment and promises peace of mind: tourists enjoy the road, business travelers save time.

## Questions

- What happens if the researcher chooses axes that do not matter to the consumer?
- Is positioning "on symbols" a base in its own right or an execution tool?
