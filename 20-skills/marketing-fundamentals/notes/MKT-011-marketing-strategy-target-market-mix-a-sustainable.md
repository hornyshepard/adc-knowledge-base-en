# Marketing strategy = target market + mix + a sustainable competitive advantage, built like a wall

> **The idea in one sentence:** a marketing strategy says whom you serve, with what mix and on what basis you will stay hard to copy; the advantage is defended like a high wall, built from several sources at the same time.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy define marketing strategy through three elements: (1) the target market or markets, (2) a related marketing mix and (3) the bases of a **sustainable competitive advantage**, that is, an advantage that is not easily copied and can be maintained over a long period. The authors' metaphor: the advantage is a wall around the firm's position. Competitors will try to break it down; the higher and thicker it is, the longer the profit lasts.

Four macro strategies build the wall (Exhibit 2.1):

| Macro strategy | How it is achieved | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Customer excellence | loyal customers, excellent customer service | Disney: the MagicBand and staff trained to recognize the lost visitor |
| Operational excellence | efficient operations, supply chain, human resource management | Amazon Prime: one-day delivery |
| Product excellence | high perceived value, effective branding and positioning | PepsiCo with bubly |
| Locational excellence | a good physical location and Internet presence | the density of Starbucks stores |

The central lesson: a single source (only low prices, only service) is rarely enough. Southwest Airlines combines on-time flights, reasonable prices, no charge for checked luggage and clear expectations (no food service, no seat assignments).

**Where they agree.** Von Scheel (SRC-007) reaches the same rule by way of Porter: an advantage built from a single source is easy to cancel out, and higher-order advantages (brand reputation, customer relationships, proprietary technology) last longer than lower-order ones such as cheap labor (MKT-050). The difference: Grewal and Levy name excellences, while von Scheel asks you to choose a *different* set of activities, not a better one.

## Application

- When you write a communication strategy, ask which of the four excellences the brand rests on. Advertising can amplify product excellence, but it cannot invent it.
- Excellent service is hard to copy precisely because it depends on people and culture, which take years to build.
- In a pitch, show how the creative idea strengthens the existing wall, not only how it draws attention.

## Example

PepsiCo (ch. 2): the wall is made of strong brands, loyal customers and the capacity to innovate (bubly took 10% of the flavored sparkling water market in about a year). Its varied portfolio also won it the contract with JetBlue, which Coca-Cola lost.

## Questions

- In digital markets, where copying is fast, are there still "sustainable" advantages? Which of the four holds up best?
- Is brand equity a fifth source of advantage, or is it part of product excellence?
