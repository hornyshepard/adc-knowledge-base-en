# The 80-15-5 rule: most activities only need to meet the standard; very few need to be unique

> **The idea in one sentence:** about 80% of what an organization does is "non-core" and only needs to follow best practices; 15% is the zone where you compete, and 5% is the real differentiation, built over years, where you innovate.

## Explanation

Von Scheel links two curves. The first is the adoption curve of a trend (p. 212):
- **Early Adopters (5%)** pilot the first initiatives and take the risk; they develop the *leading practices*.
- **Industry Adopters (15%)** spot an industry-specific application and harvest a comparative advantage; their practices evolve into industry standards.
- **Majority and Late Adopters (80%)** adopt once the benefit has been demonstrated; here the practice becomes a *best practice*.

The second curve is the map of the organization, the "golden rule" (p. 214):

| Share | Type of competency | Practice | Driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80% | non-core | best practice | transformation: digitalization, automation, integration, optimization; do less or stop |
| 15% | core competitive | industry practice | innovation, then transformation |
| 5% | core differentiating | leading practice | innovation; takes years to develop |

The consequence: a "best practice" brings only parity, not advantage. Knowing where to apply each type of practice is, for the author, the core of the question "what to focus on".

**Assessment.** The first curve is a simplified version of Rogers's diffusion curve (2.5 / 13.5 / 34 / 34 / 16%), which the book does not cite (MKT-023); the percentages and the growth thresholds are stated without data. The useful part is the second curve: the map of competencies in the value chain (MKT-007).

## Application

- For a brand, ask: which 5% of what it does cannot be copied (a ritual, a tone, a service)? That is where distinctive creative investment pays off; the rest only has to work flawlessly.
- For an agency: production that can be standardized (80%) gets automated; strategy and the idea (5%) are what you sell at a high price.

## Example

Illustration: a digital bank. Payments and security are the 80%: they must meet the standard, and nobody picks the bank for them. Savings features are the 15%: that is where it competes. The tone and the community around financial education are the 5%: that is what makes it recognizable.

## Questions

- Would you use the percentages as a rule or as a metaphor?
- What happens when "the 5%" becomes standard because everyone copies it?
