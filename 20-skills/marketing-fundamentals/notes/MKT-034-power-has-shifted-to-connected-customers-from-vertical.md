# Power has shifted to connected customers: from vertical, exclusive and individual to horizontal, inclusive and social

> **The idea in one sentence:** the internet has moved power from companies, big brands and individuals to communities, so a chance conversation about a brand is now more credible than a targeted campaign.

## Explanation

Marketing 4.0 opens with three power shifts:

1. **From exclusive to inclusive.** Economic power is dispersing (from the G7 to the G20, toward Asia); disruptive innovations bring cheaper products to the poor, formerly considered a "non-market" (Aravind Eye Care System's $16 cataract surgery); reverse innovation appears, designed for emerging markets and then taken to the West.
2. **From vertical to horizontal.** Innovation comes from the market, and companies commercialize it (P&G's connect-and-develop model). Competitors come from neighboring industries (Uber, Airbnb), so they are spotted by starting from the customer's goals. **Trust** also becomes horizontal: customers believe more in the **f-factor** (friends, families, fans and followers) than in marketing communications or in experts.
3. **From individual to social.** Most "personal" purchase decisions become social decisions. Customers are no longer passive targets but **active media** of communications: Sephora turned its community's content (the Beauty Talk platform) into a trusted medium.

The consequence: the relationship with the brand has to be peer to peer. Helped by their community, customers defend themselves against brands with false differentiation, and censoring the conversation lowers credibility. The idea continues the diagnosis in Marketing 3.0 (trust moving toward other consumers after the 2008 crisis), but ties it explicitly to the smartphone and to communal ratings.

**Who disagrees.** Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott argue that the new vocabulary (platforms, communities) does not change how advertising works (ADV-084). Marketing 4.0 itself (ch. 6) keeps a role for brand communication: it steers the conversation, even if it no longer controls it.

## Application

- In a communication plan, assume the paid message will be checked in the network: what will the f-factors say when the customer asks?
- Look for competitors starting from the customer's goal ("get home"), not from the product category ("taxi").
- Treat the community as a medium: what content do members produce, and how can you host it without controlling it?

## Example

Sony released its comedy *The Interview* first via YouTube, in response to an alleged North Korean cyberattack. A survey commissioned by Variety showed that for 13- to 18-year-olds, YouTube celebrities were more popular than Hollywood stars.

## Questions

- Is trust in strangers on the internet a constant or a phase? What happened to it after the wave of fake reviews and bots?
- If everyone believes the f-factor, does advertising still have a role?
