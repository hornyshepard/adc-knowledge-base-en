# The 10 credos of Marketing 3.0 integrate values into everyday marketing practice

> **The idea in one sentence:** the relationship between marketing and values goes through three stages (polarization, balancing, integration), and the ten credos describe what a company in the integration stage actually does.

## Explanation

In the final chapter, the authors distinguish three stages: **polarization** (values are seen as extra costs and constraints), **balancing** (ordinary marketing plus donating some of the profits) and **integration** (values give the company its personality and purpose, with no separation from marketing). For the last stage they propose ten credos, often illustrated with contributions to the United Nations Millennium Development Goals:

| # | Credo | The book's example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Love your customers, respect your competitors | Campbell's pink cans during Breast Cancer Awareness Month; seven pharmaceutical companies that together brought down the price of HIV/AIDS treatment |
| 2 | Be sensitive to change, be ready to transform | Toyota Prius; Wal-Mart transformed into a "green giant" |
| 3 | Guard your name, be clear about who you are | The Body Shop, bought by L'Oréal at a premium of 34.2 percent |
| 4 | Customers are diverse; go first to those who can benefit most from you | four market tiers: global, "glocal", local, bottom of the pyramid |
| 5 | Always offer a good package at a fair price | P&G's sachets that clean 10 liters of water |
| 6 | Always make yourself available, spread the good news | HP bridging the digital divide |
| 7 | Get your customers, keep and grow them | PetSmart Charities' in-store adoption centers (CRM) |
| 8 | Whatever your business, it is a service business | Whole Foods: service to consumers, society and employees |
| 9 | Always refine your business process in terms of quality, cost and delivery (QCD) | S.C. Johnson and the farmers in Kenya |
| 10 | Gather relevant information, but use wisdom in making your final decision | Hershey's sale to Wrigley, which the board recognized as wrong after the employees protested |

Credos 4, 7 and 9 are classic principles (segmentation, CRM, QCD); what is new is that each one is reinterpreted through its social impact.

## Application

- Use the table as an audit checklist: for each credo, note one real practice of the client and one that is missing.
- Credo 10 is useful in a pitch: a financially sound decision can be unwise if it ignores the impact on employees and the community.

## Example

In 2001, the board of Hershey Trust sold its stake in Hershey Foods to Wrigley to protect the value of the trust fund. The employees protested on Chocolatetown Square, and the board recognized that the decision was financially sound but not wise (pp. 195–196).

## Questions

- Which credos are specific to Marketing 3.0, and which are old principles repackaged?
- Credo 1 praises cooperation with competitors. Where does cooperation end and a cartel begin?
