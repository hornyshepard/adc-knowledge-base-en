# The Kotler series translates the classic concepts into horizontal equivalents: cocreation and the 4Cs instead of the 4Ps, communities instead of segments, character instead of positioning

> **The idea in one sentence:** when trust moves from companies to other consumers, each classic tool changes its central concept; Marketing 3.0 (2010) calls for replacement, and Marketing 4.0 (2017) turns it into digital equivalents that coexist with the old ones.

## Explanation

The retrospective in Marketing 3.0: the 1950s and 1960s were about product management (the 4Ps), the 1970s and 1980s about customer management (STP), the 1990s and 2000s about brand management. After the 2008 crisis, trust migrated to other consumers: according to the Nielsen Global Survey, around 90 percent of consumers trust recommendations from people they know and 70 percent believe customer opinions posted online.

| Classic concept | Marketing 3.0 (Table 2.1) | Marketing 4.0 (ch. 4) |
|---|---|---|
| The 4Ps | **cocreation** | **the 4Cs**: co-creation, currency (dynamic pricing), communal activation (peer-to-peer distribution), conversation |
| STP | **communitization** | **customer community confirmation**: the brand asks for permission (Seth Godin) and is "confirmed" or ignored |
| Brand building, positioning | **character building**: an authentic DNA | **brand clarification of characters and codes**: the core stays fixed, the outer imagery varies (Google doodles, the MTV logo) |
| Customer service | none | **collaborative customer care**, including self-service |

In 3.0, **cocreation** (Prahalad) has three processes: create a "platform" that can be customized, let individual consumers customize it, then enrich it by incorporating what they made. **Communities** (Fournier and Lee) are pools (shared values, no interaction), webs (one-to-one relationships) or hubs (a loyal fan base around a strong figure), and they exist to serve the members, not the business. 4.0 adds three blends (online with offline, style with substance, machine-to-machine with human-to-human) and a media rule: traditional marketing initiates the relationship, digital marketing drives it to action and advocacy (Fig. 4.1). The attributes of character are detailed in BRD-027.

**The revision.** 3.0 calls for replacing STP; 4.0 says STP remains valid if it is made transparent; 5.0 moves to the "segment of one" (MKT-048). The textbooks (Grewal and Levy) keep STP and the 4Ps unchanged.

## Application

- In the brief, replace "the target" with "the community" where one exists: is it a pool, a web or a hub? What do the members get, not the brand?
- For each of the client's Ps, ask what the "C" version is.
- In a rebranding, separate the fixed codes (the character) from the expressions that can vary.
- In the media plan, do not judge the opening (traditional) and the close (digital) by the same KPI.

## Example

P&G's connect and develop program, which replaced part of its internal research, produced Olay Regenerist and Swiffer Dusters. "Free Doritos", a user-generated ad, won the top spot in the USA Today Super Bowl Ad Meter, ahead of ads made by professional agencies.

## Questions

- What is left of the creative agency's job if the public also cocreates the ads?
- Dynamic pricing is efficient but can feel unfair. Where is the ethical limit of price as "currency"?
