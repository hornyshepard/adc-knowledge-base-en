# The marketing environment has two circles: the immediate environment and the CDSTEP macroenvironment (the Grewal-Levy version of PESTEL)

> **The idea in one sentence:** consumers sit at the center, surrounded by the factors the firm can influence (itself, its competitors, its partners, the physical environment) and by the large forces it can only anticipate (culture, demographics, social trends, technology, the economy, politics and law).

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy's model (Exhibit 5.1) has three layers:

1. **Consumers**, at the center: value-based marketing looks at everything from the customer's point of view.
2. **The immediate environment**: company capabilities (Corning moved its competence in glass from Edison's lightbulbs to phone screens), competitors, corporate partners and the **physical environment** (water, air, resources; the UN sustainable development goals).
3. **The CDSTEP macroenvironment**:

| Factor | What you track | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| Culture | values, customs, regional cultures | McDonald's: the lobster roll in New England |
| Demographics | age and cohorts, income, education, gender, ethnicity | Gen Alpha, Gen Z, Millennials, Gen X, Baby Boomers |
| Social trends | sustainability, health, privacy | Fairtrade, food waste |
| Technological advances | devices, data, AI | Coca-Cola's Freestyle machines, which choose flavors from data |
| Economic situation | inflation, interest rates, currency exchange rates | American tourists in London when the dollar is strong |
| Political/legal | competition, consumer protection | U.S. antitrust law |

CDSTEP covers the same forces as PESTEL, but it separates culture from demographics, which helps communication. PESTEL's ecological factor appears here in the immediate environment, as the "physical environment".

**The Kotler version** (Policarpio, SRC-002) divides things differently: the controllable internal environment (the 5 Ms: money, men, markets, materials, machinery), the external microenvironment (suppliers, intermediaries, advertising agencies, customers, competitors and the **public**) and the macroenvironment. "The public" is a useful category for communication: people outside the target audience who can build or destroy the firm's image online. Neither scheme systematically analyzes the competitive pressure within the industry; that is what Porter's five forces are for (MKT-006).

## Application

- Look for the campaign insight where a CDSTEP trend meets a personal tension of the target (sustainability × the wish to express yourself).
- Use generational cohorts to choose media, but together with other characteristics, not on their own.
- Put each CDSTEP factor in the O or T box of the SWOT.

## Example

iRobot (ch. 5) tracks demographics (an aging population that wants to stay at home), technology (the smart home) and customer frustrations (the vacuum stranded in the middle of the room with a dead battery), then adds features: returning to the docking station, an app, emptying its own bin.

## Questions

- Where do culture and social trends overlap? Is it useful to separate them?
- Does the American legislation in the textbook transfer to a brief in the EU?
