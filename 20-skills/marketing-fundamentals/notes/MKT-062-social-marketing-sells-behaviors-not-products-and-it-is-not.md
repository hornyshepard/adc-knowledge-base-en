# Social marketing sells behaviors, not products, and it is not the same thing as social media marketing

> **The idea in one sentence:** social marketing applies the tools of marketing (planning, pricing, communication, distribution, research) to change behaviors for the good of society, and success is measured in changed behavior, not in sales.

## Explanation

Policarpio (SRC-002) cites Kotler and Zaltman's definition: social marketing is the design, implementation and monitoring of programs designed to influence the acceptability of social ideas, using planning, pricing, communication, distribution and marketing research. Its primary goal is "social good", whereas commercial marketing aims to make money. The two can meet: AMC Theatres and the American Red Cross hosted a series of blood drives together. The typical fields: health, anti-tobacco and anti-drug campaigns, road safety, pollution, plastic bags.

Grewal and Levy (SRC-001) give the same logic in terms of exchange: children who wear their helmets while bicycling have become "purchasers" of the safety idea promoted by a school campaign. The authors classify public service advertising (PSA) as a form of social marketing and note that PSAs are often designed by top advertising agencies, are usually quite creative and sometimes cross the line into controversy.

**Read critically.** In its "advantages" section, SRC-002 confuses social marketing with social media marketing: "Social marketing is one of the cheapest ways of marketing", it says, and describes it through blogs and Facebook. These are different concepts: social marketing is defined by its goal (behavior change), not by its channel.

**Convergence.** Kotler and his coauthors (Marketing 3.0) reach a related idea for commercial firms: the highest level of social engagement is not donation but changing people's behavior (MKT-064).

## Application

- In a brief for a cause, define the target behavior exactly (what to do, who, when) and its "price": what the person gives up (time, convenience, status).
- Think in 4Ps: the product is the behavior, the price is the effort, the place is the access (where the action can be done), the promotion is the campaign.
- Measure the behavior, not only the campaign's awareness.

## Example

The Women's Aid campaign (SRC-001, ch. 19): the billboard with a battered woman's face healed as more passersby stopped to look. The behavior asked for (do not look away) was the very mechanism of the ad.

## Questions

- When does social marketing become state manipulation? Where is the line between a beneficial nudge and propaganda?
- Why do public-interest campaigns win so many awards at creativity festivals?
