# Pipe or platform: value is produced along a chain or co-created in a network with network effects

> **The idea in one sentence:** all business models fall into two overarching types: the **pipe** (value is produced upstream and consumed downstream, as along a chain) and the **platform** (value comes from the interaction between external producers and consumers and grows with each participant).

## Explanation

A business model describes how a firm creates value, delivers it and captures it (Chesbrough and Rosenbloom: the value proposition, the market segment, the value chain, costs and profit potential, the position in the value network, the competitive strategy). It is neither a business plan nor a strategy. The authors insist: if you do not have a product people want at a price they will pay, no model will save you.

| Pipes | Platforms |
|---|---|
| value is created upstream | value is co-created on the spot |
| customers are acquired | users can become customers |
| products are designed to meet specifications | products emerge through interaction |
| value is given by consumption | value is appreciated by interaction |

The pipe is the classic channel of distribution: the major manufacturers of consumer goods, but also Zappos (which owes its success to customer service, not to unique merchandise) or Netflix. A platform can be **two-sided** (credit cards: users and merchants) or **multisided** (Android: smartphone makers, software developers, users). Its value grows with the number of connections, and its role is to provide the rules that unleash network effects.

The idea connects with **service-dominant logic** (Vargo and Lusch): goods are, at bottom, services, and value is co-created with the beneficiary (MKT-046). Wuebben predicted in 2016 the same move, "from brands to platforms", as the first of the ten changes in marketing (MKT-060).

## Application

- Before you communicate a digital brand, find out whether it is a pipe or a platform: a platform has two audiences (buyers and sellers on Etsy) and needs messages for both.
- For platforms, communication has to solve the chicken-and-egg problem: who comes first?
- Most firms are hybrids (Amazon is both pipe and platform); do not force a single label.

## Example

**Etsy** sells nothing of its own: it connects buyers of handmade goods with the makers and takes a portion of each sale. By contrast, **Dollar Shave Club** is a disruptive pipe on a subscription model, bought by Unilever for about one billion dollars.

## Questions

- Is an advertising agency a pipe or a platform? And a network of creators?
- What happens to a platform's brand when the users, not the firm, produce the experience?
