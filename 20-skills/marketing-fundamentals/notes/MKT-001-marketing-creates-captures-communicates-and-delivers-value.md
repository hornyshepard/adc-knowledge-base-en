# Marketing creates, captures, communicates and delivers value (the AMA definition)

> **The idea in one sentence:** marketing is neither advertising nor selling; it is the set of activities through which an organization creates, captures, communicates, delivers and exchanges offerings that have value for customers, partners and society.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy start from the definition of the American Marketing Association (AMA): marketing is the activity, set of institutions and processes for creating, communicating, delivering and exchanging offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners and society at large. The authors add the word *capturing*: the firm must also collect part of the value it creates.

The definition has five core aspects (Exhibit 1.1):

| Aspect | What it means |
|---|---|
| Marketing is about satisfying customer needs and wants | the marketplace is divided into groups with different needs |
| Marketing entails an exchange | both parties are better off; the buyer gives money, but also personal information |
| Marketing creates value through the four Ps | product, price, place, promotion |
| Marketing can be performed by individuals and organizations | B2C, B2B, C2C, and also personal marketing (the résumé, the interview) |
| Marketing affects various stakeholders | partners, communities, society |

**Where the authors agree and where they differ.** Policarpio (SRC-002) gives the Kotler-Armstrong formula: companies create value for customers and capture value from customers in return, in five steps (understand the marketplace, choose whom to serve and with what proposition, build the mix, build relationships, capture value). Earlier in the same chapter, however, the book also gives a reductive definition, "the process of promoting a product, service, business, or brand", which the rest of the chapter contradicts. Kotler and his co-authors keep the AMA verbs in Marketing 5.0 as well, where marketing becomes the application of human-mimicking technologies to create, communicate, deliver and enhance value (SRC-005). The dissenting voice in the library is Wuebben (SRC-008): the classic definitions are company-centered, and marketing should be defined as an authentic connection with the people who are interested in what you do.

## Application

- When a client says "we want marketing", ask which verb is missing: creating (product), capturing (price), delivering (distribution) or communicating (promotion). Advertising solves only one of them.
- Test the claim "it was a great ad, so it was great marketing": the ad is only communication.
- Also think of the stakeholders who do not buy: the community, the partners, society.

## Example

Hydro Flask (SRC-001, ch. 1): a better product (colorful insulated bottles), samples filled with ice shipped to retailers with a note asking them to contact the company when the ice melted, then spontaneous adoption by Generation Z on TikTok. All four verbs work together.

## Questions

- Is a campaign for wearing a bicycle helmet marketing? What is being "exchanged" there?
- Is Wuebben's criticism justified, or does the AMA definition already include the relationship, through "exchange" and "stakeholders"?
