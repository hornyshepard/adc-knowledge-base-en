# The marketing team is made of three types of talent (think, do, feel), coordinated by an orchestrator

> **The idea in one sentence:** marketing roles are more usefully classified by the type of work than by title: those who think analytically (*think*), those who produce (*do*) and those who relate (*feel*). Their leader is an orchestrator who assembles temporary teams.

## Explanation

Wuebben takes up the findings of the Marketing2020 study (EffectiveBrands, now Millward Brown Vermeer, with the ANA, the WFA and others: more than 10,000 marketers from ninety-two countries) (ch. 15):

| Type | What they do | Example roles |
|---|---|---|
| **Think** | analytics: data mining, media-mix modeling, ROI optimization | analysts, data planners |
| **Do** | content, design, production | copywriters, designers, producers |
| **Feel** | consumer interaction and engagement | customer service, social media, online communities |

The CMO and the other leaders (chief experience officers, global brand managers) become **orchestrators**. They assemble cross-functional teams for an initiative, brief them, make sure they have the resources they need and oversee performance tracking, balancing the mix of think, do and feel according to the mission. The teams form, execute and disband within weeks. Wuebben adds the "leadership cells" model (from the U.S. Navy SEALs), in which a task leader and a social leader emerge. He also proposes a customer-engagement council, a *chief content officer*, a CExO and a "Listening Center" for online conversations.

As an individual tool, the book offers Gavin Heaton's skills audit, on five dimensions rated as low, medium or high (ch. 10): marketing foundations (from a SWOT analysis to copywriting), technology foundations (tracking URLs, HTML, a landing page), content production and publishing, data analytics, and a social mindset (empathy, community management).

**Convergence.** Kotler (Marketing 5.0) describes the same small, interdisciplinary, temporary teams, with management acting as coach, as the core of agile marketing (MKT-039).

## Application

- When you form a project team at school, make sure you have at least one profile of each type. Teams made only of "do" people produce good-looking work with no direction.
- Run the Heaton audit on yourself and write in your portfolio which dimension each project develops.

## Example

For important moments, Wuebben recommends a "mission control" room, where the team is authorized to engage with the audience in real time, without long sign-off chains (ch. 15).

## Questions

- Where does the brand strategist fit: think or feel?
- What does an organization risk when teams disband after every project: institutional memory, culture?
