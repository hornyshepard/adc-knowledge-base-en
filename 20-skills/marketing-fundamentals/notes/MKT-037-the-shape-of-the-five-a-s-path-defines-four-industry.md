# The shape of the five A's path defines four industry archetypes (door knob, goldfish, trumpet and funnel), with the bow tie as the ideal

> **The idea in one sentence:** if you draw how many customers pass through each of the five A's, industries have typical shapes, and the shape tells you what problem the category has and which industry to learn from.

## Explanation

By visualizing the conversion rates (attraction, curiosity, commitment and affinity), the authors identify four patterns (Fig. 7.1):

| Pattern | Distinctive feature | Where it appears | The challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Door knob** | high commitment, low curiosity: impulsive, habitual purchase; availability at the point of purchase decides | consumer packaged goods | low affinity, frequent brand switching → engagement programs (My Coke Rewards) |
| **Goldfish** | very high curiosity (ask > appeal); commoditized offerings | B2B; in B2C, family travel | every rate has to be improved, curiosity above all |
| **Trumpet** | high affinity: more advocates than buyers | luxury, lifestyle | improve affordability and access without diluting the appeal (the Tesla challenge) |
| **Funnel** | the customer goes through every stage, planned and involved | consumer durables, services | commitment and affinity; the category most prone to disruption |

The ideal is the **bow tie** (Fig. 7.2): everyone who is aware of the brand recommends it (BAR = 1), everyone who is attracted buys, and curiosity is moderate, a sign of clear positioning.

A second classification uses the **BAR median** (how willing customers in the industry are to recommend) and the **BAR range** (the gap between the highest and lowest BAR). Each combination points to a key success factor and a model industry: brand management (CPG), channel management (retail), service management (airlines) or sales-force management ("push" marketing).

**The link with the textbooks** (the note's own reading): the shapes resemble Grewal and Levy's classification of products by shopping effort (MKT-021). The door knob matches convenience products, the funnel shopping products, the trumpet specialty products. Kotler adds what the textbook lacks: the advocacy rate.

## Application

- At a pitch, draw the shape of the five A's path for the client's category and superimpose the bow tie. The gaps show where the opportunity is.
- Learn from other industries: a CPG brand with low affinity can borrow loyalty mechanics from airlines.
- Use the BAR median to decide whether word of mouth stands a chance in a category.

## Example

Hipmunk (an online travel company, a "goldfish" category) publishes the travel magazine Tailwind and offers an AI-powered travel assistant that spares customers the research (ch. 9). This moves the path one step closer to the bow tie.

## Questions

- The archetypes come from MarkPlus data that the book does not publish. How far can you rely on them?
- Can a brand change the archetype of its category, or only its position within it?
