# Value co-creation: consumer empowerment or unpaid work for the brand?

> **The idea in one sentence:** service-dominant logic says value is created together with the customer, but critics observe that the same co-creation turns the consumer into an unpaid worker, who sometimes even pays more for the fruits of their own labor.

## Explanation

Parsons, Maclaran and Chatzidakis start from the change in how marketing is conceived. **Service-dominant logic** (Vargo and Lusch) replaces goods-dominant logic: service (the application of competences) is the basis of exchange, goods are distribution mechanisms, and value is **co-created** by multiple actors, including the beneficiary, who also determines it.

The two positions in the debate:

| The celebratory position | The critical position |
|---|---|
| The consumer is informed, connected, consulted, liberated (Prahalad and Ramaswamy) | Consumers work for free: they sell on eBay, produce on YouTube, write reviews on TripAdvisor |
| Co-creation builds loyalty and experiences | "Co-creative capitalism": unpaid labor, new forms of control (Cova, Dalli and Zwick) |
| Customized products | Co-created products often command higher prices |
| Creative contests democratize advertising | The winners are often freelance or unemployed creatives, who should be properly paid |

Examples of co-creation: Doritos' "Crash the Superbowl" (1 million dollars in prize money and a showing at the Super Bowl), Threadless, Burberry's "Art of the Trench", Starbucks' "White Cup Contest", Coca-Cola's "Share a Coke". The crux of the critique is the consumer's genuine freedom, when the framing and resources of co-creation are designed to meet the company's needs.

**Who sides with whom in the library.** The celebratory position is the majority: Grewal and Levy (MKT-002), Kotler (Marketing 3.0, where the same Doritos campaign is a success of cocreation, MKT-035) and Wuebben (MKT-042). SRC-013 is the only source that sets out the critique systematically.

## Application

- When you propose a participatory campaign, ask: what does the participant get in return (recognition, money, rights)?
- For creative contests, weigh the effect on professionals in the industry.
- Use co-creation for meaning and relationship, not only as a cheap source of content.

## Example

Doritos' "Crash the Superbowl": ten years of ads made by fans, a lot of online buzz, coverage in the traditional news media. What the fans got, apart from the chance at the prize, remains the critics' question.

## Questions

- Is UGC a form of exploitation or a fair exchange for visibility?
- How would the debate change if participants received copyright?
