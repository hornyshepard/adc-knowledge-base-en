# Sustainable impact is measured on four families of indicators, and credibility comes from external audit

> **The idea in one sentence:** a sustainability claim becomes credible when it is tied to a measured indicator (emissions, resources, satisfaction, reputation), with a clear baseline and scope, and verified by a third party.

## Explanation

Viturino proposes four families of indicators (ch. 5):

| Family | What you measure |
|---|---|
| **Carbon footprint** | *Scope 1*: direct emissions (vehicles, own factories). *Scope 2*: indirect emissions from purchased energy. *Scope 3*: the rest of the value chain (raw materials, transportation, use and disposal of the product), following the GHG Protocol standard. |
| **Natural resource consumption** | water, energy (renewable vs. non-renewable), materials (including the recycled share) |
| **Customer satisfaction** with sustainable practices | NPS (the percentage of promoters minus the percentage of detractors, on a 0–10 scale), dedicated surveys, qualitative feedback |
| **Brand reputation** | indices (Dow Jones Sustainability Index, Corporate Knights Global 100), media and social sentiment, stakeholder engagement |

Concrete KPIs proposed: the percentage reduction in greenhouse gas emissions compared with previous years, energy efficiency (kWh per unit produced) and the recycling rate (recycled waste out of the total). Targets set according to scientific guidelines (*Science-Based Targets*) (ch. 3).

Credibility comes from the process: regular **internal audits**, independent **external audits** that validate the data, and periodic **sustainability reports** (the GRI standard). Unilever is given as the example for external audit, IBM for monitoring with IoT sensors and big data analytics (ch. 5).

## Application

- Every sustainability claim in a campaign must point to a KPI, a baseline ("compared with 2020"), a scope (scope 1–2, or 3 as well?) and who verified the number.
- Do not present NPS or reputation as proof of impact. They measure perception, not the reduction of harm.

## Example

A manufacturer that announces "emissions cut by 30%" for its own factories only (scope 1), when most of its emissions are in the supply chain (scope 3), tells half the truth. The book's distinction by scope makes that half visible.

## Questions

- The book puts indicators of real impact (emissions) and of perception (NPS, reputation) in the same grid. Why can mixing them be dangerous?
- Viturino lists GRI among the reputation rankings. What is GRI actually, and why does the distinction matter?
