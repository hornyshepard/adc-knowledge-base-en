# Prosperity polarization empties the middle of the market: only low-cost positioning and premium positioning through experience remain

> **The idea in one sentence:** inequality turns society from a normal distribution into an "M", and when everything polarizes, a brand has two meaningful positionings left: cheap core value or the complete premium experience.

## Explanation

Marketing 5.0 starts from the film *The Platform* (a prison tower in which food descends from the top and never reaches the lower floors) as a metaphor for inequality. Society takes an **M** shape (Ouchi, Ohmae): the greatest numbers of people are in the upper and lower classes. Polarization appears on four planes: **jobs** (the middle ones disappear), **ideologies** (protectionism vs. a borderless world, filter bubbles), **lifestyles** (minimalism vs. consumerism) and **markets** (customers trade down to no-frill offerings or trade up to premium).

Strategically, what remains is **cost leadership** (intrinsic value, unbundled offerings, no quality compromise) and **total-customer-experience innovation** (top-quality ingredients, exclusive channels, luxury brand narratives, plus "affordable luxury" for the middle tier).

The proposed solution is **inclusive and sustainable** marketing. Corporate activism becomes a **hygiene factor**: without broader vision, mission and values, brands "have no license to compete". The working framework is the 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Marketing 3.0 had already described the disappearance of the middle segment; 5.0 turns it into a positioning rule.

**Convergences and tensions.** The logic resembles Porter's warning, taken up by von Scheel, against getting "stuck in the middle" between cost and differentiation (MKT-050). On purpose, the sources disagree: for Kotler 5.0 it is hygiene, for Kotler 3.0 it was a differentiator (MKT-048), and the authors of *Kellogg on Branding* observe that some brands gain more from a clear positioning than from a higher purpose (BRD-061).

## Application

- Check where the client's brand sits on the low-cost to premium axis. If it is "in the middle", argue for a move or for a dual offer (a basic one and an affordable luxury one).
- For purpose, choose 1–3 SDGs tied to the client's value chain, not all 17.
- Communicate measurable progress, not intention.

## Example

The "Stop Hate for Profit" campaign, in which Microsoft, Starbucks, Unilever and hundreds of other companies paused advertising on Facebook, is cited as a sign that corporate activism has become a basic expectation.

## Questions

- If purpose is "hygiene", can it still differentiate?
- The polarization described is mostly American. Does it fit the markets of Eastern Europe?
