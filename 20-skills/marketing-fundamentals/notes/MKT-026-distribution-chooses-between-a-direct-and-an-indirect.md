# Distribution chooses between a direct and an indirect channel and between three levels of intensity: intensive, selective, exclusive

> **The idea in one sentence:** the third P brings the product to the right customer when that customer wants it; the firm decides whether it sells on its own or through intermediaries and in how many outlets the product appears, and the decision also sends a message about the brand.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy define marketing channel management (also called supply chain management) as integrating suppliers, manufacturers, warehouses, stores and transportation intermediaries into a seamless operation: the right product, in the right quantities, at the right place and time, at minimal cost. Students underestimate place because it happens "behind the scenes". **Each intermediary adds value**: the retailer who stocks, explains, delivers and installs a stove makes it more expensive, but also more valuable.

**Direct vs. indirect channel** (ch. 16): direct, with no intermediaries (P&G with Gillette On Demand bypasses the retailer); indirect, with one or more intermediaries (car dealers, wholesalers).

**Distribution intensity** (ch. 17):

| Level | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Intensive | as many outlets as possible | Pepsi: stores, gas stations, vending machines |
| Selective | a few selected retailers in a territory | apparel, pots and pans, consumer electronics |
| Exclusive | one or very few retailers | Coach avoids discount and off-price stores so as not to weaken its image |

Policarpio (SRC-002) adds that Drucker called distribution "Industry's Dark Continent"; the functions of the channel are logistics, facilitation, building the assortment, sharing risk and marketing. To win over intermediaries, the manufacturer uses *push* (incentives for the channel) or *pull* (demand created among consumers through promotion).

**What digital changes.** Sachdev frames the choice as omnichannel vs. disintermediation (MKT-030), and Marketing 4.0 adds peer-to-peer distribution, Airbnb-style "communal activation" (MKT-035).

## Application

- Distribution communicates positioning: a luxury brand present in a hypermarket contradicts its own advertising.
- Coordinate the campaign with inventory: nothing destroys credibility faster than a promotion with no stock on the shelf.
- A direct channel (DTC) brings customer data, but also conflict with partner retailers.

## Example

Brown Betty Dessert Boutique got into Walmart, in a test in 300 stores, with no marketing support from the retailer. It sold under the Patti LaBelle name, met strict packaging and shipping requirements, and its success extended the deal to 500 stores.

## Questions

- For a new brand of green cosmetics, why would exclusivity be an advantage for the retailer?
- What happens to "place" when a brand sells only on Instagram and TikTok Shop?
