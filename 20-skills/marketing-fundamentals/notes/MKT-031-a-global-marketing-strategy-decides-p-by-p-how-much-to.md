# A global marketing strategy decides, P by P, how much to standardize and how much to adapt: from the same product to one created for a single country

> **The idea in one sentence:** a global strategy has the same two components as a domestic one, the target markets and the mix, but each mix decision sits on a scale between "the same everywhere" and "made for this market".

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy (ch. 8) say that global segmentation, targeting and positioning (STP) are more complicated for three reasons: the cultural nuances of other countries, the subcultures within each country, and the fact that consumers view products and their role as consumers differently in different countries. That is why the same product, service or retailer often has to be positioned differently in different markets.

Each P calls for a different decision:

| P | Options | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| Product | the same product; minor adaptations; a totally new product | Apple sells the same phone but localizes the customer service in its stores; IKEA India added risers to furniture (floors are cleaned with water) and offers vegan Swedish meatballs; Nestlé Japan produces around 40 Kit Kat flavors each year |
| Price | adjusted to laws, tariffs, exchange rates and the local positioning | Zara is priced about 40 percent higher in the United States than in Spain |
| Place | longer, more complex channels in developing countries | Tupperware parties for women in Indonesia |
| Communication | literacy, available media, language, customs, religion | the Longines watch on a gloved arm in Muslim countries; the Chinese names of Nike (nai-ke) and Mercedes-Benz, adapted for each language |

**Glocalization**, in the book's glossary, is standardizing products globally while using different promotional campaigns to sell them. The text, however, uses the term inconsistently (once for "the same product everywhere"), so state the meaning when you use it. Even among English speakers, ads are adapted: British consumers did not find "What Happens in Vegas Stays in Vegas" edgy enough.

**What other skills add.** Percy and Rosenbaum-Elliott give the criterion for communication: standardization works for categories with low involvement and low emotion, in similar cultures (ADV-049). Kellogg asks that the brand essence stay central while the local context is left to people on the ground (BRD-049).

## Application

- For a regional campaign, make a table of the 4Ps and mark each one: standardized, adapted or local.
- Check the name and the slogan in every language before launch: Bing sounded like "virus" in China.
- Use international events (International Women's Day) for consistent global messages, but check that the gesture is backed by facts.

## Example

Pringles keeps the original product and the campaign themes the same globally, with limited language adaptations, but changes the flavors: paprika-flavored chips in Italy and Germany.

## Questions

- Should a Romanian brand entering the Republic of Moldova or Bulgaria standardize or adapt? Which Ps matter most?
- When does local adaptation dilute a global brand?
