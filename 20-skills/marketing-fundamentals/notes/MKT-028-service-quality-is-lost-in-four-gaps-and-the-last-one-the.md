# Service quality is lost in four gaps, and the last one, the communication gap, is created by advertising that promises too much

> **The idea in one sentence:** customers compare the service they get with their expectations, and advertising helps form those expectations; the Service Gaps Model (Zeithaml, Parasuraman and Berry) shows four places where the promise and the reality come apart.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy take up the model (Exhibit 13.3):

| Gap | Between what and what | How it closes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Knowledge gap | customers' expectations vs. the firm's perception of those expectations | research, more interaction between managers and frontline employees |
| 2. Standards gap | the firm's perception vs. the service standards it sets | appropriate standards, training, measuring performance |
| 3. Delivery gap | the standards vs. the service actually delivered | empowered service providers, support and incentives, technology |
| 4. Communication gap | the service delivered vs. what the promotion promises | realistic promises, managing customer expectations |

The case in the book: Marcia Kessler saw an ad for a motel in Maine with a reasonable rate, free babysitting, a piano bar, a heated swimming pool and newly decorated rooms. In reality, the advertised price was not available on the weekend, the room had not been cleaned, the pool water was around 50 degrees, and the continental breakfast was stale. Each broken promise widened the communication gap.

The same principle appears in the chapter on the consumer: unrealistically high expectations set through advertising lead to higher initial sales but end in dissatisfaction and negative word of mouth. Setting expectations too low is an equally dangerous strategy, because it does not bring the customer into the store.

**The same mechanism, in another field.** Greenwashing is a communication gap applied to sustainability: the green claim goes beyond what the company actually does (MKT-071). Puffery is legally tolerated, but a promised figure has to be proven (ADV-030).

## Application

- Before you write a promise, ask: can operations deliver it every time, not only on their best day?
- Test advertising for services with frontline employees: they know where the delivery gap is.
- When the client wants an "aspirational" ad, propose specific, verifiable promises instead of superlatives.

## Example

Hilton (ch. 3) built its "Expect More. Expect Hilton." campaign on social listening to travelers who had developed minimal expectations of their hotels, then promised concrete, deliverable things: free Wi-Fi, the lowest price when booking through the hotel's website, a digital key on the phone.

## Questions

- Is it the agency's job to check whether the service can support the promise?
- How does the model apply to a physical product with a large service component (a car, a subscription)?
