# Differentiation means being different, not better, and higher-order advantages such as brand reputation last longer

> **The idea in one sentence:** competitive advantage comes from deliberately choosing a different set of activities, not from doing the same thing better; advantages based on brand, relationships and proprietary technology are much harder to copy than those based on low costs.

## Explanation

Von Scheel takes up Porter's logic: the essence of strategy is to choose where you are unique and how you compete. Differentiation is not a matter of being better at what you do but of being *different* at what you do, deliberately choosing a different set of activities that delivers a unique mix of value (p. 199). Competitiveness is not found but carefully crafted, and it arises when the value created for buyers exceeds the cost of creating it.

There are three generic strategies (p. 202): **cost leadership**, **differentiation** and **focus** (being the best in a carefully chosen product, market or customer). The message is not to get stuck in the middle, while keeping a balance: a cost leader cannot offer distinctly inferior products, and a differentiator cannot afford costs above the price premium.

The sustainability of the advantage depends on three things (pp. 202–203): **the source** (lower-order advantages, such as cheap labor, are easy to imitate; higher-order ones, such as proprietary technology, **brand reputation** and customer relationships, last), **the number of distinct sources** and **constant improvement**. For key differentiators, five criteria: truly distinctive, measurable against competitors, relevant to core customers, mutually reinforcing and clear at all levels of the company (p. 204).

**Convergences.** Grewal and Levy reach the same rule of multiple sources with the metaphor of the wall (MKT-011). Kotler (Marketing 5.0) gives a market version of "do not get stuck in the middle": in the polarized society only low cost or the premium experience remain (MKT-040). Percy adds a communication nuance: the central positioning, "best at everything", is credible only for the category leader; the others have to differentiate (BRD-009).

## Application

- Run the "point of difference" of a brand platform through the five criteria. If it is not measurable against competitors or not clear to the employees in the store, it is only a statement.
- Use the hierarchy of sources as an argument for investing in the brand: reputation is a higher-order advantage.

## Example

The author recommends growing "from the core": you replicate your strongest advantage in new contexts (products, segments, geographies) through routines that everyone in the organization can follow (p. 200).

## Questions

- Is the line "If you don't have a competitive advantage, don't compete" (p. 199) realistic for a new brand?
- How does "different, not better" fit with the need for parity in the category?
