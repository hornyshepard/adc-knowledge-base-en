# Conscious marketing puts the higher purpose at the core of the business, CSR only adds it at the edge

> **The idea in one sentence:** corporate social responsibility (CSR) can be a separate activity, part of PR, whereas conscious marketing makes the higher purpose, all the stakeholders and ethics the foundation of every decision.

## Explanation

Grewal and Levy define conscious marketing by four principles:

1. **Recognition of marketing's greater purpose**, beyond profit (e.g., environmental responsibility, as at Unilever).
2. **Consideration of all stakeholders and their interdependence**, not only shareholders.
3. **Conscious leadership and culture**: values are lived at every level (The Container Store).
4. **Decisions based on marketing ethics**.

CSR is defined as actions that take stakeholders' expectations into account in order to achieve the *triple bottom line*: economic, social and environmental performance. The difference (Exhibit 4.2) is one of position, not of intensity:

| CSR | Conscious marketing |
|---|---|
| independent of corporate purpose or culture | incorporates a higher purpose and a caring culture |
| often a separate department or part of PR | puts social responsibility at the core of the business |
| sees limited overlap between business, society and the planet | business is a subset of society, society a subset of the planet |

The authors note that firms in polluting industries (tobacco, fossil fuels) can engage in CSR but can hardly become conscious marketers. Walmart runs extensive CSR programs, yet it is criticized for low pay. Chapter 5 brings the opposite risk: **greenwashing**, that is, spending more money on saying you are green than on being green.

**Where the sources converge.** Kotler (Marketing 3.0) draws the same difference as a ladder: philanthropy and cause marketing stay at the edge, socio-cultural transformation enters the business model (MKT-064). Viturino gives the vocabulary without a hierarchy (MKT-068). The authors of *Kellogg on Branding* add the practical requirement: the purpose is discovered and lived inside first, then communicated (BRD-023).

## Application

- When a client asks for a "purpose" campaign, first check whether the purpose shows in the product, the supply chain and the culture. If not, what you are proposing is communicated CSR, with the risk of greenwashing.
- The simple test: how much money goes into communicating the gesture, and how much into the gesture itself?
- Map the stakeholders the campaign affects (including those who do not buy).

## Example

City Girl Coffee (Dilemma 1.1): a family-owned company builds a new brand that buys beans only from farms owned or managed by women and donates 5% of its profits. The purpose is in the business model, not only in the advertising. Counterexample: Starbucks dropped straws and replaced them with lids that are hard to recycle (Dilemma 1.2).

## Questions

- Is the distinction workable, or is it a moral label the authors put on the firms they admire?
- Can an advertising agency turn down a "purpose" brief that has no facts behind it?
