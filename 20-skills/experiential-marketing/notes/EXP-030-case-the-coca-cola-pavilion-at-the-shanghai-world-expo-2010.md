# Case: the Coca-Cola pavilion at the Shanghai World Expo 2010 turns the "Happiness Factory" universe into a multisensory journey

> **The idea in one sentence:** Coca-Cola built a pavilion in which every touchpoint stimulated the five senses and told, step by step, the same story about happiness, from the queue to the final karaoke.

## Explanation

At the Shanghai World Expo (2010), the Coca-Cola pavilion was one of the top 10 visited attractions and generated free media coverage worth more than millions of dollars. Batat presents it as an example of holistic immersion: every touchpoint was arranged to stimulate smell, sight, sound, taste and touch, for an emotional and community connection.

The journey had a clear narrative structure:
1. **Happiness Factory Welcome**: the factory characters welcome the visitors;
2. **Path to Happiness**: a journey through the graphic properties of the brand;
3. **Bottle Through Time**: the evolution of the bottle design;
4. **Ice Story**: five stories of ice cubes in love with Coca-Cola, created with Jellymon Shanghai;
5. **Vintage Coca-Cola**: a collage of vintage ads from around the world;
6. **That Coke Feeling**: fizzing bubbles that turn into people having a Coke moment;
7. **Shanghai Skyline**: animations run on the AURORA Tower for the six months of the Expo, so that the experience extends beyond the pavilion;
8. **Open Happiness Karaoke**: the participatory finale, to the campaign song.

Outside, bottle-shaped screens displayed the crowds waiting in line and entertained them.

## Application

Lessons for an activation or a stand:
- **A single theme**: start from an existing brand universe ("Happiness Factory"), not from scratch;
- **Structure**: a sequence of "scenes" with a rhythm, like a script (Mossberg's arena, characters and structure);
- **Brand heritage**: history (the bottle, the old ads) becomes experiential content;
- **The wait is part of the experience**: the queue is designed;
- **A participatory finale**: visitors leave after doing something (singing), not only after watching;
- **Extension**: the experience continues in the city's media.

## Example

The case itself is the example; compare it with West Edmonton Mall (immersion through permanent entertainment) or with Hard Rock Cafe (theming through authentic artifacts).

## Questions

- Batat gives no data on the effect on sales or on image in China. What indicators would you have proposed?
- Holt criticizes immersion models that rely too heavily on sensory factors. Does the pavilion change the meaning of the brand, or does it only entertain?
