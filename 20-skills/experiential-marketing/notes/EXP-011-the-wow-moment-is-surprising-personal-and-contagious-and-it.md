# The WOW moment is surprising, personal and contagious, and it can be designed instead of left to chance

> **The idea in one sentence:** when great products and services have become commodities, the difference comes from the moment of delight that leaves the customer speechless, and winning companies create it through strategy, processes and trained people.

## Explanation

The epilogue of *Marketing 4.0* starts with Jia Jiang, an entrepreneur who, as therapy against his fear of rejection, made absurd requests. At Krispy Kreme he asked for a box of doughnuts in the shape of the Olympic rings, and the worker Jackie Braun made it, even getting the color sequence right. The recording has been watched over five million times on YouTube.

From this come three characteristics of a WOW:

1. **It is surprising**: it happens when the outcome clearly exceeds the expectation (a deviation from the expected outcome is what creates the WOW).
2. **It is personal**: it is triggered by the person experiencing it, often when a hidden anxiety they did not express is fulfilled.
3. **It is contagious**: whoever experiences it tells others, and the brand gets free publicity.

The authors distinguish three levels of interaction with the customer:

| Level | What the brand offers | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Enjoy**: enjoyment | superior products and services | needs and wants |
| **Experience**: experience | designed interactions (*service blueprint*), differentiated between in-store and in-digital | the customer experience |
| **Engage**: engagement | life-transforming personalization that addresses individual anxieties and desires and helps self-actualization | personal engagement |

WOW can be created by design across the five A's. In chapter 6, the book gives the Ritz-Carlton example: the staff documented the "holiday" of a stuffed giraffe a child had lost at the hotel, to back up the story the parents had told. The same case (Joshie the giraffe) appears in the digital marketing textbook by Zahay et al. ([[EXP-002-customer-experience-is-the-lived-sum-of-all-interactions|EXP-002]]): two independent sources link WOW to empowered employees, not to procedures.

## Application

- In an experience project, identify 1–2 moments on the 5A path where a surprise would have the greatest effect (usually act and advocate).
- Give frontline employees the freedom and the budget to improvise. WOW is rarely in the procedure.
- Think from the start about how the moment can be retold: what the customer sees, films and passes on.

## Example

Zappos sent flowers to a customer with damaged feet, who had ordered six pairs of shoes and returned four, just to show that it sympathized with her (ch. 6).

## Questions

- If WOW becomes standard, it is no longer WOW. How do you avoid inflating expectations?
- Can a designed tactic stay "personal" and authentic?
