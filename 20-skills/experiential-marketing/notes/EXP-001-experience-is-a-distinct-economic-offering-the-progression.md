# Experience is a distinct economic offering: the progression of value and Pine & Gilmore's four realms

> **The idea in one sentence:** value climbs from commodity to good, to service and then to experience; a memorable experience is designed across four realms: aesthetic, escapist, educational and entertainment.

## Explanation

*The Experience Economy* (Pine & Gilmore, 1998–1999) is the common starting point for almost every book in the library. Wuebben sums it up through the **progression of value** of the coffee bean: commodity → good → service → experience. Batat cites the key definition: an experience occurs when a company uses services as the stage and goods as props to create a memorable event. The work of the experience stager perishes with its performance, but the value lingers in the memory. Taken toward the person, the experience becomes a *transformation*.

Batat spells out the two axes of the typology: **absorption ↔ immersion** and **passive ↔ active participation**.

| Realm | Axes | The design question (Wuebben, ch. 16) |
|---|---|---|
| **Entertainment** | absorption, passive | What makes them laugh and enjoy the time? |
| **Educational** | absorption, active | What do you want them to learn, and through which activities? |
| **Escapist** | immersion, active | What do they do, and which other reality do they go into? |
| **Aesthetic** | immersion, passive | What makes them want to come in and stay? |

The richest experiences combine all four, and the realms can turn into one another: strong absorption turns a lesson into an escape. Where the authors part ways: Wuebben takes the model almost without reservation. Batat faults it for describing the context of consumption rather than the customer's perception, and builds her own matrix on top of it ([[EXP-014-the-experience-territory-matrix-crosses-the-customer-s|EXP-014]]).

## Application

- Check any activation or stand against the four realms. Most have only the aesthetic one: a good photo and nothing to do.
- Choose an explicit theme before the set. The theme decides what goes in and what stays out.
- Treat the staff as a cast: "Every Business a Stage", as the subtitle of Pine and Gilmore's book puts it.

## Example

At the Pike Place Fish Market in Seattle, the workers toss fish to one another and put on a show you can watch without buying anything, at almost no cost. Their four principles are acting techniques: play, make their day, be there, choose your attitude (Wuebben, ch. 8). REI, with its in-store climbing wall, adds the educational and the escapist realms.

## Questions

- Wuebben says a good experience removes the commoditization risk. Is that still true if competitors copy it?
- When does the experience become a cost with no link to the brand?
