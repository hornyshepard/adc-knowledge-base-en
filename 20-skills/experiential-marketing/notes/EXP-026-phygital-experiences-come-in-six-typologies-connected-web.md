# Phygital experiences come in six typologies: connected, web, VR/AR, socio-digital, re-creative, cyborg

> **The idea in one sentence:** Batat classifies the experiences that link the physical and the digital into six families, each with its own tools and examples, so that a brand can choose the type that fits its objective.

## Explanation

| Typology | What it is | Example from the book |
|---|---|---|
| **Connected** | connected objects (IoT) link the real and the digital world and give a "quantified return" on the experience | the Fitbit bracelet (*quantified self*); the Amazon Dash Button, ordering in one click |
| **Web** | the website becomes an experience, not a page, by adapting the five senses (see below) | (none) |
| **Virtual and augmented reality** | projecting the virtual into the real world, or immersion in an artificial world | the Pepsi Max "Unbelievable" bus shelter (AMV BBDO, London): the screen showed the street in real time, plus alien flying saucers, a meteorite, a giant robot, a tiger |
| **Socio-digital** | experiences on social networks, adapted to each platform | Tipp-Ex "A hunter shoots a bear" (Buzzman): users rewrote the ending with the correction tape; more than 50 million views |
| **Re-creative** | digital creation: strengthened (3D printing), enhanced (AI), controlled (quantified-self apps) | Burberry: image recognition that spots counterfeit products with 98% accuracy, and RFID in its stores |
| **Cyborg** | humanoid robots in the experience | host robots used as hostesses in luxury stores in Japan |

**The web through the five senses:** *visual*: a homepage with strong visuals, a design specific to the brand's DNA, HD images, 3D avatars; *touch*: selective zoom, slideshows and full-screen videos, 3D objects in 360 degrees; *sound*: a sound signature, a choice of music, sound control, vocal narration; *smell*: an olfactory signature, broadcasters in connected objects in stores; *taste*: detailed descriptions, storytelling, partnerships with chefs, tasting events.

Tools that cut across the typologies: web 3.0 (cognitive connected objects, which measure behavior, and sensory connected objects, which read emotion), interactive tools (AR, 3D printing, QR codes, geolocation) and "the flow tool".

## Application

- Start from the objective: viral awareness → socio-digital or AR; loyalty and data → connected; premium differentiation → sensory web or re-creative.
- Check that the chosen typology serves the brand story (Pepsi Max: "Unbelievable" as a promise about the sugar-free taste).

## Example

The Pepsi Max bus shelter is the reference case for ADC: a single technological idea, tied directly to the tagline, filmed with a hidden camera and turned into content shared online.

## Questions

- Which of the six typologies have stood the test of time, and which were passing trends (Dash Button, Google Glass)?
- Is the "cyborg" typology an experience in itself or only a gadget?
