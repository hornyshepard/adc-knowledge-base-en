# A memorable experience does not necessarily require the extraordinary: the ordinary vs. extraordinary debate

> **The idea in one sentence:** part of the literature calls for spectacular settings to create strong experiences; another part shows that ordinary moments, with no intervention from the brand, can also be lived intensely, and Batat places them on a continuum.

## Explanation

**The "extraordinary" position.** Arnould & Price (1993) and Pine & Gilmore (1999) argue that customers expect unusual settings to produce strong, deep, memorable experiences. Goulding and Ritzer add that people seek to escape their ordinary life into a pre-established, safe and themed sphere. Kotler et al. follow the same line when they define WOW as a clear deviation from the expectation ([[EXP-011-the-wow-moment-is-surprising-personal-and-contagious-and-it|EXP-011]]).

**The "ordinary" position.** Carù & Cova (2003, 2006) show that not all experiences need an extraordinary dimension to be immersive. Their example: the *far niente* holiday, in which people do not look for activities but for calm, and the experience is nonetheless highly immersive. In Theory Box 6.3, Batat goes further: the extraordinary makes sense only when compared with the ordinary; daily passions can be lived intensely; intensity comes from the closeness between the person and the experience, not from the exceptional nature of the setting.

**Batat's synthesis (ch. 6).** The experience is a continuum: from situations fully organized by the firm (a resort, a cruise ship, a mall) to self-organized moments with no provider at all: playing cards as a family, building a sand castle with one's grandson. Customers want freedom of choice over when they are in contact with the firm, and sometimes they want no activity imposed on them at all.

## Application

- In an activation, decide consciously where you put the spectacle and where you step back. Sometimes the best gesture is to leave space.
- Look for "the ordinary lived as extraordinary": daily rituals (coffee, the commute) that the brand can enrich discreetly.
- Do not oversaturate an experience with entertainers and stimuli; contrast needs pauses.

## Example

On the dolphin-watching tour (mini-case 4.6), the guide does not announce the dolphin school and then retreats so that visitors can take in the moment by themselves. Badoit Express brings a gourmet meal into a mundane place, the subway.

## Questions

- For which brand categories (FMCG, utilities) is "the enriched ordinary" more realistic than the spectacle?
- If the most intense experience is the one without a brand, what role is left for the brand?
