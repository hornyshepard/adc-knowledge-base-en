# Customer experience is the lived sum of all interactions with the brand: not a memorable event, and not a better CRM

> **The idea in one sentence:** customer experience (CX) is cumulative and multidimensional: it forms across all touchpoints, before, during and after the purchase, and a spectacular activation is only one part of it.

## Explanation

Two very different sources reach the same definition, both starting from Lemon and Verhoef (2016). The digital marketing textbook by **Zahay et al.** defines CX as the customer's *cumulative* experiences across multiple touchpoints and in multiple channels over time, with cognitive, emotional, behavioral, sensorial and social responses. **Batat** adds the lived dimension: CX is embedded in the sociocultural context, it is composite and evolving, it can be transformative, and it continues in the digital world.

The difference between them is one of angle. Batat explains *why* marketing changed: the CRM of the 1990s stayed centered on the product, quality, promotions and rewarding loyalty. In CX, the goal of consumption is no longer maximum utility but the lived experience, with symbolic, emotional, relational and aesthetic criteria. Her landmarks: Holbrook and Hirschman (1982), Pine and Gilmore (1998–1999), Schmitt (1999), who asks you to start from consumption situations ("grooming in the bathroom"), not from categories. Zahay et al. give the operational tool, Bruce Temkin's grid:

| Component | The question |
|---|---|
| Function | Does the product do what people want it to do? |
| Accessibility | How easily do they reach their goal? |
| Emotion | What do they feel when they use it? |

This gives agencies a useful distinction: **experiential marketing** (events, pop-ups) is part of CX, but it is not CX. A good activation does not make up for a bad call center. Both sources insist that CX is about people, that is, about empowered employees, not only about data.

## Application

- Audit the "boring" touchpoints as well (the order confirmation, the invoice, support): that is where the campaign's promise is decided.
- Reframe the category as a consumption situation: not "we sell coffee" but "the first ten minutes of the morning".
- Run every experiential idea through Temkin's grid. Use NPS only alongside other measures.

## Example

A child left his stuffed giraffe, Joshie, at the Ritz-Carlton. The hotel sent it back with pictures of his "vacation". The case also appears in *Marketing 4.0* ([[EXP-011-the-wow-moment-is-surprising-personal-and-contagious-and-it|EXP-011]]), a sign that such moments come from the freedom given to employees, not from procedures.

## Questions

- If "everything is experience", what is *not* experience anymore? Does the concept still differentiate anything?
- The ACSI shows satisfaction declining even though companies invest heavily in CX. What explains the paradox? Does CRM become just the data infrastructure of CX?
