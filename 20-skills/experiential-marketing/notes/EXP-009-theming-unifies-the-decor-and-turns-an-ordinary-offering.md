# Theming unifies the decor and turns an ordinary offering into an experience

> **The idea in one sentence:** a coherent theme, carried through every element of the setting, keeps the customer "inside" and gives them a mental reference they will remember.

## Explanation

Theming means creating a theme that the customer dives into, as defined by the company. Batat links it to Baudrillard's idea that our societies are characterized by simulation and simulacra. The goal is not only atmosphere but, above all, to **unify the decor**: every element of the setting (sound, uniforms, menu, objects) has to comply with the theme, because this consistency keeps the customer from "escaping" the experience.

Four reasons to use theming:
1. it allows for better immersion, in retail, services or leisure;
2. it has a magical dimension that disconnects the customer from daily pressures;
3. the theme becomes a mental reference that feeds pleasurable memories (Schmitt);
4. it helps the customer organize their impressions of a place (Pine & Gilmore).

Gilmore & Pine (2002) go as far as saying that an offering becomes an experience only when a theme is used. To choose one, Wuebben points to Gottdiener's ten themes (status, tropical paradise, the Wild West, nostalgia, etc.) and to the nine "domains" of Schmitt & Simonson (history, religion, fashion, popular culture, the arts, etc.). Reality can be recreated in two ways: through **real authenticity** (world heritage sites, for consumers with high expectations) or through **reconstructed, hyper-real authenticity** (Las Vegas), for those who accept the "fake" and enjoy it. Mossberg (2008) names three success factors: an **arena** (the experiencescape), **characters** (the staff and other customers) and a **structure** (the story).

## Application

- Choose the theme starting from the brand's DNA, not from a visual trend.
- Draw up an "element × theme" table: does each touchpoint respect the theme or break it?
- Decide explicitly: authentic or hyper-real? Does your audience accept the replica?
- Write the arena, the characters and the structure like a script.

## Example

Hard Rock Cafe (founded in 1971 in London by Isaac Tigrett and Peter Morton, 139 cafés in 59 countries at the time of the book) exhibits authentic rock artifacts on its walls, loops music videos on screens, serves American food and sells souvenirs: everything tells the same story. Las Vegas, with its half-scale Eiffel Tower and the Statue of Liberty in front of the New York–New York Hotel & Casino, offers an "alternative reality" in which visitors escape the rules of daily life.

## Questions

- When does theming become kitsch? Where is the line between hyper-real and cheap?
- The ordinary/extraordinary debate suggests that immersion does not require a theme. When would you drop the theme?
