# Experience quality is measured beyond satisfaction: EXQUAL extends the logic of SERVQUAL

> **The idea in one sentence:** Batat proposes EXQUAL, a tool that measures the quality of the experience from the perspectives of both the consumer and the company, starting from SERVQUAL but adding pleasure, the customer's personality and current state.

## Explanation

**What experience quality is.** Batat defines it as the degree of pleasure or annoyance a consumer feels in the lived experience while in contact with the company. It results from how well the customer's expectations of utility and enjoyment were fulfilled, in light of their **personality** (a coherent pattern of feelings, thoughts and actions) and their **current state** (temporal or situational changes). The same service can therefore be rated differently by the same person on different days.

**The SERVQUAL legacy.** The scale of Parasuraman, Zeithaml and Berry (1988), born of a Marketing Science Institute research program (1983) in four service sectors, has five dimensions:
- *Tangibles*: physical facilities, equipment and appearance of personnel;
- *Reliability*: the promised service, performed dependably and accurately;
- *Responsiveness*: willingness to help and prompt service;
- *Assurance*: the knowledge and courtesy of employees that convey trust and confidence;
- *Empathy*: caring, individualized attention.

The scale has been replicated in education (EDUQUAL), health and art museums (ARTSQUAL), but it has limits: the gap measure (expectations minus perceptions) is complex, services that group multiple encounters are hard to capture, and evaluation while shopping may work differently from evaluation in everyday life.

**EXQUAL** (Table 4.1) brings together the dimensions believed to represent customer experience quality, from both perspectives. Note: the table with the items is missing from the text copy of the book; check it in the printed edition. In chapter 2, the author adds that an experience should be judged by the intensity of the memories and pleasure it leaves, not only by satisfaction.

## Application

A working proposal for a project (not the author's exact tool):
- keep the five SERVQUAL dimensions for the functional side;
- add: the intensity of emotion at key moments, memorability (what the customer remembers a week later), perceived respect (the ETM axis), consistency across channels;
- record the customer's state at the moment of the evaluation (hurry, tiredness, first visit).

## Example

A retailer that measures only NPS and checkout time can miss the fact that an experience was correct but bland: nothing to tell anyone. The memorability question ("What do you remember from last week's visit?") brings this to light.

## Questions

- Can a scale capture an experience that the author herself describes as subjective and an assemblage?
- Who rates "the company's perspective" in EXQUAL, and how are the two perspectives reconciled?
