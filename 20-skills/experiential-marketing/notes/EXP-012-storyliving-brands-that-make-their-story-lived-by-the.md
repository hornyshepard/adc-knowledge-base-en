# Storyliving: brands that make their story lived by the customer outperform brands that only tell it

> **The idea in one sentence:** storytelling imagines stories to appeal to the audience; storyliving turns the story into lived action, with the customer at its center, here and now.

## Explanation

Batat takes the term from anthropology ("lived-story", Emigh) and brings it close to *storydoing*. The difference: a story has meaning and value only if it is lived first, if it reflects an emotion or a real exchange between the brand and its customers. Otherwise, the story "remains an empty shell." Storyliving has flourished above all in virtual reality and in live video (Periscope, Snapchat, Instagram), which bring an ideal of spontaneity that appeals to young audiences.

Storytelling remains the starting point, but storyliving requires two things: it must engage all the company's functions (marketing, communication, HR, R&D), and it must put the customer and their expectations at the center of the story. A long-running example: Montblanc, a brand linked to the culture of writing, has promoted access to writing with UNICEF since 2004.

**Five storyliving strategies:**

| Strategy | What it does | Case |
|---|---|---|
| **Instructive** | practical and educational information about the product | Expedia Norway: a VR journey of under an hour on the Flåm railway |
| **Social and emotional** | enjoyable interactions among customers and emotions turned into actions | McDonald's × Rovio: the Angry Birds characters in 3D in the restaurant |
| **Immersive** | real or virtual interaction that absorbs | Oreo: the cookie factory in 360-degree VR, with rivers of milk |
| **Experiential** | projects the customer into a real or virtual world that anchors the brand | Google Spotlight Story "HELP", a 360-degree animated film |
| **Ideological** | commitment to a cause | Toms: customers taken to a village in Peru |

Batat also mentions "seven steps" of storyliving (Figure 12.1), but the content of the figure does not appear in the available text.

## Application

- Test every idea with the question: what does the audience *do*, not only what does it *watch*?
- Choose one of the five strategies according to the objective (education, socializing, launch, cause).
- Check that the action is backed by the rest of the company; otherwise it is just a communication stunt.

## Example

Nescafé's "The Hello Bench" (Publicis, Italy): a bench that retracts little by little when two strangers sit on it, bringing them closer so they start a conversation. The brand's value, that coffee is better when it is shared, becomes a lived experience.

## Questions

- Is storyliving something new, or just a new name for experiential marketing and *storydoing*?
- How do you measure the effect of an action lived by a few hundred people and watched by millions online?
