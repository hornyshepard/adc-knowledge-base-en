# Emotional touchpoint cards: a six-step workshop to find out what the customer feels at each contact

> **The idea in one sentence:** with a set of picture cards (emotions and touchpoints), customers and employees can say concretely what they felt and why, going beyond the easy answer "it's ok".

## Explanation

Batat adapts the toolkit of Dewar et al. (2009), from the medical field, and the card-based tools of Clatworthy (2011), from service design. The set has three kinds of cards:
- **emotion cards**: joy, happiness, anger, sadness, etc., with visual expressions;
- **touchpoint cards**: Internet, phone, TV, call center, salespeople, store, product;
- **neutral cards**: blank, filled in by the participants.

The toolkit requires four things: the firm maps its touchpoints as it sees them; customers pick from the map one or more touchpoints they want to talk about, possibly focusing on a positive or a negative emotion; emotions are recorded in the customer's own words; the team decodes what matters to them.

**The six-step workshop** (mini-case 9.2):
1. prepare the emotion and touchpoint cards;
2. invite customers, break the ice, explain the purpose, make them feel valued;
3. spread out the touchpoint cards and ask each person to pick one;
4. spread out the emotion cards, ask for the word that best expresses the experience, and ask "why?";
5. discuss together what could be improved;
6. agree on some actions and thank them.

The cards can also be built as an "innovation game" for co-designing new experiences with customers and with employees from marketing, communication or digital. The tool also works with employees, as an empathy exercise.

## Application

- Run the workshop with 6–8 customers from one segment; you come out with an emotion × touchpoint map and a list of negative peaks to fix.
- Repeat the exercise with frontline employees and compare the two maps: the differences show where the firm does not understand its customers.
- For an agency: the cards can feed the creative brief with real situations and emotions, phrased in customers' words.

## Example

A restaurant chain may discover that the strongest emotion is not tied to the food but to a mundane detail (waiting for the bill, the welcome at the door). That moment becomes a design priority.

## Questions

- How do you keep the choice of cards from being swayed by what the participant thinks the firm wants to hear?
- Is the workshop suitable for sensitive topics (health, money)? What adaptations would it need?
