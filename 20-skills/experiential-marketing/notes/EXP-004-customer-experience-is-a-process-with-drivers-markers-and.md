# Customer experience is a process with drivers, markers and outcomes (the customer experience marketing framework)

> **The idea in one sentence:** Batat describes customer experience as an iterative process: some forces trigger it (drivers), some features characterize it (markers), and in the end it produces effects (outcomes) that feed the next experiences.

## Explanation

The framework in chapter 3 is the backbone of the book; the 7Es mix translates it into management decisions. It has three levels:

| Level | Components |
|---|---|
| **Idiocentric drivers** (self-oriented) | the meaning the consumer gives the experience (symbolic consumption); immersion ([[EXP-008-immersion-is-the-way-into-the-experience-five-factors-in\|EXP-008]]); flow, that is, the intensity of the experience |
| **Allocentric drivers** (others/environment-oriented) | socialization (family, peers, schools, media); marketing and communication actions: consumer engagement, theming ([[EXP-009-theming-unifies-the-decor-and-turns-an-ordinary-offering\|EXP-009]]), storytelling, sensory marketing ([[EXP-010-sensory-marketing-gives-the-brand-a-signature-for-each\|EXP-010]]) |
| **Markers** | the experience is holistic, cumulative, subjective, an assemblage and contrasting |
| **Outcomes** | value, transformation, competency, memorability |

The idiocentric drivers come from the customer's subjectivity: the firm does not control them, but it can understand them. The allocentric drivers include the levers the marketer has at hand. The outcomes are cumulative: what remains from one experience (a competency, a memory) changes how the next one will be lived.

The author insists that the people in contact with the customer are also a driver: a good product alone does not guarantee a good experience. She names four key success factors for training that staff: the company's culture and maturity in terms of customer experience, the atmosphere of the workplace, the customer relationship philosophy, and internal synergies.

## Application

- Use the framework as a diagnostic for an activation: which drivers do we activate? Which markers do we respect (for example, is the experience coherent from end to end)? Which outcome are we after (a memory? a new competency?)?
- In the strategy, separate what you control (theming, senses, story) from what you can only anticipate (personal meaning, the influence of friends).

## Example

American Express (mini-case 3.6) illustrates the engagement driver for CSP+ customers: customization based on LinkedIn data, the "Sleep for miles" app (miles earned while you sleep: 30,000 downloads, 100,000 registrants) and the partnership with TripAdvisor for reviews among cardholders.

## Questions

- Batat lists the idiocentric drivers as "meaning, immersion, engagement", but deals with engagement under the allocentric drivers. Where would you put it, and why?
- The framework leaves out the cost or profitability of the experience: how would you add this dimension?
