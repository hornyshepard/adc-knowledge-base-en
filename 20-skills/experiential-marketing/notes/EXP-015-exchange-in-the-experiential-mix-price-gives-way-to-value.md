# Exchange: in the experiential mix, price gives way to value co-created with the customer

> **The idea in one sentence:** the second E shifts attention from how much the customer pays to what value is created together with them when they take part in the offering, and this requires dialogue, access, risk management and transparency.

## Explanation

For Batat, "Exchange" replaces "Price": value is no longer set by the firm but arises in the exchange between firm and customer. She starts from Prahalad & Ramaswamy's co-creation (2004): provider and customer create value together, on an "engagement platform" where each shares its own resources, integrates those of the other and learns. The Internet ended the firms' monopoly on information.

What the customer gets in exchange is not only money saved but values such as self-fulfillment, confidence in their skills, joy, fun, belonging, accomplishment, self-respect, excitement. Originally, customer participation (fast food, supermarkets) was just a free workforce in exchange for a lower price; in the experiential logic, it becomes a reward in itself, and the customer feels legitimate and competent.

The **DART** model (Prahalad & Ramaswamy) gives the rules:
- **D**ialog: a constructive conversation between the firm and its customers;
- **A**ccess: the customer gets tools and devices to take part;
- **R**isk-benefits: the dialogue is managed so that risks are reduced and explained;
- **T**ransparency: information is shared.

Batat notes that "true" co-creation, in which the customer actually contributes to the offering, is rare. In services, co-creation is more informal: the firm offers a set of facilities from which the customer picks their experience.

## Application

- Before a participatory campaign, run it through DART: does the customer have a real dialogue, tools, a clear idea of the risks and access to information?
- State the "price" of the experience in non-monetary values too: what does the participant gain (recognition, competence, belonging)?

## Example

- *My Starbucks Idea* (2009): customers submit and vote on ideas; this is how Wi-Fi in all Starbucks stores and the free birthday drink came about.
- *CNN iReport*: unpaid citizen journalists send in reports; the best stories are broadcast on TV. The limit: the value of information produced for free.
- *McDonald's "Create Your Taste"*: the burger built at the kiosk from 22 ingredients, tested in Los Angeles in 2013.
- *Nike+* (2006, with Apple): runners' data helped design new products.

## Questions

- When does co-creation become exploitation (free labor) rather than an exchange of value?
- Does it still make sense to talk about "price" in the experiential mix? What is lost if you take it out?
