# Empathy capital replaces "People": employees must first think like the customer

> **The idea in one sentence:** the fifth E is not satisfied with interpersonal skills and service training; it requires people able to understand what the customer feels today and what they need because of it.

## Explanation

Empathy is the ability to take the other's perspective, to understand their reasoning and emotional state. The term comes from the German *Einfühlung* ("feeling into"), from aesthetics, and was translated into English by Titchener. Carl Rogers adds the "as if" condition: you understand the other's world as if you were them, without forgetting that you are not. Empathy is not **sympathy**: sympathy means sharing a feeling through "affective contagion" and being concerned for the other; empathy means representing what they feel while keeping the distinction between self and other.

Batat defines the empathic experience through three somewhat independent components (after Decety):
- **emotional**: the salesperson shares the customer's emotional state (important above all in disappointment), but can also react by withdrawing;
- **cognitive**: they consciously put themselves into the customer's mind and anticipate tacit needs (this can be trained through reading or films);
- **motivational**: they care about the customer's well-being.

Following Zaki & Ochsner, employees are "perceivers" and customers "targets"; the processes are experience sharing, mentalizing and prosocial concern. The author's conclusion: the aim of empathy is to help the customer, not to sell them something.

As early as chapter 1, the author speaks of employees' **emotional capital**: a positive attitude toward work, spontaneity and kindness, recognition of the customer's rights. These principles are obvious but hard to follow when people are tired or standing for hours.

## Application

- In activation briefs involving staff (promoters, hosts, salespeople), describe the customer's likely state at each moment, not just the script.
- Train teams on the three components: recognize the emotion, understand the need, act out of care.

## Example

Car rental (mini-case 8.1): a customer in the city for the first time gets a map; one who arrives at night, tired, is helped with the luggage and offered a drink; the car's options are set in their language. Small gestures, but they differentiate in an industry where the offer is almost identical.

## Questions

- Can empathy be demanded "on command" without producing exhausting emotional labor?
- In which situations does excessive emotional empathy block the employee instead of helping them?
