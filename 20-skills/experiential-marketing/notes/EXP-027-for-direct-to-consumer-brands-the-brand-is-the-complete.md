# For direct-to-consumer brands, the brand is the complete chain of the experience

> **The idea in one sentence:** when you sell directly to consumers, you no longer have a single moment of truth at the shelf but a chain (website, delivery, unboxing, use, replenishment), and one broken link damages the brand and can reach social media at once.

## Explanation

Sergio Pereira (president of Quill.com, formerly at Barilla and Conagra) describes the shift from the linear journey of packaged consumer goods (ad → shelf → home) to the experience brands created by the smartphone and by disintermediation. For them, experience is the heart of the brand:

- **Blue Apron**: the first moment of truth is the website (creating an account, choosing the meal plan); next comes the box arriving at the doorstep (ice, perishable ingredients, intact packaging), then the prepping and cooking (clear versus unclear instructions) and the finished product. Weak customer retention signals a suboptimal link somewhere. One detail: being asked for payment information before seeing the menu choices creates uncertainty and loses prospects early in the journey.
- **Harry's**: an easy purchase, a clever box, blades that are "good enough" and cheaper, flexible replenishment emails. Harry's owns the experience throughout the process; Gillette surrenders it when the product leaves the factory.
- **Indochino**: a custom suit for the cost of an off-the-rack suit; the in-store measurement makes customers feel special and catered to, but a tailoring mistake that means sending the suit back breaks the chain.

The impact on the Four Ps: the **product** is designed for microsegments with their own values (Allbirds, UNTUCKit); a premium **price** holds if the experience is flawless; **place** (distribution) is the most changed of the Four Ps (packaging designed for the shelf breaks in single-unit shipments); **promotion** is threatened by reviews and posts about failures, so social listening and the customer service team become sources of insight.

The experience designers interviewed in *Goddess Guide to Branding* (SRC-016) complete the picture. Janet T. Planet (Disney, Virgin Voyages) always asks how to turn a guest from an observer into a participant, and she insists on unboxing, because expectations have risen enormously (her example: the box of Seed probiotics). Katie Conway recommends investing emotionally in two or three key moments, where the category has pain points.

Pereira calls for UX/CX talent, agile research and a supply chain centered on customer delight, not only on efficiency: "the buyer is in control."

## Application

- Map every link in the experience of a DTC brand and mark where disappointment can occur.
- Treat the shipping box, the confirmation email and the returns page as brand materials, with the same care as the advertising.
- Remove unnecessary friction from the first steps (payment requirements, long forms).

## Example

Harry's later also entered Target's stores: a sign that brands born online also look for a physical presence to build awareness.

## Questions

- How do you build a DTC brand when you do not control the courier?
- Which link in the experience should be the brand's "signature"?
