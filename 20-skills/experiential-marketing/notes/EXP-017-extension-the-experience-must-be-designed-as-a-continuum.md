# Extension: the experience must be designed as a continuum without ruptures, from the store to the home

> **The idea in one sentence:** the third E replaces the static "place" of distribution with the idea that the experience continues over time and across spaces (store, online, home), and any rupture becomes a source of disappointment.

## Explanation

Batat starts from the finding of a **discontinuity**: many customers do not get the experience they expected because the chain breaks somewhere. She draws on John Dewey: each experience takes something from past ones and changes the quality of future ones, through two principles: **continuity** and **interaction**. Experiential learning makes the second wine tasting different from the first, and an online luxury purchase shape the visit to the store.

Two dimensions of "Extension":
1. **The experience continuum.** Carù & Cova show that experiences range from those fully organized by the provider (a resort, a mall) to self-organized ones (the skier who builds their own holiday). Sometimes the customer does not co-create but creates value alone (auto-creation).
2. **Intra- and extra-domestic experience.** Quality is usually studied in the store, hotel or restaurant, but the customer's home matters just as much. Designer furniture bought in an elegant store can be spoiled by an external delivery provider who is untrained, late and without a uniform. The firm has to choose and train its partners so that the experience continues into the private sphere. Pereira (Kellogg) describes the same risk for direct-to-consumer brands ([[EXP-027-for-direct-to-consumer-brands-the-brand-is-the-complete|EXP-027]]).

## Application

- Map the "handoffs": where does the customer pass from you to a partner (courier, installer, call center, platform)? That is where ruptures happen.
- Design objects that carry the experience home: packaging that reproduces the restaurant's atmosphere, local products to take with you.
- For novices (a first sports car, a first gourmet restaurant), strengthen continuity with explanations and guidance.

## Example

The "tourist-eater" (mini-case 6.1) goes through three linked stages: preparing through guides, films and testimonials (a cognitive stage); discovering on the spot the codes of another food culture; then bringing products and recipes home, turning the everyday into a "new elsewhere." Uber Eats, Airbnb and AmazonFresh (mini-case 6.2) move the food experience from outside the home to inside it.

## Questions

- Who is responsible for the home experience when delivery is outsourced: the brand, the retailer or the courier?
- Can a brand be too present in the continuum (notifications, e-mails) and break the experience through excess?
