# Customer experience has five markers: holistic, cumulative, subjective, assemblage, contrasting

> **The idea in one sentence:** an experience cannot be designed piece by piece, because it is lived as a whole, it builds up over time, it differs from person to person, it combines heterogeneous elements and it mixes opposites.

## Explanation

Batat names five features that set an experience apart from a simple act of purchase:

| Marker | What it means | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Holistic** | the whole is superior to the sum of its parts; it involves the person physically, psychologically, socially, culturally | do not optimize a single touchpoint in isolation |
| **Cumulative** | past experiences inform the present and the future; emotions and knowledge build up in layers | each interaction prepares the next one |
| **Subjective and personal** | the same restaurant experience is pleasant to one person and unpleasant to another | leave room for personalization; do not assume a shared perception |
| **Assemblage** (Deleuze & Guattari) | a dynamic combination of sensorial, physical, relational, emotional and cognitive elements | if you reorganize the elements, you get a different experience |
| **Contrasting** | the customer seeks hedonic and utilitarian benefits at the same time and judges both rationally and emotionally | do not class the product as only "hedonic" or only "utilitarian" |

The cumulative marker is, in fact, the definition of customer experience in the digital marketing textbook by Zahay et al. ([[EXP-002-customer-experience-is-the-lived-sum-of-all-interactions|EXP-002]]); here the sources agree completely.

For the assemblage, the author takes up Bernd Schmitt's five categories: *Sense* (sensory), *Feel* (affective), *Think* (cognitive), *Act* (physical experiences, behaviors, lifestyle), *Relate* (belonging to a group or a culture). For the contrasting marker, the example is a sneaker: the buyer weighs its practical qualities (comfort, resistance) and, at the same time, its stylishness and brand image.

## Application

- Check any experience concept with the five questions: is it coherent as a whole? What does it leave behind for next time? How do different profiles live it? Which elements does it combine? Which rational benefit and which pleasure does it offer at the same time?
- When the communication is "boring by nature" (instructions, safety), add the hedonic layer.

## Example

- *Holistic:* the "New Sephora Experience" links the trends table at the entrance, the Beauty Hub, the connected mirror with the "Virtual Artist", the Color Profile application, the Beauty Board community and the Beauty Classes.
- *Cumulative:* Heathrow ("Make every trip more pleasant") treats parking, the rail link, the shops and the Wi-Fi as a single experience, even though they are separate companies.
- *Contrasting:* the safety videos of Air New Zealand (themed on *The Hobbit*), Virgin America (a musical) and Delta (the 80s) turn a mandatory briefing into entertainment that passengers memorize and share.

## Questions

- If the experience is completely subjective, can a firm still "design" it? Where is the limit?
- Are the five markers independent, or do "holistic" and "assemblage" basically say the same thing?
