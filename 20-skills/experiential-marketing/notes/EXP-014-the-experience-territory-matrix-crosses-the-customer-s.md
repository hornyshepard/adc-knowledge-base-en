# The Experience Territory Matrix crosses the customer's perception of respect with the company's customer-centricity

> **The idea in one sentence:** the first E of the mix (Experience) is managed as a portfolio, and each experience falls into one of four "territories", according to how respected the customer feels and how customer-centric the company is.

## Explanation

Batat builds the Experience Territory Matrix (ETM) as a long-term planning tool: the firm reviews its portfolio of experiences and decides where to invest, what to renew and what to create. She starts from Pine & Gilmore's four realms (entertainment: passive, absorption; educational: active, absorption; escapist: active, immersion; aesthetic: passive, immersion), but faults them for describing the context of consumption rather than the customer's perception.

The two axes of the ETM:
- **the consumer's perception of respect**: attention and valuing, understanding, responsibility;
- **the company's customer-centricity** (after Shah et al., 2006): management commitment, organizational shift, process support, revised financial metrics. The obstacles are organizational structure, culture, process and financial metrics.

| | Low customer-centricity | High customer-centricity |
|---|---|---|
| **High respect** | **Enchantment**: the pleasure comes from interaction among customers, with the brand present | **Re-enchantment**: the customer feels respected, valued, unique |
| **Low respect** | **Disenchantment**: frustration, disappointment, switching to competitors | **Enchantment-gap**: the company is customer-oriented but does not understand what respect means to the customer |

## Application

- Place each of the brand's experiences (store, call center, event, app) in the matrix, based on customer data, not on the company's intentions.
- For "enchantment", invest in socializing among customers; for "enchantment-gap", turn to qualitative research to find out what respect means to them.

## Example

- *Enchantment:* the giant "Sprite shower" on the beaches of Rio, shaped like a soda fountain.
- *Re-enchantment:* SNCF / DDB Paris, "Welcome, Goodbye": travelers surprised at the station with details from their personal lives (a heavy metal fan greeted by a band); more than 1 million views in the first week.
- *Disenchantment:* H&M's "Coolest monkey in the jungle" hoodie, worn by a black boy.
- *Enchantment-gap:* Epson printers, accused of planned obsolescence.

## Questions

- Why would "enchantment" arise precisely when the company is not very customer-centric? Are you convinced by the author's social explanation?
- Perceived respect has no validated scale. How would you measure it in a study?
