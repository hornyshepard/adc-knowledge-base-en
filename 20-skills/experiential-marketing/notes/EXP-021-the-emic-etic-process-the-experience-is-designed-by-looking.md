# The emic/etic process: the experience is designed by looking at the consumption culture from the inside and from the outside

> **The idea in one sentence:** the seventh E replaces the firm's top-down process with an iterative one that combines the meanings customers hold from inside their culture (emic) with the firm's comparative view from the outside (etic).

## Explanation

The distinction comes from the linguist Kenneth Pike (1954, 1967): *phonemic* (the sounds that carry meaning in a particular language) vs. *phonetic* (the sounds common to all languages). Applied to behavior, the **etic** viewpoint studies the system from outside, the **emic** one from inside.

| Emic | Etic |
|---|---|
| behavior seen from inside the system | behavior seen from outside it |
| a single consumption culture or subculture | several cultures, compared |
| hidden meanings, revealed by the observer | meanings constructed by the analyst |
| criteria valid on the inside | criteria considered universal |

The emic view requires ethnographic immersion: getting familiar with the culture, learning its norms and codes, getting close without being intrusive, listening actively and building empathic relationships. The etic view allows comparisons across markets but cannot capture what it means to belong to a culture. Batat asks for the two to be combined, and the model of Berry & Parasuraman (1991) shows how: you start with "imposed" constructs from the outside, enrich them with the particularities of each culture and refine them to remove what is not equivalent. What survives are the "derived etics". The background framework is Consumer Culture Theory (Arnould & Thompson, 2005).

**Sociocultural mapping of the customer journey.** The traditional map is linear and one-way (prepurchase, purchase, postpurchase). Batat proposes an iterative, bi-directional and culturally embedded map, built from customers' stories, not from the stories the firm tells itself about them. It reveals the "decalage" (mismatch) between firm and customer.

## Application

- For a regional campaign: start from the global platform (etic), test it in each market through immersive research (emic), keep only the elements that prove equivalent.
- Build the journey map from customer interviews and diaries, not from an internal workshop.

## Example

The meaning of a month in the calendar depends on culture: August means summer holidays and heat in the northern hemisphere, but winter and work in New Zealand (Batat's example, after Geertz). A "global" promotional calendar ignores this emic meaning.

## Questions

- Batat considers emic and etic complementary, but in the models she cites the emic view disappears from the final explanation. Does it matter?
- How "emic" can a researcher be who is also an employee of the brand?
