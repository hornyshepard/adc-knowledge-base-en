# Collaborative marketing has three levels of involvement: DIY, collaboration, co-innovation

> **The idea in one sentence:** not every customer can or wants to co-create in the same way; the brand chooses the level of involvement according to the competence and engagement it can ask for.

## Explanation

Collaborative marketing puts customers "to work" in innovation, services, design or communication: they vote for a design, a name or a logo, animate a community, or co-create the product with the firm. Batat ranks the practices on three levels:

| Level | What the customer does | Competence required |
|---|---|---|
| **DIY** | customizes an object from the options offered; the product becomes an extension of their identity | low |
| **Collaboration** | *passive*: gives an opinion; *active*: takes part in design or promotion, in a project defined and framed by the firm | medium |
| **Co-innovation** | makes technical contributions that the firm integrates into specifications, launch, commercialization | high |

The advantages for the firm: it benefits from customers' creative potential, gets to know them better and retains them longer, and co-creators talk about what they created. The customer can be involved upstream (ideas), along the way (testing, prototypes) or downstream (communication).

## Application

- For a UGC campaign, set the level first: a simple vote (passive collaboration) needs a different mechanic from a product contest (co-innovation).
- Promise a reward proportional to the effort: visibility, money, the name on the packaging.
- Think from the start about how participants will talk about their contribution; that is where the media gain lies.

## Example

- *Lay's "Do Us a Flavor"*: ideas submitted through Facebook, votes on Twitter, Vine and Instagram, more than 14 million proposals; the winner received a million dollars, after judging by culinary experts.
- *Finnair "Quality Hunters"*: more than 5,300 applications from 90 countries, four bloggers who traveled and wrote freely; 260 new service ideas.
- *LEGO Ideas*: a project that gathers 10,000 votes is reviewed; the inventor gets a percentage of the sales and is credited on the packaging.
- *Mountain Dew "DEWmocracy"* (2007): an online game in which fans created the "Voltage" flavor.
- *Disney*: families filmed their children's reaction to the announcement of a trip; the winners appeared in a TV advertisement.

## Questions

- What reputational risks arise when you let the public make proposals (Lay's odd flavors)? How do you manage them?
- Is voting on a name "co-creation", or just engagement marketing in disguise?
