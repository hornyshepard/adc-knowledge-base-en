# Emotional touchpoints: a touchpoint is defined by the emotion it produces, not by the channel

> **The idea in one sentence:** the sixth E reverses the usual logic: you do not start from the list of channels (website, store, call center) but from the moments that moved the customer, and only then identify the contact that produced them.

## Explanation

In common practice, touchpoints are the functional transactions between customer and firm along the journey: the ad, the reviews, friends, the website, the store, customer service. The CRM literature calls them "channels" and optimizes them for efficiency and profitability. Shostack (1984) had introduced the idea in service design (*service blueprinting*), speaking of the "orchestration" of touchpoints. Yet integrated marketing did not treat emotion as a connector either.

Batat proposes **emotional touchpoints**: attention shifts to the emotional impact of the moments when the customer interacts with services and people, especially the moments that activate memories. A short, mundane contact can matter as much as a whole dinner. The method comes from nursing: Dewar and colleagues (2009) used it in a UK program on compassionate care, based on the stories of 16 patients and 12 relatives.

Benefits for the firm: you learn how customers feel; you involve them in designing the service; you capture both the positive and the negative aspects; you learn from real stories; you see the sociocultural factors; employees develop their empathy. The method relies on storytelling: the customer tells their story in their own words. Because people usually have a limited emotional vocabulary (Freshwater & Stickley), the firm offers them words and images for emotions.

## Application

- In a CX audit, start with the question "which moment stayed with you, and what did you feel then?", not with the channel map.
- For a campaign insight, look for moments with a strong negative emotion: that is often where the tension the brand can resolve lies.
- Analyze in four stages: preparation, carrying out, follow-up, moving to action.

## Example

In Dewar's study, the patients' stories led to practical changes, but also to discussions about the sociocultural dimensions of care that a satisfaction questionnaire would not have brought to light.

## Questions

- What is lost if you abandon the functional channel map entirely?
- Can an FMCG brand with no direct customer contact use the method?
