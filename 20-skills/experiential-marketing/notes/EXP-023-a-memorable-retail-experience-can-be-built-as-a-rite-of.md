# A memorable retail experience can be built as a rite of passage: preparation, separation, liminoid phase, reassimilation

> **The idea in one sentence:** a commercial space becomes extraordinary when it takes customers out of their usual status and leads them across a threshold, as in a ritual, and what stays afterwards is not the decor but the sense of being transformed.

## Explanation

Parsons, Maclaran and Chatzidakis criticize the reduction of space to the "p" of place in the marketing mix and to store atmospherics. From the Parisian arcades and the department stores to themed flagship stores, retail has always built dream worlds, some of them almost "cathedrals" of the brand. To explain why some of them leave a mark on people, the authors turn to anthropology.

Arnold van Gennep described **rites of passage** in three stages: separation, liminal period and reassimilation. In the liminal phase, social status is suspended: the person is "betwixt and between" identities. Victor Turner adds that people caught between statuses develop a sense of *communitas* through their shared experiences. Themed spaces that convey a sense of "elsewhereness" have exactly this quality. They can produce **transformational** experiences in Pine and Gilmore's sense: the memory of sensory stimuli fades, the transformation lasts.

The restaurant **Dans le Noir?** (Paris, London, Barcelona, St Petersburg), where guests eat in the dark, served by blind staff, follows the structure step by step:

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| Preparation | lit bar, color-coded menus, bags, watches and phones left in lockers (being stripped of social status) |
| Separation | darkened corridor, left hand on the shoulder of the person in front, the blind server as "ritual elder" |
| Liminoid phase | roles reverse: customers become helpless, the servers move with confidence; touch, regression to childhood |
| Reassimilation | the return to the light; pride, empathy, a feeling of being transformed |

The authors set beside it the other kind of meaningful place, the everyday one: the "third place" that draws people with a sense of homeyness ([[EXP-022-physical-space-survives-as-a-third-place-a-social-place|EXP-022]]).

## Application

- Design an activation or a launch as a rite: a clear threshold, a rule that suspends normality, a moment of reversal, a memorable exit.
- Decide what customers "leave behind" at the entrance (phone, hurry, social role) and what they get on the way out.
- Use senses other than sight: the darkness at Dans le Noir? makes taste and touch the protagonists.

## Example

The name with a question mark, Dans le Noir?, asks who is in fact "in the dark": for one dinner, the sighted customer becomes the disabled one. The commercial experience also carries an idea about empathy.

## Questions

- How would you turn a product launch into a rite of passage? What would the threshold be?
- When does a brand "ritual" become empty theater, with no real transformation?
