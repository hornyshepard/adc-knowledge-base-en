# Immersion is the way into the experience: five factors in Batat, five design elements in Kotler, largely the same

> **The idea in one sentence:** the customer "enters" an experience when a setting takes them out of their routine; Batat and Kotler's *Marketing 6.0* describe, with different lists, almost the same conditions for this to happen.

## Explanation

For Batat, immersion is a mode of access to a pleasurable experience: the customer is transported into a setting different enough from daily habits to feel intense emotions. To get there, they re-acquire the setting through three processes (Carù & Cova): nesting, exploration and tagging. The physical setting is Bitner's **servicescape** (1992): ambient conditions, spatial layout, signs and symbols. It triggers approach (you stay, you explore) or avoidance (crowding and clutter make you leave).

*Marketing 6.0* starts from the metaphor of the live concert and gives a design list. Side by side, the two lists largely overlap:

| Batat: immersive factor | Kotler 6.0: element of immersion | What you check |
|---|---|---|
| Atmospheric (light, smell, sound) | Multisensory | are several senses involved, coherently? |
| Functional (information on price, delivery, use) | Frictionless | tickets, payment, access with no hassle? |
| Human and social | Interactive + participative | is there dialogue and something to *do*? |
| Symbolic and identity | Storytelling | which story ties everything together, and what does it mean for the customer? |
| Cognitive (the customer's expertise) | (none) | can a novice get in too? |

The differences are instructive. Batat adds the **cognitive factor**: those who know more enter the brand's universe more easily. Kotler puts more weight on **participation** and illustrates it with the Apple Store: products to try out, trained frontline staff, "Today at Apple" sessions, payment anywhere in the store. Online, Batat separates the symbolic factor from the identity factor and arrives at six factors.

## Application

- Use the table as a checklist for a pop-up, a stand or a website: does each row have at least one designed element?
- Do not forget the cognitive row: a short initiation (a guided tasting, a tutorial) makes immersion accessible to novices.
- Watch the tension between "frictionless" and "participative": participation takes effort, so dose it.

## Example

West Edmonton Mall (Canada), built under the influence of the traditional urban bazaars of Persia, attracts around 22 million visitors each year with a waterpark, a skating rink and themed streets (Batat). Coca-Cola's "Friendly Twist" put bottles on campuses that could only be opened when twisted together with a matching bottle: interactivity and participation in a single gesture (Kotler 6.0).

## Questions

- Holt (1995) criticizes immersion models for overrating sensorial factors. Did you agree after the last activation you saw?
- Can a brand create immersion without a themed physical setting?
