# The experiential marketing mix (7Es) replaces the 7Ps with decisions centered on the customer experience

> **The idea in one sentence:** Wided Batat proposes replacing each of the 7Ps of the classic mix with an "E": a controllable area of decision that starts from the experience the customer lives, not from the product.

## Explanation

Batat starts from the observation that almost any offering is sold today as an "experience", while the 7Ps logic (product, price, place, promotion, people, process, physical environment) stays centered on the good and on the firm. She proposes an **experiential marketing mix** with seven components, each detailed in a separate note:

| Classic 7Ps | 7Es (Batat) | What changes | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product | **Experience** | you manage a portfolio of experiences, not of products | [[EXP-014-the-experience-territory-matrix-crosses-the-customer-s\|EXP-014]] |
| Price | **Exchange** | what counts is the value co-created and exchanged, not only the price | [[EXP-015-exchange-in-the-experiential-mix-price-gives-way-to-value\|EXP-015]] |
| Place | **Extension** | the experience is a continuum, including at home and online | [[EXP-017-extension-the-experience-must-be-designed-as-a-continuum\|EXP-017]] |
| Promotion | **Emphasis** | brand culture and storytelling instead of "push" messages | [[BRD-037-brand-culture-is-a-universe-of-myths-rituals-and-values\|BRD-037]] |
| People | **Empathy capital** | employees take the customer's perspective | [[EXP-018-empathy-capital-replaces-people-employees-must-first-think\|EXP-018]] |
| Physical environment | **Emotional touchpoints** | touchpoints are defined by the emotion they produce | [[EXP-019-emotional-touchpoints-a-touchpoint-is-defined-by-the\|EXP-019]] |
| Process | **Emic/etic process** | an iterative process: the customer's view from the inside plus the firm's view from the outside | [[EXP-021-the-emic-etic-process-the-experience-is-designed-by-looking\|EXP-021]] |

The seven Es are interdependent: a decision in one area affects the others, and each company composes its own mix. The mix rests on the drivers–markers–outcomes framework of chapter 3 and applies both in store and online, in the context the author calls *phygital*.

Batat is not the only one to rewrite the mix. Wuebben proposes EP² (Engagement, Experiences, Personalization, Passion), also from a critique of company-centered Ps. The two proposals agree on the diagnosis, but the 7Es keep a one-to-one match with the 7Ps, so they are easier to use as an audit grid.

## Application

- Use the table as an audit grid: for each P of a brand, ask what the E equivalent is and what is missing.
- In activation or retail briefs, write objectives by E ("Emotional touchpoints: what emotion do we want at the store entrance?", "Extension: what stays at home after the event?").
- Do not treat the 7Es as a mechanical checklist: choose the 2–3 Es where the brand can make a real difference.

## Example

Nespresso (mini-case 2.3) touches several Es at once: the concept store with coffee tastings (Experience), the club in which each buyer is treated as an "insider" (Exchange, Empathy), the campaign with George Clooney and "What else?" (Emphasis), online and offline channels linked to each other (Extension).

## Questions

- Are the 7Es a new model or a relabeling of the 7Ps and of the ideas of Pine & Gilmore and Schmitt? Which concrete decision changes?
- Batat ties "Extension" sometimes to Place, sometimes to "Physical environment" (ch. 6), although the physical environment goes to Emotional touchpoints (ch. 9). How would you resolve the ambiguity?
