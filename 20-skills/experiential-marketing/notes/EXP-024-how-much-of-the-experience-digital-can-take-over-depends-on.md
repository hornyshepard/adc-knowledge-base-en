# How much of the experience digital can take over depends on the human role: four scenarios, from the checkout to hospitality

> **The idea in one sentence:** the experience of the future is hybrid, and the right decision is not "digital or human" but how central the human is at that touchpoint: from a mere facilitator of the transaction to the product itself.

## Explanation

After describing the five elements of immersion ([[EXP-008-immersion-is-the-way-into-the-experience-five-factors-in|EXP-008]]), chapter 4 of *Marketing 6.0* shows that the future is also **hybrid**. After the pandemic, 71 percent of consumers shop in physical stores as often or more often than before (Mood Media). Physical touchpoints bring instant gratification, the senses and human-to-human interaction. Digital touchpoints bring efficiency, a broader assortment, reviews and personalization. There is no one-size-fits-all strategy: the mix depends on the role the human plays.

| Scenario | Human role | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Human presence **facilitates transactions** (cashier, ticket counter) | minimal | full automation, frictionless (kiosks, Amazon One palm payment at Panera) |
| 2. Human engagement **bridges the trust gap** (cars, houses) | important for trust | partial digitalization (VR test drives, Tesla remote test drives with no salesperson), but the dealership stays |
| 3. Human relationship **enhances the product** (wealth management, B2B sales engineers) | co-creator | hybrid: self-service dashboard plus remote interaction |
| 4. Human experience **is the product** (hospitality, health care) | central | the human augmented by technology, not replaced |

The typical mistake is to automate in scenario 4 to save money. A global PwC survey cited by the authors found that two-thirds of customers believe businesses have disregarded the human element of the customer experience. The logic is the same as in *Marketing 5.0* ("Machines Are Cool, but Humans Are Warm"), now applied to each touchpoint.

## Application

- For each touchpoint in a CX project, decide the scenario (1–4) before you propose technology.
- In scenarios 1 and 2, technology removes waiting and fear; in 3 and 4, it gives the human information and time.
- Turn the store from a sales channel into an experiential center. Otherwise it becomes a slower e-commerce.

## Example

Ritz-Carlton, scenario 4: every staff member can spend up to $2,000 per incident and guest to resolve a problem, without approval. The authors note that even the most advanced AI cannot replicate such discretionary decision making. In hospitality and health care, technology takes over administrative tasks, so frontline staff have more time for customers. Henn na, the hotel staffed by robots, had to replace half of them with human personnel.

## Questions

- Which scenario would you put a cosmetics brand with in-store consultation in? And a digital bank?
- Is automation in scenarios 3–4 a false economy? What data would you need to prove it?
