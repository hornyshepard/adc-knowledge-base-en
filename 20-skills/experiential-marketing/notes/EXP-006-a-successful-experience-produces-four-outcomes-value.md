# A successful experience produces four outcomes: value, transformation, competency, memorability

> **The idea in one sentence:** the success of an experience is not just satisfaction: it creates perceived value, it can change the customer's identity, it builds their competencies and, above all, it leaves a memory that decides whether they come back.

## Explanation

**1. Value.** Batat brings together several classifications of customer value:

| Type | In short |
|---|---|
| *Value-in-exchange* | economic: what I get for what I give (price, quality for the price) |
| *Value-in-marketplace* | Woodall's forms: net value, marketing value, sale value, rational value, derived value |
| *Value-in-time* | anticipated, transaction, consumption, remembered: a mirror of the stages of the experience |
| *Value-in-use* | the service-dominant logic: value arises when the service is used |
| *Value-in-possession* | possessions as an "extended self" and a sign of success (Belk) |
| *Value-in-experience* | Holbrook's dimensions: extrinsic/intrinsic, self-/other-oriented, active/reactive |

**2. Transformation.** Some experiences produce an irreversible change: a new set of beliefs, values and desires replaces the old one (becoming vegan, immigrating, becoming a grandparent).

**3. Competency.** The customer learns from experiences. According to Bonnemaizon & Batat, consumer competence has a cognitive side (interpreting marketing messages), an instrumental side (using products) and a usage side (expressing oneself through products).

**4. Memorability.** Memory is a broad notion; memorability (Carù & Cova) is about what is unforgettable. A memorable experience is a meaningful episode, recalled after it has ended, selected on the basis of emotional evaluation. It predicts reliving, repurchase and recommendation. In chapter 2, Batat even suggests measuring the experience through the intensity of the memory and the pleasure, not only through satisfaction.

## Application

- For each activation, define a target outcome: what value, what does the customer learn, what concrete memory do they leave with?
- Design "memory anchors": the souvenir photo, a summary at the end, an object taken home.
- Place the anchors where the peak-end rule works (Kahneman, cited in [[PIT-019-the-s-a-l-t-ending-controls-the-last-impression-following|PIT-019]]): at the emotional peak and at the end.

## Example

Abercrombie & Fitch (mini-case 2.2) photographed customers with the models at the entrance, a "memory mix" that is tangible (the photo) and intangible (the nightclub-like concept store). The tour guides in mini-case 4.6 sum up at the end what was seen and how rare it is.

## Questions

- Can all four outcomes be tracked with KPIs? Which one stays only qualitative?
- Can a brand deliberately trigger a "transformative" experience without becoming manipulative?
